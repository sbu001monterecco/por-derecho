## 1. Controlling proposition

The 2016 burofax episode must not be described as a simple “unsent draft”.

The controlled record separates four propositions:

1. **Preparation.** A formal burofax to the Administrador Concursal was prepared in July 2016 following the wider June 2016 forensic/accounting work and professional discussions.
2. **Counsel refusal / mandate conflict.** On 13 July 2016 Juan Tomás Parrilla Suárez wrote that he would not evaluate a pleading affecting a proceeding he directed because he had not prepared it, and stated in substance that either he directed LPB’s concurso representation or another lawyer did. Patricia Domínguez immediately clarified that Gil was not seeking to replace him, that the purpose was to correct serious discrepancies, and that the document had been prepared for Parrilla to review, adapt and sign/send. She later asked him not to resign while the issue was discussed.
3. **Notice nevertheless sent.** Gil Marer states directly that he personally ensured the burofax was sent to the Administrador Concursal. Existing repository controls already record the 12 July 2016 burofax as sent/received with delivery proof. Therefore **Parrilla’s refusal did not cause the notice never to reach the AC**. The relevant professional-responsibility question is the refusal to adopt, review, adapt, sponsor or execute the requested protective route as instructed, and the consequences of the wider pattern of advice/action/inaction — not a false proposition that the AC never received notice.
4. **PwC contemporaneous involvement.** PwC/Landwell professionals were engaged in the same 2016 factual/professional perimeter. The email record shows PwC involvement in the June 2016 Community/accounting work; PwC coordination with the client and Parrilla; and later confirmation by Miguel Hernández Lorenzo that PwC had spoken with the AC. Hernández stated on 12 September 2016 that the conversation with the AC had been general, that it did not go into detail on the proposed contracts, and that PwC had not produced a contemporaneous written report or exchanged emails with the AC about it. Patricia then asked for a written reconstruction for a meeting with Parrilla.

## 3. Separation-of-responsibility rule

Do not reduce the matter to a single actor.

The current analysis must preserve independent, cumulative lanes:

- **Molina / Montelanza dissident lane** — actor-specific conduct and authority issues.
- **Acosta Matos / private-actor lane** — actor-specific acquisition, control, access, debt, title, operation and benefit questions.
- **Administrador Concursal lane** — statutory/court-appointed duties, knowledge, decisions, omissions, liquidation, protection of the estate and response to notice.
- **Judicial lane** — rulings, procedural control and later challenged conduct, kept separate from the AC and private actors.
- **Juan Tomás Parrilla lane** — mandate, instructions, advice, filings, refusals, follow-through, withdrawal/continuity, counterfactual protection and causation.
- **PwC lane** — contemporaneous knowledge, advice/work product, discussions with Parrilla, client and AC, and what PwC did or did not escalate/document.
- **Other counsel/adviser lanes** — individual mandate and action records.

Separate responsibility does not mean separate reality: common chronology, knowledge and causation may be analysed together, but liability and intent remain actor-specific.
