# 7 June 2018 — AC knowledge, affirmative action and enablement test

**Status:** evidence-led analytical report. This report does not state a criminal finding or conclude intent as a fact. It separates what the accessible record establishes from what remains an inference or production question.

## Priority thesis

The strongest current evidential formulation is not limited to omission. The accessible record supports a sequence in which the Administrador Concursal (AC), Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte, had prior knowledge of the Community/exploitation conflict, exercised affirmative control over LPB possession/access questions in early 2018, and—immediately after the 7 June events—adopted a contemporaneous justificatory position, as reported by LPB counsel, concerning the Community's security arrangements and Acosta Matos's presence in the complex.

This is materially stronger than a theory of passive inaction. It supports a focused inquiry into **knowledge → authority → affirmative act/validation → effect → later follow-through**.

It does **not yet prove** that the AC instructed the physical acts of 7 June in advance, knowingly relied on authority he knew to be absent, or acted with criminal intent. Those propositions require the missing pre-event communications, Community/security authority record, title verification and any AC–Community/CAM/Matos communications.

## 1. Longstanding knowledge benchmark — November 2015

A contemporaneous email chain records that Juan Tomás Parrilla asked the AC to become involved after being told that the Community was contemplating selling the complex or ceasing hotel exploitation. The AC replied that direct management of the Community exceeded his functions at that phase, but stated that the debtor side should bring proposed actions with his authorisation and that he would consider actions proposed in defence of the interests of the concurso.

**Supports:**
- the Community/exploitation threat was brought directly to the AC years before June 2018;
- the AC expressly recognised a mechanism for considering protective action in defence of the concurso;
- later assertions that the Community/exploitation controversy was outside his awareness should be tested against this record.

**Does not prove:**
- that the 2015 concern was identical to the 2018 events;
- that a particular protective action was legally required in 2015;
- later intent.

## 2. January 2018 — affirmative possession/access perimeter

The 3 January 2018 request attributed to the AC was framed around taking material possession of the **apartments belonging to LPB**, together with their keys/access credentials and information about third-party occupants.

The 18 January follow-up broadened the wording. In addition to requesting documentary support for LPB assets and receivables, it stated that, **for the purpose of taking physical possession of the complex**, the AC requested the electronic access code and instructions to modify it.

Cuatrecasas received this exchange on 19 January 2018 after the client expressly raised concern and requested advice.

**Priority question:** what was the legal and physical perimeter of the AC's intended possession, and what safeguards separated LPB apartments from Matkator assets, CEXP rights, Community/common access, third-party units and the wider hotel operation?

**Supports:** affirmative AC action concerning possession and complex-wide access credentials before 7 June.

**Does not prove:** an instruction to CAM/Matos or an unlawful purpose.

## 3. 7–8 June 2018 — direct knowledge plus contemporaneous validation

The accessible correspondence records the 7 June physical/security/access events being reported immediately to LPB/Aweswell counsel.

The strongest affirmative-commission evidence located so far is the report by Juan Tomás Parrilla of his direct conversation with the AC on 8 June. According to that contemporaneous report, the AC's position was that:

- security in the complex had been agreed by the Community at a meeting the previous week;
- Acosta Matos could be in the complex because, according to the AC, it owned approximately 20 apartments; and
- Acosta Matos could be present in its properties and common areas.

This is not merely an absence of objection. It is a contemporaneous **justification/validation position attributed directly to the AC** immediately after the event.

**Supports:**
- direct and immediate AC knowledge of the event;
- an affirmative position treating Community security authority and Acosta Matos's asserted ownership/common-area status as sufficient to justify at least aspects of the presence/control complained of;
- a powerful production question as to what documents, title data, Community resolutions and legal analysis the AC relied upon before adopting that position.

**Does not yet prove:**
- that the AC ordered the 7 June physical acts;
- that the AC supplied keys, guards, locksmith instructions or operational directions to CAM/Matos;
- that the AC knew the asserted Community authority or Acosta Matos title premise was false;
- criminal intent.

## 4. The “20 apartments” premise requires forensic verification

A contemporaneous February 2018 internal acquisition/title workbook derived from the 30 June 2017 Gesvalt ownership dataset contains no entry under Acosta Matos/CAM/Construcciones Acosta Matos in the reviewed owner fields. That dataset therefore does **not** corroborate the reported 8 June “20 apartments” premise.

However, this does not establish that CAM owned no units by 7 June 2018: acquisitions may have occurred after the June 2017 valuation, and the workbook was not a certified 7 June 2018 Registry snapshot.

**Required proof:** obtain the certified finca-by-finca Registry position as at 7 June 2018 and identify the source relied upon by the AC for the “20 apartments” statement.

Even if ownership of individual apartments is established, a second and distinct question remains: **what legal authority did ownership of individual units confer to participate in complex-wide security, access control, lock changes, hotel operational control or exclusion of LPB/Matkator/CEXP interests?** Ownership and authority must not be collapsed.

## 5. 14–15 June 2018 — document-production and protective-response gap

Juan Tomás Parrilla records repeated attempts to obtain the Community meeting material from the AC. The AC's formal response, as relayed by Juan Tomás, was that he had not received the Community minutes. Juan Tomás also considered a generic “protect the estate” filing premature absent a precautionary measure from Arrecife, while inviting more concrete proposed relief and simultaneously working on an appeal and exceptional suspension request.

This evidence prevents an overbroad claim that counsel simply did nothing. It sharpens the AC question instead:

> Before relying upon or validating Community/security authority affecting LPB and the complex, what independent evidence did the AC obtain of the meeting, resolution, mandate, security contract, payer, instructions and legal limits?

The absence of minutes in his possession does not answer what verification was performed before adopting the 8 June justificatory position.

## 6. Intent: current evidential classification

**Direct knowledge:** strongly supported by contemporaneous communications.

**Affirmative acts:** supported by the January possession/access demands and the 8 June validation/justification position attributed to the AC.

**Affirmative facilitation/enablement:** supported as a serious inference to be tested, particularly if the AC's position functioned defensively to legitimise the private actors' continued presence/control and if no prompt corrective boundary was imposed.

**Pre-event operational enablement:** not yet established. No direct pre-7-June AC→CAM/Matos instruction, key transfer, security instruction or similar communication has yet been located in the accessible Gmail corpus.

**Knowing absence of authority:** not yet established as a fact. The record strongly supports that authority, debt and Community legitimacy were disputed and had been notified; proving that the AC knew a specific asserted authority was legally absent on 7 June requires the controlling Community, Registry and court documents plus evidence of his receipt/knowledge.

**Intent to enable the takeover:** presently an inference, not a fact. It should be tested against the full sequence of prior notice, January possession/access conduct, any pre-event communications, immediate post-event validation, absence or presence of corrective measures, later transaction/convalidation conduct, and the resulting allocation of control/benefit.

## 8. Public website formulation

Use the following hierarchy:

### Priority question — omission or affirmative enablement?

> The record now raises a question beyond omission. Before 7 June 2018, the AC had already asserted a role in LPB possession and requested the complex's electronic access credentials. Immediately after the 7 June events, LPB counsel reported that the AC justified the Community security arrangement and Acosta Matos's presence by reference to Community authority and asserted apartment ownership. What documents and legal authority supported that position, and what limits did the AC impose to protect LPB, Matkator, CEXP and third-party rights?

### Evidential boundary

> This supports direct knowledge and an affirmative contemporaneous position attributed to the AC. It does not yet prove that he instructed the 7 June physical acts in advance, knew the asserted authority was absent, or acted with criminal intent. Those propositions require the pre-event communications and authority record now requested.

## 9. Present-day do-over

## Preservation rule

Do not downgrade this workstream to “omission only.” Preserve the separate analytical fields of **prior knowledge, affirmative act, contemporaneous validation, operational enablement, knowing absence of authority, intent, effect and later follow-through**. Equally, do not promote inference fields to fact without primary evidence.
