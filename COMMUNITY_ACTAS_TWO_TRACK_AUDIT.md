# Two Competing Governance Records: Comunidad, Exploitation and the Disputed Majority (2008-2022)

**Control memorandum — 19 August 2026**  
**Status:** working forensic synthesis; evidence before conclusion.  
**Public-page relationship:** supplements `en/community-instrumentalisation/`, `en/community-instrumentalisation/minutes-2011-2022/` and the Spanish equivalents. It does not replace the primary-source chronology already published there.  
**Naming control:** “capture” remains an attributed/audit hypothesis to be proved, not the evidential label for the record itself.

## 1. Purpose

This memorandum preserves the current audit hypothesis that the Sun Park record should be reconstructed on **two parallel governance tracks**, with a later actor-specific adoption/culmination phase from 2017/2018 through 2022 and beyond:

- **Track A — disputed Owners’ Community governance/debt chain (2011 onward):** minutes and related acts produced after the February–June 2011 governance rupture, where LPB’s majority position was progressively treated as non-voting through attributed community debt. The legal and factual validity of each act must be tested individually; the fact that a numerical minority acted does not by itself prove invalidity.
- **Track B — original / majority / exploitation-side record (2008 onward):** the 2008 constitutional and exploitation framework, majority-backed records, later LPB/Gil meetings, and separate CEXP records. CEXP minutes must never be silently relabelled as Owners’ Community minutes.
- **Later adoption/culmination phase — 2017/2018–2022 onward:** CAM/JDAM/HNT-era acts may arise in a different title/control epoch, but that difference neither cures the alleged earlier apparatus nor insulates later knowing adoption, use, signature, certification, escalation or benefit. It is unnecessary to prove that each later actor designed a single plan in 2011 before testing that actor's own conduct and criminal relevance.

The objective is not to pre-judge guilt or validity. It is to test every minute against ownership, voting entitlement, notice, proxies, debt, the Community statutes, the exploitation instruments, the unitary-exploitation tourism framework, actual cost-bearing and the later use made of the minute, while running the mandatory parallel criminal/investigative analysis required by `ND-COM-CRIM-001`.

### Criminal non-derogation

Por Derecho expressly alleges **criminal instrumentalisation of the Community organ and its documentary outputs**, not merely civil, LPH, mercantile or insolvency invalidity. That attributed thesis must remain visible. It does not convert every defect into a crime or establish any named person's guilt; it requires actor-specific proof of knowledge, intent, act/use, benefit, prejudice, causation and the elements of the potentially applicable offence.

## 2. Core forensic proposition to test

The strongest current proposition is more precise than “a minority held invalid meetings”:

> **Operator/CEXP or the hotel bears common operating expenditure → Owners’ Community attributes quotas/debt to LPB → LPB is characterised as in arrears → LPB’s approximately 73–77% ownership position is deprived of voting effect → the remaining eligible voters become the apparent voting majority → resolutions are adopted on offices, debt, certificates, litigation, administration, security, access or related matters → those records are later relied upon before courts, the insolvency administrator, authorities or counterparties.**

This is an **audit hypothesis**, not an adjudicated conclusion. The decisive question is whether the attributed debt and resulting voting exclusion were legally and economically sound at each meeting.

## 4. The “double dipping” / wrong-debtor audit

Use **“double dipping” only as shorthand**. The repository-grade issue is whether the same economic cost was borne once through the hotel/operator/CEXP and then attributed again, in whole or in part, as Owners’ Community debt — especially where the second attribution was used to remove LPB’s vote or support litigation/certification.

Create a transaction-level ledger with at least these fields:

1. invoice/vendor and date;
2. nature of expense;
3. finca/common-service perimeter;
4. entity invoiced;
5. entity that actually paid;
6. bank/payment evidence;
7. inclusion in hotel/operator/CEXP accounts;
8. minority-owner contribution, if any;
9. later Community budget/account line using the same or equivalent cost;
10. amount allocated to LPB;
11. amount relied upon to classify LPB as in arrears;
12. voting consequence at the next meeting;
13. certificate, claim or court filing using that balance;
14. later correction/reduction/reversal, if any.

### Evidence already located

- The 2016 PwC/Jonathan Simó correspondence uses the formulation **“cuentas de comunidad soportadas por la explotadora”**, showing that operator-borne Community costs were a live contemporaneous issue before the 2018 expert report.
- The 2018 David Espejo/eXW report reconstructs the original model as one in which the exploitation entity managed the tourism activity and integral maintenance and bore operating expenditure. It gives the expert opinion that the Community debt asserted against LPB was fictitious and that the economic position ran in the opposite direction, including a substantial CEXP receivable against minority owners. **This is expert opinion, not an adjudicated fact.**

## 5. Three independent compliance gates for every minute

### Gate A — Owners’ Community / LPH / statutes

For each minute record:

- convener and asserted office;
- notice date, means and agenda;
- first/second call;
- persons present and represented;
- proxy instruments;
- finca-by-finca ownership on the meeting date;
- quota denominator used;
- debt attributed to each owner;
- whether the debt was due, challenged or consigned as relevant under the law applicable at that date;
- owners deprived of voting rights and stated basis;
- quota/person majority actually required for each resolution;
- vote result;
- signatures/book insertion/rectification;
- later suspension, challenge, judgment or adverse evidence.

**Do not argue simply that a minority could never pass a resolution.** The legal audit must apply the meeting-call and voting rules applicable at the relevant date and the specific type of resolution.

### Gate B — CEXP / exploitation contract / contractual authority

Even a procedurally regular Owners’ Community resolution does not automatically confer powers belonging to an operator, CEXP, a registered owner or another contract party. Test:

- who held the operating/enabling title;
- who had contractual responsibility for integrated maintenance and supplies;
- who employed/contracted relevant personnel;
- who controlled reception, bookings, guests, keys and revenue;
- termination/subrogation clauses and competent-body authority;
- 1 Feb 2012 CEXP–Monterecco notice and its scope;
- whether any later CEXP resolution actually reversed, rescinded or replaced prior arrangements.

### Gate C — Canary tourism / unidad de explotación

For every act purporting to fragment, substitute or exercise hotel operation, test the tourism-law framework applicable **on that date**, including the unitary-exploitation principle and documentary enabling title from owners. Do not state that a tourism-law defect automatically nullifies an Owners’ Community minute; keep the Community-law, contractual and tourism-law consequences analytically separate.

## 6. 2011 “desalojo” — chronology discipline

Do not compress the history into a single unsupported statement that “the whole hotel was physically evicted in 2011.” Current evidence is stronger when split into stages:

1. **February–June 2011:** governance displacement / debt-and-vote rupture;
2. **2011 onward:** desahucio litigation and attempts to assert unit-level autonomy/control;
3. **subsequent years:** continuing debt, litigation, custody and access conflict;
4. **7 June 2018:** clearly documented hotel physical-control/access event followed by lock/chain/security/service consequences.

This supports a testable multi-year causal sequence without overstating the evidence.

## 7. High-value comparator: 10 August 2012

The 2012 LPB/Gil-side record should be used as a governance-behaviour comparator, subject to final authentication against the diligenced book and signed original:

- LPB/Gil appears from a numerical majority position (~72–73% in the working reconstruction);
- where the necessary accounts/supporting information were not available, the record did not simply use numerical control to force the decisions through.

The evidential comparison is therefore:

> **Majority + insufficient information → no forced vote**

versus the disputed chain to be tested:

> **Attributed debt → majority loses vote → remaining minority becomes voting majority → further debt/authority resolutions**.

This is more probative and less rhetorical than labelling one side “real” and the other “fake.”

### Source-status rule

Every public proposition should be labelled as one of:

- **documented primary fact**;
- **party attribution/allegation**;
- **expert opinion**;
- **evidence-based inference**;
- **open question**;
- **official/judicial outcome**.

A later consequence is not proof that the originating minute was valid. Conversely, a disputed minute is not proved invalid merely because LPB/Gil objected to it.

## 11. What would materially change the analysis?

The present interpretation should be narrowed or corrected if primary records establish, meeting-by-meeting, that:

- LPB’s attributed arrears were valid, due and not duplicated with operator-borne costs;
- deprivation of LPB’s vote complied with the law/statutes applicable at the date;
- the remaining voters satisfied the legally required thresholds for each resolution;
- the relevant Community body had the material power later attributed to it;
- CEXP/operator instruments validly transferred the disputed powers;
- tourism enabling titles covered the relevant units and acts;
- the reported 2018 vote percentages or 2012 comparator are wrong when reconstructed finca-by-finca.

This falsifiability is part of the repository’s open-kimono evidential method.
