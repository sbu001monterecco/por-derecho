# Treasury Resolution 90/2026 — disclosure and traceability control

**Date:** 21 August 2026  
**Body:** Dirección General del Tesoro y Política Financiera (Gobierno de Canarias)  
**Resolution:** 90/2026, 1 April 2026  
**Originating access request:** 3 March 2026

## Controlling proposition

The text of Resolution 90/2026 reviewed in the source corpus grants access to the requested public information concerning Treasury management/traceability architecture and states, in the relevant passage, the **absence of formally approved protocols by that Directorate-General for the management of its competences**, with reliance instead on the identified normative provisions.

This must be stated narrowly. It does **not** establish that the Canary Government had no procedures, no controls, no records or no traceability. It identifies the position of this Directorate-General in response to the specific access request and the requested category of separately formalised protocols.

## Documents disclosed / transmitted

The Resolution identifies the applicable published normative framework and refers to three additional documents supplied to the requester:

1. Joint Resolution 529/2023 of 10 July 2023 concerning Social Security contribution processing/liquidation;
2. Resolution 31/2021 of 24 February 2021 concerning Treasury cash-forecast information duties of spending centres;
3. a DGTPF Instruction establishing the procedure for liquidity requests by public-sector entities with estimative budgets.

A subsequent forwarding communication states that the Annex and liquidity Instruction were transmitted separately because of a technical inability to attach them with the Resolution.

## What the disclosed framework shows

### Resolution 31/2021

Treasury receives and monitors forward payment information, compares forecasts and obligations, and can manage timing/status of payment proposals within the SEFLogic framework. This is a cash-management and payment-planning control, not a criminal-investigation function.

### Joint Resolution 529/2023

The instrument documents accounting/payment controls for Social Security obligations and expressly recognises that permanent financial control may analyse causes of surcharges and resulting detriment to the Canary Treasury. It demonstrates financial-control architecture; it does not make DGTPF a general anti-fraud authority.

### Liquidity Instruction

The Instruction is relevant to identifying how public-sector entities request liquidity and what records may exist where Treasury liquidity mechanisms were used.

## Structural-funds routing

Resolution 90/2026 interprets the request's reference to European-funds managers in the structural-funds sense and identifies the competent planning/budgetary Directorate-General under the then-current organisational framework. This is relevant to routing and custody, not proof that a particular Sun Park / MYND operation received EU funding.

## Relevance to Sun Park / MYND Yaiza

The Resolution is a **traceability checkpoint**. Read together with the later Intervención clarification of 19 August 2026, it supports a finite institutional question:

> Which competent body and native file hold the records capable of showing any Treasury, regional-incentive, RIC/idoneidad, EU/ERDF or other public-support flow connected to the Sun Park / MYND Yaiza project, and what are the dates, beneficiary, eligible costs, certifications and payments?

It does not support a claim that Treasury confirmed fraud, double funding, concealment or an EU payment.

## Public-language lock

Use:

> “Resolution 90/2026 states that the DGTPF had no separately formally approved protocols of the kind requested and relied on the identified normative framework. The point is relevant to custody and traceability; it is not a finding that controls or records were absent.”

Do not use:

> “Treasury admitted there were no protocols or controls.”
