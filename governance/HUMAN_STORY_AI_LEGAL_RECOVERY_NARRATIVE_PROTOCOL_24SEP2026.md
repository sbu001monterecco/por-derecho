# HUMAN STORY / AI LEGAL RECOVERY NARRATIVE PROTOCOL — 24 September 2026

**Control ID:** `PD-GOV-HUMAN-AI-20260924-01`  
**Status:** mandatory public-narrative and AI-research governance rule  
**Applies to:** Por Derecho / Project Sun Rock public-interest support, OpenAI/AI research, LinkedIn/social publication, book/documentary narrative, collaboration/support pages, legal-AI explainers, public speeches and any summary that explains why AI matters to the project.

## 1. Controlling purpose

The evidence repository answers **what happened and how we know**.

The human narrative must also answer **what it was like to live through it, what was at stake, why ordinary corrective mechanisms became part of the evidential question, and why AI became necessary**.

Do not reduce Por Derecho to:
- a technology project;
- a generic corruption allegation;
- a procedural chronology;
- a fundraising request; or
- a list of repositories, cases and file numbers.

The public story must remain personal, concrete, source-disciplined and falsifiable.

## 2. Mandatory three-layer separation

Always preserve three different layers:

1. **Evidence layer** — primary sources, official acts, contemporaneous records, source-controlled testimony and source-status limitations.
2. **Narrative-intelligence layer** — what those sources may mean for lived experience, chronology, institutional design, resource asymmetry, access to justice and AI research.
3. **Public prose layer** — the first-person story presented to a reader.

Never upgrade layer 2 or 3 into layer 1. Good prose is not evidence. AI-generated analysis is not evidence.

## 3. First-person rule

When Gil Marer's lived experience is the subject, prefer direct first-person language:
- "I remember..."
- "I was trying to..."
- "I allege..."
- "The record shows..."
- "What I could not understand at the time was..."
- "At times it felt like..."

Do not invent emotions, dialogue, motives or sensory details. Use only experiences Gil has adopted, contemporaneous records, or clearly labelled reconstruction.

## 4. Concrete-scene rule

A human story must contain specific dated scenes rather than only abstract conclusions.

For the current canonical narrative, the June 2018 sequence is a mandatory anchor where relevant:

- **6 June 2018:** Clubotel/ONA hotel-industry lease and suspensive annex signed.
- **7 June 2018:** Gil alleges a material-control event at Sun Park involving access/control measures, locks/chains/security and exclusion; actor-specific proof and lawful alternatives remain required.
- **12 June 2018:** ONA-sourced Stoneweg/Varia conditional binding offer issued.
- **13 June 2018:** Daniel Irigoyen reports presenting the funded exit to the insolvency judge and then the Insolvency Administrator.

This four-date sequence is documentary/narrative context. It does not by itself prove deliberate sabotage, criminal participation, causation or a completed funded exit.

## 5. "One against the local world" boundary

Gil may describe the lived experience as feeling like **"one person against the local world"** or equivalent language.

That phrase is a description of **resource, information and institutional asymmetry**, not an assertion that all local actors, courts, prosecutors, professionals, companies or administrations formed one conspiracy.

Whenever the phrase or concept is used, the narrative must visibly preserve:
- actor-by-actor responsibility;
- proceeding-by-proceeding separation;
- no automatic transfer of knowledge, intent, guilt or liability;
- strongest lawful/contrary explanations; and
- relevant adverse outcomes.

The preferred analytical term is **institutional convergence failure**: several normally corrective layers become simultaneously disputed, fragmented or incapable of supplying a trusted whole-system memory.

## 6. Correction-layer inversion rule

Explain the ordinary corrective architecture before describing its alleged failure:

**administrator → court supervision → prosecution → appeal/review → regulators/public authorities → professional advice**

The project may ask what happens when several of those layers themselves become part of the evidential question.

Do not say the whole system was captured unless a competent source establishes that proposition. State instead which layer, act, date and evidential question is disputed.

## 7. Adverse-evidence rule

A strong personal narrative must include material evidence against the project-side theory.

For the current Sun Park story, where relevant, preserve at least:
- the 2018 provisional criminal dismissal and appellate confirmation;
- materially adverse judicial outcomes, including the 2023 classification judgment where in scope;
- lawful explanations or institutional positions;
- unresolved gaps; and
- the difference between registration, investigation, archive, merits determination and acquittal/exoneration.

Adverse evidence increases credibility; it is not to be hidden for rhetorical effect.

## 8. Naming / anonymisation rule

Use names when:
- identity is necessary to understand the sourced act or capacity;
- the person is already a public/procedural actor in the controlled record; and
- the attribution is fair, specific and source-backed.

Prefer role-based descriptions or partial anonymisation when naming adds heat, privacy risk or insinuation without adding evidential value.

Never use anonymisation to blur different actors into one collective adversary.

## 9. AI role rule

AI is **a second pair of eyes, not a judge**.

The narrative may say that AI helps:
- maintain long-horizon memory;
- compare documents across years and proceedings;
- detect contradictions;
- preserve identity/entity/capacity boundaries;
- carry corrections forward;
- surface adverse evidence;
- separate fact/allegation/inference/outcome/gap;
- reconcile bilingual and cross-jurisdictional records; and
- reduce the cost of work that would ordinarily require a multidisciplinary team.

The narrative must not claim that AI:
- determines guilt;
- authenticates evidence without source verification;
- replaces legal advice, courts, prosecutors or experts;
- converts repeated allegations into corroboration; or
- proves causation from chronology.

## 10. Self-correction test

The strongest public proposition about Por Derecho is not that AI confirms Gil's case.

It is:

> **The system should make it progressively harder for anyone — including Gil Marer — to maintain a proposition that the evidence does not support.**

Every public AI narrative must be consistent with that test.

## 11. Support independence rule

Any grant, credits, sponsored capacity, professional help or recoverable support must support the **capacity to test the record**, not purchase a conclusion.

Public copy should preserve the principle:

> **Help the capacity. Do not buy the answer.**

## 12. Publication workflow

For any materially revised human/AI story:

1. read current remote `main`;
2. traverse relevant evidence, adverse outcomes, funded-exit, institutional and AI-method sources;
3. compare GitHub and GitLab current state;
4. incorporate directly relevant ChatGPT continuity context without treating memory as primary evidence;
5. produce a source-controlled unitary digest;
6. draft reader-facing EN/ES prose from the digest;
7. run attribution, identity, privacy, link and publication gates;
8. merge only through the normal protected review path;
9. verify live/public state where tooling permits;
10. record unresolved deployment or source gaps honestly.

## 13. Mandatory public-safe distinction

A personal story may be emotionally strong.

It may not become evidentially stronger than its sources.
