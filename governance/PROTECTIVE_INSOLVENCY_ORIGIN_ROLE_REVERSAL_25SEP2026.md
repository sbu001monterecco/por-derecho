## 6. Formal Calificación legal boundary

Under the current TRLC, a culpable classification judgment identifies the persons affected by the classification and, where applicable, accomplices. For a corporate debtor, persons affected may include its de jure/de facto administrators or liquidators and directors-general within the statutory period; accomplice status has its own statutory cooperation / intent or gross-negligence test.

Accordingly:

- **formal Calificación** remains a statutory, debtor-centred insolvency mechanism;
- **criminal liability** is separately actor-, offence- and element-specific;
- **Insolvency Administrator professional/civil/criminal accountability** follows its own duties, complaint, removal, liability and criminal routes;
- **judicial accountability** follows its own appellate, disciplinary, criminal and other lawful routes;
- **private third-party accountability** follows the route applicable to that person's own acts/capacity.

Do not use "calificación culpable" as if it were itself the legal mechanism by which a judge or unrelated third party is declared guilty.

These are **review cues**, not findings of deliberate deception.

## 10. Comparative-Western insolvency boundary

Current UK official material shows two simultaneous realities:

1. the insolvency enforcement system prominently investigates and disqualifies directors for unfit conduct; and
2. authorised insolvency practitioners themselves are separately subject to complaints, professional discipline and published sanctions.

This supports rejecting any analytical assumption that wrongdoing must sit on only one side of the insolvency relationship. It does **not** establish how common criminal office-holder misconduct is in the UK, Spain or "the West", and no such prevalence claim may be made without a reliable denominator.
