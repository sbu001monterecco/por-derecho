## Evidence classes

Every item must be assigned separately to one or more of these states:

1. **CLIENT BRIEFING** — background, chronology, instructions or factual material supplied to the expert. Not an expert conclusion unless expert authorship is established.
2. **DRAFT** — expressly marked draft or lacking final/signature evidence.
3. **SIGNED / FINAL** — signature or reliable final-version evidence is located.
4. **TRANSMITTED** — reliable evidence shows who sent what to whom. Transmission does not itself establish court filing.
5. **AUTHOR-REPORTED FILED** — the author later states that an item was filed; retain this as provenance evidence until the docket independently confirms it.
6. **COURT-CONFIRMED FILED** — a filing receipt, certified index, docket entry or judicial source specifically establishes incorporation.
7. **COURT-CONFIRMED ROLE** — a court-originating record confirms procedural participation, testimony or other role.
8. **CITED / RELIED UPON** — a later filing, report, hearing or decision expressly cites or uses the item. Record each downstream reuse as a separate evidential event.
9. **UNKNOWN / OPEN** — status not yet supportable from the located corpus.

Never upgrade a status by inference.

## Contemporaneous notice / knowledge

For every claimed warning, objection, report or notification, record independently:

- date of creation;
- exact version;
- author/source;
- intended recipient;
- evidence of transmission;
- evidence of receipt;
- court-file/piece incorporation, if any;
- whether it predates or postdates the challenged act;
- later citation/reuse;
- what remains unproven.

A document created before a challenged event can be important **contemporaneous / ex-ante source evidence** that an issue was being documented or raised. It does **not** by itself establish formal notice to the court, insolvency administration or another actor.

Where a report says prior notice had been given, treat that statement as evidence of asserted notice and locate the underlying email, filing, acknowledgement or other transmission record before treating recipient knowledge as established.

## Extemporaneity rule

Use `extemporaneous`, `out of time`, `late filing` or equivalent language only where the evidence establishes:

1. the procedural act and applicable deadline;
2. the actual filing/service date; and
3. where relevant, the court's procedural treatment or consequence.

Hard rules:

- **Not filed ≠ filed late.**
- **Later discovery ≠ late filing.**
- **Created contemporaneously ≠ filed contemporaneously.**
- **Transmitted to counsel/client ≠ filed in court.**
- **Filed in one proceeding/section/track ≠ filed or known in another.**
