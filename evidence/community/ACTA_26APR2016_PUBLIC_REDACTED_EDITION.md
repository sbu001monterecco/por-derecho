## Redaction / publication rule

This edition preserves the meeting structure, dates, percentages, agenda, resolutions, named office-holders, professional advisers, representatives and institutional/corporate actors where they are materially relevant. It omits or redacts personal/professional contact details, street-level addresses, telephone/fax numbers, email addresses, DNI/NIF/CIF identifiers, bank/account identifiers and private financial detail that is not necessary to understand the governance mechanism.

The private 77-page extraction remains controlled outside the public repository. This public edition therefore does **not** reproduce every private-owner payment/debt row or every contact/registration field from the annexes. Page numbers below refer to the 77-page preserved source.

## What the signed act records

### Pages 1–3 — constitution, attendance and voting filter

The document is headed **“ACTA DE LA JUNTA GENERAL ORDINARIA DE LA COMUNIDAD DE PROPIETARIOS SUN PARK.”** It records the meeting at Playa Blanca on 26 April 2016, states that those present consented to recording the meeting, and records the presence of a security person hired by the Community. It identifies Juan Carlos Prieto Puente as Community lawyer and Leopoldo Soler Cullen as tax/accounting adviser.

The attendance section records:

- **LPB: 72.976%** participation;
- **total present or represented: 89.727%**;
- a separate list of owners treated as current in Community payments and therefore entitled to vote, totalling **11.039%**.

The act records, among other representation relationships:

- Elena Santos Ramos → Víctor Fernández Cox;
- Miguel Hernández Lorenzo → Juan Carlos Fernández Cox;
- Antonio Cogolludo Rojas → Inversiones Salinetas, S.L.;
- Patricia Domínguez Montelongo → Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. and Mariano Palacios Fernández;
- Francisco Matos Matas → Francisco Luis Molina Molina;
- Carlos Saavedra Rodríguez → Simon Peter Thompson.

**Evidential limit:** those percentages and the applied payment filter are documentary facts from the act. Whether the underlying debt and voting exclusion were legally/economically correct is a separate question.

## Pages 4–6 — historical dispute and incomplete accounts

The management report recites the Community-side procedural history concerning the 2 February 2011 and 22 June 2011 meetings, subsequent litigation, and the competing 2014 governance record. These passages are part of the Community's account of that history and should be read with the underlying court files.

Under agenda item 5, the administrator states that the information available for the 2010–2015 accounts was incomplete and that the Community had only recently obtained bank extracts. The act records a proposal **not to approve** the 2010–2015 accounts because the required information was not complete.

The act then records Miguel Hernández, Carlos Saavedra, Elena Santos and Patricia Domínguez questioning why the item had been brought to the agenda without the full information. The accounts were not approved.

**Key sequence:** incomplete historical accounting was expressly acknowledged before later governance, quota, arrears and recovery items were addressed.

## Pages 6–9 — offices, administrator, retroactive quota and arrears

The vote-eligible pool then approved the presidency/board and administrator, with disagreement by the LPB/Cox/Palacios/Thompson represented perimeter recorded in the act.

Under the 2016 budget item, the administrator describes the Community's then-current situation as atypical and proposes reducing the bungalow quota from approximately **€171.89** to **€22.26**. The act records a proposal to apply that lower quota **retroactively from January 2010 to December 2015**.

The subsequent morosity item states that, as a result of the point-8 decisions, Community arrears fall considerably. The meeting then proceeds to authorisations concerning debt balances, certifications and judicial recovery.

**Evidential limit:** this sequence does not itself prove that earlier debt was fabricated or that the later vote was unlawful. It does establish that the meeting itself connected incomplete historic records, a large retrospective quota adjustment, changed arrears and governance/debt-enforcement decisions.

## Pages 9–12 — access, administration, institutional complaints and promised reconciliation

The act addresses Industry/OCA/access questions, Costa Papagayos, complaints/communications to public bodies and the continuing dispute over access to the complex. Those statements are attributed to the speakers recorded in the act and are not converted here into independent findings.

In requests/questions, Patricia Domínguez states that accounting documentation in her possession would be delivered and asks for answers concerning payments said to have been made on Community account. The administrator undertakes that, after receiving the documentation, there will be:

1. an **exhaustive audit report within 15 days**;
2. a dedicated meeting with the president; and
3. if no agreement is reached, an **extraordinary Junta** to decide the matter.

**Open evidence question:** `documents delivered → receipt proof → 15-day audit/report → dedicated meeting → extraordinary Junta if required → final reconciliation/non-reconciliation`.

## Governance-mechanism question supported by this source

The 26 April record is particularly important because it makes the following relationship directly auditable:

`historic costs / incomplete records → asserted Community debt → payment-status filter → voting eligibility → governance decisions → retrospective quota recalculation → changed arrears → debt certification/recovery authority`

That is a **mechanism visible in the record**, not a finding of unlawful capture.
