## Pages 1–3 — constitution, relocation, attendance and vote filter

The act says the meeting began at **13:30** in Playa Blanca. Before formal constitution, representatives of Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. (“LPB”) sought to speak. The act uses hostile and evaluative language to describe the exchange. It records that the chair and administrator rejected speaking before constitution but offered later turns.

The meeting was moved from the initially prepared premises to apartment 509 after a dispute over permission to use the first room. LPB's representatives then attended the relocated meeting.

The document records:

- **6** owners present and **20** represented; **26** total attendance/representation entries;
- LPB represented across apartments, premises, solaria and pools with coefficients listed in the act totalling approximately **75.671%**;
- a separate payment-current voting list containing **7 owner/entity entries** totalling approximately **6.930%**; and
- attendance of lawyer Esteban López Noriega, invited by the administrator to address legal questions.

The two percentages are repository arithmetic sums of the listed coefficient rows, not certified totals expressly stated by the minutes.

One private NIF and the non-material names/units of private owners are redacted from this digest. Named office-holder and invited-adviser identities are retained where the act assigns them a formal role material to the governance mechanism; the complete identity fields remain in the private master.

## Agenda

1. Read and, if appropriate, approve the previous meeting's minutes.
2. Chair's report on the complex, particularly Community accounts.
3. Approve alleged arrears and judicial recovery; empower the chair to instruct lawyers and procuradores.
4. Ratify termination of CEXP's maintenance arrangement, request rendition of accounts and approve a new maintenance contract.
5. Approve appointment of the new administrator.
6. Questions.

## Page 4 — Item 1: 2-February-2011 minutes

Francisco Mario Matos Matas, acting as Community administrator, read the 2-February-2011 minutes. LPB's representative said LPB had been duly represented, disputed the act's accuracy and said it had not been received.

The act says the previous minutes were approved unanimously by those listed as entitled to vote. It does not record a vote by coefficient of all present/represented ownership interests.

## Pages 4–8 — Item 2: chair's report and interventions

The chair's written report states, as the chair's account rather than an independently audited finding, that:

- she and other owners said their units remained in CEXP without consent or return;
- the CEXP maintenance arrangement was valued at €88 per apartment, proportionally for premises;
- administration and Costa Papagayo Community items allegedly remained unpaid;
- residents complained of access, water, electricity and maintenance conditions;
- a Community administrator and security had been engaged;
- cadastral, waste, road-asphalting and lighting issues were being addressed;
- the maintenance arrangement with CEXP had been cancelled and a lower 2012 budget was anticipated; and
- owners would be treated as owing Community charges unless CEXP proved the relevant payments.

The report expressly describes the understanding attributed to the 25-July-2008 arrangement: owners integrated in CEXP were exempted from direct monthly Community charges in exchange for CEXP paying **all** Community expenses. It then uses an illustrative calculation of €88 × 166 LPB apartments and asks for proof of water, electricity, social-security, insurance, salary and works payments.

The chair said that from 1 July owners should pay the Community directly and pursue CEXP under any separate arrangement. In response to a question, she said no LPB Community-charge payment appeared in the account for the previous three years other than a €500 payment associated with obtaining administrator services and a meeting.

LPB's representative distinguished a community of property/owners from a community of goods/exploitation, raised the tourism/licence consequences of units outside operation, denied the chair's authority to cancel the maintenance contract, proposed an agreement and asked that the alleged debt be explained. Other owners described disputes over unit keys, returns, accounts and benefits. A represented owner's written points included opposition to Community charges pending CEXP accounting and a request for tax inspection. The invited lawyer described coexistence of CEXP and the Owners' Community, referred to the 2008 maintenance arrangement and encouraged settlement.

All accusations, arrears and condition statements in this section remain statements recorded by the act. The minutes alone do not prove their truth.

## Pages 9–12 — Item 3: alleged debt, voting exclusion and litigation authority

The administrator presented an owner/unit-level schedule extending through **262 numbered entries**. The schedule attributes balances to LPB and many other owners/entities. It is not republished row by row because it contains unnecessary personal financial data and was not independently reconciled in the act with bank ledgers, CEXP expenditure, invoices, title changes, limitation periods or prior approved accounts.

After the schedule, the administrator states that some owners considered the charges paid or justified through CEXP. Until CEXP provided accounts and proof, he treated the owners as the debtors whom the Community could pursue. He also recorded uncertainty over sales and unidentified new owners.

The act says everyone present other than LPB's two representatives understood that explanation, while **all persons on the earlier payment-current voting list** voted to approve it. The same eligible pool authorised the chair to bring Community litigation, grant powers and retain lawyers. LPB's representatives were not in the eligible voting pool despite LPB's approximately 75.671% represented coefficient.

This is the central contemporaneous **alleged debt → voting exclusion → litigation authority** mechanism. It proves that the minutes record that mechanism; it does not decide the legal validity of the debt, voting restriction, notice, calculation, office-holders or resolutions.

## Page 13 — Item 4: CEXP maintenance termination and Pamanil proposal

The administrator read the chair's 14-June letter to CEXP (Annex I). The chair said only one maintenance/administration proposal had been submitted, by Pamanil, S.L., which she said offered more services at lower cost. The act says all persons in the eligible voting list voted in favour.

LPB's representative said the chair lacked authority to terminate the arrangement and that the decision made no sense. The chair relied on the reasons in the letter.

## Page 13 — Item 5: Pamanil as administrator

The chair said she had contracted Pamanil approximately two months earlier after the prior administrator resigned, and that Pamanil sought meeting approval in the interests of transparency. The act says all persons in the eligible voting list voted in favour.

This is documentary evidence of a stated prior engagement and ratification. It is not proof that a separate signed contract existed, that the appointment was lawful, or that Pamanil performed or was paid for every proposed function.

## Page 13 — Item 6 and closure

The act records no further intervention other than the chair's thanks and closes at **16:40**. Signature blocks appear for chair and secretary/administrator. Authentication of signatures and comparison with the diligenced minute book remain open.

## Pages 14–15 — Annex I: 14-June-2011 letter to CEXP

The annex is addressed to CEXP's director. It says the chair intended to end, with effect from **30 June 2011**, CEXP's apparent integrated-maintenance services for common areas. It describes the 25-July-2008 Community resolution as the origin of an unformalised arrangement and alleges inadequate control, missing periodic settlements, unpaid sums and maintenance deficiencies.

The letter demands:

1. cessation from 30 June of activities said to be performed for the Owners' Community and withdrawal of relevant personnel; and
2. rendition and final liquidation of accounts within 15 days of receipt.

The annex records the sender's position. The current source set does not by itself prove delivery, receipt, the chair's authority, legal termination, CEXP's response, or the truth and reconciliation of the alleged balances.

## Page 16 — Annex II: Pamanil written proposal

The proposal is headed as a budget for the Owners' Community and contains two workstreams:

### Maintenance / cleaning / gardens / porter service / Community legal advice

- porter, gardening, cleaning and general maintenance;
- **two full-time workers**, totalling **80 weekly hours**, Monday to Friday, plus supervisory time;
- Pamanil to provide and maintain tools/machinery;
- consumables to be paid by the Community;
- large palms excluded and separately budgeted;
- payment between days 5 and 10 of the month after service; and
- price **€5,200 per month plus tax** for this first proposal section, whose heading expressly includes Community legal advice; the listed operational bullets do not separately particularize that advice.

### Property administration

The separate property-administration section lists ordinary administrator functions, records/documents custody, payments/collections, accounting, two meetings/minutes and debtor claims, excluding procurador and travel, at **€1,040 plus tax for the 2011 property-administration package**. The source does not place legal advice in this second section or establish the €1,040 figure as an annually recurring price.

The proposal contains approval/signature areas for Sun Park and Pamanil. The present public edition does not authenticate those marks or establish a later executed service contract.

## Evidential conclusions and limits

1. The act directly contradicts the literal proposition **“Pamanil did nothing”** insofar as it proves a written proposal, recorded approval/ratification and administrative participation. It does **not** prove actual workers, payroll, utilities, integrated maintenance, lawful authority, invoices, payment or performance.
2. The act itself records the Community side's understanding that CEXP had historically absorbed Community costs for participating owners. It simultaneously reconstructs direct owner debt because proof was said to be missing. The missing reconciliation is therefore central, not collateral.
3. LPB's large represented coefficient and the much smaller eligible voting pool are visible on the face of the act. Whether the voting exclusion complied with the applicable Property Horizontal Act, deposits/consignation, notices, title and voting rules requires independent legal analysis.
4. The source contains no audited reconciliation of CEXP-paid expenditure against Community charges, no bank statements, no creditor-by-credit schedule and no proof of Pamanil's later performance.
5. Gil alleges that specified Pamanil/Community acts may have been used in conduct he characterizes as fraudulent and as manufacturing debt and authority. That allegation remains attributed and unadjudicated. Criminal analysis requires actor, capacity, statement/document, falsity, knowledge, intent, reliance, causal effect, benefit/harm and the strongest lawful explanation for each act.
