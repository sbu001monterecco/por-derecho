## How the defence enters the LPB / Concurso 36/2012 record

The defence does not merely address the 2008 financial instruments in isolation. It expressly relies on the insolvency history of **Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.** and the record of the **Administración Concursal**.

### Page 1 — preliminary exceptions

The defence advances, among other points:

- lack of Aweswell Limited's standing under insolvency legislation; and
- a **res judicata / cosa juzgada** argument based on credits recognised in the LPB insolvency record, including the hedging derivative.

This is a litigation position advanced for CaixaBank, not a finding by the Valencia court.

### Pages 3–8 — standing and the AC record

The defence states that LPB was declared insolvent in **Concurso Ordinario 36/2012** before Juzgado de lo Mercantil nº 1 de Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. It then relies upon the Administrador Concursal report to dispute Aweswell/Monterecco Sun Park Limited's creditor status and to delimit the scope of the action said to have been authorised by the insolvency administration.

### Pages 9–11 — recognised credit / preclusion argument

The defence identifies the lender succession in the relevant credit discussion as **Caja Canarias → Bankia → CaixaBank**, refers to the credit recognised in the Administrador Concursal report/textos definitivos, and argues that the recognised credit should have preclusive / res judicata effect against the later challenge.

Again, this records **CaixaBank's pleaded legal position**. Whether that position succeeds, and the precise legal effect of the insolvency list/textos definitivos, are matters for the court.
