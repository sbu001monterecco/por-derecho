# 60 / 61 / approximately 44 — origin and reconciliation

Updated: 19 August 2026.

## Rule

These three quantities are not interchangeable. No document presently located equates all three.

## 60 apartments

**Source class:** working/historical acquisition-status evidence.

A 2017 `List of Fincas SPL - Acquisition - Status` reconstruction, preserved in later evidential material, identifies **60 apartments** within a broader 71-element consolidation/acquisition universe. The exact native 2017 source remains a target for recovery and verification.

A separate Drive source, `List of Fincas SPL - Junio 2014 - Apartamentos en Explotación 2014.pdf` (Drive id `1ReMjvQSg59geYk0kVAKU5Wysu6E4cOFY`), is relevant to earlier operating-status reconstruction but must not be substituted for the missing native 2017 acquisition-status source.

Present meaning: wider minority/acquisition-status universe, not a physical-condition count.

## 61 units — now directly found in ACTÚA's April counter-report

**Source class:** PRIMARY ACTÚA statement about information supplied to it; underlying source/list unresolved.

The recovered original 15 January ACTÚA valuation does not visibly enumerate `61` as a condition set. But ACTÚA's **3 April 2018 counter-report**, later filed by CAM through LexNET on 17 April, expressly states:

`Existen al menos 61 que se encuentran sin reformar conforme se nos informa`.

This materially changes provenance:

- ACTÚA itself recorded **at least 61 unrefurbished units**;
- ACTÚA qualified the proposition as information provided to it (`conforme se nos informa`);
- the supplier of the information is not identified in the located passage;
- no 61-unit schedule has yet been located;
- the passage does not identify the 61 as architectural Blocks 4 and 5.

A 17 April 2018 email to Cuatrecasas separately describes `2 bloques con 61 apartamentos sin uso y en mal estado`. That email therefore remains important for the **two-block / unused / poor-condition** characterisation, but the number 61 itself is now independently found in ACTÚA's own technical response.

Present meaning: ACTÚA-recorded supplied information about at least 61 unrefurbished units; exact identity/source and relation to the `two blocks` statement remain unresolved.

## Approximately 44 poor-condition units

**Source class:** professional expert opinion.

GESVALT's 1 March 2018 critical expert report states, in substance, that ACTÚA applied the same 57.14% depreciation across the apartment stock although only approximately **44 units** were in poor condition and the remainder were usable.

The recovered ACTÚA materials do not presently enumerate a 44-unit poor-condition schedule.

Present meaning: GESVALT's expert assessment/estimate of the poor-condition subset.

## Blocks 4 + 5 = exactly 44 architectural apartments

Confirmed physical capacity:

- Block 4: apartments 401–408 and 451–458 = 16;
- Block 5: apartments 501–514 and 551–564 = 28;
- combined = **44**.

This is a significant numerical alignment with GESVALT's `approximately 44` but remains a **spatial hypothesis**, not proof of identity.

The contemporaneous `two blocks / 61` characterisation also means Blocks 4+5 cannot literally account for 61 architectural apartments.

## ACTÚA's access limitation materially affects reconciliation

ACTÚA's 3 April counter-report also says:

- it could not access the interior of **100% of the complex**;
- because it lacked access to all interiors, it relied in part on **general exterior images** to conclude that the general state of the whole complex was `DEFICIENTE`;
- it checked historical GrafCan imagery and said part of the complex, specifically the south-pool area, had been inoperative from 2013;
- it defended depreciating the functional unit as a whole rather than applying condition-specific depreciation finca by finca.

This creates an important new question: **how many of the supplied 61 units were actually inspected internally by ACTÚA, and how many were known through information supplied by others?**

## Primary ACTÚA photo result

The original valuation contains 13 substantive photographs on pages 16–21.

Primary-source review materially limits the earlier visual-sampling theory:

- the photographic record is mixed rather than wholly negative;
- only three clear apartment-interior photographs appear, all on page 21;
- those interiors have no visible apartment/finca/block captions;
- common-area images include intact spaces, a reception/front office and people visibly present;
- the original valuation states actual `SITUACIÓN Y OCUPACIÓN: Se desconoce`;
- ACTÚA applies a uniform 20-year / 57.14% physical depreciation;
- in April ACTÚA expressly defends 57.14% for the **total functional unit** rather than finca-specific depreciation.

Therefore ACTÚA's visible photographic record does not itself prove 44 or 61 bad apartments, and it does not identify Blocks 4–5.

## Best current reconciliation

**Working inference only:**

> 60/61 may concern a broader minority/closed/acquisition/refurbishment universe, while approximately 44 may concern the physically poor-condition subset identified by GESVALT. Blocks 4+5 remain one candidate for the approximately-44 subset because their architectural capacity is exactly 44.

The direct ACTÚA April statement strengthens the reality of a **61-unit information input**, but does not prove its source, identity or two-block geography.

This inference is weakened if the ACTÚA apartment interiors geolocate outside Blocks 4–5, a recovered 61-unit list spans many blocks, or a GESVALT unit schedule identifies a different approximately-44 set.

It is strengthened if independent contemporaneous records show that GESVALT's approximately 44 poor units are the complete stock of Blocks 4 and 5 and that the remaining part of ACTÚA's supplied 61 was unrefurbished but not poor-condition.
