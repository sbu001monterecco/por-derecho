## 1. Controlling correction

The repository and website must not state as fact that **“LPB withdrew”** or that **“LPB decided to abandon PP 1041.”**

The strongest accurate formulation is:

> **A withdrawal was filed in the name of Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U.; CAM did not oppose it; the Court ended PP 1041 on that filing; the decree identified Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte as LPB's lawyer; and the specific insolvency-court authorisation required for withdrawal has not yet been located in the available material.**

This formulation separates:

1. the act recorded by the Court;
2. the authenticity and authority of the attributed LPB decision;
3. the author, instruction and filing chain;
4. the legal duty owed to LPB and the estate;
5. the objective and alleged intended benefit;
6. any later civil, insolvency, professional or criminal characterisation.

## 3. Historical legal duty and authority gate

The historical Ley 22/2003 applicable at the material time must be used, not a later text substituted retrospectively.

### Article 35

The Insolvency Administration had to act with the diligence of an orderly administrator and loyal representative.

### Article 36

The administrators were answerable for damage to the estate caused by acts or omissions contrary to law or performed without due diligence.

### Article 51.2

Where the debtor's powers were suspended, the Insolvency Administration substituted the debtor in pending judicial proceedings. But withdrawal, admission of claim and settlement required **authorisation from the insolvency judge**, with the debtor to be heard or given notice.

### Article 145

Opening liquidation produced suspension of the debtor's powers and the corresponding substitution effects.

### Present evidential position

No application for Article 51.2 authorisation, no hearing of LPB and no Commercial Court order authorising withdrawal from PP 1041 has yet been located in the controlled material.

This is not to be converted silently into proof that no authorisation exists. The correct classification is:

- **authorisation not located**;
- **certified court file required**;
- **absence, if confirmed, potentially case-changing**.

## 5. Functional-agency test

The repository uses “de facto functional agency” as a forensic attribution hypothesis, not as an autonomous cause of action.

The test is:

> **Did the Insolvency Administrator use a power entrusted for the protection of LPB/the estate in order, or with the conscious effect, of solving a problem that CAM could not solve for itself, removing an information route adverse to CAM and producing a private benefit without documented estate benefit?**

Evidence relevant to the threshold:

- CAM expressly invoked Administrator conformity as its procedural defence;
- the Administrator immediately controlled counsel and procurator;
- a withdrawal was filed in LPB's name;
- CAM did not oppose;
- the disclosure route ended;
- no estate-interest memorandum or Article 51.2 authorisation has been located;
- the creditor position later continued through recognition, valuation and dación.

Evidence still required for the highest attribution level:

- direct communications or instructions;
- common planning;
- knowledge of the intended downstream route;
- conflict disclosures or absence of disclosure;
- ratification;
- benefit allocation;
- personal decision roles of JDAM, LPAM or others;
- consideration, inducement or other corruptive link, if alleged.

## 6. Causation and harm

The harm analysis must not assume that Article 1535 would automatically have succeeded.

The stronger causal case is that the act removed an already-admitted route capable of producing information with independent value:

- complete transfer deed;
- acquisition price;
- payment date;
- transaction costs;
- material for negotiation, debt reduction, redemption, accounting, challenge or refinancing;
- material for evaluating whether the nominal credit and the economic acquisition price had diverged;
- material relevant to the later credit-to-control-to-title architecture.

Potential harm categories, subject to proof:

1. loss of the information order and procedural opportunity;
2. loss of a chance to reduce or extinguish debt;
3. impairment of convenio/refinancing/payment routes;
4. additional interest, cost or insolvency duration;
5. loss of equity or estate surplus;
6. contribution to later valuation/dación consequences;
7. litigation and corrective costs;
8. harm arising from unauthorised procedural representation.

The counterfactual requires financeability, legal availability, timing, conditions precedent and alternative-cause analysis.

## 8. Strongest defence

The Administrator and affected parties may argue:

- liquidation placed LPB under suspension and the Administrator controlled litigation;
- PP 1041 was commenced without required approval;
- Article 1535 was unavailable and later rejected;
- withdrawal avoided unnecessary cost;
- the procurator possessed sufficient authority;
- Article 51.2 authorisation exists but has not yet been recovered;
- another route remained available;
- no firm finance existed for redemption;
- the later order broke causation;
- no personal instruction or benefit by CAM, JDAM or LPAM is proved.

The response must be evidential, not rhetorical. The defence strengthens materially if the native file shows:

- a timely reasoned Article 51.2 application and order;
- LPB hearing;
- independent legal advice;
- conflict review;
- comparison of costs, prospects and alternatives;
- a documented estate benefit;
- no CAM instruction or coordination;
- no viable financing or economic advantage from disclosure.
