# Insolvency Administrator — credit-to-title gatekeeper

Updated: 20 August 2026.

## Strongest supported conclusion

> The Insolvency Administrator was not a spectator to the conversion of credit into control and title. He was LPB's statutory gatekeeper. His acts and omissions controlled representation, access to assignment information, the estate's litigation position, valuation and liquidation proposals, response to material-control events, implementation of the adjudication and the final accounting of debt, value and surplus.

The strongest present allegation goes further in PP 1041/2017. Por Derecho alleges that the Administrator used powers entrusted for LPB's protection to remove the lawyer who had obtained a disclosure order, control the procurator and enable a withdrawal filing falsely attributed to LPB, without any specific Article 51.2 authorisation located to date, for the deliberate detriment of LPB and the functional benefit of CAM/Acosta Matos.

That allegation is serious and supported by a primary documentary sequence. It is not a final criminal, civil or professional finding. Authority, authorship, intent, conflict, communications, benefit and each offence element remain to be determined.

## Controlling correction

The repository must not say:

> “LPB withdrew.”

It must say:

> **A withdrawal was filed in LPB's name; CAM did not oppose; the Court ended PP 1041 on that filing; the decree identified Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte as LPB's lawyer; and the authenticity, authority, instruction, LexNET chain, Article 51.2 authorisation, estate interest and beneficiary remain to be determined.**

## Why the gatekeeper analysis is central

The credit could not become estate title merely because a private creditor desired it. The conversion required a chain of institutional acts:

`creditor recognition → LPB representation and litigation position → assignment-information route → withdrawal filed in LPB's name → valuation/liquidation proposal → treatment of alternatives → control/possession response → judicial authorisation → deed/registration → accounts and surplus`

At several of those points, the Insolvency Administrator was the statutory actor speaking or acting for LPB or reporting to the Court.

## PP 1041 primary chain

1. **12 January 2018:** the Court ordered CAM to produce the PH122→CAM deed and disclose price, payment date and costs.
2. **24–25 January 2018:** CAM invoked the absence of Insolvency Administration conformity as a procedural objection.
3. **25 January 2018:** the Administrator ended Cristo Suárez Pimentel's formal LPB mandate, required him to cease further LPB action and controlled future procurator filings.
4. **After that intervention:** a withdrawal was filed in LPB's name.
5. **5 March 2018:** the Court ended PP 1041; CAM did not oppose; the decree identified the Administrator himself as LPB's lawyer.
6. **Current P0 gap:** no withdrawal pleading, LexNET chain, specific power, Article 51.2 application/order, LPB hearing or estate-interest memorandum has yet been located.

## Direct causation already supported

The 25 January 2018 intervention is not merely temporal correlation. It removed from PP 1041 the lawyer who had obtained the disclosure order, imposed Administrator consent over future filings and controlled the procurator. The later decree proves that a withdrawal was filed in LPB's name and that the proceeding ended on that filing.

It does not yet prove:

- who drafted, signed or filed the withdrawal;
- that the Administrator personally instructed the precise filing;
- that an Article 51.2 authorisation did not exist;
- that the Article 1535 route would certainly have succeeded;
- that JDAM or LPAM personally instructed or ratified the act;
- that any crime has been committed.

But the burden is now precise: produce the authority, instruction, metadata, advice, conflict analysis, estate-interest record, alternatives and benefit chain.

## Historical legal barrier

Under the then-applicable Ley 22/2003:

- Article 35 required the diligence of an orderly administrator and loyal representative.
- Article 36 imposed responsibility for estate damage caused by unlawful or insufficiently diligent acts or omissions.
- Article 51.2 allowed substitution of the suspended debtor in pending proceedings but required insolvency-judge authorisation to withdraw, admit or settle, with the debtor heard.
- Article 145 applied suspension upon opening liquidation.

No such Article 51.2 authorisation has yet been located in the controlled material. The certified Commercial Court file must resolve the point.

## Strongest responsibility and functional-agency formulation

> Por Derecho alleges that in PP 1041 the Administrator used insolvency-derived power that CAM did not possess to solve a procedural problem CAM had expressly identified, removing an information route capable of disclosing the credit-acquisition price and preserving debt-reduction, extinguishment or funded-exit options. In that episode, the Administrator functioned as a de facto agent for the CAM/Acosta Matos perimeter, to its objective benefit and the benefit of decision-makers or beneficiaries to be determined, including José Daniel Acosta Matos and Laura Patricia Acosta Matos.

“De facto functional agency” is a forensic attribution hypothesis. It requires evidence of knowledge, intent, communications, instruction, conflict, ratification and benefit. It is not a substitute for actor-specific proof or a final legal conclusion.

## The 15 February 2018 order must remain in the analysis

The Commercial Court recognised CAM as assignee, rejected LPB's strict litigious-credit position and corrected part of the amounts. That is a real adverse ruling.

It does not eliminate the separate questions of:

- who used LPB's name to withdraw and with what authority;
- whether Article 51.2 authorisation existed;
- what the Administrator verified before reporting;
- why the parallel deed-and-price route was abandoned;
- whether later valuation and liquidation protected estate value;
- whether the debt used in 2022 was legally payable and secured;
- whether a surplus existed;
- whether material control before title was properly addressed.

## Criminal relevance — separated from proof

The 18 June 2026 complaint establishes that Por Derecho formally alleged criminal conduct and requested investigation. It does not establish the truth of the offences alleged.

If the certified record establishes false attribution of LPB's intention, absence of authority, knowing use of a procedural document, deception, procedural or patrimonial advantage and harm, the competent authority should test documentary/procedural falsity, procedural fraud, disloyal administration, participation/cooperation and civil liability arising from any independently proved offence element by element.

## Strongest defence

The Administrator may contend that:

- liquidation placed LPB under suspension and gave him litigation control;
- PP 1041 was commenced without required approval;
- Article 1535 lacked merit and was rejected;
- changing counsel and withdrawal avoided costs;
- the procurator had sufficient authority;
- Article 51.2 authorisation exists but has not yet been recovered;
- ACTÚA was a professional valuation used for a lawful liquidation plan;
- ONA and other proposals were conditional and not executable;
- no recoverable surplus existed;
- no CAM, JDAM or LPAM instruction is proved.

Those defences must be answered with the native decision and filing record, not assumed away.
