# Insolvency Administrator — credit-to-title gatekeeper

Updated: 20 August 2026.

## Strongest supported conclusion

> The Insolvency Administrator was not a spectator to the conversion of credit into control and title. He was LPB's statutory gatekeeper. His acts and omissions controlled representation, access to assignment information, the estate's litigation position, valuation and liquidation proposals, response to material-control events, implementation of the adjudication and the final accounting of debt, value and surplus.

This is not a finding that the Administrator agreed to an unlawful plan or committed a crime. It is a direct responsibility proposition: where the office-holder possessed the legal power, information or procedural position necessary to protect the estate, the estate-interest reason for each consequential act must be reproducible.

## Why the gatekeeper analysis is central

The credit could not become estate title merely because a private creditor desired it. The conversion required a chain of institutional acts:

`creditor recognition → LPB representation and litigation position → valuation/liquidation proposal → treatment of alternatives → control/possession response → judicial authorisation → deed/registration → accounts and surplus`

At several of those points, the Insolvency Administrator was the statutory actor speaking or acting for LPB or reporting to the Court.

## Direct causation already supported

The 25 January 2018 act is not merely temporal correlation. It directly caused the end of Cristo's formal LPB mandate in PP 1041. The later decree proves that LPB withdrew and that the proceeding ended on that basis.

What that does **not** yet prove is equally precise:

- that the Administrator personally drafted the withdrawal;
- that he instructed the procurador to file it;
- that the decision was made to benefit CAM;
- that the underlying Article 1535 or disclosure claim would certainly have succeeded;
- that the act was dishonest, disloyal or criminal.

The present burden is therefore narrower and stronger: produce the instruction, authority, advice, estate-interest analysis, alternatives and economic consequence.

## Strongest responsibility formulation

> The Administrator's responsibility is not alleged merely because CAM ultimately benefited. It arises for determination because his office controlled indispensable LPB gates and because at least one consequential intervention—the termination of LPB's formal lawyer mandate in the assignment-information proceeding—is directly documented. The court or competent body should determine whether each act was a reasoned exercise of statutory duty for the estate or whether, cumulatively, the office operationalised the creditor-acquisition route while LPB lost information, alternatives, value and reversibility.

## The 15 February 2018 order must remain in the analysis

The Commercial Court recognised CAM as assignee, rejected LPB's strict litigious-credit position and corrected part of the amounts. That is a real adverse ruling.

It does not eliminate the separate questions of:

- what the Administrator verified before reporting;
- why the parallel deed-and-price route was withdrawn;
- whether later valuation and liquidation steps protected estate value;
- whether the debt used in 2022 was legally payable and secured;
- whether a surplus existed;
- whether material control before title was properly addressed.

## Strongest defence

The Administrator may contend that:

- he acted within statutory powers and under judicial supervision;
- the Article 1535 theory lacked merit and was rejected;
- terminating or changing counsel was justified by estate control, cost, strategy or conflict;
- ACTÚA was a professional valuation used for a lawful liquidation plan;
- ONA and other proposals were conditional and not executable;
- material-control disputes belonged to separate owners, operators or proceedings;
- the 2021–2022 dación was judicially authorised;
- no recoverable surplus existed;
- later judicial and registry acts break or limit causation.

Those defences must be answered with the native decision file, not assumed away.
