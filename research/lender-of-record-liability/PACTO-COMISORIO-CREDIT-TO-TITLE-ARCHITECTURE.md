# Pacto comisorio / credit-to-title architecture

Updated: 20 August 2026.

## Executive conclusion

The strongest formulation presently supported by the controlled record is not that a classical commissory clause has already been proved. It is that there is a **serious, judicially determinable case of an indirect appropriation architecture**:

> A distressed secured credit was placed inside a sequence of creditor-side decision control, rejection or non-acceptance of alternative exits, transfer to a locally connected acquirer, a disputed valuation, material control before formal title, insolvency-administration gatekeeping, a withdrawal filed in LPB's name that eliminated an already-obtained disclosure order, judicial dación and later universal succession—without a complete, reproducible debt-value-surplus account yet being available.

The legal question is therefore not confined to one clause. It is whether the **substance and combined operation** of the steps converted security into ownership while defeating the safeguards that distinguish lawful realisation from prohibited appropriation.

This is the strongest defensible case. It is not yet a final judicial finding of pacto comisorio, fraud, collusion or crime.

## Central PP 1041 correction

The repository must not state that **“LPB withdrew.”**

The verified fact is that **a withdrawal was filed in LPB's name**, CAM did not oppose, and the Court ended PP 1041 on that filing. The decree identified Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte as LPB's lawyer. It does not establish an authentic voluntary LPB decision or identify the instruction, drafter, signer, filer, LexNET user, authority or Article 51.2 authorisation.

Por Derecho formally alleges that the filing falsely attributed to LPB a non-existent intention, deliberately harmed LPB and the estate, and was functionally executed for the benefit of CAM/Acosta Matos after the Administrator used insolvency-derived power that CAM could not exercise itself. Por Derecho further identifies José Daniel Acosta Matos and Laura Patricia Acosta Matos within the decision/benefit perimeter requiring actor-specific reconstruction. Those allegations are not final judicial findings.

## Why this is more than a sequence of lawful-looking documents

The case gains force from the convergence of eight proved or seriously evidenced features:

1. **The creditor perimeter received and decided upon workout proposals.** This was not a passive inbox.
2. **The borrower repeatedly proposed payment, refinancing, recapitalisation, bridge funding or sale routes.** The complete committee comparison has not been produced.
3. **CAM acquired the credit and rapidly connected it to a debt/valuation/dación proposal.**
4. **LPB obtained a concrete court order requiring CAM to disclose the assignment deed, price, payment date and costs.**
5. **CAM invoked Administrator conformity; the Administrator then removed the lawyer who had obtained that order and controlled the procurator; a withdrawal was filed in LPB's name.** No specific Article 51.2 authorisation has yet been located.
6. **The valuation used in the CAM route was contemporaneously attacked by another professional report on specific methodological and physical-condition grounds.**
7. **Material control preceded formal title.** Later title cannot retrospectively answer the authority for earlier control.
8. **The end-to-end debt, value and surplus ledger remains incomplete.** Without it, one cannot prove that the creditor received no more than lawful satisfaction.

The proposition is not that each step is automatically unlawful. It is that the steps must be examined together because each supplied an indispensable bridge to the same beneficiary outcome.

## Legal characterisation

Spanish law prohibits the creditor from appropriating mortgaged or pledged property merely by reason of non-payment. Spanish insolvency law may, however, permit judicially authorised dación or other realisation. The decisive distinction is substantive:

- Was this a genuine post-default realisation with independent valuation, contradiction, market testing, lawful debt calculation and surplus protection?
- Or did the formal route operationalise a pre-shaped acquisition in which alternatives, information, value and reversibility were progressively removed?

The PP 1041 episode strengthens the second hypothesis if the certified file confirms absence of authority, authentic LPB decision, estate-interest analysis or independent benefit, particularly if actor communications show knowing assistance to CAM.

The absence of a located classical ex ante appropriation clause remains a real weakness. It does not end the inquiry if the evidence establishes an indirect, simulated, fiduciary or fraud-on-law architecture whose practical function was prohibited appropriation.

## Strongest pleading formulation

> The court should determine whether the acquisition of the secured credit, the documented creditor-side control over workout decisions, the restriction or loss of alternative exits, the removal of an already-obtained assignment-price disclosure route through a withdrawal filed in LPB's name, the disputed valuation, material control before formal title, insolvency-administration gatekeeping and the later dación formed an integrated realisation mechanism by which the creditor obtained the secured assets without genuine informational symmetry, objective valuation, authentic estate choice or preservation of the debtor estate's excess value. If so, the legally dependent arrangement should be characterised, to the appropriate extent, as an indirect prohibited commissory appropriation and/or fraud on law, with accounting, restitution, surplus integration, partial ineffectiveness and other remedies determined actor by actor and asset by asset.

## Strongest opposing case

The defence will say:

- the original mortgage long pre-dated CAM;
- the dación was negotiated after default and therefore ex intervallo;
- the transaction extinguished debt rather than creating a future appropriation option;
- the liquidation route was judicially authorised;
- ACTÚA was a professional valuation and competing appraisals served different purposes;
- PP 1041 was initiated without required Administrator approval;
- the 15 February 2018 order recognised CAM as assignee and rejected LPB's strict Article 1535 position;
- Article 51.2 authorisation existed or withdrawal avoided costs;
- alternative transactions were conditional;
- later registry and third-party rights may survive independently;
- no CAM, JDAM or LPAM instruction or personal benefit is proved.

This defence is serious. It is answered only by primary evidence showing lack of authority, independence, estate benefit, value protection or surplus accounting—not by rhetoric.

Public wording may state that the controlled record supports a serious case requiring judicial determination of an indirect appropriation architecture and that Por Derecho formally alleges a false, unauthorised and deliberately harmful withdrawal filing in LPB's name.
