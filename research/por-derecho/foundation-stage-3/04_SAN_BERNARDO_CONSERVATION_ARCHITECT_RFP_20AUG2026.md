# SAN BERNARDO 27 — CONSERVATION ARCHITECT REQUEST FOR PROPOSAL

**Status:** prepared professional brief — not sent, not accepted and not authority to access, inspect, photograph, survey or alter the property.  
**Date:** 20 August 2026  
**Proposed client/commissioning perimeter:** to be confirmed by buyer-side counsel and the person with lawful authority.  
**Institutional context:** proposed future Palacete Por Derecho; acquisition not completed.

## 1. Purpose

This request seeks a conservation-led architect or multidisciplinary team able to test whether the proposed institutional uses of San Bernardo 27 can be reconciled with the building’s fabric, legal status, safety, accessibility, privacy and long-term maintenance.

**The house is not the Foundation.** The controlling conservation rule is: **institutional plan subordinate to safety, fabric and legal constraints**. A room name, public narrative or desired programme cannot overrule what the building, the law or the persons with authority permit.

The first phase is deliberately non-intrusive and desk-based. It is not acquisition diligence, a condition survey, valuation, planning opinion, heritage consent, technical project, cost plan, works instruction or representation that Por Derecho or any buyer owns or controls the property.

## 2. Absolute access and communication gate

No professional may:

- enter the property;
- request keys or access directly from an owner, occupant, agent or public body;
- measure, open, sample, probe, test or move any element;
- take new photographs, scans, recordings or drone images;
- approach neighbours, staff, contractors or institutions;
- contact the selling family about scope, defects, price or negotiation;
- publish the address, images, plans, history or proposed appointment;

unless written authority has first been confirmed through the appropriate property and legal channel. Buyer-side coordination presently runs through Carlos Llamas Sanz; this statement does not appoint him to the architectural mandate or authorise an approach without his confirmation.

## 3. Required professional profile

The lead should demonstrate:

- recognised architectural qualification and current professional capacity in Spain;
- conservation/restoration experience with historic urban buildings;
- Canary Islands planning, building-control and heritage familiarity, or a named local collaborator;
- ability to coordinate structure, fire, accessibility, MEP, acoustic, security, archive and digital-infrastructure disciplines;
- experience phasing interventions while a building remains protected or partially occupied;
- independence from the seller, buyer, funder and potential works contractor, or full disclosure of any relationship;
- careful treatment of family memory, archival material and institutional history;
- willingness to distinguish facts, hypotheses, permissions, defects and future design ideas.

A visually impressive hospitality portfolio is not sufficient by itself. The brief requires conservation judgment and institutional restraint.

## 4. Proposed institutional uses — to be tested, not assumed

The current concept includes:

1. **Casa de la Memoria Jurídica** — library and archive working environment.
2. **Sala del Patronato** — confidential governance and recorded decision-making.
3. **Sala de Trazabilidad** — controlled document comparison, chronologies and research.
4. **Responsible law-and-technology infrastructure** — discreet, secure and removable systems.
5. **Salón San Bernardo** — small institutional conversations, teaching and convening.
6. **Mesa de la Casa / hospitality** — limited professional hospitality, not a restaurant or hotel use.
7. **Research and working rooms** — staff, fellows, visiting academics and professional reviewers.
8. **Roof/terrace and Atlantic garden** — only if lawful, safe and compatible with fabric and neighbours.
9. **Accessible visitor route** — dignified access without erasing significance.
10. **Secure service/back-of-house route** — deliveries, archive handling, waste, plant and maintenance.

The architect must identify uses that are unsuitable, over-intensive or require legal/physical conditions not presently established.

## 5. Stage 0 — desk-based inception

Before access, the selected professional should review only authorised material and provide:

- list of information received and its provenance;
- list of assumptions and unverified matters;
- preliminary understanding of building type and likely significance;
- public-source planning/heritage screening, expressly labelled preliminary;
- questions for counsel, seller-side advisers and future surveyors;
- proposed lawful access and survey protocol;
- conflicts and independence declaration;
- professional team and insurance details;
- fee proposal for later stages, separated by gate.

No conclusion about condition, compliance or development capacity should be drawn from photographs alone.

## 6. Stage 1 — authorised documentary baseline

Only after written authority, compile:

- title/site-plan material supplied through counsel, without giving title advice;
- available plans, historic drawings, photographs and prior projects;
- planning, heritage and building-control records supplied or lawfully obtained;
- known uses, alterations and institutional occupation chronology;
- utilities, maintenance and fire/access records if available;
- family identifications and oral-history contributions with attribution/permission;
- a controlled image and document register.

The deliverable is a provenance map and evidence-gap schedule, not a merits opinion on the acquisition.

## 7. Stage 2 — significance and conservation framework

Prepare a draft statement of significance distinguishing:

- architectural/fabric significance;
- family and social history;
- Canary public-institutional history;
- legal and civic associations;
- later alterations and their value;
- elements whose date or meaning remains uncertain;
- views, spaces, fixtures, materials or records requiring protection;
- areas where change may be more acceptable.

Principles:

- repair before replacement where feasible;
- minimum intervention;
- legibility of new work;
- reversibility or future removability where reasonably achievable;
- honest distinction between historic fabric and contemporary interpretation;
- documentation before, during and after intervention;
- no invented historical narrative.

## 8. Stage 3 — non-intrusive survey plan

The architect should design, but not conduct without further authority, a coordinated survey including as relevant:

- measured survey and photographic record;
- structural appraisal;
- roof, drainage and moisture investigation;
- façades, windows, joinery, finishes and decorative elements;
- fire strategy baseline;
- accessibility audit;
- electrical, data, HVAC, water and drainage baseline;
- environmental conditions for books and archives;
- acoustic and neighbour-impact considerations;
- asbestos, hazardous-material or contamination screening by competent professionals;
- security and privacy zoning;
- maintenance liabilities and urgent stabilisation risks.

The plan must identify intrusive methods separately and require explicit consent for each.

## 9. Stage 4 — institutional-use compatibility matrix

For each proposed room/use, record:

- purpose and maximum credible occupancy;
- confidentiality/security level;
- accessibility requirements;
- fire and escape implications;
- structural and environmental needs;
- MEP/data requirements;
- acoustic/privacy implications;
- likely heritage impact;
- reversible solution options;
- prerequisites and permissions;
- conflicts with other uses;
- recommendation: compatible / compatible with conditions / relocate / do not pursue.

The matrix should prevent a prestigious room label from overruling the building’s constraints.

## 10. Technology principle

The intended character is:

> Ambassadorial in the public rooms. Intellectual at the heart. Technology almost invisible.

Please test a strategy based on:

- segmented and secure networks;
- minimal visible cabling and plant;
- removable furniture and equipment;
- localised environmental control rather than unnecessary whole-building intervention;
- protected meeting and archive zones;
- no concealed recording or surveillance of visitors;
- explicit recording consent for training/research events;
- power, heat and maintenance resilience;
- no public display suggesting an automated court or command centre.

## 11. Accessibility, fire and dignified use

The design must treat accessibility and life safety as institutional conditions, not late compliance exercises. The architect should explore:

- dignified step-free entry or lawful equivalent;
- inclusive routes and facilities;
- visual, auditory and cognitive accessibility;
- safe occupancy limits;
- compartmentation, detection, alarm and evacuation;
- safeguarding archives and historic fabric without compromising life safety;
- staff procedures and maintenance obligations;
- where the proposed use should be reduced if compliance would cause disproportionate harm or cannot be achieved.

## 12. Privacy and operational zoning

Propose a zoning model for:

- public visitor areas;
- invited professional/event areas;
- governance and confidential deliberation;
- restricted archive and research;
- protected technical systems;
- staff/service spaces;
- private family-history material not authorised for general display;
- interfaces with neighbours and the street.

The building must not become a public evidence-intake centre or informal legal clinic unless a future Patronato, counsel and data-governance process expressly authorise a separate service.

- has inspected the property before lawful access;
- provides valuation, legal, tax or structural certification outside competence;
- guarantees planning or heritage consent;
- has been appointed by Fundación Por Derecho, which is not yet registered;
- represents or has approval from the family;
- may publicise the commission;
- may proceed to works without further written instruction.

## 15. Selection criteria

- privacy/security awareness;

- willingness to recommend less intervention or no intervention;
- references for comparable work.

Price should be considered, but lowest fee should not determine selection.
