# San Bernardo 27 — preservation and institutional-use brief

**Date:** 20 August 2026  
**Status:** public-interest preservation concept only  
**Acquisition status:** not completed  
**Controlling separation:** this brief is not acquisition diligence and creates no authority over the property

## 1. Purpose

This document records a preservation-first vision for San Bernardo 27 as a possible future home for Por Derecho. It is intended to guide later conversations—only if legally authorised—with the family, architects, conservation professionals, engineers, historians, users, neighbours, insurers and competent authorities.

It is not a promise to acquire, occupy, alter or open the building.

## 3. Core institutional boundary

> **The house is not the Foundation.**

Por Derecho must be capable of operating without San Bernardo. If the property is acquired and lawfully brought into use, it may provide:

- permanence;
- memory;
- research and teaching space;
- institutional hospitality;
- a visible commitment to careful stewardship.

It must not provide any seller, owner, funder, donor, occupier or property counterparty with influence over:

- methods;
- appointments;
- findings;
- proceedings;
- publication decisions;
- conflicts decisions;
- access to protected records.

Ownership, occupation, financing, conservation, governance and method must remain legally and operationally distinguishable.

## 4. Significance framework to be verified

The present working hypothesis is that San Bernardo has significance in several overlapping layers:

1. **family and domestic history**;
2. **architecture and urban setting**;
3. **Canary public-institutional history**;
4. **legal and constitutional research**;
5. **economic and social dialogue**;
6. **rights, complaints and public-administration accountability**;
7. **the property’s own later legal and planning history**;
8. **a possible future layer of legal memory and responsible technology**.

None of these layers should be treated as finally established until source, date, rights and context are verified. A significance statement should identify:

- what is known;
- the source and version;
- what is inferred;
- what remains unknown;
- who may correct it;
- publication and intellectual-property rights;
- the effect of new evidence.

## 5. Archive and oral-history protocol

### 5.1 Authority first

Before collecting or copying material, confirm:

- who owns the physical item;
- who owns copyright or related rights;
- who may consent to digitisation;
- privacy and data-protection restrictions;
- embargo, attribution and withdrawal conditions;
- whether the item can be published or only preserved privately.

### 5.2 Minimum catalogue fields

- unique identifier;
- title or provisional description;
- creator/author where known;
- date or date range;
- physical/digital format;
- source/provenance;
- rights holder and permissions;
- people or places depicted;
- certainty level;
- related sources;
- conservation condition;
- access classification;
- correction history.

### 5.3 Oral history

Interviews should use:

- informed consent;
- agreed recording and publication scope;
- transcript review;
- correction and withdrawal rules;
- sensitive-person and third-party controls;
- separation of memory from independently verified fact;
- preservation of uncertainty and disagreement.

## 6. Conservation principles

### 6.1 Understand before changing

No intervention should be defined without a baseline understanding of fabric, chronology, condition, significance, use and risk.

### 6.2 Minimum necessary intervention

Retain and repair where feasible. Replacement requires a documented reason based on safety, performance, regulation, failure or disproportionate maintenance risk.

### 6.3 Reversibility

New elements should be reversible where technically and economically reasonable and where reversibility does not undermine safety or performance.

### 6.4 Legibility

New work should be compatible without creating false historical uniformity. Earlier and contemporary layers should be documented and distinguishable.

### 6.5 Maintenance as conservation

A funded inspection, cleaning, repair, environment and record programme is more important than a one-time visual restoration.

### 6.6 Safety, accessibility and dignity

Fire, evacuation, structure, health, accessibility and safe use are conditions of operation. Compliance should be achieved through an options appraisal that respects significance and equal access.

### 6.7 Climate and resource responsibility

Prioritise:

- passive comfort;
- shading and ventilation;
- water management;
- fabric maintenance;
- efficient, maintainable systems;
- whole-life carbon and repairability;
- monitoring before irreversible replacement.

### 6.8 Quiet, reversible technology

Technology should:

- serve research, preservation, safety and access;
- be physically discreet;
- use segmented networks and role-based access;
- permit safe shutdown and manual fallback;
- avoid unnecessary sensors or data capture;
- preserve privacy by room and function;
- avoid turning a historic building into a technology showroom;
- be documented for maintenance and removal.

## 8. Professional sequence and gates

### Gate 0 — lawful authority

Required before any site activity:

- written authority identifying the person granting access;
- permitted people, areas, dates and activities;
- confidentiality, privacy, insurance and safety conditions;
- photography, copying and publication permissions;
- prohibition of intrusive activity unless separately authorised.

### Gate 1 — documentary baseline

- title and transaction diligence by the authorised professional team, kept private;
- public-source and archive baseline kept separate;
- initial significance questions;
- known restrictions and occupants;
- records plan.

A documentary baseline does not authorise physical intervention.

### Gate 2 — non-intrusive survey

Potential disciplines:

- measured survey;
- architectural and conservation assessment;
- structural visual inspection;
- services overview;
- fire and accessibility review;
- environmental and moisture monitoring;
- external/urban context.

Scope, competence, insurance, access and limitations must be written.

### Gate 3 — targeted investigation

Only where necessary and specifically authorised:

- opening-up;
- samples;
- material tests;
- structural tests;
- drainage/camera inspection;
- hazardous-material investigation.

Use the least destructive method, record location and make good safely.

### Gate 4 — legal and regulatory framework

Determine through competent advice and authority engagement:

- planning status;
- listed/protected status and significance;
- lawful uses;
- building and fire requirements;
- accessibility duties and options;
- occupancy and event limits;
- licensing;
- neighbour and public-realm effects;
- archaeology or other constraints where relevant.

### Gate 5 — conservation management plan

Include:

- statement of significance;
- condition and risk register;
- urgent stabilisation;
- maintenance plan;
- intervention policies;
- options and impact assessment;
- phasing and dependencies;
- cost and contingency ranges;
- permissions matrix;
- archive and change-record requirements;
- review cycle.

### Gate 6 — concept and consultation

- test alternative programmes;
- minimise alteration;
- engage users and accessibility expertise;
- consult authorities where appropriate;
- assess operation, staffing, security and costs;
- record rejected options and reasons.

Concept images must be labelled as studies, not present condition, approved design or authorised work.

### Gate 7 — design, permissions and procurement

- competent design team;
- coordinated technical design;
- statutory approvals;
- cost plan and funding authority;
- procurement and conflicts controls;
- insurance and contract strategy;
- change control;
- neighbour and construction management.

### Gate 8 — works and handover

- competent direction and inspection;
- protection of retained fabric;
- quality and conservation records;
- testing and commissioning;
- accessibility and safety evidence;
- as-built information;
- building and archive manuals;
- defects and maintenance plan.

### Gate 9 — operation and review

- planned maintenance;
- environmental and energy monitoring;
- fire, accessibility and security review;
- incident and near-miss register;
- visitor and neighbour feedback;
- mission-use evaluation;
- periodic conservation review.
