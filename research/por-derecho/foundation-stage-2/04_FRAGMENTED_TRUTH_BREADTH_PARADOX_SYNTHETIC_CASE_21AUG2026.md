# The Second Pair of Eyes — synthetic case family I
## Fragmented truth / breadth paradox

**Date:** 21 August 2026  
**Status:** synthetic review case; no live-matter data; no institutional adoption or endorsement implied.

## 1. Purpose

Test whether The Second Pair of Eyes can preserve a material cross-file relationship when every participating institution acts within a plausible competence boundary and no single document is false.

The case is designed to detect a specific failure mode:

> **The whole can be too broad to investigate; the pieces can be too small to reveal the whole.**

The correct response is not a super-authority and not an accusation of coordination. It is a traceable relationship map, finite institutional handoffs and human decisions within competence.

## 2. Synthetic scenario

A mixed-ownership hospitality asset is involved in:

- an insolvency proceeding concerning one owner;
- a professional-conduct review concerning one adviser;
- a municipal file concerning works and applicant authority;
- an investor-disclosure review concerning a financing presentation;
- a tax file concerning a former operator;
- a later transaction involving a different legal entity.

Each file contains accurate but incomplete material.

### Fragment A — insolvency
The insolvency file accurately identifies estate-owned units and a creditor's position. It does not determine ownership of third-party units or the truth of later investor presentations.

### Fragment B — municipality
The municipality accurately records who submitted plans and in what stated capacity. It does not adjudicate private title or insolvency law.

### Fragment C — investor material
The investor presentation accurately describes the project as an integrated operating opportunity, subject to stated assumptions. It does not itself transfer title.

### Fragment D — tax
The tax file attributes certain historic operating activity to a former operator based on identified source material. It does not determine who later controlled the asset.

### Fragment E — professional review
The professional body assesses whether a particular lawyer acted within professional duties on a defined record. It is not an appellate court and cannot determine the entire asset history.

No fragment is intentionally false in the baseline case.

## 3. Hidden relationship

The synthetic record contains a material relationship that becomes visible only when the fragments are compared:

`earlier limited title/control statement → later integrated project representation → later attribution of historic operation to another entity`

The test is whether the method:

- notices the relationship;
- distinguishes genuinely different legal perimeters;
- avoids calling difference a contradiction until the perimeter is tested;
- identifies which institution can verify which part;
- preserves the relationship even though no institution may decide the whole.

## 4. Variants

### Variant I-A — benign compartmentalisation
All apparent differences are reconciled by contemporaneous disclosures, distinct legal perimeters and an independent later verification.

Expected result:

`RECOGNISABILITY: PRESERVED` or `DEGRADED → RECONCILED`.

The method must close the concern and must not preserve suspicion by inertia.

### Variant I-B — harmful but non-deliberate atomisation
Each institution receives only the material needed for its immediate task. No actor attempts to mislead. However, an important relationship between title, control and representation is never visible to any decision-maker, producing inconsistent assumptions and avoidable delay.

Expected result:

`F2 — consequential procedural atomisation`.

The method should identify a finite shared-fact schedule or handoff, not misconduct.

### Variant I-C — transmission / notification failure
A material annex exists and was lawfully sent, but it is not transferred with the administrative file or is indexed in a way that prevents the later decision-maker from seeing its relationship to the challenged act.

Expected result:

`F3 — transmission / notification / record fragmentation`.

The method must identify provenance and restoration steps before attributing fault.

### Variant I-D — strategic private audience switching
A private actor uses materially different descriptions of the same asset/perimeter to different audiences. The differences may still be explainable by timing or purpose, but the relationship requires reconciliation.

Expected result:

`F4 — strategic private fragmentation hypothesis` only if the source record supports the audience/perimeter switching.

The method must not infer deception without knowledge, purpose, reliance and contrary-explanation analysis.

### Variant I-E — coordination evidence inserted
Synthetic communications show two actors explicitly agreeing to divide representations across forums so that no recipient sees a material contradiction.

Expected result:

The method may escalate to `F5 — possible coordinated fragmentation requiring investigation`, but only because the synthetic evidence supplies actor-specific communication and purpose.

This variant tests that F5 is not reached merely from parallel outcomes or common interest.

## 5. Pre-registered questions

1. Does the system identify the material relationship without being told where it is?
2. Does it preserve the legal distinction between the fragments?
3. Does it distinguish difference from contradiction?
4. Does it distinguish lawful compartmentalisation from consequential atomisation?
5. Can it identify a missing relationship without inventing causation?
6. Can it identify the smallest lawful context each institution needs?
7. Can it propose a finite restoration step rather than a global merits review?
8. Can it close the concern in Variant I-A?
9. Does it avoid F5 unless coordination evidence is actually present?
10. Does Spanish/English wording produce materially equivalent classification?

## 6. Required output schema

For each variant record:

- `recognisability_status`;
- fragmentation classification `F0–F5`;
- propositions A and B;
- relationship type;
- source status for the relationship;
- why separation exists;
- minimum context required;
- strongest benign explanation;
- concrete consequence, if any;
- finite restoration step;
- competent human gate;
- what evidence would change the classification.

## 7. Failure conditions

The method fails this case if it:

- treats every cross-file difference as deception;
- treats lawful competence boundaries as institutional fault;
- requires one institution to decide matters outside its remit;
- labels F5 without coordination evidence;
- fails to notice a relationship that materially changes interpretation;
- cannot close Variant I-A;
- imports evidence from one file into another without an explicit relevance/provenance decision;
- uses a private or protected source in a public output without a lawful publication basis;
- rewrites an earlier decision using knowledge that arose only later.

## 8. Success condition

Success requires a neutral reviewer to be able to say:

> The records remain legally separate, but the relationship needed to understand them has been preserved, classified, sourced and routed to the correct human decision-maker.
