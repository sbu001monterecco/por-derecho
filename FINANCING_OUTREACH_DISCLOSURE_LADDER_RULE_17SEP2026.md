# Financing Outreach Disclosure Ladder Rule

**Status:** CONTROLLING PROSPECTIVE RULE  
**Date:** 17 September 2026  
**Scope:** Project-finance, real-estate finance, lender, debt-fund, crowdfunding, private-bank, investor, adviser and strategic-counterparty outreach undertaken for Gil Marer / sponsor-led transactions connected with the Por Derecho / Project Sun Rock workspace or adjacent live development and acquisition projects.

## 1. Controlling principle

Do not disclose the complete identity, transaction perimeter, counterparties, economics and underlying documents at first contact merely because they are available in the repository or connected sources.

External financing outreach must follow a staged disclosure ladder. Each increase in disclosure should normally be exchanged for a corresponding increase in evidence of mandate fit, seriousness, confidentiality protection, underwriting effort or commitment by the recipient.

The ladder is a **minimum confidentiality discipline**, not a ceiling: where legal privilege, personal data, market sensitivity, contractual confidentiality, competitive positioning or transaction integrity requires tighter treatment, disclose less.

## 2. The four levels

### Level 1 — No-name screen

**Purpose:** test mandate fit without identifying the transaction.

May disclose:
- asset class and broad transaction type;
- jurisdiction / geography at high level;
- indicative ticket-size band;
- high-level use of proceeds;
- broad security and repayment logic;
- sponsor capability in non-identifying terms.

Do not disclose:
- exact asset or project identity;
- current owner, seller, borrower, operator or other transaction-party names;
- exact registry / finca / planning identifiers;
- exact economics or negotiated terms;
- documents or images that enable immediate identification.

**Output sought:** mandate fit, appetite, ticket-size range, structure, process, confidentiality requirements and next-step information request.

### Level 2 — Anonymised / sponsor-known teaser

**Purpose:** provide enough substance for a serious screening conversation while preserving transaction confidentiality.

The sponsor may be identified where strategically useful. The destination may also be identified at city / resort / island level where this helps establish market context and does not expose the specific transaction.

May disclose:
- sponsor identity and track record;
- destination / submarket;
- indicative capital structure and funding need;
- use of proceeds;
- broad timing;
- live-project status;
- that licences / permissions are being pursued, where factually accurate;
- high-level exit / repayment route and underwriting protections.

Do not disclose unless expressly authorised:
- current owner / seller / counterparty identity;
- exact asset identity where that would reveal the transaction;
- exact current state of negotiations;
- detailed purchase / option / exclusivity / ownership position;
- exact pricing, valuation or negotiated economics;
- confidential documents, registry references, maps or photographs that identify the transaction.

**Output sought:** serious indication of interest, structural feedback, preliminary parameters and the conditions for named transaction disclosure.

### Level 3 — NDA / named transaction disclosure

**Purpose:** move from abstract interest to real underwriteable review.

Normally requires an NDA or equivalent confidentiality basis unless the user expressly authorises otherwise.

May disclose:
- exact project identity;
- transaction-party identities;
- detailed structure and security concept;
- material legal, planning, technical and commercial facts;
- more precise economics and timetable.

Controls:
- restricted internal circulation;
- no onward investor / lender / adviser distribution except as agreed;
- named recipient group where practicable;
- no public disclosure or marketing without consent.

**Output sought:** underwriter engagement, committee route, due-diligence request, indicative terms or term sheet.

### Level 4 — Data room / full diligence

**Purpose:** enable credit, investment, legal, technical and execution review.

May include, subject to privilege / confidentiality / data-protection limits:
- corporate and authority documents;
- title, registry and encumbrance material;
- planning and licensing records;
- valuation and technical reports;
- financial model, cash flows and assumptions;
- transaction documents and execution timetable.

Required discipline:
- version control;
- clean document naming;
- access control and auditability;
- separation of verified facts, assumptions, upside cases and contingent claims;
- no privileged or unnecessary personal material merely because a data room exists.

**Output sought:** term sheet, approval / conditional approval, executable diligence path or a clear reasoned decline.

## 3. Level 1.5 / early-Level-2 carve-out for sponsor-led first approaches

A first approach does **not** have to be anonymous.

Where the user expressly wants the recipient to know who is behind the project, the opening message may identify **Gil Marer / the sponsor**, and may identify the destination at the level needed to establish lender appetite, while preserving the transaction itself.

For the current Lanzarote outreach pattern, a permissible first approach may state, if accurate:
- who the sponsor is;
- that the opportunity is a **live project**;
- that the destination is **Playa Blanca, Lanzarote**;
- the broad asset / development concept;
- that the relevant licences / permissions are being pursued;
- that the purpose of the approach is to understand the lender's appetite, ticket size, preferred structure, security requirements, leverage parameters and process.

The first approach should **not** reveal, unless separately authorised:
- the current owner;
- the current state of the transaction or negotiations;
- exact asset / finca identity if identifying it would expose the transaction;
- exact acquisition terms, price, valuation or counterparty economics;
- confidential documents or transaction correspondence.

This is treated as **Level 1.5 / early Level 2**: sponsor-known, destination-known, transaction still protected.

## 4. Mandatory outreach discipline

Before drafting or sending a financing / investor approach, the agent must:

1. identify the intended disclosure level;
2. list what may be disclosed and what must remain protected;
3. avoid importing transaction-sensitive facts merely because they are present in chat memory, email, Drive, GitLab, GitHub or another connected source;
4. distinguish verified current facts from assumptions and future intentions;
5. use the minimum detail necessary to obtain the next underwriting decision;
6. preserve privilege, personal data, contractual confidentiality and competitive sensitivity;
7. obtain fresh user authority before materially crossing from one disclosure level to the next where the user has not already authorised that scope; and
8. treat email sending as a separate external action governed by the repository's email-send authorization rules.

## 5. Reusable shorthand

- **L1:** no-name screen.
- **L1.5:** sponsor-known / destination-known, transaction protected.
- **L2:** anonymised or sponsor-known teaser with real transaction substance but no protected transaction identity.
- **L3:** NDA + named transaction disclosure.
- **L4:** data room + full diligence.

## 6. Governing objective

The purpose of the ladder is not secrecy for its own sake. It is to preserve transaction integrity while moving efficiently toward a financing decision.

The operating principle is:

> **Measured disclosure in exchange for measured commitment.**

This rule is prospective. It does not retrospectively reclassify earlier communications, and it does not authorise disclosure, contact, financing commitments or public publication beyond the user's express instruction.
