## 1. Why the ETJ remate affects the Acosta perimeter

The ETJ 163/2020 disposition cannot be analysed solely as a dispute between Cuatrecasas and Matkator. Matkator alleges that the physical space corresponding to its property was already altered, incorporated into and exploited through the MYND Yaiza hotel by the HNT/Canarian Hospitality perimeter.

Accordingly, adjudication of the remaining registered title may:

- remove the title anchoring Matkator’s challenge to that integration;
- weaken restitution, restoration and accounting claims;
- introduce a formal title holder while the existing operator retains practical hotel control;
- consolidate or protect the hotel perimeter’s present position even if it is not the named formal recipient;
- affect the evidence and remedies in criminal, regulatory, tax, public-finance and recovery tracks.

This is an evidence-based routing hypothesis, not a finding of criminal conduct or a proved cession arrangement.

## 2. Three recipient identities

Every Acosta/HNT action must distinguish:

1. **formal adjudicatario** — the person/entity receiving adjudication;
2. **formal cesionario** — any later formally recorded assignee;
3. **real/terminal beneficiary** — the person/entity retaining the hotel use, possession, revenue or economic benefit.

The current formal recipient is not proved. The specific terminal-beneficiary hypothesis is the CAM/HNT/Canarian Hospitality/Acosta Matos-controlled hotel perimeter because that perimeter is alleged to control and exploit the integrated establishment.
