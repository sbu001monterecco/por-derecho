### 4.3 Article 68 subsanación issue

The source contains a section entitled `SIN ANEXOS: RECUPERACIÓN, CONEXIÓN Y SUBSANACIÓN`. It asks that if a previously registered piece cannot be found, the missing item and search be identified and a reasonable period be allowed to provide it before drawing an adverse consequence. Inspection request 12 repeats this logic.

**Controlled position:** because the Decree itself relies on Article 66.1(c), the absence of an identified Article 68 cure step is a direct issue for reconsideration or judicial review. This record does not prejudge whether another specific statutory regime displaces or qualifies Article 68 in this Inspection context.

## 5. Immediate options and interaction

### A. Optional reconsideration — statutory period: one month

Potential grounds:

- source structure and numbered requests contradict the proposition that the object was unintelligible in the abstract;
- specific proceedings were identified;
- individual attribution was requested as a finite inspection task rather than assumed;
- the Decree's own Article 66 framework raises the Article 68 cure question;
- request a specific statement of what fact, reason or petition was considered deficient;
- seek revocation/reopening or, subsidiarily, a formal cure opportunity.

**Critical interaction:** Article 123.2 Law 39/2015 provides that once reconsideration is filed, a contentious-administrative appeal cannot be filed until reconsideration is expressly resolved or deemed dismissed.

### B. Direct contentious-administrative appeal — statutory period: two months

Alternative to A, not a simultaneous merits filing. The Decree itself directs this route to the Supreme Court. LJCA Article 46 supplies the ordinary two-month period; Article 128 provides that August does not run for the ordinary contentious-appeal period, subject to its stated exception. Exact deadline calculation must be verified for the chosen route before filing.
