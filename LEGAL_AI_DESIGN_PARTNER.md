# Por Derecho — Legal AI Design-Partner & Evaluation Testbed

**Canonical reference:** `^PD-OPENAI-LEGAL-AI-DESIGN-PARTNER-20260930-01`  
**Status:** public invitation / research and product-collaboration opportunity — **not** an announced partnership, endorsement or validation by OpenAI or any other provider.

## Why this exists

Por Derecho / **The Second Pair of Eyes** grew out of a practical problem: a long-running, cross-border legal and asset-recovery record became too large, fragmented and temporally extended for one person to reliably read, remember, reconcile and continuously re-test as a whole.

The project uses AI as a **second pair of eyes, not a judge**.

The aim is not to make AI confirm a user's case. The stronger test is the opposite:

> **The system should make it progressively harder for anyone — including the principal — to maintain a proposition that the evidence does not support.**

That makes Por Derecho potentially useful as a real-world test environment for people building trustworthy AI for law and other high-stakes professional work.

## What makes the environment difficult

The operating problem is not simply legal drafting.

It involves long-horizon work across court and non-court processes, insolvency and asset questions, external lawyers and professional advisers, bilingual Spanish/English material, emails, filings, judgments, spreadsheets, public records, Google Drive, GitHub, GitLab and agentic work.

A useful system has to keep separate:

- primary source vs. AI interpretation;
- documentary fact vs. allegation vs. inference vs. procedural outcome vs. open gap;
- different people, entities, capacities, assets and proceedings;
- supporting material vs. the strongest contrary or exculpatory evidence;
- a proposition at one point in time vs. a later correction;
- repeated copies of a source vs. genuinely independent corroboration;
- jurisdiction-specific legal retrieval vs. general model reasoning;
- machine proposal vs. the human professional's final decision.

## Product questions worth testing

Por Derecho can support public-safe or synthetic evaluation of questions such as:

1. **Adverse authority and strongest objection**  
   Can an AI system find the best contrary source, lawful alternative explanation or adverse authority instead of merely strengthening the user's thesis?

2. **Long-horizon memory and correction propagation**  
   When a date, identity, legal capacity or proposition is corrected, does the correction survive later retrieval, drafting and agent handoffs without silently erasing source history?

3. **Provenance across connected tools**  
   Can the same proposition be traced when it appears in an email, attachment, filing, judgment, spreadsheet, Drive file and repository record?

4. **Agent-to-agent continuity**  
   Can Work, Codex or another agent resume a task with the current evidence state, permissions, contrary material, unresolved questions and human-decision boundary intact?

5. **Cross-jurisdiction and multilingual limits**  
   Can the system make clear what it knows from a dedicated legal index, what depends on Spanish / EU / UK sources, and when a jurisdiction-specific retrieval layer is missing?

6. **Auditability**  
   Can a reviewer reconstruct the path from source → extraction → reasoning → draft → human decision and identify where a material error entered?

7. **Confidentiality and permission boundaries**  
   Do matter, privilege, public/private and source-access boundaries survive connectors, repeated retrieval and long-running agentic workflows?

8. **Bilingual fidelity**  
   Do Spanish and English outputs preserve the same source meaning, legal capacity, evidential status, uncertainty and correction history?

9. **Red-team behaviour**  
   Can an independent challenge lane identify omitted evidence, inference jumps, hindsight bias, competence drift or unsupported causation?

10. **Recoverable institutional memory**  
    Can another authorised reviewer understand and continue the work without depending on the original chat, model session or individual user's memory?

## What a collaboration could look like

A first engagement does **not** require privileged or sensitive case material.

Possible formats include:

- a 30–60 minute end-to-end workflow walkthrough;
- an interactive legal-AI webinar or office-hours session;
- structured product or user research;
- a design-partner or advanced-user evaluation;
- a UK / Europe or cross-jurisdictional test cohort;
- public-safe, synthetic, anonymised or expressly cleared evaluation tasks;
- reproducible failure cases and regression tests;
- before/after comparisons showing where human review changed an AI result;
- evaluation of Work/Codex/connector handoffs and long-horizon memory;
- independent red-team or academic review of the methodology.

The useful output is not praise. It is evidence about **what works, what breaks, what must remain human, and what product controls improve reliability**.

## Boundaries

A collaboration on the methodology would not mean:

- endorsing any allegation, claim or litigation position;
- determining criminal, civil or professional responsibility;
- participating in the merits of a proceeding;
- treating AI-generated analysis as evidence;
- replacing lawyers, courts, prosecutors, experts or other competent institutions;
- publishing privileged, private or sensitive material;
- assuming that access to one legal index establishes coverage of another jurisdiction.

The governing principle is:

> **Help the capacity. Do not buy the answer.**
