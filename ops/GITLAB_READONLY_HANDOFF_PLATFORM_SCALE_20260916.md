## Strategic financial relationship architecture

The institutional proposition is broader than “who will lend for one transaction?”. The public-safe architecture gives a first-class financial institution three **independent** reasons to engage:

### 1. Family / private wealth

Potential relationship areas include liquidity, custody, investments, lending, family balance-sheet advice, future liquidity events and appropriate governance/succession work.

### 2. Aweswell / Sun Rock

Potential corporate relationship areas include treasury, banking, FX, HoldCo liquidity, corporate finance, Sponsor capital and recurring hospitality opportunities.

### 3. Individual ProjectCos

Potential transaction areas include acquisition/development finance, senior hotel debt, preferred equity, private credit, co-investment, refinancing, recapitalisation and eventual M&A/asset sale.

Each pool remains separately governed and underwritten. One relationship does **not** imply that every desk or capital pool will invest.

Do not add a public logo wall of banks/investors and do not imply endorsement, mandate or committed financing where none exists.

## Early private capital / public-offering boundary

The public website is informational. It is **not** a retail securities offering.

Preserve:

- no “invest now” CTA;
- no retail subscription or public application form;
- no statement that financing is committed unless separately documented;
- no use of future platform scale or unresolved damages as present collateral;
- the existing private-capital / non-retail UK-Spain-EEA regulatory boundary; and
- the principle that “HNW” status by itself does not automatically solve financial-promotion or securities-law requirements.

If a Loan Note, HoldCo facility or comparable private instrument is later pursued, it must stand on the actual issuer, actual contractual rights, covenants, controlled accounts, permitted uses, repayment rights, security actually granted, legally effective progressive collateralisation and clear maturity/liquidity events.
