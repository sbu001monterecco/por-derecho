## 4. Grace period / acceleration control

The 20-Dec-2011 debt-liquidation notarial package records the 27-May-2010 €850,000 mortgage and an 18-month grace period. Bankia's 9-Dec-2011 liquidation recorded:

- remaining principal: **€839,441.00**
- ordinary interest: **€17,236.08**
- default interest: **€696.73**
- total: **€857,373.81**
- ledger field: **capital unpaid / capital in arrears: €0.00**

**Verified accounting fact:** the recorded pre-acceleration arrears were not missed principal instalments; the bank then accelerated the remaining principal.

**Deed-level position now verified:** the executed 27-May-2010 deed provides **18 months of grace for capital amortisation**, with the first capital-amortising instalment on **1-Jan-2012**, while ordinary interest accrued and was liquidated monthly during the grace period. Bankia's December liquidation is consistent with that structure in one critical respect: it reports **€0 matured unpaid capital** and identifies unpaid interest from July 2011 before accelerating the €839,441 outstanding principal.

The Bankia enforcement pleading's statement that LPB had failed to pay both interest and capital amortisation from July 2011 is therefore not consistent with the deed/accounting as to **capital**: no scheduled capital instalment was due in July–December 2011. This is a material factual imprecision, but it does not by itself defeat acceleration if unpaid interest independently triggered a valid, registered acceleration clause.

**Then-current law control:** the pre-Law-1/2013 version of LEC art. 693 allowed total maturity where non-payment of one of the different scheduled capital/interest maturities triggered an agreed acceleration clause and that covenant was registered. The later three-instalment rule did not govern the January-2012 filing. Exact registry inscription of the acceleration pact and compliance with LEC arts. 572–574/685–686 remain source-binding tasks.

Do not use “the 18-month grace period proves the July-2011 default was impossible.” It proves only that **capital** was not yet scheduled; interest remained separately payable.
