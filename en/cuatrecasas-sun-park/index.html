Cuatrecasas and Sun Park: current documentary position.

The present position is cumulative, not alternative. The Acosta Matos / CAM / HNT / MYND-connected perimeter remains the alleged principal actor and beneficiary of the underlying control, transformation, operation and value-capture chain. Cuatrecasas is not presently evidenced as part of that perimeter or of a common plan. Its potential responsibility arises separately from the mandate it accepted and performed, the adequacy of its protection and implementation, and its later affirmative execution acts against Matkator.

Privileged advice and confidential settlement material remain outside the public layer.

Our current position

1. Primary underlying responsibility

The Acosta Matos-connected private perimeter remains the focus of the alleged takeover, control, hotel transformation, operation, financing and economic-benefit chain. Nothing in the Cuatrecasas analysis reduces or replaces that case.

2. Integrated mandate

The formal entry proposal was lender-facing and narrower. The work actually accepted, performed and invoiced to Aweswell was broader and integrated: multidisciplinary due diligence, insolvency monitoring, ONA documents, financing, security and coordination of the actors needed to deliver the hotel rescue.

3. Strongest failure allegation

The sustainable allegation is not that Cuatrecasas did nothing. It is that an indivisible June 2018 asset-protection emergency may have been under-reacted to and fragmented, without sufficiently preserving the physical, evidential, ownership, operational and financing status quo needed for the ONA/concurso-exit solution.

4. Later execution inversion

The later move from rescue adviser to enforcing creditor against Matkator created a separate track. The 2024–2026 applications for adjudication, reservation of assignment and opposition to suspension or fresh expert verification were affirmative professional choices, not merely the passive passage of time.

Controlling thesis: the Acosta Matos perimeter is alleged to have committed and benefited from the underlying displacement and consolidation of the hotel platform. Cuatrecasas had built and understood the lawful ONA–financing–insolvency-exit alternative. Its possible responsibility is cumulative: first, for any failure to protect and implement that integrated solution with due diligence; and later, for its own execution conduct against an asset within the same hotel perimeter.

June 2018: the strongest sustainable failure case

What the record prevents us from saying

It would be inaccurate to say that Cuatrecasas was silent or took no protective step. The firm identified a Matkator route, discussed precautionary and insolvency-court action, prepared a filing and later intervened before the Property Registry.

What the record permits us to investigate

Whether the response fragmented one emergency into separate partial tracks; whether timing around ONA displaced immediate disclosure or preservation; whether an agreed physical and evidential baseline was created; whether LPB and Matkator were protected as distinct owners within one operating hotel; and whether the client received and approved a recorded decision tree.

The current allegation is under-reaction and incomplete integration—not guaranteed failure prevention and not absolute inaction.

Decisive missing records: the final June emergency advice; the prepared and filed versions of the Mercantile-Court submission; instructions from the client; communications to ONA, financiers, the insolvency administrator and the court; any notarial, architectural, keys/access or preservation baseline; and the internal explanation for the chosen sequence and timing.

October 2019: an implementation and loss-of-opportunity window

The October 2019 communications are important because they join three facts: Cuatrecasas attributed two favourable decisions to its recent work; it considered that those results might help save the complex; and it stopped or suspended further work because fees had not been regularised. This does not prove that the hotel would otherwise have been recovered. It does require a disciplined reconstruction of what remained to be implemented, what was handed over, what could still realistically have been achieved, and what loss of opportunity—if any—was caused by discontinuity rather than by funding, judicial, counterparty or client constraints.

Fair defence: a professional firm may withdraw or suspend for unpaid fees subject to law, professional duties, warning, protection of urgent interests and an adequate handover. The issue is not whether Cuatrecasas was obliged to finance the litigation itself, but whether the exit was managed consistently with the urgency, reliance and foreseeable consequences of the integrated rescue work.

Strongest case and strongest defence

Strongest evidence-led case

Cuatrecasas performed an integrated rescue mandate, knew the interdependence of title, operation, possession, ONA, financing and insolvency exit, and faced a unitary June 2018 threat. Its response may have been too fragmented to preserve the transaction and the hotel status quo. A later favourable window was not completed. Years later, the firm made affirmative choices to pursue adjudication of a Matkator asset within the same hotel perimeter while resisting fresh verification and reserving assignment to an unidentified third party.

Strongest defence

The formal engagement was limited; LPB also had separate counsel; Cuatrecasas did take protective action and obtained favourable results; ONA and financing were conditional; counterparties, courts, funding and client payment were outside its control; valid fees and promissory notes may justify enforcement; registered title remained transmissible; the court made the decisions; and neither a connected assignee nor deceptive intent has been proved.

What is not established

  • No evidence presently proves that Cuatrecasas joined the Acosta Matos perimeter, agreed a common plan with CAM/HNT/MYND/RICPE or caused the insolvency court's decisions.
  • No evidence presently identifies an actual remate assignee or proves that any assignee was connected to Acosta Matos.
  • No evidence presently proves misuse of confidential former-client information, although the controls and information used in the recovery decision require examination.
  • The fact that finca 8584 may have been physically or economically integrated into a hotel does not by itself extinguish Matkator's registered title or prevent legal transmission.
  • The current materials do not prove a completed estafa procesal, criminal intent or judicial error beyond the finite hypothesis requiring investigation.
  • The fact that Cuatrecasas may bear professional responsibility would not exonerate or reduce the alleged primary conduct and benefit of the Acosta Matos-connected perimeter.
