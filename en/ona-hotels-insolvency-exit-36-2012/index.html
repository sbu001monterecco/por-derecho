JUNE–SEPTEMBER 2018 · OPERATION + FINANCE + EXIT + NORMALISATION

The commercial alliance around ONA Hotels for exiting Insolvency Proceeding 36/2012

This was not a “rescue” of a commercially dead hotel. It was an architecture intended to pay the recognised insolvency liabilities, restore professional hotel operation, return LPB to ordinary business capacity and address an operating fragmentation that pre-dated the insolvency proceeding.

The central point

By June 2018 a developed commercial package already existed: hotel-operation documentation, operator business plan, Cuatrecasas due diligence, ECO valuation, security, external finance and alternative transaction routes. The financing proposition does not depend on publicly identifying a single lender: the record preserves parallel and successive external finance routes before and after the 13 June judicial meeting.

WHAT THE PACKAGE ACTUALLY WAS

Operator + business + valuation + finance + security + exit

Hotel operation

A signed hotel-industry/operation instrument and operator business plan existed. Transaction correspondence described ONA as the intended hotel operator; the signed instrument must still be read with the exact legal identity of its parties, without merging ONA and Clubotel La Dorada.

Professional transaction work

The package included Cuatrecasas due diligence and an ECO valuation presented in the transaction at approximately EUR 25.6 million. That does not turn every commercial assumption into a legal fact, but it does show that the project had moved well beyond an embryonic idea.

Financial exit

The structure sought financing sufficient to pay the relevant insolvency liabilities, conclude the proceeding and then create the property security appropriate to the post-exit stage. The later signed financing document expressly confirms that staged sequence.

Important: Por Derecho does not say that on 13 June an unconditional, irrevocable, condition-free drawdown already existed. Some routes remained conditional. What is documented is a real, developed and continuing finance architecture with more than one external route.

SECURITY IN STAGES

Not yet having an LPB mortgage did not mean there was no security

The contemporaneous documents distinguish between the security available while LPB remained within the insolvency/liquidation process and the security that could be created once LPB recovered ordinary corporate capacity.

1 · Before exit

Provisional/interim security over shares, available assets, credit rights, corporate guarantees and elements linked to the hotel-operation agreement.

2 · Closing conditions

The finance route required documentation, due diligence, valuation, evidence of an amount sufficient to exit and formalisation of the security package. Conditions had to be satisfied, evidenced or, where applicable, accepted/waived under the particular transaction.

3 · After exit

The later executed structure expressly separates a “Security Package on closing” from a “Security Package once the Company exits liquidation”, including a first-ranking LPB mortgage after exit.

Why this matters.

A reading that reduces the transaction to “there was no mortgage, therefore there was no finance” erases the timing structure built into the documents themselves. The LPB mortgage was specifically contemplated for the stage when LPB could create it after exit.

13 JUNE 2018

The structure reached the Court as an exit proposal, not an abstract hope

Daniel Irigoyen left a contemporaneous account that same evening of his meeting at the Las Palmas Mercantile Court and his later meeting with the Insolvency Administrator.

According to that account, he explained a route coordinating fund + shareholder/company + operator to provide the necessary money, pay the insolvency debt and conclude the proceeding. He also presented restoration of LPB's legal autonomy, recovery of operating capacity and the ability to pursue the company's rights outside insolvency.

Irigoyen reported that the Judge allowed the route to be explored, asked for the application to be pursued and said he would speak with the Insolvency Administrator. Irigoyen then informed the AC and requested cooperation, including correct updating of the debt figure.

Evidential limit: the email is a contemporaneous professional account, not judicial minutes. The exact later filing, annexes, docket/LexNET record, judicial order, AC debt figure and complete procedural treatment still have to be certified.

NOT DEPENDENT ON ONE LENDER

Finance routes continued before and after 13 June

JUN 2018
Advanced but conditional finance. One route reached an instrument described in correspondence as a binding offer, although the primary document contained material conditions. Counsel later warned that this route was not yet a “safe harbor”.
AUG 2018
Another route reached an executed Binding Term Sheet. The instrument signed by both sides was expressly intended to finance LPB's recognised debt so the company could leave the liquidation/insolvency process, and it contained an expressly staged security structure.
SEP 2018
Work moved into financing and security documentation. The correspondence records transmission of the executed term sheet and further work between advisers on loan and security-package documents.

Lenders are not identified here. Their identity is unnecessary to establish the commercial proposition and parts of the finance material are confidential. The complete evidence remains in the private corpus and is re-queried when legally necessary.

NORMALISING OPERATION

The later financing also contemplated addressing the minority-unit problem

The later executed term sheet was not confined to debt repayment. If finance remained available and its conditions were satisfied, it contemplated potential acquisition of assets held by minority owners. It also linked the transaction to unitary hotel operation and the ONA documentation.

This matters because the commercial architecture was designed to address two linked problems at once:

Exit insolvency

Pay the recognised debt/applicable interest and return LPB to ordinary corporate operation.

Operate professionally

Activate the hotel operation with operator, business plan, investment and security linked to operating cash flows.

Reduce fragmentation

Create a route, where viable, to acquire minority units and rebuild a more coherent operating perimeter.

2008 → 2011 → 2014

The Monte Lanza–Multimatrix connection needs precise wording

Alimarket reported contemporaneously on 16 July 2008 that Monte Lanza had reached an agreement in June with Multimatrix to transfer the property and operation of the Sun Park aparthotel, then described as a 220-apartment complex. That report is useful evidence of how the transaction was publicly presented in 2008; it is not by itself the controlling source for the exact legal form of every vehicle. Contemporaneous source: Alimarket, 16/07/2008.

The controlled project record then fixes a different bridge: on 1 December 2011, the UK company then called Monterecco Sun Park Limited acquired LPB's shares from Multimatrix. That same UK company changed its name in 2014 and became Aweswell Limited.

Important correction.

Do not say “Aweswell is Multimatrix's successor company”. Multimatrix was the preceding shareholder/seller. The acquirer was Monterecco Sun Park Limited, and that same company was later renamed Aweswell. Any separate acquisition or subrogation into other Multimatrix economic rights is an additional documentary question and is not the same as corporate identity or succession.

DID THE ONA STRUCTURE “CURE” THE 2008 BREACH?

The strongest formulation the present evidence supports

The group maintains that the 2008 sale/exploitation architecture was partially breached or frustrated by the seller/minority perimeter and that the resulting fragmentation continued to impair unitary hotel operation. Later corporate and party records preserve that position.

But the complete primary 2008 package — contract/deed, exact obligations, seller by seller, unit by unit, performance, breach and remedies — is still required before stating as an independent legal conclusion that a particular Monte Lanza contractual obligation was breached and later legally “cured”.

Controlled formulation

The 2018 ONA-centred architecture was designed to resolve or normalise the same ownership and operating fragmentation that the group traces back to the incomplete/frustrated 2008 Monte Lanza–Multimatrix transaction. The later finance structure expressly contemplated possible acquisition of minority-held units and actions directed at restoring the unitary operating perimeter. Whether that legally constituted the cure of any specific 2008 contractual breach remains open until the contract and completion chain are complete.

WHY THIS MATTERS TO THE CLASSIFICATION

The commercial counter-record belongs beside the obstruction/non-collaboration narrative

The existence of this architecture does not establish that every act of Gil, Aweswell or LPB was legally correct, or that the Court or Insolvency Administrator had to accept any particular commercial proposal.

It does prevent a fragmented analysis of any categorical portrayal of abandonment, indifference to creditors or absence of efforts to preserve value.

The proper question is:

What happened to a structure combining operator, business plan, due diligence, valuation, external finance, staged security, debt payment, operating normalisation and potential minority-unit acquisition; what did the Court and AC formally receive; which conditions were satisfied, outstanding or prevented; and how was that history incorporated into the later reasoning of Judgment 163/2023?

Gil Marer alleges that the need for an extraordinary structure of this kind arose from private interference, governance/operation problems, acts of the Insolvency Administration and judicial handling. That causal proposition remains to be tested actor by actor.
