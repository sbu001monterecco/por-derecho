OWNER-LED EXIT · BEFORE, ON AND AFTER 7 JUNE 2018

The owners’ funded exit, with ONA as operating anchor

The owners worked through successive and overlapping banking, investment and bridge-finance routes to settle LPB’s liabilities and restore professional hotel operation with ONA. Gil Marer’s position is direct: the funded exit was fully in place and the adverse parties sabotaged it. The records below show the arrangements, what each participant reported, and where execution depended on access, preservation, debt certification and a coordinated closing.

The central point

By 12 June 2018 the package combined signed ONA operating documents, Cuatrecasas due diligence, an ECO valuation, staged security, the ONA-arranged Stoneweg/Varia lender-issued Oferta Vinculante Condicionada and the documented Elaia/Lagune EUR 26m downside route. The next day that integrated package was taken to the Las Palmas judge and then the Insolvency Administrator. In September the parties worked toward exit through payment or consignation to all creditors, and in 2019 Clubotel sought personation. Conditions remained for transaction completion; the package cannot accurately be reduced to an abstract, merely embryonic or LPB-adverse possibility.

Security was designed to bridge the closing sequence

A mortgage scheduled after LPB’s exit does not mean that the lender was meant to release money unsecured. The January term sheet and June correspondence describe security available before or during closing, followed by the LPB mortgage. This is a sequence of linked steps, with different safeguards in different proposals.

1 · Put interim protection in place

The proposals included pledges over Aweswell and relevant group-company shares, guarantees, receivables and unencumbered assets outside LPB’s estate. Gil states that the Aweswell shares were and remain available to pledge; present availability requires current title and encumbrance evidence.

2 · Coordinate funds, payment and releases

Agreed documentation would coordinate drawdown or controlled funds with payment or consignation of the definitive debt, the necessary releases and the steps toward conclusion. Later proposals used a bank guarantee or lender-controlled blocked account; these are distinct dated mechanisms.

3 · Complete exit and the LPB mortgage

Once payment, releases and the necessary court steps allowed it, the lender would receive the contemplated first-ranking mortgage over the specified LPB assets. The precise package depended on the route and final closing documents.

The record supports a secured, staged transaction design. It does not establish that every proposed security was perfected, every condition fulfilled or a loan actually drawn. A later lender’s insistence on a day-one mortgage must not overwrite the different interim-security route discussed in June.

LPB was the debtor. LPB was not the whole hotel.

Calling the entire hotel, or its entire operating business, “in insolvency” solely because LPB was in Proceeding 36/2012 is false and misleading. The relevant questions are which legal person was the debtor, which assets and rights it owned, and who held the separate operating rights.

LPB and its estate

Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. (LPB) was the named corporate debtor. Its estate comprised its own assets and rights, subject to the applicable estate and title rules.

Other owners and assets

LPB owned substantial hotel assets, but not all of them. Matkator and other owners’ property cannot be silently treated as LPB property. Even the administrator’s July 2019 opposition described LPB as owning 159 of 220 units in the stated perimeter. In its February 2021 pleading, CAM itself distinguished its 54 apartments from LPB’s apartments, which it said it did not yet own.

Operation and exploitation

Ownership of units, common-property governance and the hotel’s operating business are different legal and factual layers. An order concerning LPB assets does not, merely by that description, establish title to every hotel asset or the whole operation.

The 159/220 figure belongs to the July 2019 document and its perimeter. It must not be combined with the later 190/54/18 schedule or converted into a 2018 percentage of area, value, votes or operating ownership. The administrator’s adverse filing also opposed Clubotel’s standing and disputed its offer and solvency. CAM’s February 2021 pleading said its 2018 internal works concerned its own units and attributed shared-area measures to community decisions. These are opposing positions, not findings that the disputed conduct was lawful. A deliberate change of position must be demonstrated through specific paired statements; it is not assumed here.

GIL MARER’S DIRECT ATTRIBUTION · SECURITY PACKAGE

This was not a condition Aweswell had simply left outstanding.

Gil Marer says Aweswell had performed or could perform every condition within its control. He alleges that the decisive operative inputs depended on third parties and the insolvency/judicial sphere: preserving the asset and its operation, retaining access for due diligence and valuation, fixing/certifying the debt, permitting deposit and exit, preserving collateral and formalising staged security. Gil attributes to the five private actors, the creditor in material possession and the acts and omissions of the insolvency administrator and Judge a coercive parallel-governance and asset-control mechanism that degraded or blocked that package so the debtor could not pay or refinance the debt.

On that attribution, the ONA instrument’s suspensive conditions were not isolated Aweswell promises left unperformed: deposit, approval/operating perimeter and operator activation depended on finance, the insolvency debt certificate/authority, court action, asset access and cooperation or non-obstruction from the adverse perimeter.

Immediate proof boundary. This is Gil’s direct causal and criminal attribution—not a judicial finding or an established organised-crime label. The documents verify staged security and a multi-route architecture but retain lender/counterparty conditions and third-party approvals. ONA/Clubotel was signed; the 1 August Ben Oldman Binding Term Sheet is available in a fully executed copy, distinct from the earlier incomplete-signature variant; Lagune signed its nonbinding LOI while seller/Elaia acceptance blocks remain blank. A condition-by-condition ledger is needed to prove what was satisfied, ready, prevented, waived or pending and who caused each blockage.

WHAT THE PACKAGE ACTUALLY WAS

Operator + business + valuation + finance + security + exit

Hotel operation

A hotel-industry lease and suspensive annex were signed on 6 June 2018 by the Sun Park Owners’ Community and Clubotel La Dorada. ONA was the intended operator, not a funder. The signed annex preserved contractual effect from signature while making the hotel letting subject to sufficient funds being deposited in Concurso 36/2012 and the required owner approval, with a 31 May 2019 deadline subject to the annex’s extension provisions.

Professional transaction work

The package included Cuatrecasas due diligence and an ECO valuation presented in the transaction at approximately EUR 25.6 million. That does not turn every commercial assumption into a legal fact, but it does show that the project had moved well beyond an embryonic idea.

Financial exit

The structure sought financing sufficient to pay the recognised final insolvency debt, conclude the proceeding and then create the property security appropriate to the post-exit stage. A fully executed 1 August 2018 Binding Term Sheet confirms that contemplated sequence; it was not itself a facility agreement or proof of drawdown.

Important: Por Derecho does not say that on 13 June an unconditional, irrevocable, condition-free drawdown already existed. Some routes remained conditional. What is documented is a real, developed and continuing finance architecture with more than one external route.

SECURITY IN STAGES

Not yet having an LPB mortgage did not mean there was no security

The contemporaneous documents distinguish between the security available while LPB remained within the insolvency/liquidation process and the security that could be created once LPB recovered ordinary corporate capacity.

1 · Before exit

Provisional/interim security over shares, available assets, credit rights, corporate guarantees and elements linked to the hotel-operation agreement.

2 · Condition and responsible actor

The finance route required documentation, due diligence, valuation, evidence of an amount sufficient to exit and formalisation of the security package. Gil says Aweswell could perform its side while the blocking outputs—intact asset/collateral, access, debt figure/certificate and insolvency/court authority—depended on the AC, court and adverse perimeter. Each condition must be assigned and proved.

3 · After exit

The fully executed August external-finance term sheet expressly separates a “Security Package on closing” from a “Security Package once the Company exits liquidation”, including a first-ranking LPB mortgage after exit. Its conditions and expiry still governed whether the contemplated financing could close.

Why this matters.

A reading that reduces the transaction to “there was no mortgage, therefore there was no finance” erases the timing structure built into the documents themselves. The LPB mortgage was specifically contemplated for the stage when LPB could create it after exit.

NORMALISING OPERATION

The later financing also contemplated addressing the minority-unit problem

The fully executed term sheet was not confined to debt repayment. If finance remained available and its conditions were satisfied, it contemplated potential acquisition of assets held by minority owners. It also linked the transaction to unitary hotel operation and the ONA documentation.

This matters because the commercial architecture was designed to address two linked problems at once:

Exit insolvency

Pay the recognised debt/applicable interest and return LPB to ordinary corporate operation.

Operate professionally

Activate the hotel operation with operator, business plan, investment and security linked to operating cash flows.

Reduce fragmentation

Create a route, where viable, to acquire minority units and rebuild a more coherent operating perimeter.

Two layers that must not be conflated: personal declaration and external documentary proof.

Gil Marer states on his personal responsibility, recorded on 24 September 2026, that before 7 June 2018 there was complete readiness to comply with and complete the funded exit and that all conditions would have been satisfied. That is preserved as his direct evidence as a participant in the process. The independent documentary layer establishes a professionally developed and active exit architecture — Article 176, investors, DD, operator, security and closing conditions — but it cannot turn a future event that did not occur into an already-completed historical act. The distinction is evidential; it is not a retraction of the declaration.

Important correction.

Do not say “Aweswell is Multimatrix's successor company”. Multimatrix was the preceding shareholder/seller. Any separate acquisition or subrogation into other Multimatrix economic rights is an additional documentary question and is not the same as corporate identity or succession.

DID THE ONA STRUCTURE “CURE” THE 2008 BREACH?

The strongest formulation the present evidence supports

The group maintains that the 2008 sale/exploitation architecture was partially breached or frustrated by the seller/minority perimeter and that the resulting fragmentation continued to impair unitary hotel operation. Later corporate and party records preserve that position.

But the complete primary 2008 package — contract/deed, exact obligations, seller by seller, unit by unit, performance, breach and remedies — is still required before stating as an independent legal conclusion that a particular Monte Lanza contractual obligation was breached and later legally “cured”.

Controlled formulation

The 2018 ONA-centred architecture was designed to resolve or normalise the same ownership and operating fragmentation that the group traces back to the incomplete/frustrated 2008 Monte Lanza–Multimatrix transaction. The later finance structure expressly contemplated possible acquisition of minority-held units and actions directed at restoring the unitary operating perimeter. Whether that legally constituted the cure of any specific 2008 contractual breach remains open until the contract and completion chain are complete.

WHY THIS MATTERS TO THE CLASSIFICATION

The commercial counter-record belongs beside the obstruction/non-collaboration narrative

The existence of this architecture does not establish that every act of Gil, Aweswell or LPB was legally correct, or that the Court or Insolvency Administrator had to accept any particular commercial proposal.

It does prevent a fragmented analysis of any categorical portrayal of abandonment, indifference to creditors or absence of efforts to preserve value.

The proper question is:

What happened to a structure combining operator, business plan, due diligence, valuation, external finance, staged security, debt payment, operating normalisation and potential minority-unit acquisition; what did the Court and AC formally receive; which conditions were satisfied, outstanding or prevented; and how was that history incorporated into the later reasoning of Judgment 163/2023?

Gil Marer alleges that the need for an extraordinary structure of this kind arose from private interference, governance/operation problems, acts of the Insolvency Administration and judicial handling. That causal proposition remains to be tested actor by actor.
