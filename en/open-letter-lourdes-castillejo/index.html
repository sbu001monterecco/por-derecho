OPEN LETTER · 16 AUGUST 2026

Lourdes Castillejo: who asked to control Sun Park's digital identity?

Your name appears in a documented sequence of ownership or access requests concerning Sun Park's Google business profile during the transition toward MYND. I do not treat it as proved that you personally made every request. That is exactly why I am asking you to help clarify what happened.

This letter asks finite, verifiable questions. It is not a finding of guilt.

If you acted under legitimate corporate instructions, explain who instructed you and what title was relied upon. If someone else used your name, say so and help identify that use. If you know facts of public-interest significance, there is a lawful framework for reporting them without treating you as part of a collective accusation.

What is documented

28 October 2021A direct Google notification identifies an ownership request for Sun Park associated with “Lourdes Castillejo”, role “Business owner”, using mynd.hotels@gmail.com.
24 February 2022The next preserved request again shows “Lourdes Castillejo”, this time as “Employee”, using the same Gmail account.
20 June 2022Preserved Google material records another request as “MYND Hotels, Employee”, again linked to mynd.hotels@gmail.com.

That establishes a sequence of requests which Google's notifications associated with those names, roles and account. It does not yet establish who was physically using the device, who controlled the shared account, who gave the instruction, what IP address was used, what documents were supplied or who authorised the use of your name.

There is an important caution that we ourselves recorded in February 2022. Contemporaneous correspondence expressly considered the possibility that a third party might have used your name without your consent. We cannot erase that possibility now because it weakens an accusatory theory. It has to be resolved by evidence.

The earlier piece: a Gmail account speaking to our customers as “SUN PARK”

On 6 June 2019, a preserved communication was sent from SUN PARK <sunpark264@gmail.com> to a historical hotel customer. It asked the customer to remove belongings from the complex because Sun Park would be refurbished “shortly”. The historical operation immediately challenged the legitimacy of that Gmail address and asked that the information be preserved.

On the same date, a second technical item was preserved. During Google account recovery for sunpark264@gmail.com, Google's own interface displayed this masked recovery-email clue:

ros••••••@aco••••••••.com

That pattern is compatible with the domain acostamatos.com. A specific address under that domain was entered during the contemporaneous check, but the full address was typed by the person carrying out the test and, by itself, does not establish which recovery address was actually configured.

The correctly stated evidential connection is this: Google generated a recovery clue for sunpark264@gmail.com compatible with @acostamatos.com. That creates a technical link requiring native-provider verification with the Acosta Matos perimeter. It does not yet identify the person who created, controlled or used the account, and it does not prove that CAM, any named person or Lourdes sent the 2019 message.

I am not alleging that you had anything to do with the 2019 episode. The link to you is later and functional: first, a Gmail account spoke directly to Sun Park customers and carries a recovery clue compatible with @acostamatos.com; later, Google records requests concerning control of Sun Park's business profile associated with your name / MYND and mynd.hotels@gmail.com.

2019: sunpark264@gmail.com → Sun Park customers → recovery hint compatible with @acostamatos.com

2021–2022: Lourdes / MYND → mynd.hotels@gmail.com → requests concerning the Sun Park Google Business profile

The finite evidential question is: who had the credentials, instructions and authority to assume or displace Sun Park's digital identity and customer relationship at each stage?

Why the Gmail accounts call for an explanation — without turning them into “proof of collusion”

The use of sunpark264@gmail.com in 2019 and later mynd.hotels@gmail.com in the 2021–2022 requests matters for traceability: who owned each account, who could recover passwords, which person or entity administered it, which devices used it and who was able to act through a business-facing identity.

But a Gmail address, even together with a recovery clue, does not by itself prove collusion, concealment, fraud or common authorship. Those conclusions require native records, instructions, purpose, knowledge, effective control and effect.

What I am asking you to clarify

These are finite questions capable of factual answers:

  • Were you the person who submitted or authenticated the October 2021 and February 2022 requests?
  • Who controlled mynd.hotels@gmail.com and who could access it?
  • Did you know of the existence or use of sunpark264@gmail.com, or of any recovery route linked to the acostamatos.com domain? If not, say so expressly.
  • Which entity were you representing on each date: MYND, Canarian Hospitality, Hotel New Trend, CAM or another entity?
  • Who instructed you and what ownership, authorisation or operating documents were used?
  • What access was obtained and what profile name, website, telephone, photographs, status, links or categories were changed?
  • Were historical Sun Park customer lists, reviews, photographs, telephone numbers, websites, reservations, PMS information or other digital assets migrated or reused?
  • If you did not make one or more of those requests, who used your name?

What Google can resolve objectively

This need not rest on recollection or inference. Provider records can, through the appropriate legal route, establish creation and recovery of sunpark264@gmail.com and mynd.hotels@gmail.com, recovery history, IP addresses, devices and sessions, Google Business request identifiers, documents supplied and permission/profile changes.

This is no longer only a Google question

The later MYND Yaiza chronology is separately documented across layers involving RICPE investment and regional incentives and FEDER-linked financing. Those layers must remain legally distinct.

The digital-identity issue matters as a question of provenance and due diligence: what asset and commercial platform were being represented, who could represent continuity to customers, what title existed at each date, and what information later reached investors, managers, financiers or public authorities. A Google request or Gmail account does not prove that any later funding or incentive was improper. It does make exact reconstruction of the chain reasonable.

If you know the chain, you can help resolve it

This site has already published an open letter to current and former workers precisely to separate employment from responsibility. That distinction applies to you as well: appearing in a Google notification does not justify jumping automatically to a criminal conclusion.

But if your role gave you knowledge of internal instructions, the origin of these requests, migration of profiles, accounts, domains, reservations, PMS, commercial data or communications with CAM, HNT, Canarian Hospitality, MYND, RICPE or other actors, that information may have public-interest and evidential significance.

My invitation is specific: come forward toward the evidence.

Correct what is wrong. Explain what has a legitimate explanation. And if you witnessed or learned of matters that should be reported to an authority, consider using a lawful, competent channel. Spain's Law 2/2023 protects, within its requirements and limits, people who report certain infringements learned in a work or professional context and prohibits retaliation in the circumstances provided by the law.

I am not asking you to steal documents, enter systems without authorisation, unlawfully copy databases or give me company secrets. Preserve intact any originals you already lawfully possess, do not alter metadata, obtain independent advice if needed and use a competent channel.
