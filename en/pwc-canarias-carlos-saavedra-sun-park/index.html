9 · Criminal relevance / deontological consequences

The underlying continuum may warrant criminal investigation without predetermining PwC culpability.

The Project alleges that disputed Community authority, debt-based voting exclusion, minutes/records, access/control measures, private-unit/title displacement and later transaction/funding representations cannot responsibly be reduced to disconnected civil, horizontal-property, tourism and insolvency disputes. If objective falsity or absence of authority, knowing use and patrimonial consequence are established, criminal-law questions may arise.

PwC threshold: prior knowledge is strongly documented. Criminal complicity is not. A later same-asset mandate/action, actual knowledge/access, concrete assistance or legally sufficient omission, material causation and the required mental element would need to be established person by person.

Professional/deontological pressure point: if PwC records establish a later adverse or materially connected mandate concerning the same Sun Park asset while prior-client confidential information remained relevant or accessible, the matter is capable of engaging former-client, confidentiality, conflict, independence and professional-conduct consequences. PwC should be able to show the conflict analysis, information barriers, consent/waiver position and team/access controls. The former-client and professional-conduct analysis must therefore take account not only of individual Spanish entities but of Aweswell Limited's position as the UK HoldCo and foreign investor whose Spanish subsidiary and asset perimeter formed the subject of the earlier PwC professional work. Separate PwC legal entities are relevant, but are not a substitute for evidence about actual people, systems, referrals and access.
