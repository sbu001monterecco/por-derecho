OUR PRESENT POSITION

There is a serious case of indirect commissory appropriation to be determined. The case does not depend on pretending that a classical appropriation clause has already been located. It depends on proving what the combined machinery did: creditor-side decision control, rejection of alternative exits, transfer to CAM, a specifically contested valuation, material control before title, Insolvency Administrator gatekeeping, judicial dación and an unreconciled debt–value–surplus account.

This is not yet a final judicial finding of pacto comisorio, collusion or crime. It is the strongest legal characterisation the existing evidence is capable of carrying.

The legal distinction that decides the case

Lawful realisation

A post-default dación can be lawful where the process is genuine, judicially authorised where required, based on independent and appropriate valuation, open to contradiction and alternatives, limited to legally payable debt and protective of any remnant for the estate.

Indirect prohibited appropriation

The prohibition becomes engaged where apparent legal steps operate together as a security-to-ownership mechanism that strips out genuine valuation, choice, contradiction or surplus protection and leaves the creditor with the asset because of non-payment.

The decisive question: was the 2022 dación the independent end of a lawful market-tested liquidation, or the final formalisation of an acquisition route already shaped through creditor decisions, information control, a contested value premise, pre-title control and institutional gatekeeping?

Legal anchors: Spanish Civil Code arts. 1859 and 1884; insolvency rules governing realisation, dación, secured-credit satisfaction and the estate's remnant. Temporal and transitional law must be applied to each act.

The strongest opposing case belongs here

CAM and the other affected actors may say that there was no ex ante appropriation clause; the original mortgages pre-dated CAM; the proposal was an ex intervallo post-default settlement; the dación extinguished debt; the Court authorised the liquidation route; ACTÚA was a professional valuation; competing appraisals served different purposes; ONA and refinancing proposals were conditional; the 15 February 2018 order recognised CAM and rejected LPB's strict Article 1535 position; and later registered or third-party rights survive independently.

That is a serious defence. It does not answer the case without the native decision, valuation, debt and surplus records.

What a court should determine

  1. The legally payable and secured debt as at 21 February 2022, component by component.
  2. The date-specific value and exact property/business perimeter CAM received.
  3. Whether the liquidation route genuinely tested alternatives and market value.
  4. Who controlled the 2015–2017 creditor decisions and why the proposals were rejected.
  5. Why LPB's deed-and-price route was abandoned after the Administrator changed its representation.
  6. How the GESVALT critique was treated and why ACTÚA supplied the decisive premise.
  7. What authority supported material control before formal title.
  8. Where the €400,000 premises line, income, costs, debt extinguishment and any remnant were finally accounted for.
  9. Which later HNT or third-party rights rest on independent title and which depend on the contested bridges.

Our strongest present formulation

The court should determine whether the credit acquisition, documented creditor-side decision control, loss or restriction of alternative exits, disputed valuation, material control before title, Insolvency Administrator gatekeeping and later dación formed an integrated mechanism by which the creditor obtained the secured assets without a genuinely objective valuation and without preserving the estate's excess value.

If that is proved, the legally dependent arrangement should be characterised, to the appropriate extent, as an indirect prohibited commissory appropriation and/or fraud on law, with accounting, restitution, surplus integration, partial ineffectiveness and other remedies determined actor by actor and property by property.
