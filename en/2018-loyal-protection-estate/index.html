7 June 2018: omission—or affirmative enablement?

The highest-priority documentary question is now narrower and stronger. The record must establish not only what the insolvency administration failed to do, but what it affirmatively knew, requested, validated, authorised or allowed to continue before and immediately after the 7 June material-control events.

Perimeter rule. One physical and commercial hotel did not mean one owner, debtor, insolvency estate, operator, Community, client or source of authority. LPB, Aweswell, Matkator, CEXP, the Community, Pink Canary, CAM and third parties must remain separate act by act.

PRIORITY QUESTION · 7 JUNE 2018

What did the AC affirmatively do—and what authority supported it?

The accessible record supports more than a pure omission theory. In January 2018 the AC first requested material possession of the apartments belonging to LPB. On 18 January, his follow-up stated that the purpose was to take physical possession of the complex and requested the main electronic access code and instructions for its modification. The client escalated that change in wording to Cuatrecasas the next day.

Immediately after the 7 June events, Juan Tomás Parrilla reported a direct conversation with the AC in which the AC's position was that the Community had authorised the security arrangement and that Acosta Matos could be present in the complex because it supposedly owned approximately twenty apartments and could use its properties and common areas.

What Community resolution, title record, court authority or legal analysis did the AC verify before adopting that justificatory position—and what limits did he impose to prevent individual-unit ownership or Community security from becoming complex-wide control over LPB, Matkator, CEXP or third-party rights?

Ownership is not the same question as authority. A February 2018 working ownership workbook derived from the June 2017 Gesvalt dataset does not itself corroborate an Acosta Matos/CAM ownership entry in the reviewed owner fields. It is not a certified 7 June 2018 Registry snapshot, so it cannot prove CAM owned no units at that date. The correct next step is a certified finca-by-finca Registry position and the source relied upon by the AC for the reported “twenty apartments” premise.

Evidential boundary: the present record strongly supports prior knowledge, affirmative AC possession/access conduct and an immediate contemporaneous justificatory position attributed directly to the AC. It does not yet prove that he instructed the physical acts before 7 June, knew the asserted authority was absent, or acted with criminal intent. No direct pre-event AC→CAM/Matos operational instruction has yet been located in the accessible Gmail corpus. Those communications and authority records are now P0 production targets.

SECONDARY INSTITUTIONAL QUESTION · 2018

Where was the effective protection of LPB and the estate?

On 28 December 2018, CUATRECASAS approached the Tías Property Registry because, according to the record currently described and pending control against the original, it had identified a legal, patrimonial and registration risk concerning the CAM-facing transaction, the liquidation plan, suspension decisions and links between relevant assets.

If the risk was serious enough for external counsel to approach the Registry, what complete, sustained and accounted-for protection did the court-appointed insolvency administration provide before, during and after the 2018 events?

The question does not absolve the private actors. It distinguishes their direct conduct from the responsibilities attached to the insolvency office: verifying debt and authority; preserving value; preventing faits accomplis; distinguishing LPB assets from Matkator, CEXP and third-party rights; controlling funds; seeking restitution or an account; informing the Court; and documenting each decision.

Evidential boundary: the Registry action shows at least one protective act by Cuatrecasas and prevents a statement that all lawyers were wholly inactive. It does not, by itself, prove breach by the insolvency administrator, liability of private actors, or that every available remedy was implemented or exhausted.

Correct order of accountability

1. Direct private conduct

Each person and entity must answer for what it did, signed, represented, authorised, received or benefited from. There is no collective responsibility based on family, Community, firm or group association.

2. AC knowledge and commissions

The insolvency administrator occupied a court-appointed office. The priority evidence test is knowledge → authority → affirmative act or validation → effect → corrective action, followed by omissions and accounting.

3. Lawyers: subsidiary question

Their responsibility depends on client, mandate, instructions, knowledge, available remedies, work actually performed and continuity. They did not occupy the AC’s office.

4. Present do-over

Reconstruction, production, accounting, preservation, restitution and current remedies. It is not a procedural erasure or a premature liability conclusion.

Subsidiary question: where were the lawyers?

CUATRECASAS cannot be described as wholly inactive: the described Registry action and other rescue/protection work are located. Juan Tomás also sought Community material and pursued appellate/suspension work in June 2018. The sustainable question is whether those separate actions were sufficiently broad, urgent, integrated, sustained and implemented across Community legitimacy, voting, possession, Matkator/CEXP, suspension, Registry, restitution, accounting, AC accountability, funds, damages and handover.

The lawyers did not occupy the AC's statutory office. Their client, mandate, instructions, advice, filings, decisions on timing, available remedies and follow-through must therefore be assessed separately.
