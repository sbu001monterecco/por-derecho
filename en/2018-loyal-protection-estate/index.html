CENTRAL QUESTION · 2018

Where was the loyal protection of the estate?

On 28 December 2018, CUATRECASAS approached the Tías Property Registry because, according to the record currently described and pending control against the original, it had identified a legal, patrimonial and registration risk concerning the CAM-facing transaction, the liquidation plan, suspension decisions and links between relevant assets.

If the risk was serious enough for external counsel to approach the Registry, what did the court-appointed insolvency administration do—before, during and afterwards—to protect LPB and the insolvency estate?

The question does not absolve the private actors. It distinguishes their direct conduct from the responsibilities attached to the insolvency office: verifying debt and authority; preserving value; preventing faits accomplis; distinguishing LPB assets from Matkator, CEXP and third-party rights; controlling funds; seeking restitution or an account; informing the Court; and documenting each decision.

Evidential boundary: the Registry action shows at least one protective act by Cuatrecasas and prevents a statement that all lawyers were wholly inactive. It does not, by itself, prove breach by the insolvency administrator, liability of private actors, or that every available remedy was implemented or exhausted.

Correct order of accountability

1. Direct private conduct

Each person and entity must answer for what it did, signed, represented, authorised, received or benefited from. There is no collective responsibility based on family, Community, firm or group association.

2. Insolvency protection

The insolvency administrator occupied a court-appointed office. The evidence must connect knowledge, the applicable duty/power, action or omission, result, account and the contrary explanation.

3. Lawyers: subsidiary question

Their responsibility depends on client, mandate, instructions, knowledge, available remedies, work actually performed and continuity. They did not occupy the AC’s office.

4. Present do-over

Reconstruction, production, accounting, preservation, restitution and current remedies. It is not a procedural erasure or a premature liability conclusion.
