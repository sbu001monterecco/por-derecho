ALLEGATION ANALYSIS · NOT A FINDING

Why the entry matters—and exactly how far it goes

DP 3205/2014 is relevant to Gil Marer’s wider attributed allegations about improper use of Community authority because the summary attaches Community titles to two private persons in connection with a criminal complaint directed at Gil Marer and another person. That makes it an authority-verification event in the wider attributed Community-instrumentalisation theory.

Legitimate question raised

Was this personal action or institutional action?

The original complaint and contemporaneous governance record should show who acted, for which exact legal person, under what appointment or mandate, who instructed and funded the act, and how the court treated the capacity claimed.

Not established by this source

No culpability follows from the entry

The summary does not establish that either reported complainant lacked authority, fabricated an allegation, harassed anyone, abused process, participated in shadow administration, acted in concert, committed an offence or bears civil or criminal responsibility.

Further non-inferences. The source does not prove the exact legal person meant by “the Community”; valid appointment or authority; institutional rather than personal action; the alleged incident or injury; truth, falsity, justification or abuse of the complaint; knowledge, intent, agreement, harassment, procedural fraud or shadow administration; responsibility of either respondent; joinder to another proceeding; or procedural outcome and finality.

Reasonable non-culpable alternative. An ordinary personal complaint following a reported altercation, with Community titles used descriptively rather than as proof of institutional action, remains live. The primary court file and contemporaneous governance records are needed to distinguish that explanation from the wider attributed misuse-of-authority hypothesis.

Actor-specific proof test

For each reported complainant, any investigation must separately establish:

  • exact identity;
  • the specific act and date;
  • personal or institutional capacity;
  • the exact legal person represented;
  • appointment and mandate;
  • knowledge and intention;
  • filing and funding chain;
  • court treatment;
  • procedural or patrimonial effect;
  • any benefit or harm.

Association, title, chronology and repeated copies of one source cannot substitute for those elements.
