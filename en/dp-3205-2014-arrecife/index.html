CONTROLLED INCIDENT SUMMARY · NOT A FINDING

Why they said they were there and how they described the altercation

Contemporaneous reason. The complainant said she and the administrator attended in their claimed Community capacities to carry out unspecified work. She said access had been altered; after unanswered calls, a man who identified himself as a tourist appeared and they obtained entry.

Entry and refusal to leave. According to the complaint, he invited them to speak with another person and, after she invoked her claimed presidential status, told them to leave. She refused and called the Guardia Civil to seek mediation. While they waited, she said they inspected owners' apartments and left a key in a specified unit.

Key and physical contact. She alleged that the man, who was frequently using a mobile telephone, tried to take the key and that she tried to stop him. She said he held her tightly by the arm and may have elbowed her in the back. She said the administrator suffered a left-thumb dislocation and was then pushed when he intervened. These are attributed allegations, not medical findings.

Gil, witness scope and later call. The complaint attributes the access-dispute context to Gil, but no presence, instruction, agency or physical contact. The complainant said only that the alleged actor might be related to him. She said her husband witnessed the surrounding events and prior conflict, not the alleged assault itself; the Guardia Civil arrived afterwards. She also reported receiving a later call from an omitted number about a possible agreement. The complaint does not identify Gil as caller or recipient.

Other reasons remain open. A later account by the reported president mentions accessing property belonging to her husband's relatives, but does not formally link that episode to 3205/2014. A regulatory and safety complaint bears a 27 August internal date but was officially received after the incident: it does not prove filing, receipt or action before 3 September.

MULTI-TRACK ANALYSIS · NOT A CRIMINAL CONCLUSION

What an altercation with a hotel guest or occupant can mean

Independent reason to be present

Gil supplied private identity and capacity detail that is not reproduced because the combination could indirectly identify the anonymised person. The public rule is sufficient: guest status, acquaintance or voluntary assistance does not establish employment, marketing appointment, security responsibility, agency, instruction or concert.

Operating 50+ community context

Contemporaneous 2013–2014 communications describe real guests and residents and an active-retirement/community offer for semi- and fully retired people aged 50+. That establishes an operating setting in which a guest or occupant had an ordinary reason to be present. It does not prove the private person's incident capacity.

Authority and property

If the two claimed offices and work mandate were invalid, entry, unit inspection and key handling may support holding-out, arbitrary-self-help, trespass/coercion or complaint-truth inquiries. If valid authority and consent existed, a good-faith access attempt remains possible. Neither route decides the use-of-force question.

Physical contact and defence

A guest or occupant may seek departure or protect property, but lawful defence still requires the first act, necessity and proportionality. Injury requires medical causation. The complaint attributes the physical acts to the anonymised respondent and no presence, act or instruction to Gil.

Cannot be concluded: proved assault, false complaint, lawful defence, unlawful entry, abuse of authority, harassment, agreement, instrumentalisation, shadow administration, civil or criminal liability, or institutional bias.

Reasonable alternatives. A good-faith Community or owner-access attempt under disputed authority, a personal or family visit, excessive or unauthorised entry, a mutual or defensive key confrontation and independent intervention by a guest or occupant remain live.
