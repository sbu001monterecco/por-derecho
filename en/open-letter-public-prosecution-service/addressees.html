Objective and addressees of the open message

This is not addressed only to one territorial prosecution office. It is addressed to the Public Prosecution Service as an institution, and to every level that has received, decided, referred, supervised or reported on a material part of the matter.

Updated thesis

The thesis is not “Las Palmas Fiscalía is the problem.” It is broader: a system can become functionally instrumentalised when each isolated decision may appear defensible, yet no level reconciles the whole record, corrects inherited premises, distinguishes later facts from earlier ones, or tests whether repeated routing into the same decisional perimeter produced genuinely cumulative reconsideration.

The question is not only who was territorially competent, but whether the full prosecution architecture produced objective, traceable and evidence-responsive reconsideration.

Objective

We seek an auditable institutional response showing, for each level: what it received, what it decided, what it referred, what fell outside its decision, what later evidence altered earlier premises, and what happened next. Unity of action and hierarchy should correct fragmentation and error, not merely route files.

Wider audience

This message is also addressed to judges, prosecutors, lawyers, journalists, academics, investors and citizens concerned with rule of law, effective judicial protection and EOMF legality/impartiality. They are not asked to adopt our conclusion, but to test the documentary sequence.

Discipline and evidential ceiling. Naming a person means attributing a documented public act or identifying the current holder of a relevant office. The located acts support investigation of institutional self-neutralisation and an asymmetrically protective practical effect in the limited sense of classification/accumulation without an exposed public matrix; its absence does not mean no internal review existed. They do not prove favouritism, capture, agreement or coordination, knowing suppression or intent, prevarication or criminality.
