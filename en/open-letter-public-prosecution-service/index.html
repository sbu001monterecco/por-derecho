Open Letter to the Public Prosecution Service

Legality, impartiality and the right to independent reconsideration.

This letter does not ask the Public Prosecution Service to believe our account. It asks it to test our account—and to test with the same rigor the accounts, conduct and documents of every other relevant actor.

Has the evolving body of facts and evidence received sufficiently cumulative, objective and evidence-responsive reconsideration, or have institutional frames formed when the parties occupied materially different procedural roles continued to operate?

We do not ask for a favourable outcome; we ask for a verifiable process

We do not say every complaint must proceed, every requested investigative step must be carried out, or every territorial referral is improper. We ask that “the same facts” be tested by date, actor, conduct, asset, injured party and evidence; that LPB, Matkator, the Community and operators not be conflated; and that earlier decisions not turn their own factual premises into propositions immune from later evidence.

We do not ask the prosecution service to believe us. We ask it to scrutinise us rigorously—and to scrutinise those we identify with the same rigor.

The first institutional frame and the reversal of roles

The relevant history reaches back to the 2011–12 conflict. Later records identify a September 2013 insolvency-administrator communication/complaint as the origin of the earliest located prosecution route; a criminal complaint was subsequently filed against Gil Marer, Patricia Domínguez and related companies, leading to DP 332/2014. We do not conceal that adverse history.

In 2018, while that earlier route remained active, Gil Marer and Aweswell became complainants regarding conduct attributed to the insolvency administrator, Construcciones Acosta Matos and other actors.

Could the direction of prosecutorial scrutiny reverse when the direction of the allegations reversed?

Specific investigative steps concerning the insolvency administrator, José Daniel Acosta Matos and Laura Acosta Matos were later requested in DI 248/2018. The file was archived on 7 May 2019. The complete certified record should allow requested steps to be compared with those actually carried out. We do not publish as fact that they were not carried out.

New evidence, new events and distinct legal perimeters

“Same facts”, “civil/insolvency matter” and “already judicialised” can be legally correct categories, but they require demonstration. A 2022 transaction could not literally have been investigated in a 2019 archive. Matkator property does not automatically enter LPB's insolvency estate because it sits within the same physical complex. Community authority over common functions does not itself prove authority over every private space.

Relationship between facts is not identity of facts.

DIP 2/2026 and institutional correction

The record documents a tension between a premise concerning the absence of an appeal and the judicial record of RPL 3319/2025. The contradiction was formally notified on 11 March 2026. We do not say the error proves bad faith. We ask what happened after the correction was communicated.

Territorial competence is not the same as independent reconsideration

Territorial competence may legitimately explain many referrals back to Las Palmas/Lanzarote. But deciding which office is competent does not necessarily answer whether new evidence and contradictions in earlier decisions received cumulative and independent assessment.

Unity of action should not become unity around an uncorrected factual premise. Hierarchical dependence should not mean hierarchical inheritance of error.

Capture or instrumentalisation?

We do not assert as established fact that the prosecution service has been captured by private interests, that there is collusion with the insolvency administrator, minority owners or Acosta Matos, or that individual prosecutors acted from personal affinity or a protective purpose.

We do raise the possibility of functional instrumentalisation: an early allocation of credibility becoming an inherited frame; new evidence repeatedly returning to the same decisional perimeter; later events being absorbed as already examined; insolvency characterisation substituting for examination of potentially criminal conduct; or referral functioning in practice as a substitute for substantive reconsideration.

The documentary history makes that possibility serious enough to warrant a review capable of demonstrating independence rather than merely asserting it.

Effective judicial protection

We do not invoke effective judicial protection as a right to a prosecution or to every investigative step requested. The concern is practical effectiveness when the true object is not examined, distinct assets are conflated, new facts are treated as old without an identity matrix, a material premise remains unreconciled after contradiction, or fragmentation prevents any body from seeing the cumulative picture.

What we do not ask readers to ignore

There was a prosecution complaint against Gil Marer and Patricia Domínguez. There is a materially adverse first-instance culpability judgment under appeal. Several complaints/files have been archived. Territorial competence may justify referrals. Prosecutors need not automatically perform every requested step. Different proceedings may legitimately have different objects.

Our concern survives those facts; it does not depend on hiding them.

What we ask now

  1. An identifiable superior/coordinated review of the complete institutional route.
  2. An identity matrix before any renewed “same facts” conclusion.
  3. Certified reconstruction of DI 248/2018: requested versus performed steps, evidence available and archive reasoning.
  4. The primary 29 March 2021 DP 332/2014 order and its exact basis.
  5. Primary review of the 25 April 2019 prosecution rent expert report; it has been located, but we do not publish its conclusion until primary review is complete.
  6. Identification of any response to the 11 March 2026 DIP 2 correction.
  7. The signed prosecution report in DP 1901/2026, its author and subsequent court decision.
  8. The territorial reference, assigned prosecutor, completeness of transfer, investigative steps and status following GUB 86/2026 in Arrecife.
  9. Express separation of LPB, Matkator, Community and operator perimeters.
  10. An answer to the cumulative question: whether the sequence as a whole produced effective reconsideration before later consequences became consolidated.

An invitation to contradict us with documents

If an investigative step we have not located exists, identify it. If a referral included substantive assessment, identify it. If facts we regard as distinct are legally identical, explain the identity by comparing their elements. If a contradiction was corrected, incorporate the correction.

We do not want to preserve errors because they favour our position. We want the record to be more accurate than any competing narrative.

The final question

Can the Public Prosecution Service demonstrate that when the direction of the allegations reversed, the direction of scrutiny reversed with it?
Can it demonstrate that evidence arising after an archive was examined as new evidence when it truly was new?

We ask for no privilege and no immunity. We ask for legality, objectivity, impartiality, traceability and verifiable reconsideration.

The strongest answer to a concern about capture or instrumentalisation is not a declaration that it does not exist. It is a process through which that can be verified.

Evidential discipline. This letter distinguishes documented facts, inference and open questions. A complaint, archive, referral or procedural participation does not by itself establish illegality, bias or collusion. Open evidential gaps will be updated as primary evidence is incorporated.
