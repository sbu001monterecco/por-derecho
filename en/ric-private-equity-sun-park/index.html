The control thesis

One chain: source, authority, control, capital and benefit.

The unitary question: how did a legally fragmented hotel asset, subject to insolvency proceedings and third-party rights, come to be presented as an acquired, unencumbered and investment-ready Acosta Matos project; which controls did it pass; when was that picture qualified or corrected; what new evidence permitted its re-entry; and who ultimately obtained the operating platform, finance, support, income and value?

RIC is not a peripheral third party in this record. Its materials introduced Sun Park into a regulated investment perimeter and described financial, legal, fiscal, risk, executive and Board filters. The public question is what it received, independently verified, communicated and preserves.

Control point · provenance before appearance

The name “Community” is not an authority audit.

This is the bridge the reader must stop and test. A document apparently coming from an owner community, or a person presenting as its president or administrator, does not by itself establish appointment, resolution, scope, representation of third-party units or power to produce the requested consequence.

Reading rule: never accept “the Community authorised it” as a conclusion. Require the exact community, organ, current appointment, meeting notice, quorum, voting-entitled owners, resolution and minutes, mandate, units covered, each third party’s title or consent, and the verification performed by the recipient.

The chain that must be proved

An appearance of authority cannot become legitimacy merely by circulating.

Downstream repetition can amplify an unverified premise. Every arrow requires its own independent, contemporaneous documentary bridge.

Bounded conclusionAn isolated Community defect is not described as automatically criminal. The allegation is repeated propagation of unproved authority with proprietary and public effects, requiring independent reconstruction rather than deference to the label.

Primary legal framework: the Horizontal Property Act, Articles 13 and 19, identifies the organs, the president’s representation and minutes requirements; Law 39/2015, Article 5, requires representation for applications, declarations, communications and appeals made for another person to be proved and placed in the administrative file; the Consolidated Insolvency Act imposes diligence, efficiency, impartiality and independence on the insolvency administrator and provides liability for unlawful acts or omissions or lack of due diligence. None permits authority over a privately owned unit to be inferred merely from use of the name “Community”.

2020–2023 · from promotion to RIC materialisation and public support

The investment, RIC materialisation and grant award are no longer hypotheses.

Read with the benefit of the later record, the sequence does not end with the 2020 brochure or the defensive qualification of July 2021. It continues through a Sun Park-linked capital resolution, insolvency adjudication, segregation to Hotel New Trend, two participating loans, two RIC-materialisation share series and a regional-incentive award. The question is whether each decision-maker received the complete truth about title, control, litigation and third-party rights.

Unitary thesis · public interest

This exceeds a private dispute: contested patrimony became the substrate for finance, tax materialisation and public aid.

Gil Marer alleges that harm to LPB, Aweswell and Matkator was used as the asset-base for a chain of appearances: control of the complex, investor presentation, adjudication, segregation, RIC materialisation, refurbishment and employment finance, regional aid and hotel operation. Each layer supplied resources and legitimacy to the next. The investigable hypothesis is that the chain converted an anticipated representation of ownership and availability into a later economic reality without first reconciling the full proprietary and extra-insolvency perimeter.

The test is not a label of neutrality: it examines the acts and effects of the private perimeter, CAM’s defence, the insolvency administrator, the court, the RIC vehicle and public authorities. Functional convergence is documented by dates and outcomes; any clandestine agreement, intent and individual liability must be proved separately.

Prior notice · extra-insolvency title · current harm

The whole-title picture was formally challenged before adjudication, and Matkator titles remained live afterwards.

Documented regulatory notice On 13 January 2021 a CNMV filing identified the brochure, webinar, Sun Park, CAM and the alleged use of third-party property; on the same day AEAT issued a receipt for a tax complaint concerning “RIC PE investor fraud”. CNMV opened reference ED/36/2021, referred the filing to the competent department and, in 2026, confirmed that supervisory actions had been carried out. It also confirmed that the material obtained in those actions did not contain RICPE’s 20 July 2021 certificate or CAM’s 21 July court filing enclosing it.

Insolvency-court knowledge Aweswell’s 11 February 2021 filing told the Insolvency Court about the CNMV report, reproduced the investor presentation and supplied webinar and website material; it sought suspension, asset protection and an end to the public ownership attribution. Its 28 June filing added RICPE’s 19 June press item. Responding on 21 July, CAM asked that this new material not be incorporated while also saying it had never presented itself as sole owner and acknowledging 54 properties. The 28 February 2021 public post time-stamped the same warning; it proves contemporaneous notice, not by itself the truth of every accusation it contains.

Matkator title Deeds dated 26 June 2015 and 1 June 2016 document Matkator’s purchase of registered properties 8,584 and 8,588. Land Registry notes issued on 30 January 2025 still show Matkator holding 100% full title to both. A 4 March 2026 Cadastre response separately identifies three references associated with Matkator’s access position; Cadastre and registered title must not be conflated. The 2022 insolvency deed did not convey those two Matkator properties.

Continuing exposure The official MYND Yaiza website continues to market rooms, services and day passes and describes the Sun Park modernisation as co-financed by the European Union. Without a property–use–consent map, risk to possession, use, income, value and control of extra-insolvency units is current, not historical; so is exposure affecting RIC investors, taxpayers, public/EU funds, jobs, suppliers and guests.

Bounded conclusion: the answer to whether the integrated ownership-and-availability picture presented in 2020 was complete is no on the later documentary record itself. What remains open is the complete file received by each later decision-maker, what corrections were made, and the knowledge, intent or responsibility of each person. That requires the CNMV, RIC/AEAT, GC/836/P06, municipal, works and operating files.

What was communicated, who received it and why this was not merely a private dispute.

This was not one identical email sent to everyone. It was a connected sequence of reports, complaints and court filings, using overlapping source material, that placed the same central incompatibility before the regulator, tax authority, criminal channel and insolvency proceeding.

Formal knowledge · decision · registered effect

From attempted exclusion to operative title: the record fixes who received the chronology and what followed.

Precision correction It would be inaccurate to say CAM’s defence physically prevented the court from seeing the documents. The filings and exhibits reached the proceeding. The documented point is more precise: CAM asked the court not to admit or treat Aweswell’s new chronology as an integrated part of the decisional record while introducing its own certificate and a materially narrower proprietary account. The insolvency administrator also sought exclusion and reduced the harm to a hypothesis.

  1. 28 Jun–21 Jul 2021
    The chronology entered formally and met two convergent oppositions.

    Aweswell supplied RICPE’s 19 June publication as later material. The 8 July order gave the parties notice under Article 270 LEC. On 20 July the insolvency administrator opposed admission, said he “knew nothing about it” and treated as hypothetical any harm from CAM pre-marketing the assets in anticipation of adjudication. On 21 July CAM sought exclusion of Aweswell’s filing, denied ever presenting itself as sole owner, acknowledged 54 properties and simultaneously offered its RICPE certificate as an “illustrative” document.

  2. 7 Sep 2021
    The file reached the judge.

    The court office joined five filings and placed the case before the judge so that the new documents might, “where appropriate”, be considered when deciding the reconsideration challenge to the 18 May order. From this date, an account based on the court’s material ignorance is untenable.

  3. 15 Oct 2021
    Two orders issued on the same day described the decisive event incompatibly.

    One dismissed the challenge as having lost its object because the auction had been held and the properties awarded to the best bidder. The other said the bidding “did not even take place” because the improving bidder failed to attend and insisted it was not an auction. Both preserved the CAM-favouring outcome.

  4. 25 Oct 2021–26 Jan 2022
    The contradiction was challenged; the insolvency administrator opposed correction.

    LPB and Aweswell sought clarification and raised the omission of the new documentary material. On 2 November the administrator maintained that the orders contained no obscurity or error requiring correction. On 26 January the judge acknowledged the contradiction, said it was cured by relabelling the event a “bidding appearance”, confirmed that the July documents had been admitted and held them irrelevant; the order added that the court had exhausted transparency safeguards “to the point of exhaustion”.

  5. 8–21 Feb 2022
    The corrected decision became a conveyancing instrument while challenge continued.

    On 8 February CAM requested certified copies “with full formal effect”. The court office issued them on 14 February with a statement of finality. Aweswell challenged that step on 16 February, alleging no stated purpose and disputed finality. A later Land Registry note records a dation-in-payment deed to CAM authorised on 21 February 2022.

  6. 20–26 Apr 2022
    Registration preceded resolution of the challenges to the certified copies.

    The Land Registry note issued on 6 May fixes entry 21 in CAM’s favour on 20 April. Decrees dated 26 April then dismissed LPB’s and Aweswell’s challenges, holding that identification of the recipient was enough for issue of the copies and that the orders were final for all purposes.

Attributed personal statement · Gil Marer

“I do not regard it as credible that the insolvency administrator and judge were external, passive or uninformed.”

I, Gil Marer, take personal responsibility for this conclusion: CAM’s defence sought to prevent the decision-maker from treating a connected documentary chronology as one record and sought to replace it with a narrower and materially misleading account. Documented alert · two legal planes

Evidential exclusion, a duty to react and a protected reporting channel are not the same question.

The distinction: the 2021 filings were submissions within Insolvency Proceeding 36/2012; they were not reports to today’s SIINF@GOBCAN, and Law 2/2023 does not automatically turn a court exhibit into admissible evidence. Where its material, personal and procedural requirements are met, it does protect reports about acts or omissions after the Directive entered into force; reasonable grounds for believing the information true are required, not conclusive proof.

What occurred was not simple silence: the insolvency administrator opposed admission and minimised harm; the court received the chronology, later confirmed admission and held it irrelevant. The stronger question is which substantive response reconciled title, third-party units, promotion and impact on the estate before the court produced the bridge to deed and registration.

Verifiable duties in 2021: where a civil proceeding reveals a fact appearing to constitute an offence prosecutable ex officio, Article 40.1 LEC requires the civil court to notify the Public Prosecutor. Articles 80, 82 and 94 TRLC required diligence and loyalty from the insolvency administrator, placed the office under judicial supervision and addressed liability for harmful acts or omissions lacking due diligence.

Court

Produce any order or certified referral sent to the Public Prosecutor under Article 40.1 LEC, and the decision reconciling the proprietary alert; or state that no such record has been located.

Administrator supervision

Produce any Article 82 TRLC request or specific report addressing advance marketing, private units, control, works and estate risk; or state that no such record has been located.

Current channels

Law 2/2023 sanctions obstructing reports or frustrating or delaying follow-up. Canary Islands Decree 91/2024 requires acknowledgment, an admissibility decision, a response within three months —extendable by three for special complexity— and immediate referral to the Spanish or European Public Prosecutor where applicable.

Bounded inference: the absence of an identified reconciliation, combined with the administrator’s opposition, the judicial irrelevance ruling and continuation to title, supports documented optics of functional alignment requiring independent investigation. Neither silence nor an isolated procedural failure proves collusion by itself. The law itself recognises authority collusion as a circumstance capable of justifying public disclosure, but its conditions and the presumption of innocence, privacy and third-party rights still apply.

The CGPJ internal channel is limited to information obtained in a labour or professional context with the Council itself and expressly excludes external complaints about courts, tribunals and their personnel. It should not be presented as a substitute for appeals, prosecution, the competent disciplinary route or a legally suitable external channel.

Media · public notice · preservation

Public exposure operates as a flywheel of knowledge and accountability; it does not replace registry or judicial protection.

Legitimate function Each dated update preserves the chronology, enables authorities, investors, funders, purchasers, advisers, media and witnesses to locate the records, opens a right of reply and makes a later narrative of ignorance harder to sustain. Publication reconnects harms that procedural fragmentation treated separately and can generate new evidence or institutional correction.

Registry boundary General website publicity is not automatically personal notice and does not by itself destroy every future acquirer’s good faith. Articles 33 and 34 of the Spanish Land Registry Act require an individual analysis: registration does not validate a void transaction, while an onerous third party may be protected where it acquires in good faith from the person shown by the Register as entitled and then registers. Public exposure therefore complements—but does not replace—current certifications, targeted notice, interim measures, nullity or rectification proceedings and specialist registry advice.

Open property workstream Gil Marer alleges that further Matkator units were affected by unauthorised transfers. That allegation will not become a generic public list: each property will be added only after checking historic and current title, transfer instrument, entry, possessor, use, income, notice recipients and available remedy. The priority is to prevent further harm without confusing exposure with adjudication of guilt.
