Book 4 · institutions

Justice in Pieces

A system can hold every record and still lose the whole history.

A systemic investigation into courts, prosecution, insolvency, appeals, registries, public administration and institutional memory. It asks when legitimate procedural compartments stop being merely organisational boundaries and begin to obstruct understanding of cumulative real-world consequences.

The systemic question

Who sees the whole when every institution sees a lawful part?

Procedural boundaries exist for good reasons. The book examines whether justice systems also need mechanisms that preserve chronology, prior knowledge, contradictions, economic effects and later developments across those boundaries.

Different from Reason to Believe

From lived case to institutional structure

Reason to Believe is the first-person documented stress test. Justice in Pieces asks whether the same mechanisms of fragmentation recur elsewhere and what institutional design could preserve continuity without collapsing distinct jurisdictions into one proceeding.

Canary Islands / Spain

Case-study geography, not a pre-written verdict.

Sun Park and Las Palmas provide a starting laboratory, not proof of a systemic conclusion. Wider claims require comparison with other Spanish and, where useful, European cases. Facts, allegations, inference, adverse evidence and missing evidence remain separate throughout.
