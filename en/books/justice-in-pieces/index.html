Book 4 · institutions

Justice in Pieces

A system can hold every record and still lose the whole history.

A broader investigation into effective judicial protection, courts, prosecution, insolvency, appeals, public administration and institutional memory. Sun Park is the starting case study, but the book asks a larger comparative question: what happens when legally necessary compartments become barriers to understanding cumulative reality?

The thesis under test

Fragmentation can have consequences

Procedural boundaries exist for good reasons. The question is whether courts and institutions also need mechanisms capable of preserving continuity across them: chronology, prior knowledge, contradictions, economic effects and later developments.

Canary Islands / Las Palmas

Case-study geography, not a pre-written verdict

The book will test whether Las Palmas and the Canary Islands display distinctive institutional features or whether Sun Park simply makes wider Spanish problems unusually visible. Systemic conclusions must be comparative and evidenced rather than extrapolated from one dispute.

Method

Facts, allegations, inference and missing evidence stay separate

The book is designed as institutional nonfiction, not an accusation catalogue. Decisions, roles and outcomes are documented; disputed conduct is identified as disputed; hypotheses remain hypotheses; and adverse evidence is part of the analysis.
