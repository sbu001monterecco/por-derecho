Law-mower Man

They had the files. He found a way to read them all.

A nonfiction exploration of what changes when one person, pushed through years of legal and documentary complexity, gains AI-assisted capacity to search, compare and keep asking questions across the whole record. The machine does not supply the truth. It changes the economics of finding, testing and remembering it.

The central question

Can justice remember the whole case?

Legal systems can possess records without possessing continuous memory. This book examines institutional amnesia, fragmented proceedings, information asymmetry, provenance, hallucination risk and the possibility of AI as a disciplined memory layer rather than an automated judge.

The laboratory

One asset. One history. Many files.

Sun Park / MYND Yaiza provides the lived laboratory: one physical asset whose history became distributed across insolvency, enforcement, company, registry, administrative, tax and criminal/prosecutorial tracks. The broader question is whether AI can restore factual continuity while preserving legal distinctions.

Reader promise

Not an AI-superhero story

The transformation is informational, not physical: buried → searching → mapping → seeing → testing → public memory. The core discipline is simple: follow the title, credit, money, control, decisions, knowledge and consequences — and return every claim to source evidence.
