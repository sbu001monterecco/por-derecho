INDEPENDENT MATTERS · EXACT JUDGMENT OVERLAP · NO BORROWED LIABILITY

One firm. Two high-value clients. One repeated question.

The public Edgeworth Capital proceedings concern an allegation that Cuatrecasas failed to advise on the effect of two 2019 Spanish Supreme Court decisions before a June 2019 transaction. The independent Aweswell / Sun Rock record contains the same STS 112/2019 of 20 February 2019, sent to Cuatrecasas on 31 May 2019 with its asserted economic significance explained.

Boundary: these are separate matters with different parties, transactions, duties, causation questions and alleged losses. Cuatrecasas denies the Edgeworth claim. The English court has not determined professional-negligence liability. Nothing here transfers liability or findings from one matter to the other.

The public UK record

Claim

Edgeworth Capital (Luxembourg) S.à r.l. v Cuatrecasas Gonçalves Pereira LLP, BL-2024-000237 / [2025] EWHC 1014 (Ch), records a pleaded €213 million professional-negligence and breach-of-retainer claim.

2019 decisions

The pleaded allegation concerns two early-2019 Spanish Supreme Court decisions, including STS 112/2019 and STS 227/2019, before a June 2019 transaction.

Posture

Cuatrecasas denies the claim. The 2 May 2025 judgment addressed procedural issues and did not determine duty, breach, causation or loss on the merits.

The independent Aweswell record

On 31 May 2019, Aweswell sent Cuatrecasas an email attaching “Sentencia Tribunal Supremo - Intereses 20FEB2019.pdf”, identified in the preserved record as STS 112/2019. The email explained the client's asserted consequence for insolvency interest. Cuatrecasas replied that the clarifications were important and said the points would be made clear if they arose in the planned conversation.

What this establishes: receipt of STS 112/2019 within the Aweswell / Sun Rock professional relationship, together with a contemporaneous explanation of its claimed economic relevance. What it does not establish by itself: negligence, causation, loss, deliberate conduct, or the merits of the Edgeworth claim.

When critical Spanish Supreme Court law was available, was it adequately converted into effective client protection?

Two independent matters now raise a comparable professional-risk question. That is a reason to examine each record carefully, not a basis to transfer liability, motive, damages or findings.

No propensity inference: two allegations involving the same law-firm brand do not establish repeated negligence or institutional culpability. Each proposition requires its own retainer, knowledge, duty, advice, implementation, counterfactual, causation and loss analysis.
