SCOPE OF THE APPEAL

Grounds found in the reviewed appeal briefs

These are grounds pleaded by appellants, not findings made by the Provincial Court. This summary neither replaces nor expands the filed briefs.

Procedural safeguards

Defencelessness and nullity

Gil Marer and Pink Canary/Patricia allege a denial of effective representation concerning the 25 July 2023 hearing, the absence of their own counsel and the requested adjournment.

Reasoning

Consistency and sufficient reasons

The briefs allege insufficient reasoning, internal tensions and inadequate treatment of material questions raised by the parties.

Facts and causation

Rent, the Pink contract and aggravation

The appeals challenge the treatment of rent, the Pink contract and costs, expert evidence, and the causal link between the alleged conduct and the creation or aggravation of insolvency.

Other challenged conclusions

Accounts, timing, cooperation and sanction

Depending on the appellant, the reviewed briefs challenge the accounting analysis, timing of the insolvency filing, cooperation, alleged defects in the administrator's report and the reasoning for the ten-year disqualification.

LEGITIMATE APPELLATE REVIEW

What is—and is not—asked of the court

The legitimate expectation is narrow and exacting.

RPL 2523/2025 must be decided on its record, grounds, adversarial process and applicable law. The public criminal-misuse thesis is not extra-record evidence and seeks no predetermined outcome. The Court is asked to confront independently the pleaded defence, evidence, reasoning, causation and proportionality issues, identify which propositions survive scrutiny and explain why. Institutional independence includes the capacity to correct first-instance error; it is not deference to it. All other civil, criminal, regulatory and disciplinary rights remain expressly reserved in their proper forums.

The Court is not asked to adopt Por Derecho's public theses, investigate the entire Sun Park history de novo, or consider documents not admitted through a lawful procedural route. An adverse appellate decision would not prove offence, retaliation or capture; a successful appeal would not prove the criminal thesis.

Public criticism of institutional conduct and judicial decisions is compatible with respect for judicial independence. This page excludes threats, personal pressure, advance attribution of criminal conduct, and any suggestion that legal disagreement alone proves bias.
