Claim - reconstruction from the judgment

SECONDARY RECONSTRUCTION: the filed claim and documents 1-7 are not in the located corpus.

The judgment records that Garrigues sued Gil and Patricia as guarantors of Luchy's obligations under a January 2012 legal-services proposal. It sought €63,441.67, statutory interest and costs.

The engagement was described in three blocks: an executive report on Luchy's position; updating its litigation, with advocacy separately chargeable; and Bankia debt restructuring. The first two were charged at €310 per hour. The third carried a stated fixed price of €85,000 and, according to the judgment, was not part of this claim.

Garrigues said it performed document, corporate, litigation, notarial and financial review work between January and June 2012. The relationship ended after Garrigues learned that the insolvency petition had been filed with different counsel. A central difficulty recorded in the judgment was that the monthly fee claims mixed work under the first two blocks with Bankia restructuring, tax advice and separately priced litigation.

Defence - reconstruction from the judgment

SECONDARY RECONSTRUCTION: the filed defence under Juan Tomás Parrilla's direction and its annexes are not in the located corpus.

The judgment records these defence lines:

  • the claim was premature while the invoices' treatment in Luchy's insolvency remained unresolved;
  • the guarantee clause did not create internal joint-and-several liability among every co-guarantor;
  • the executive report was not delivered, or at least was neither produced nor described;
  • the work updating all proceedings was not adequately evidenced;
  • the emails mixed subject matters and did not allocate hours to specific tasks;
  • Bankia restructuring was a separate fixed-price block and not part of this claim;
  • the €2,835 tax invoice fell outside the guaranteed engagement; and
  • the €9,450 invoice belonged to a separate litigation engagement and was not personally guaranteed by Gil and Patricia.

The judgment does not reproduce the defence's full legal grounds, literal prayer for relief, annexes or their procedural order. Those remain open evidence.

Why the claim failed

The judge found that a professional relationship existed and that Garrigues performed multiple meetings, tasks and pieces of work. The dismissal did not find that no work had been done. It held that the evidence did not allow the court to quantify what could properly be recovered from Gil and Patricia under the guarantee.

Mixed work

Entries combined Bankia restructuring, separately billed litigation and other matters outside the claimed perimeter.

Unallocated hours

The invoices and supporting narrative did not adequately tie each proved task to time spent.

Guarantee perimeter

Tax advice and a separate litigation engagement could not automatically be passed to the individual guarantors.

English summary of 344/2013

Non-certified translation and summary: this English page translates and summarises the Spanish Judgment 41/2014 for access. It is not a separate judgment, certified translation, filed pleading or substitute for the Spanish source.

In summary, Garrigues sued Gil and Patricia under a guarantee for six LPB invoices. The court accepted that professional work had been carried out, but found that the material did not adequately allocate tasks and hours within the guaranteed scope and mixed Bankia restructuring, tax and separately engaged litigation. It therefore dismissed the claim in full and ordered Garrigues to pay first-instance costs. The judgment said it was appealable; appeal and finality records have not been located.
