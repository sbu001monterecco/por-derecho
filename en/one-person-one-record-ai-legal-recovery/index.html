Gil Marer · a personal account

I did not set out to build legal AI.
I was trying not to lose the truth.

This began with a hotel, a business and an insolvency. Years later I found myself trying to hold together one continuous history while courts, prosecutors, administrators, companies, regulators and professional advisers each held different pieces of it.

At times it has felt like one person against the “local world”. I do not mean that everyone was acting together. I mean that, one by one, the places I expected to correct the last problem became part of the next question.

This started before AI

I thought an insolvency came with safeguards.

Sun Park in Playa Blanca was not an abstract legal file to me. It was a hotel, an operating business, property, income, years of work and the base of a larger hospitality plan. In 2012 one of the Spanish companies, Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U., entered Concurso 36/2012 in Las Palmas.

I understood what that was supposed to mean. There would be an insolvency administrator. There would be judicial supervision. Lawyers could challenge mistakes. Appeals existed. If evidence of crime appeared, prosecutors and police existed. Different institutions were meant to check one another.

That architecture matters to this story because my later experience was not simply “I lost a case.” It was that the corrective layers themselves gradually became part of what I was asking somebody else to examine.

Important boundary. That is my experience and my allegation about particular acts and failures. It is not a claim that every judge, prosecutor, lawyer, official or company in the Canary Islands acted together, and it is not an adjudicated finding that the system was corrupt.

The week the story changed.

By June 2018 I was not sitting back waiting for the insolvency to consume the business. We were trying to build a financed exit and put the hotel back into professional, unitary operation.

Those four dates are why I cannot tell the story as “a hotel failed and somebody else later bought it.” They show a live operating and financing effort immediately before, during and after the 7 June event.

They do not prove my strongest theory. They do not by themselves prove deliberate sabotage, criminal participation by the judge or administrator, or that the transaction would certainly have closed. Those are separate propositions requiring separate evidence.

And there is adverse evidence I do not hide: the 2018 criminal route was provisionally dismissed and that dismissal was confirmed on appeal. That outcome is part of my record too.

The part that is hardest to explain

What happens when the check on the check becomes part of the question?

In a normal dispute, if you believe a private party has acted unlawfully, you go to a lawyer. If the problem concerns an insolvency estate, there is an administrator and a court. If there may be a crime, there is a prosecutor. If a decision is wrong, there is review or appeal. If public money or regulated investment is involved, there are specialist authorities.

My experience became different. I began raising questions not only about private actors, but—separately and with different evidence—about the conduct of the court-appointed insolvency administrator, judicial handling, prosecutorial handling and the way evidence moved, or did not move, between institutions.

By 2026 there were distinct routes including DP 1901/2026, DP 1956/2026, DIP 2/2026 and E.G. 745/2026. They are not one proceeding and they do not prove one another. But to me they all touch different parts of the same underlying history.

That is what I mean by a convergence failure.

Not “everyone is in on it.” Not inherited guilt. Not a conspiracy created by association.

I mean that several layers designed to correct one another can become simultaneously disputed, fragmented or incomplete. The individual then has no single place where the whole factual memory lives.

Some of my positions have lost. Some have been archived. Some remain open. Some of my strongest allegations may ultimately fail. That is exactly why I need a system that remembers the evidence against me as well as the evidence I rely on.

Why ChatGPT became important

I needed memory before I needed prose.

People often hear “AI and legal work” and imagine a machine drafting a pleading. That is not the most important thing ChatGPT has done for me.

The difficult part is remembering that a sentence in a 2016 email may matter to a 2018 access event; that a 2020 investor presentation cannot silently rewrite title as it existed at the time; that a 2022 deed cannot be backdated into proof of 2018 authority; that a 2023 judgment must be read alongside the appeal; and that a 2026 registration of a complaint is not the same thing as somebody examining or accepting it.

I also need the system to remember that Luchy Playa Blanca is not Matkator; that the Spanish company formerly called Monterecco Sun Park is not AWESWELL LIMITED in the UK; that an insolvency estate is not the whole hotel; that a payment is not automatically bribery; that benefit is not intent; that repetition is not corroboration.

That is why Por Derecho became a GitHub/GitLab/ChatGPT evidence-governance system. The repositories are not proof that I am right. They are an attempt to make it harder for me—or anybody else—to quietly change what the record means from one day to the next.

The most valuable thing AI can do here is not tell me I am right. It is make it progressively harder for me to keep saying something the evidence does not support.

That is the standard I want the system judged by.

Why this matters beyond my own recovery

This is a real-world test of AI under institutional and resource asymmetry.

I am both an affected person seeking recovery and, separately, someone who says he has been making public-interest / whistleblower reports. The wider record touches insolvency, alleged economic crime, tax incentives, public support, regional incentives, possible European financial interests and the later Meeting Point / FTI / German insolvency record.

That makes this more than a question about one hotel. It creates a research question that can be tested even if some of my allegations are ultimately rejected:

Can AI give one person a disciplined, auditable institutional memory when the underlying dispute is too large, too fragmented and too expensive for that person to hold together alone?

A useful system should preserve provenance, contrary evidence and corrections. It should know when it does not know. It should keep courts, prosecutors, companies, estates and people separate. It should help a human ask better questions without pretending to answer questions that only lawful institutions can decide.

That is Por Derecho's potential contribution to legal AI.

AI asks. Humans decide.

What I am asking for now

Help the capacity. Do not buy the answer.

I already pay for AI and use it intensely. What I need is more sustained capacity to keep this record coherent while the recovery and reporting work continues.

Support can be a grant, sponsored AI or compute, professional time, an introduction, or—only under a separate private agreement—recoverable support if actual recoveries occur.

No supporter gets to decide what the evidence says.
