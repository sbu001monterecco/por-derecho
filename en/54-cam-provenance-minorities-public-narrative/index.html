“54 CAM” is not the beginning of the story. It is a provenance question.

RIC Private Equity's July-2021 internal certificate is decisive because it records fragmented title. But the 54 properties attributed to CAM must be reconstructed seller by seller, deed by deed and payment by payment before they are converted into an “acquired hotel” narrative.

Why a minority unit could be legally saleable yet economically difficult

Sun Park functioned physically as a hotel while title remained divided property by property. After the 2008 transaction, LPB concentrated most of the property perimeter, other properties remained outside it, and exploitation was organised separately through owner-derived structures. A minority owner could hold genuine individual title while remaining economically dependent on access, utilities, hotel operation, licences, common elements and relationships with the Community/operator.

Provincial Court Judgment 89/2014 is important contrary evidence: it upheld the individual proprietary/possessory position of an 18-unit claimant perimeter against Monterecco/Pink's possession/operation. That confirms that private title did not disappear because of hotel operation. It also means a later sale transferred whatever the seller could lawfully transfer in that particular property; it did not turn the buyer into owner/operator of all Sun Park.

Market pressure must be investigated, not presumed

The 2015 and 2016 minutes show an environment potentially relevant to marketability: very high attendance/representation but very limited voting entitlement, attributed Community debt above €3.6m, 2010–2015 accounts not approved because complete records were unavailable, a retrospective fee reduction from €171.89 to €22.26, and recalculated balances, certificates and claims.

This does not prove that any owner was forced to sell. It does require asking, for every property later acquired by CAM, whether disputed Community debt, voting exclusion, litigation, operating uncertainty, access or services affected price, bargaining position and purchaser knowledge.

Montelanza / Molina / JSP: heightened review

The internal register separates people, companies and properties and forbids use of one undifferentiated “Molina family” or bloc without evidence. The public Acosta Matos dossier already identifies two P0 controls: JSP properties 8503–8507 from the founding/developer perimeter, and Montelanza properties 8499–8500, whose bridge between a conditional 2016 route, the 2017 Registry position and a 2018 schedule already naming CAM still requires the complete transfer instrument, authority, price/payment and Registry entry.

Every CAM acquisition from those perimeters therefore requires reconstruction of prior title, seller authority, transfer instrument, price, payment or set-off, Community debt, operating status, possession and purchaser knowledge. Heightened review is not a presumption of invalidity or fraud.

The buyer was not necessarily a stranger to the wider problem

CAM/JDAM appears at different times as creditor, owner of specific properties, Community participant, project promoter and an actor connected with the later RICPE/HNT/MYND structure. Those capacities must remain legally separate, but they justify a knowledge question: what did the purchaser know, when each property was acquired, about minority litigation, CEXP, Community debt, LPB-estate limits, access and other owners' rights?

2020 versus 2021: the contradiction controlling the public narrative

The November-2020 public webinar attributes categorical statements to José Acosta that the complexes had been acquired with own funds, belonged to the company and were unencumbered, alongside an integrated Sun Park presentation. In July 2021, RICPE's internal certificate recorded a different picture: 54 CAM, 190 LPB and 18 third-party properties, future/conditional title to the LPB block, no signed LOI and comprehensive due diligence not yet begun/completed.

Bounded conclusion: the integrated 2020 presentation was not a complete and accurate account of the title/control position that RICPE itself documented internally in 2021. That does not by itself decide intent, criminal liability or validity of an individual acquisition.

How that narrative reached the media

Separate: upstream representation → attributed publication → independent verification → knowingly false publication. A corporate representation, or an article repeating it, does not prove that the outlet independently checked it or knowingly published something false.

For La Voz/EKN, the 9 December 2022 report expressly relies on Government approval, RICPE's financing architecture and statements by Enrique Guerra. The 23 March 2023 report says the refurbishment was presented by Canarian Hospitality and later uses promoter-attributed language. That supports investigation of the propagation of an upstream project narrative, not an accusation that the outlet knowingly lied.

We do not say CAM or RICPE “caused” a particular publication without recovering the email, press release, dossier, briefing or other transmission bridge connecting source and newsroom. We ask the outlet to identify that bridge and what it independently checked.

Twelve questions that close the gap

  1. Which were the 54 properties?
  2. Who transferred each one?
  3. Which came from Montelanza/Molina/JSP or other minority perimeters?
  4. What litigation or operating right affected each property?
  5. What Community debt, set-off or dispute existed?
  6. What instrument transferred title?
  7. What was the price and how was it paid?
  8. When was each transfer registered?
  9. What did CAM know in its different capacities?
  10. What rights beyond bare unit title were later asserted?
  11. How did a 54-property position become a public narrative of an integrated hotel project?
  12. What source did each outlet receive and what did it independently verify?
