The Insolvency Administrator was not an observer. He was the statutory gatekeeper.

The credit could not become title to insolvency-estate assets merely because a private creditor wanted the property. The route required institutional acts affecting representation, litigation, information, valuation, alternatives, patrimonial protection, implementation and LPB's final accounting.

STRONGEST SUPPORTED POSITION

The office controlled indispensable LPB gates. On 25 January 2018 the Administrator ended Cristo Suárez Pimentel's formal mandate in the proceeding that had already obtained an order requiring CAM to produce the PH122→CAM deed and disclose price, payment date and costs; he also controlled future filings by the procurator. A withdrawal was then filed in LPB's name, and the proceeding ended on that filing.

The issue is no longer whether the office affected the route. It affected it directly. The issue is whether fiduciary powers were used to protect and maximise the estate or whether, as Por Derecho alleges, they operationalised CAM's procedural need and deliberately removed a route adverse to the Acosta Matos perimeter.

CENTRAL CORRECTION: “LPB withdrew” is not an established fact

Established fact: a withdrawal was filed in LPB's name; CAM did not oppose; the 5 March 2018 decree ended PP 1041 and identified Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte as LPB's lawyer.

Por Derecho's formal allegation: the withdrawal falsely attributed a non-existent intention to LPB, occurred without any located specific judicial authorisation, deliberately harmed LPB and the estate, and was executed by the Administrator as a de facto functional agent for the CAM/Acosta Matos perimeter, for its benefit and that of decision-makers or beneficiaries to be determined, including José Daniel Acosta Matos and Laura Patricia Acosta Matos.

The allegation is tested against the pleading, signature, LexNET user, metadata, instructions, communications, conflict, benefit and Article 51.2 authorisation. It is not presented as a final criminal conviction.

Direct causation and functional sequence

The 25 January 2018 act did not merely occur before the withdrawal filing. It removed from the proceeding the lawyer who had obtained the disclosure order, imposed Administrator consent over future filings and controlled the procurator. The later decree proves that a withdrawal was filed in LPB's name and that PP 1041 ended on that filing; it does not establish an authentic LPB decision or yet identify the material author of every step.

The sequence is especially serious because CAM had invoked precisely the absence of Administrator conformity as its defence. The office-holder's intervention objectively met the procedural problem identified by CAM and produced disappearance of the disclosure order.

Article 51.2 authorisation is not a detail

The then-applicable Insolvency Act required the Insolvency Administration to act with the diligence of an orderly administrator and loyal representative (Article 35) and imposed liability for estate loss caused by unlawful acts or omissions or lack of due diligence (Article 36).

Where the debtor's powers were suspended, Article 51.2 allowed the Insolvency Administration to substitute the debtor in pending proceedings, but required authorisation from the insolvency judge to withdraw, with LPB to be heard or given notice. Opening liquidation produced suspension and applied those effects (Article 145).

No application for authorisation, hearing of LPB or Commercial Court order authorising withdrawal from PP 1041 has yet been located. Failure to locate it does not itself prove non-existence; it makes the certified court file P0 evidence that the specific Court must produce.

Responsibility cannot be reduced to “the Court decided”

Independent verification

The Court depended in part on what parties and the Administrator placed before it. What did he verify about title, balance, price, valuation, withdrawal authority and alternatives?

Institutional implementation

Even where an order existed, implementation required accuracy in perimeter, debt, notice, deed, Registry and accounting.

Continuing estate duty

Judicial approval does not itself account for the €400,000 line, income, costs, debt components or remnant. Final accounts must do so.

The 15 February 2018 order is a real adverse decision and remains prominent. It does not decide who filed the withdrawal, with what authorisation, or every later question about the 2021–2022 valuation, debt, dación, surplus, pre-title control or final accounts.

De facto functional agency: strongest formulation

Por Derecho alleges that in PP 1041 the Administrator used insolvency-derived power that CAM did not itself possess to remove a procedural obstacle CAM had identified, eliminating a route capable of revealing the credit-acquisition price and preserving reduction, extinguishment or funded-exit options.

The de facto functional-agency hypothesis does not depend on a formal agency agreement. It requires proof of knowledge, intent, communications, instruction, conflict, ratification and benefit. Attribution to the perimeter and to José Daniel Acosta Matos and Laura Patricia Acosta Matos identifies decision-makers/beneficiaries to reconstruct; it does not replace that proof.

The strongest defence

The Administrator may say that he acted within statutory powers and under judicial supervision; that PP 1041 was initiated without his approval; that Article 1535 was legally unavailable and rejected; that changing counsel and withdrawal avoided costs; that the procurator had sufficient authority; that judicial authorisation existed but has not yet been incorporated; that ACTÚA was a professional valuation; that ONA and other routes were conditional; that the dación was judicially authorised; and that no surplus existed.

Those answers must be tested against the native file. The Article 51.2 authorisation, withdrawal pleading and metadata, instructions, estate-interest analysis, funding file and CAM communications decide the point.
