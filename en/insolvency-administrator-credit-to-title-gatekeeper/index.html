The Insolvency Administrator was not an observer. He was the statutory gatekeeper.

The credit could not become estate title merely because a private creditor wanted the assets. The route required institutional acts affecting LPB's representation, litigation, valuation, alternatives, patrimonial protection, implementation and final accounting.

STRONGEST SUPPORTED POSITION

The Administrator's office controlled indispensable LPB gates. At least one consequential intervention is directly documented: on 25 January 2018 the Administrator ended Cristo Suárez Pimentel's formal LPB mandate in the PH122→CAM deed-and-price proceeding. LPB then withdrew, and the proceeding ended because of that withdrawal.

The question is no longer whether the office affected the route. It did. The question is whether each act was a reasoned exercise of statutory duty for the estate or whether, cumulatively, the office operationalised the creditor-acquisition route while LPB lost information, alternatives, value and reversibility.

Direct causation already supported

The 25 January 2018 act is not merely an event occurring before the withdrawal. It directly ended Cristo's formal LPB mandate in PP 1041. The later decree proves that LPB withdrew and that the proceeding ended on that basis.

This narrows and strengthens the case. It does not yet prove who drafted or filed the withdrawal, that the Administrator personally ordered it, that the Article 1535 route would have succeeded, or that the intervention was dishonest. It does require production of the authority, instruction, advice, estate-interest analysis, alternatives and measurable consequence.

The Administrator's responsibility cannot be reduced to “the Court decided”

Independent verification

The Court depended in part on what the parties and Administrator placed before it. What did the Administrator verify about title, balance, price, valuation and alternatives?

Institutional implementation

Even where an order existed, implementation required accurate perimeter, debt, notice, deed, Registry and accounting steps.

Continuing estate duty

Judicial approval does not itself account for the €400,000 line, income, costs, debt components or remnant. Final accounts must do so.

The 15 February 2018 order is a real adverse decision and remains prominent. It does not decide every later question about the 2021–2022 valuation, debt, dación, surplus, pre-title control or final accounts.

Strongest responsibility formulation

The Administrator's responsibility is not asserted merely because CAM ultimately benefited. It requires determination because his office controlled indispensable LPB gates and because a consequential intervention in LPB's assignment-information proceeding is directly documented.

The competent court or professional body should determine whether each gatekeeping act protected and maximised the estate—or whether the cumulative effect was to operationalise CAM's acquisition while LPB lost information, alternatives, control, value and the practical ability to reverse the outcome.

The strongest defence

The Administrator may say that he acted within statutory powers and under judicial supervision; that LPB's Article 1535 theory lacked merit and was rejected; that changing counsel was justified by estate control, cost, strategy or conflict; that ACTÚA was a professional valuation; that ONA and other routes were conditional; that control disputes belonged to other owners or proceedings; that the dación was judicially authorised; and that no surplus existed.

Those answers must be tested against the native decision and accounting file. They are not established merely by the existence of later orders.
