Takedown → preservation → lawful evidence extraction

A restriction can become an additional evidential event.

Where a third party reports or complains about a Por Derecho post, account, repository or publication and a platform later restricts, removes, demotes or disables it, the event does not disappear into a customer-support process. It becomes a potential evidence-preservation and evidence-extraction event.

Por Derecho will use lawful data-access, transparency, appeal, preservation, regulatory and judicial routes to seek the provider-native chronology: what was reported, when, under which category, whether one or multiple reports were involved, whether sources were provider-linked, what automated or human review occurred, what rule was applied, and what disposition followed.

Important legal boundary. A complaint, report or takedown does not by itself prove retaliation, abuse, bad faith or criminal conduct. If provider-native and other reliable evidence establishes that platform mechanisms or other conduct were used to disadvantage a protected reporting person because of qualifying reporting or public disclosure, Por Derecho will place that evidence before the competent authority and ask it to determine the applicable consequences.

For Spain, Law 2/2023 Article 36.2 defines retaliation; Article 63.1(b) classifies intentional retaliation against protected persons as a very serious administrative infringement, and Article 65 provides the sanctions framework. Any criminal consequences would arise only under separate applicable criminal law and proof. Equivalent or related protections will be invoked only where their territorial and substantive requirements are met, including Directive (EU) 2019/1937, Germany's Hinweisgeberschutzgesetz and the UK protected-disclosure framework.

Concrete request to LinkedIn and peer platforms

A durable context and protection mechanism for repeatedly targeted public-interest accounts

Por Derecho invites LinkedIn to consider a persistent, provider-authenticated public-interest / repeated-abusive-reporting review note—or, where a public label is inappropriate, a durable internal integrity marker—for verified accounts that repeatedly enter restriction or reporting workflows while publishing documented public-interest material.

This should not exempt the account from platform rules or imply endorsement. It should link prior cases, trigger enhanced human review, explain that unusually frequent posting can reflect an ongoing evidence-publication project, prevent serial reports from being assessed in isolation, and preserve a complete audit trail before irreversible restriction.

  1. Link historic and current integrity cases.
  2. Preserve every report, trigger and disposition before irreversible action.
  3. Detect linked or coordinated reporting while protecting lawful reporter privacy.
  4. Provide usable reasons and appeal routes.
  5. Carry verified public-interest context into future moderation/recovery reviews.
  6. Maintain correction and right-of-reply paths for affected third parties.
