23 April 2025

What the removal application alleged

Aweswell sought removal of Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte under article 100 TRLC. Its opening case alleged a continuing pattern of omission, patrimonial harm and opacity and said alternatives to liquidation were not adequately explored. Its seven blocks were:

Annual accounts

Alleged continuing failure to formulate and deposit LPB accounts after suspension of the debtor's powers, relying particularly on a 20 March 2025 procedural order that located neither an exemption order nor annual-account documents.

Financial-risk contract

Alleged inaction and later obstruction concerning a 2008 financial-risk contract said to have caused more than EUR 500,000 harm and potentially to support an estate claim.

Credit-assignment price

Alleged obstruction of access to the true consideration and complete assignment terms despite their relevance to litigation, negotiation and estate strategy.

Transfer in payment

Alleged insufficiently critical acceptance or support of a harmful transaction, including valuation, perimeter and authority issues. The allegation concerns estate advice and protection; it does not recast a later judicial order as Administrator conduct.

Operating unity

Alleged inaction in the face of fragmentation of the hotel as an economic unit and access, operation and third-party-property issues.

Remuneration

Alleged accrual and receipt beyond liquidation time limits and interest on fees. This block overlaps with, but does not replace, the separate civil action.

Opacity and conflicts

Alleged information asymmetry and failures to disclose or manage relationships and conflicts capable of affecting independence, transparency and confidence in the office.

Relief sought: removal; a new appointment; accounts; Public Insolvency Register notice; referral to AEAT concerning annual accounts; and referral to the Public Prosecutor to assess any possible criminal significance. None of this means the court accepted the allegations.

Two adverse decisions · merits unresolved

What the orders actually decided

Order 1377/2025 · 12 September

Lack of active standing

The Court accepted the Administrator's objection: it treated the former Monterecco/Aweswell credit listed at EUR 0 as exclusion and relied on the absence of a challenge to the provisional creditor list. It dismissed the application expressly without needing to enter the merits grounds.

Decides: Aweswell's standing on that application.

Does not decide: accounts, the financial contract, assignment price, transfer in payment, operating unity, remuneration or conflicts.

Order · 11 November 2025

LPB and Aweswell reconsideration

It confirmed the earlier order, rejected article 427 because it was not invoked in the original 58 pages and considered merits analysis unnecessary once the procedural objection was upheld. It added that it saw no indication requiring ex-officio removal or an article 82 report. It rejected both appeals without costs and opened a non-suspensive appeal.

Contrary evidence preserved: that ex-officio assessment is materially contrary evidence, but it is not an act-by-act documentary adjudication of the seven blocks.

Text control: the first order appears to refer internally to “article 100(3)” after reproducing article 3; the second names Aweswell in the final sentence of the LPB section although the operative part dismisses both appeals. They are recorded as internal citation/name inconsistencies without inferring intent or altering the operative ruling.

1 September 2024

The remuneration recovery claim for the estate

The professional-liability action was brought against the Administrator and liability insurer. It alleged that, after liquidation opened on 19 December 2017, remuneration continued beyond the ordinary statutory period and interest was charged on common/convenio-phase remuneration. It sought return to the estate—not direct payment to Aweswell—of EUR 110,956.97, or the sum produced by the pleaded bases, plus interest and costs.

18 June 2026

Administrator complaint · daily intake reference 22

The 48-page filing was framed as a criminal complaint/notitia criminis concerning the Administrator. The controlled intake material associates physical presentation with handwritten daily intake reference 22. The official allocation document fixing the NIG, court and present state has not yet been located.

Its main provisional hypothesis is possible disloyal administration under Criminal Code article 252. The filing expressly distinguishes crime from error, bad management, negligence or insolvency disagreement and applies this test:

Entrusted power → verifiable knowledge → act/decision/failure to use an available measure → patrimonial harm → possible corresponding third-party advantage.

The episodes include Community/debt; the funded 2018 exit; access, keys, security and control; the ob rem transaction and approximately EUR 400,000; credit, liquidation, adjudication and possible surplus; fruits, rents, income, treasury, recovery and accounts. The complaint does not ask the criminal court to re-decide removal; it seeks investigation of the estate manager's own specific acts.
