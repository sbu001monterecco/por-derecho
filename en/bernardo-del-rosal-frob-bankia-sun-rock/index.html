Criminal Code

Article 130.2

Spanish law expressly addresses transformation, merger, absorption and split of a legal person and separately addresses concealed or merely apparent dissolution.

The question always comes after: was there first an applicable criminal responsibility of the legal person?

Lender in possession

The comparative concept that connects credit to own conduct, not inherited guilt.

Project Sun Rock uses “lender in possession” / “creditor in material control” as a functional comparative description. It is not presented as a standalone formal status created by Spanish insolvency law. The question is factual and legal: if a secured creditor crosses from ownership/enforcement of the credit into acts of material possession, access, exclusion, security, operation, works, exploitation or disposition, what authority existed for each act and what consequences follow from its own conduct?

Do not inherit intent

Each assignee answers for its own conduct

Criminal attribution requires actor, capacity, knowledge, required intent, act/omission, benefit and effect. NPL purchase price or later benefit does not replace those elements.

CP 31 bis

Own corporate liability

There must be an eligible offence, the legally required natural-person attribution channel, direct or indirect corporate benefit and the historically applicable control/supervision regime. It is not a universal clause.

CP 130.2

Succession only afterwards

Once a legal person's criminal responsibility is established, if applicable, the analysis asks whether transformation, merger, absorption, split or concealed/apparent dissolution activates the succession rule.

Questions for a possible consultation

What we would want Professor del Rosal to examine —without asking him to accept our theory.

  1. Which parts of his 2018 FROB/BFA/Bankia analysis does he consider most useful today for the later Bankia → CaixaBank succession?
  2. Should the Bankia → SAREB statutory transfer be treated as an asset-transfer lane separate from art. 130.2 unless accompanied by a listed structural corporate event, and what does that separation mean for civil ex delicto liability?
  3. How should art. 130.2 apply to an economic-unit segregation described as universal succession once the project, deed, balance and passive/litigation schedules are available?
  4. How should CAM's later total demerger among multiple beneficiaries be analysed and any pre-existing responsibility allocated?
  5. Where an assignee does not “inherit” criminal responsibility, how should its own later conduct —knowledge, adoption, ratification, benefit or omission— be kept analytically separate from the assignor?
  6. How should pre-23 December 2010 facts be separated from later corporate acts given the commencement date of Spanish legal-person criminal liability?
  7. What is the correct boundary here between corporate criminal succession, civil ex delicto, restitution/benefit tracing and ordinary civil/product liability?
  8. Which documents would he regard as indispensable before reaching any view?
