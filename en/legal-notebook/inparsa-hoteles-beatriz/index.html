Legal Notebook · Inparsa / Hoteles Beatriz · restructuring plan

When creditor recovery becomes corporate control.

The 18 September 2025 judgment approved a creditor-led restructuring opposed by Inparsa and its shareholders. It is a consequential Canary Islands test of acceleration, insolvency, enterprise value, funded alternatives and debt-to-equity transfer—not a proven story of judicial wrongdoing.

What the published judgment records

A revised plan succeeded where an earlier proposal had failed.

The accessible text of Judgment 61/2025 identifies the debt, expert materials, insolvency analysis, voting support and control outcome. It is the governing starting point; later press allegations do not rewrite it.

  1. 2023
    Guidebridge acquires a reported 51% majority position.

    Contemporary reporting says Guidebridge Opportunities I became Inparsa’s majority shareholder. The transaction and subsequent financing history matter to the change-of-control and acceleration dispute.

  2. 21 FEB 2024
    Restructuring expert and valuation work.

    The judgment records Lexaudit’s appointment by Mercantile Court no. 2 and a CBRE valuation of the Hotel Beatriz Playa guarantee at €38.789 million dated 11 March 2024.

  3. 16 JUL 2024
    An earlier homologation attempt is refused.

    The published record and professional commentary describe a first court refusal. The later decision concerned a further plan and a developed evidential record; the outcomes should not be treated as rulings on an identical package without comparison of both instruments.

  4. 18 SEP 2025
    Judgment 61/2025 approves and extends the plan.

    The judgment records affected debt of €63.606 million, support from 89.9% of affected debt, €10 million of new liquidity and a partial debt-to-equity conversion. It says Meru received approximately 71% of the equity and extends the plan to dissenting or non-participating creditors, shareholders and the debtor.

  5. NOV 2025
    Inparsa announces a complaint to the CGPJ.

    Press reports attribute allegations of delay, inconsistent treatment, valuation defects and denial of effective judicial protection to Inparsa. These remain the position of a party, not established findings.

  6. 13 FEB 2026
    Provisional TSJC review is reported.

    La Razón reported provisional admission or screening of a criminal complaint alleging prevaricación. The report itself said the court still had to determine whether an investigation should open. No public primary TSJC order or later definitive outcome was located by the evidence-check date.

Evidence classification

The finding, the two accounts and the unresolved bridge.

Judgment

The court held that the change-of-control clause survived the debt novations, that acceleration was valid and that Inparsa was in current insolvency. It relied on expert evidence recording matured and unpaid financial debt, constrained cash and negative equity, and rejected the oppositions to homologation.

Judgment

The decision records a Lexaudit going-concern enterprise value of approximately €12.6 million and capitalisation of a PPL claim of approximately €12.5 million. Those figures cannot be compared responsibly with gross hotel-asset values or shareholder claims without aligning date, perimeter, debt and valuation purpose.

Inparsa alleges

Inparsa’s public account says its equity and hotel assets were materially undervalued, important evidence was disregarded, and a Fortress-backed financing or full-payment route existed in 2025. It also disputes the treatment of debt maturity and insolvency. These are serious, testable allegations; they have not been established merely by publication or by filing complaints.

Blantyre/Meru says

The competing public account says the financial debt validly matured, Inparsa was insolvent, the Lexaudit valuation was valid, approximately 90% of financial creditors supported the plan, and the restructuring preserved business value and employment after the shareholder-led refinancing effort failed.

Legal issue

The hard question is not whether a creditor may enforce, vote, restructure, capitalise debt or receive equity—it may, subject to law and the instrument. The question is whether the route from debt to control remained demonstrably connected to lawful satisfaction and value preservation through transparent valuation, coherent process and reasoned treatment of funded alternatives.

Procedural control: a CGPJ complaint is not a judgment; provisional reception or screening of a criminal complaint is not the opening of a merits investigation; and neither establishes prevaricación. Any primary TSJC order or later procedural decision should supersede the present press-based status immediately.

Reporting agenda

Six records could materially clarify the case.

1 · Plan comparison

What changed after July 2024?

Publish a redline of the rejected and approved plans: classes, affected perimeter, valuation, liquidity, governance and treatment of shareholders.

2 · Acceleration

Which transaction triggered which clause?

Place the original debt instruments, novations, change-of-control clause, notices and maturity calculation on one dated chain.

3 · Valuation

Can every headline number be reconciled?

For Lexaudit, CBRE and the values advanced by Inparsa, identify subject, standard, date, debt treatment, scenarios, discount rate and sensitivity.

4 · Funded exit

Was the 2025 payment route executable?

Establish committed funds, conditions, creditor acceptances, closing mechanics, expiry and the legal reason any creditor could or did refuse it.

5 · Process

What happened on 17–18 September?

Publish the order refusing termination and the following day’s judgment with the submissions and evidence they addressed.

6 · Later status

What did the CGPJ and TSJC actually decide?

Replace press summaries with the complaint, docket, primary orders, any opening or dismissal decision and current appeal status.

No published overlap finding: the matters involve different debtors, parties, mechanisms and records. This note does not establish that the same persons, advisers, funds or conduct connect them. It identifies shared legal tests—valuation comparability, funded alternatives, route to control and effective judicial protection—that reporters can investigate independently.
