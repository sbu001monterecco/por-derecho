CORE THESIS

Two distinct legal routes, one functional operator-displacement question

Track A used private-owner possession/exploitation litigation over 18 dissident units, later extended against Monterecco, to obtain a bounded exclusionary result against the operator. Track B later used insolvency-administration litigation, possession/keys narratives, rent/termination claims and calificación to challenge and displace Pink’s operating position within the LPB/concurso perimeter, with the concursal judge later adopting or rejecting particular propositions in a separate judicial capacity. The hypothesis to test is same practical direction / different juridical mechanism: did the later institutional route arise independently, merely converge with the earlier private route, reuse its assumptions or evidence, or broaden its practical exclusionary effect? That question must be proved proposition-by-proposition; it does not itself establish coordination, common intent or criminal agreement.

Gil Marer's attributed allegation — mutual instrumentalisation across proceedings. Gil alleges that the private actors and their successors, the insolvency administrator and later judicial acts have been used functionally as instruments for one another across separate proceedings: a pleading, possession theory, Community act, AC report, testimony, order or judgment generated in one lane can become an input, legitimating premise, evidential shortcut, enforcement effect or practical lever in another. “Interchangeably” here means cross-use of propositions and effects, not that the actors or legal capacities are the same. The required proof question is always: origin → receiving actor → knowledge route → later source → adoption/use → effect → benefit/harm → contrary evidence. Functional reuse does not itself prove a common plan, direction, corrupt agreement or transferred intent.
