Protect public-interest reporting and its digital evidential record

LinkedIn, GitHub, GitLab, independent websites and document repositories can together operate as a distributed self-publishing and evidence-preservation infrastructure. Where the legal conditions for protected reporting or public disclosure are satisfied, the fact that a reporting person publishes directly rather than through a traditional newspaper does not by itself remove the protections provided by applicable law.

What we ask for: protection, preservation, transparency and lawful scrutiny — not immunity from platform rules and not acceptance of allegations without verification.

The modern reporting channel

Independent publication can perform an evidential function.

Public-interest information is now communicated through professional networks, source repositories, mirrored websites and documentary archives as well as traditional media. Por Derecho uses these channels to preserve chronology, sources, corrections and competing explanations while bringing matters to the attention of competent institutions.

The underlying work includes alleged or documented matters potentially affecting public funds, EU and national funding, regional incentives, tax incentives, corporate and asset transactions and other issues capable of engaging criminal, regulatory, administrative, fiscal or civil scrutiny. Publication is not a declaration of guilt; documented fact, attributed allegation, inference and open proof must remain distinct.

Complaint or takedown → evidential event

Suppressing a publication does not necessarily suppress its evidential significance.

When a third party complains about a Por Derecho publication, post, repository or account and a provider later restricts, removes, demotes or disables it, Por Derecho will treat that sequence as a potential evidence-preservation and evidence-extraction event.

Through lawful access, transparency, appeal, preservation, regulatory and judicial routes, we will seek to establish the provider-native chronology: the target of the report, timestamps, reason codes, number and linkage of reports where lawfully ascertainable, automated and human review, policy basis, enforcement action and disposition.

Proof boundary. A complaint or takedown is not itself proof of retaliation, reporting abuse, coordination or crime. Attribution follows evidence.

If provider-native records and other reliable evidence establish that platform mechanisms or other conduct were deliberately used to disadvantage a protected reporting person because of qualifying reporting or public disclosure, that evidence will be placed before the competent authority for legal classification and any appropriate sanctioning, regulatory, civil, judicial or criminal action available under the applicable law.

Restoration does not end the evidential exercise. Restoring an account is welcome, but it does not by itself withdraw pending requests for reasons, appeal determination, data-access/source information or preservation, and it does not erase the provider-native record of what occurred.

Legal protection — carefully bounded

Protection depends on the requirements of each regime.

In Spain, Law 2/2023 Article 36.2 defines retaliation. Article 63.1(b) treats intentional retaliation against protected persons as a very serious administrative infringement, and Article 65 provides the sanctions framework. Article 36.2 is therefore not itself a criminal-prosecution provision; criminal consequences require a separate applicable offence and proof.

Directive (EU) 2019/1937 protects qualifying public disclosures and prohibits retaliation subject to its scope and conditions. Germany's Hinweisgeberschutzgesetz §36 prohibits reprisals and includes a burden-of-proof rule in defined circumstances; §40 provides administrative-offence consequences. The UK protected-disclosure regime protects qualifying workers against specified detriment where its statutory employment and disclosure conditions are met.

Request to LinkedIn, GitHub, GitLab and peer providers

Preserve first. Explain. Review. Keep lawful evidence producible.

  1. Preserve provider-native reports, flags, moderation, security and appeal records.
  2. Keep user-initiated changes distinguishable from provider-imposed restriction, removal or demotion.
  3. Provide meaningful reasons and effective review routes where required.
  4. Apply appropriate human review to significant restrictions affecting documented public-interest publication.
  5. Detect abusive or coordinated use of reporting systems where provider-native evidence establishes it.
  6. Preserve non-disclosable source material for lawful production to competent courts, regulators or law-enforcement bodies.
To affected parties: lawful criticism, complaint, correction and reply remain legitimate. Our position is not “do not complain”. It is that the complaint and the provider response may become part of the evidential record. If the record proves an error, we correct it; if it proves legitimate platform enforcement, the reasons should be preserved; if it establishes prohibited retaliation or other actionable conduct, we ask the competent authority to apply the law.
