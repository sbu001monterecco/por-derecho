Documented process update · 29 September 2026

Three CTBG transparency proceedings: what the signed initiation communications establish — and what they do not.

Por Derecho has preserved signed initiation communications for state-scope transparency complaint proceedings 3929/2026, 3953/2026 and 3954/2026. These are procedural initiation acts, not decisions on the merits.

Press statement

The Consejo de Transparencia y Buen Gobierno has formally opened three state-scope transparency complaint proceedings relevant to the current record: 3929/2026 on 11 September 2026, and 3953/2026 and 3954/2026 on 17 September 2026. Each proceeds under Article 24 of Spain’s transparency law. The communications do not decide the merits, order disclosure, establish wrongdoing or endorse the underlying allegations. Their significance is procedural and evidential: the complaints are formally in the CTBG decision process and can now be tracked against a preserved source record.

What is verified

3929/2026

Signed initiation communication preserved. Initiation date stated by CTBG: 11 September 2026.

3953/2026

Signed initiation communication preserved. Initiation date stated by CTBG: 17 September 2026.

3954/2026

Signed initiation communication preserved. Initiation date stated by CTBG: 17 September 2026.

For all three, the preserved communications identify the state-scope complaints procedure under Article 24 LTAIBG. The 3953/3954 communications also state processing by the Subdirección General de Reclamaciones de ámbito estatal and a maximum three-month period to resolve and notify.

What is not established

No merits ruling

None of these initiation communications is a favourable or adverse final resolution. They do not decide disclosure entitlement, tax merits, retaliation, liability or wrongdoing.

3953/3954 parent filing

The signed initiation pages do not print the originating REGAGE filing or challenged AEAT expediente. Current chronological linkage is therefore an analytical bridge, not a published fact.

No automatic joinder

The existence of consecutive expediente numbers does not, by itself, prove accumulation or joinder of the proceedings.

Why the chronology matters

10 September: an earlier state transparency complaint was filed; the preserved record later identifies CTBG proceeding 3929/2026.

16 September: two separate Article 24 complaints were filed to CTBG concerning two AEAT transparency decisions, with accumulation requested in the filing architecture.

17 September: the two later complaints were routed into CTBG’s registry minutes apart; both newly preserved signed communications state 17 September as their initiation date.

29 September: the signed 3953/3954 initiation communications were received, hashed and preserved in the controlled evidence system.

This chronology strongly supports a working pair-level connection between 3953/3954 and the two 16 September AEAT complaints. The exact one-to-one mapping remains explicitly labelled as an inference until a primary CTBG or administrative source states it.

Useful insight for the public record

The significance is not that CTBG has “agreed” with either side. It has not yet decided the merits. The useful development is that three separate transparency disputes now have stable CTBG proceeding identities and preserved initiation dates. That turns a potentially diffuse correspondence trail into a trackable institutional decision chain.

For journalists, that means future reporting can test discrete questions: what each authority received, what it classified or withheld, what CTBG later decides, and whether the final reasoning addresses the specific categories of requested administrative traceability. It also makes later claims of silence, delay, access or refusal testable against a dated source record.
