MANDATE INVERSION · EXECUTION PERIMETER

One property is presently being realised. The monetary enforcement is against Matkator.

CURRENT OBJECT

Finca 8,584 only

The official auction record identifies a single lot: finca registral 8,584, historical apartment 758. Cuatrecasas then requested adjudication at 70% of auction value with reservation of the right to assign the remate to a third party.

EXECUTED DEBTOR

Matkator, S.L.

ETJ 163/2020 names Cuatrecasas as executing creditor and Matkator as executed debtor. The present remate object is therefore narrower than the enforcement-debt perimeter.

WIDER EXPOSURE

Other Matkator assets may matter

If the adjudication does not satisfy the enforceable balance, further enforcement could potentially reach other attachable Matkator assets or patrimonial rights, subject to the court order, proportionality, legal restrictions and the procedural requirements of the LEC.

The distinction is decisive: the repository must never say that several Matkator units are presently being auctioned if the controlled ETJ record identifies only finca 8,584. But it must equally avoid the opposite error of implying that the creditor's monetary claim is legally confined forever to that one finca.

What “other Matkator assets” can mean

Potentially attachable property can include money/accounts, patrimonial credits and rights, rents/fruits, shares or interests, movable assets and real estate. A litigation claim or receivable should be described as exposed only where it is a legally attachable patrimonial right and is sufficiently identified; not every procedural or personal right is executable.

Why this is a fee-coherence issue

The controlled 22 November 2018 email records that the pagarés were delivered to cover pending invoices and incurred costs. The 11 June 2019 financing email separately identified Matkator's free assets as available collateral within the rescue-financing discussion. The later use of fee-derived instruments against Matkator therefore supports a mandate-inversion question: did the machinery used to collect the mandate's fees become capable of stripping the patrimonial base the mandate was meant to finance, protect or recover?

Why this can be more than “disloyalty”

The issue is not merely interpersonal loyalty. It can bear on contractual performance, conflict, good faith, causation, damages, the allocation/cause of the fee instruments and the net amount—if any—ultimately recoverable after a full invoice-by-invoice and loss analysis.

THE ACHILLES-HEEL / GATEWAY DISTINCTION

Matkator can be a gateway into the client's economic position without becoming an automatic execution against Aweswell.

DOCUMENTED GROUP CONNECTION

Aweswell → Matkator

In July 2019, while discussing funds, Íñigo de Luisa expressly checked his understanding that Aweswell held 100% of Luchy and Matkator. That makes Matkator's asset base economically material to Aweswell's investment, rescue and recovery position.

Loss of Matkator property, receivables or hotel-related patrimonial claims can therefore reduce the value and strategic utility of the subsidiary owned by the UK client.

CORPORATE-PERSONALITY FIREWALL

No automatic parent liability or asset seizure

Aweswell is not identified as the executed debtor in the October 2024 adjudication request. A Matkator enforcement does not, merely because Aweswell owns Matkator, permit seizure of Aweswell's own separate property.

The accurate “gateway” is consequently an economic/control/equity gateway: depletion of a wholly owned subsidiary can penetrate the value of the client's investment and constrain the parent's hotel-recovery options. Any direct route against Aweswell itself would require an independent legal basis.

Repository formulation: “The current remate is asset-specific; the enforcement debt is debtor-wide; the debtor is Matkator; and Matkator is a documented wholly owned subsidiary within Aweswell's group perimeter. The legal Achilles heel is therefore not automatic veil-piercing, but the ability of a fee enforcement against the subsidiary to deplete assets and patrimonial rights that form part of the UK client's wider hotel-recovery value chain.”
