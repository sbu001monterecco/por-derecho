CAM ROUTE

Commercial Court No. 1 · Insolvency 36/2012

CAM sought recognition as the new holder of the credit. The insolvency administration reported favourably. Insolvency administration: effect, control and estate-interest test

C5 · DIRECT CAUSATION OF FORMAL-MANDATE END / DOCUMENTED CAM-FAVOURABLE EFFECT

In this sequence the insolvency administration performed two materially connected roles: it supported CAM's creditor recognition and, after liquidation opened, exercised direct control over who could act procedurally for LPB. On 25 January it terminated Cristo's formal LPB mandate; by 5 March the proceeding records Borja as LPB's lawyer and documents withdrawal filed in LPB's name.

The question that now requires a document-led answer: what contemporaneous estate-interest analysis justified abandoning the already-admitted deed-and-price route, and who took and implemented that decision?

The sequence is sufficient to investigate causation, loyalty, conflict and estate benefit. It is not, by itself, proof of collusion, criminality, agreement with CAM or an intention to harm LPB.

Commercial Court: adverse litigious-credit ruling and deference to the insolvency administration

The order with an 8 February 2018 body date was not uniformly favourable to CAM: it corrected part of the amounts in LPB's favour. It nevertheless rejected LPB's litigious-credit theory and recognised CAM, relying for creditor-substitution verification on the insolvency administration's favourable report.

Controlled reading: the contrast with Court of First Instance No. 2 supports an evidentiary and institutional asymmetry question: LPB obtained a disclosure order to learn the assignment economics, while CAM's recognition rested in part on verification attributed to the insolvency administration. The two courts were addressing different applications, so the contrast is not automatically proof of legal incompatibility or improper bias.

What the withdrawal does not prove

Article 1535 does not make every disputed credit a litigious credit. The order with an 8 February 2018 body date reached an LPB-adverse conclusion on that question, which is real counterevidence to any suggestion of a guaranteed winning retracto.

But substantive merits weakness and information value are different questions. The deed, price, payment and costs could remain relevant to debt reconciliation, negotiation, economic reconstruction, assignment analysis and any appeal decision. The current record still lacks the contemporaneous analysis showing why withdrawal was better for the estate.
