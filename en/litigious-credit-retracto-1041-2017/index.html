LPB obtained a court route to learn the assignment price. CAM obtained insolvency recognition of the credit. The question is what LPB obtained when the former route stopped producing value.

This record separates three issues that should not be collapsed: whether the credit technically satisfied Article 1535 of the Civil Code; whether LPB had a right to obtain the assignment economics in order to test that position; and whether the loss or abandonment of that route produced any objectively measurable benefit for the insolvency estate.

CONTROLLED CONCLUSION

The current reconstruction has not located a measurable LPB benefit arising from loss/termination of the 1041/2017 route. It does locate a concrete CAM-favourable effect: recognition as assignee creditor with express reliance on the insolvency administration's favourable report. That supports the description functional CAM-favourable delivery in effect; it does not, by itself, establish collusion, improper purpose or improper judicial bias.

The estate question: if LPB lost an already-admitted court route to obtain the assignment deed, price, payment and costs and preserve possible extinguishment or negotiating optionality, what did the insolvency estate receive in return?

The asymmetry that requires explanation

The two courts were deciding legally different applications, so it would be wrong to say they necessarily had to apply the same documentary standard. The contrast is nevertheless material.

For LPB to test its rights

Court of First Instance No. 2 ordered production of the deed and transaction economics. The information itself was the object of an admitted judicial measure.

For CAM to be recognised in the insolvency

Commercial Court No. 1 said that all documentation supporting CAM's title was not required: the favourable insolvency-administration report allowed the court to presume that the necessary verification had been performed.

Controlled reading: this supports an evidentiary and intervention asymmetry question. It does not by itself prove that the two rulings were legally incompatible or that an unlawful agreement existed.

Insolvency administration: the strongest formulation currently supported

FUNCTIONAL_DELIVERY_CAM_FAVOURABLE_EFFECT

The direction of effects can be measured without speculating about motive. The insolvency administration supported the substitution; that report was an express basis of the judicial decision recognising CAM; the court presumed that the administration had verified the requirements; CAM retained its recognised credit position; and LPB's parallel route to the transaction economics has not, in the present reconstruction, produced a recovered benefit, debt reduction or other measurable estate advantage.

Formulation: the insolvency administration's intervention produced a functional or directional delivery in favour of the Acosta Matos perimeter in this sequence. The phrase describes the testable institutional outcome. It is not used as a synonym for collusion, unlawful agreement or intent.

Question for the insolvency administration: what contemporaneous estate-interest analysis explains why supporting CAM's continued/recognised creditor position while LPB's deed-and-price route lost value was the better outcome for LPB and its creditors?

Commercial Court: active intervention + passive deference

The 15 February order signed by Alberto López Villarrubia was not uniformly favourable to CAM: it corrected part of the amounts in LPB's favour. That fact must remain visible.

The same order nevertheless has two features relevant to institutional analysis:

Active · CAM-favourable effect

It recognised CAM as assignee creditor and rejected LPB's Article 1535 / litigious-credit theory.

Passive/deferential · CAM-favourable effect

It declined to require all title documentation sought by LPB for creditor substitution and relied on the presumption that the insolvency administration had completed the necessary verification.

The strongest responsible public description is “an intervention-and-deference pattern with CAM-favourable effect” or “intervention asymmetry”. A finding of improper personal judicial bias requires additional evidence about the applicable standard, knowledge, duty to intervene, comparators and alternative explanations.

Did “killing” the retracto route help LPB?

On the evidence currently located, no explanation showing how has been found. To establish that ending it benefited LPB, the record would need something such as:

Economic benefit

Debt reduction or waiver, settlement consideration, quantified savings, or a limitation on enforcement exposure.

Procedural benefit

A written analysis showing that expected cost and risk clearly exceeded the expected value of the information, legal option and negotiating leverage.

The adverse Commercial Court ruling is a serious reason to question the prospects of a substantive Article 1535 action. But “the merits may have been weak” and “the information no longer had value” are not the same proposition. The deed, price and costs could still matter to credit reconciliation, negotiation and reconstruction of the economic chain.

Counter-record: why the thesis must not be overstated

Article 1535 does not make every unpaid or disputed credit a litigious credit. Supreme Court case law applies a strict concept, requiring a qualifying declaratory controversy over existence/enforceability, and recognises important limits in some bulk transfers. The 15 February order adopted an LPB-adverse view. Por Derecho therefore does not state that LPB had a guaranteed winning Article 1535 claim.

It is also legitimate in principle for an insolvency administration not to fund a claim whose expected cost exceeds expected estate value. What is missing from the present record is the documentary bridge between that possible justification and the actual outcome: what was decided, by whom, on what analysis, and what the estate gained.
