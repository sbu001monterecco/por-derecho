The PH122 → Construcciones Acosta Matos assignment requires four questions to be kept separate: what credit was assigned, for what amount, at what price and with what opportunity to challenge it.

This record does not declare that the credit was technically “litigious” for Article 1535 purposes, or that the assignment was invalid. It publishes the documentary dispute that existed before and after the assignment and identifies the material still needed to resolve it.

CONTROLLING ADVERSE DECISION

The 15 February 2018 order expressly rejected LPB’s litigious-credit argument, recognised Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. as the assignee and also corrected part of the amounts in LPB's favour. The order further reasoned that complete title documentation was not required for the creditor change because the insolvency administration had reported favourably and was presumed to have verified the requirements. That mixed and adverse ruling is central to this record.

Article 1535: the technical legal question remains controlled

Spain’s Civil Code provides in Article 1535 for the consequences of a sale of a litigious credit and states that a credit is considered litigious from the point at which the claim relating to it has been answered.

Later Supreme Court case law applies a strict concept of litigious credit. An economic dispute or mortgage-enforcement proceedings therefore do not by themselves establish that every technical Article 1535 requirement was met on 20 October 2017.

LPB’s position

In December 2017 it maintained that the credits were litigious and requested assignment information in order to preserve its rights.

Commercial Court ruling

On 15 February 2018 it rejected that classification and recognised CAM, with the amount corrections stated in the order.

Current status: a documented dispute exists, together with a judicial ruling adverse to LPB’s theory. The technical Article 1535 analysis remains open to the complete pre-assignment procedural record and the appeal/finality chain.

The estate-interest question that now requires an answer

The new point is not that LPB had a certain winning claim. It is that LPB had obtained a judicial route to the deed and transaction economics while, in parallel, CAM obtained insolvency recognition with express reliance on the insolvency administration's favourable report.

Question: if PP 1041/2017 ended or lost its utility before producing that value, what measurable benefit did LPB or the estate obtain in return?

The current reconstruction has not yet located a debt reduction, settlement consideration, enforcement-risk limitation or contemporaneous cost-benefit analysis that answers that question.
