The PH122 → Construcciones Acosta Matos assignment requires four questions to be kept separate: what credit was assigned, for what amount, at what price and with what opportunity to challenge it.

This record does not declare that the credit was technically “litigious” for Article 1535 purposes, or that the assignment was invalid. It publishes the documentary dispute that existed before and after the assignment and identifies the material still needed to resolve it.

CONTROLLING ADVERSE DECISION

The 8 February 2018 order expressly rejected LPB’s litigious-credit argument, recognised Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. as the new creditor and, at the same time, accepted that the assignment could not alter the amount already recorded in the final insolvency texts. That adverse ruling is central to this record.

It does not by itself prove unlawful conduct by CAM, final non-compliance with the measure, or that the assignment price necessarily produced any particular legal outcome.

Article 1535: the technical legal question remains open

Spain’s Civil Code provides in Article 1535 for the consequences of a sale of a litigious credit and states that a credit is considered litigious from the point at which the claim relating to it has been answered.

For that reason, an economic dispute or the existence of mortgage-enforcement proceedings does not by itself justify publishing that every technical requirement of Article 1535 was indisputably met on 20 October 2017. For each credit, the relevant pre-assignment claim, opposition or judicial incident and the responsive pleading or act that precisely joined issue on existence or amount must be identified.

LPB’s position

In December 2017 it maintained that the credits were litigious and requested assignment information in order to preserve its rights.

Commercial Court ruling

In February 2018 it rejected that classification because the credits were recorded in the final insolvency texts and, in its reasoning, mortgage enforcement alone did not make them litigious.

Current status: a documented dispute exists, together with a judicial ruling adverse to LPB’s theory. The technical Article 1535 analysis remains open to the complete pre-assignment procedural record and any later decision confirming, modifying or revisiting that reasoning.

The amount issue did not disappear with the assignment

The 8 February 2018 order contains a particularly testable proposition: assignment changes the creditor, not the amount already recognised. The court therefore accepted LPB’s amount objection to that extent and effected the creditor substitution on the figures already contained in the final insolvency texts.

That point must be read alongside the quantified 2016 dispute and the later economic reconciliation. An accounting discrepancy is not by itself proof of deliberate inflation or fraud. It does require reconstruction from original records of principal, interest, partial releases, payments, pledge, second mortgage, costs, assignments, dación and any residual balance.

Controlled conclusion

Fact: before creditor substitution there was a quantified dispute notified to the insolvency administration; after learning of the assignment LPB formally requested the deed and price; another court allowed a preliminary measure to obtain that information; and the Commercial Court later ruled against the litigious-credit argument while limiting the substitution to a change of holder without changing the recognised amounts.

Open question: what the complete deed, price, loan-level allocation and pre-assignment procedural record show about the rights LPB could exercise and the correct reconciliation of the credit.

Evidence-led publication. It is not a judicial determination and does not declare fraud, unlawful concealment, favouritism or criminal responsibility. All parties are invited to provide the complete deed, later rulings, explanations and exculpatory or corrective material.
