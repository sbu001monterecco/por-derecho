Credit chain · financial package · alleged overreach

Lender of Record.

This page separates the mortgage credit from the disputed financial package, then tracks how the lender position moved from the original bank perimeter to Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. and how that position was allegedly used beyond the limited LPB concurso estate.

LPB concurso*: limited estate, repeated overreach

A limited LPB concurso was repeatedly used, or allowed to be used, as if it were authority over the whole hotel, the whole ownership perimeter, the CEXP/exploitation layer, Matkator/third-party units, works, finance and later hotel operation.

On this site, "LPB concurso" means Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. and the estate in Concurso 36/2012 only. It does not mean that the whole hotel, all Sun Park owners, the CEXP/exploitation layer, Matkator units, third-party units, works, finance or later hotel operation were placed under that proceeding.

Public wording rule: avoid phrases such as "the insolvent hotel", "Sun Park insolvency", "the hotel was in insolvency" or "the administrator controlled Sun Park" unless the statement is qualified by estate, perimeter, date and evidence.

01

Assignable mortgage credit

The credit chain is treated as a record sequence: Caja General de Ahorros de Canarias (La Caja de Canarias) / Bankia, S.A.; Sociedad de Gestión de Activos Procedentes de la Reestructuración Bancaria, S.A. (SAREB); Promontoria Holding 122 B.V.; and Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. (CAMSA).

The exact originator and every assignment step must be pinned to the signed deeds, notices and court substitutions.

02

Disputed financial package

The disputed package is a separate liability and causation lane: first mortgage, floor clause, swap or cobertura, pledged deposit, 2010 second mortgage/refinance, account closures, default notices, execution filing and auction pressure. The live Valencia claim against CaixaBank, S.A. sits in this lane.

The working thesis is that the package caused or materially contributed to the alleged default, the execution threat and the defensive LPB concurso.

Timeline

From lender to claimant, and from claimant to alleged control narrative

2008 - original Sun Park finance

Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. acquired the majority Sun Park ownership position with finance recorded internally as a La Caja de Canarias loan. The original deed must confirm the exact corporate lender and the later succession route into Bankia, S.A.

2008 to 2011 - financial-package dispute matures

The pleaded position is that the loan relationship included a floor clause and a swap/cobertura sold as protective finance while operating economically as an additional cost exposure. This is the layer that must be separated from a simple mortgage-credit balance.

2010 - second mortgage and pledged deposit

A second mortgage/refinancing facility of approximately EUR 850,000 was put in place. The internal record says it was used to regularise swap arrears and retain a pledged deposit or balance, linking the second mortgage to the disputed package rather than to an independent hotel trading need.

January to June 2012 - execution pressure and defensive concurso

Bankia, S.A. pursued enforcement of the second mortgage after the relevant grace period and promoted an auction over LPB assets. The concurso filing was a defensive step taken to stop the execution and protect the asset and business while the financial-package dispute and restructuring options were unresolved.

2013 - SAREB credit acquisition

The case record says Bankia transferred the mortgage credits to Sociedad de Gestión de Activos Procedentes de la Reestructuración Bancaria, S.A. The working position is that the mortgage credit moved, while the swap/cobertura liability and mis-selling claim remained a separate Bankia/CaixaBank responsibility lane unless the assignment documents prove otherwise.

December 2014 - Project Meridian and Promontoria Holding 122 B.V.

Public reporting states that Cerberus Capital Management, L.P. won SAREB's Project Meridian, a EUR 133 million hotel-sector loan portfolio secured by 26 hotels in Spain. The Sun Park record identifies Promontoria Holding 122 B.V. as the acquiring vehicle for the relevant credit. The asset schedule, price and debtor notices must be produced and reconciled with the court record.

Public source: CoStar Finance report on Project Meridian.

October 2017 to 2018 - CAMSA enters as creditor, not owner of the hotel

The internal record says Promontoria Holding 122 B.V. sold the mortgage credits to Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. in October 2017. A creditor position is not the same as ownership of 100% of Sun Park, authority over the community of owners, or authority over CEXP/hotel exploitation.

2018 onward - alleged instrumentalisation

The LPB concurso, mortgage credit and auction posture were allegedly used by later actors as if they supplied wider authority over the hotel, its commercial exploitation, works, third-party ownership, investment narrative and later operation. That alleged overreach is separate from, but causally connected to, the Bankia/CaixaBank financial-package lane.

2022 onward - title, Hotel New Trend, S.L. and operation

The later formal title and segregation route involving Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. and Hotel New Trend, S.L. must be read against the earlier creditor, auction, works and operational record. It cannot be used to erase prior questions about authority, estate limits, funding, possession, income or damage.

Working conclusion

The lender-of-record chain matters because it shows two stories travelling together: an assignable mortgage credit and a disputed financial package. The first may explain who claimed the credit at each point in time. The second may explain why the defensive concurso was requested, why LPB's solvency and hotel viability were misread, and why later actors should not use a limited LPB proceeding as a shortcut to wider hotel control.
