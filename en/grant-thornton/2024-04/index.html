The institutional question

Is it sufficient to conclude that a person was not an employee or partner when Grant Thornton's own response acknowledged commercial services to a Grant Thornton partner company in Spain located in the Canary Islands?

The question does not presume a breach. It asks whether employment or partnership status exhausted the relevant analysis, or whether the acknowledged commercial relationship required identification of the entity, supervision, controls, conflicts, third-party risk and relevant reputational perimeter.

Two statements that should be read together

Formal position communicated

Not employee · not partner

Grant Thornton communicated that Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte was not an employee or partner of a Grant Thornton entity and, on that basis, that there had been no employment-type onboarding process in this case.

↔

Relationship acknowledged by GT Spain

Commercial services to a partner company

In its communication of 8 January 2025, Grant Thornton Spain stated that he only provided services on a commercial basis to a Grant Thornton partner company in Spain located in the Canary Islands.

What this comparison does not prove: it does not by itself establish breach, conflict or Grant Thornton liability. What it does: it defines a perimeter question that a traceable decision should be able to explain.

Questions for reconsideration

Open question

1. Which partner company?

Identify the Grant Thornton partner company in Spain located in the Canary Islands to which, according to the firm's own communication, commercial services were provided.

Open question

2. What commercial relationship?

Clarify the contract, scope, duration, invoicing, functions, access, representation and limits of that engagement.

Open question

3. What supervision and knowledge?

Who authorised, knew of or supervised the relationship, and what controls belonged to the partner company?

Open question

4. What independence or third-party controls?

What independence, conflict, reputation, quality or third-party/provider risk assessment applied, if any?

Open question

5. Why did “no onboarding” close the perimeter?

Explain why the absence of employment or partnership status resolved the compliance question despite the commercial relationship expressly acknowledged.

Open question

6. Was the later material examined before closure?

Clarify how later judicial, regulatory and professional developments were evaluated before concluding on 10 December 2025 that no materially different new matters had been identified.

Proportionate public request: Grant Thornton may maintain its decision if it considers it correct. If so, it is invited to explain the perimeter and expressly reconcile the acknowledged commercial relationship with the closure reasoning. Alternatively, it may reopen or independently reconsider 2024_04.
