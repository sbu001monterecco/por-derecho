The document that stopped elEconomista's proposed publication was not an “order” declaring “Sun Park” culpable.

It was Judgment 163/2023 of 28 September 2023, issued in the insolvency-classification section of Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. (LPB) Concurso 36/2012. The judgment was materially adverse and must be treated as such. But its legal nature, subject, procedural posture and object are materially narrower than the description supplied to the journalist in January 2025.

Five corrections that change the reading of the episode

1 · JUDGMENT, NOT ORDER

The label matters

The attachment received by Javier Romera was named AUTO CONCURSO CULPOSO.pdf. The judicial document inside is, however, Judgment 163/2023. The ruling itself identifies its date, court, proceeding and appeal route.

2 · LPB, NOT “SUN PARK”

The debtor is not the whole hotel

The operative part classifies the insolvency of Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L. as culpable. Sun Park is a mixed-ownership hotel complex; LPB was one owner and the insolvency debtor. The judgment does not turn the hotel, every owner or every property unit into the insolvency debtor.

3 · APPEALS ALREADY FILED

The procedural picture was incomplete

The preserved record shows appeals filed in October/November 2023 and appellate personation steps before January 2025. The judgment itself also states that Gil Marer's disqualification runs from the finality of the ruling.

4 · LIMITED OBJECT

It did not decide whole-hotel title

The insolvency classification does not adjudicate ownership of every Sun Park property, extinguish the rights of other owners, or determine Matkator's extraconcursal title. Nor is it a judgment deciding who was entitled to commercialise the hotel as a whole.

5 · IT DID NOT ANSWER THE PITCH

Adverse, but non-responsive to the proposed story

The material supplied to the newspaper raised questions about commercialisation, Meeting Point/FTI, CAM, RIC/RICPE, public and European incentives/funding and control of the asset. Judgment 163/2023 does not decide those questions.

We do not minimise the culpable classification. It is a materially adverse first-instance judgment. That is exactly why it must be described precisely: who the debtor is, what the operative part decides, what consequences it imposes, what appeals existed and what subjects remain outside its scope.

The documentary sequence, now more complete

2020 · commercial evidence independent of the later press exchange. A preserved Meeting Point Hotels corporate brochure presents the company as founded by FTI Group, includes Club Sei among its brands and describes Club Sei Lanzarote as a 315-room hotel in Playa Blanca. A 27 January 2020 sonnenklar.TV capture offers Club Sei Lanzarote at Calle Janubio 3; a historic Sun Park website capture identifies Sun Park, Calle Janubio 3. The address match is direct evidence of public hotel presentation/distribution of the same location within the FTI/Meeting Point ecosystem, while leaving the underlying contract, title/authority and booking/payment trail open.
3 January 2025 · Lars Creutzmann / Meeting Point. A formal request for confirmation, document disclosure and preservation was sent concerning Sun Park / Club Sei / MYND Yaiza. It expressly asked whether Meeting Point or group entities had marketed, distributed, sold, promoted, controlled inventory for, or contracted in relation to the hotel. The Gmail search plus the refreshed Drive/File Library scan located the notice and supporting copies but no inbound Lars reply during January 2025; that does not exclude a response through another channel.
16 January 2025 · press note to Javier Romera. The material proposed a public-interest investigation into the commercialisation and control of Sun Park and the CAM–Meeting Point/FTI–RIC/RICPE architecture, together with public/European financing and incentive questions. The press note is party-authored: it establishes what the newsroom was asked to investigate, not the truth of every allegation.
17 January 2025 · two incompatible accounts. A contemporaneous email sent to Romera records that Meeting Point was saying it had never commercialised the hotel, while Laura/CAM was saying Meeting Point had commercialised it at some stage. The attribution still requires authentication against each interlocutor's native message/audio. But the Meeting Point Hotels corporate brochure and the sonnenklar.TV offer at Calle Janubio 3 now provide independent corroboration that Club Sei Lanzarote was publicly presented and distributed within that ecosystem.
17 January 2025 · editorial standard. Romera denied that there was any attempt at censorship and explained that before publication he needed the relevant facts properly tied down. That caution is legitimate and provides the standard against which the later stopping document should also be tested.
19 January 2025 · Meeting Point 357/2024. Registry material was sent to Romera concerning creditor-negotiation proceeding 357/2024 before Commercial Court No. 3 of Las Palmas, involving Meeting Point Hotelmanagement (Canaries), Meeting Point Spain and Meeting Point Investment. The 24 October 2024 order extended the effects of the creditor-negotiation communication. Its relevance here is preservation, traceability and potential custody of records; it does not itself prove Sun Park commercialisation.
20 January 2025 · publication stops. Romera wrote, in substance, that “we have been sent this order” declaring the Sun Park insolvency culpable and Gil disqualified, and that “with this” they could not publish. The email proves receipt, the description used and the communicated editorial decision. It does not identify the sender.

Meeting Point: the contradiction no longer rests only on two competing accounts

The record now contains direct commercial evidence predating the elEconomista exchange. Meeting Point Hotels' February 2020 brochure presents Club Sei as one of its portfolio brands and describes Club Sei Lanzarote as a 315-room hotel in Playa Blanca. The 27 January 2020 sonnenklar.TV capture offers the property at Calle Janubio 3, exactly the address that the historic Sun Park website capture gives for Sun Park.

That does not by itself establish which Spanish company signed the contract, which property units were included, who authorised inventory, whether bookings were completed, or who received the money. But it does make a categorical version that Meeting Point/FTI “never commercialised” the property difficult to sustain if commercialisation includes public presentation and distribution as a hotel product. The position attributed to Laura/CAM —that Meeting Point had commercialised it at some stage— is materially corroborated in that limited sense without treating the attributed statement itself as an authenticated admission.

The 3 January 2025 request to Lars Creutzmann had already sought exactly the documents needed to move from public marketing evidence to the legal and economic architecture: contracts, inventory, bookings, distribution, commissions, invoices, payments, instructions and communications.

Convergence point: public commercialisation of Club Sei Lanzarote at the same address as Sun Park is documented. What remains open is who authorised what, over which units and under what title, and how that activity was treated in Concurso 36/2012, the rights of extraconcursal owners, and later preservation/disclosure within the Meeting Point perimeter and proceeding 357/2024.

What the judgment did not answer

Commercialisation

It does not determine the lawfulness, scope or authority of the 2020 public offering/distribution of Club Sei Lanzarote shown in commercial sources.

Mixed ownership

It does not declare CAM or LPB owner of 100% of the complex, nor determine every non-debtor owner's rights.

RIC / RICPE

It does not decide the investment, governance, information, approval or funding architecture associated with RIC/RICPE, which is examined separately.

Public/EU incentives

It does not decide eligibility, award, payment, use, control or recovery of public or European incentives/funding associated with the hotel project.

Extraconcursal control

It does not automatically validate use, works, exploitation or commercialisation over assets or rights that were not part of LPB's insolvency estate.

Who sent the document?

Romera's email says only that “we have been sent” the document. We do not publish the identity of the physical sender as a verified fact. The contemporaneous sequence places the judgment's arrival within a verification round in which responses attributed to the CAM/Acosta Matos side and to Meeting Point were being received. The strongest present working inference is therefore that the judgment entered the exchange from or on behalf of the CAM/Acosta Matos response side; but the evidence does not yet establish which individual pressed “send”, from which account, or under whose specific instruction.

There is presently insufficient documentary basis to name the insolvency administrator or any other specific person as the sender. That attribution requires the native inbound message, headers, metadata or equivalent testimony/documentation.

EDITORIAL ACCOUNTABILITY, NOT AN ALLEGATION OF COLLUSION

The difficult question for elEconomista

This reconstruction contains no evidence that Javier Romera knew the description supplied to him was legally overbroad, or that he acted in bad faith. It is entirely possible that the newsroom was given an incomplete or misleading picture of the judgment's scope.

But there is an objective question made unavoidable by Romera's own verification standard: if the proposed story had to be properly tied down before publication, was the same scrutiny applied to the document that caused publication to stop?

  • Was it checked that the document was a judgment, not an order?
  • Was it checked that the insolvency debtor was LPB, not “Sun Park” as a whole?
  • Was it checked that appeals had already been filed?
  • Was it checked that the judgment itself tied the disqualification period to finality?
  • Was the categorical “never commercialised” account checked against the Meeting Point Hotels corporate brochure and the Club Sei Lanzarote offer at the same address as Sun Park?
  • Was it examined whether the judgment actually answered the Meeting Point, commercialisation, RIC/RICPE, funding/incentive and extraconcursal-ownership questions in the proposed story?

If those checks were performed, we ask that their documentary basis be identified. If they were not, the chronology warrants factual correction and a renewed editorial review of the story that was abandoned.

Ten finite documentary questions

  1. Who sent elEconomista the file named AUTO CONCURSO CULPOSO.pdf, from what account and with what accompanying text?
  2. Was the sender acting personally, for CAM/Acosta Matos, for a lawyer/adviser, for the insolvency administrator, or for another person/entity?
  3. Was the newspaper told that Judgment 163/2023 was final? Was it told about the appeals already filed?
  4. Was it explained that the operative part concerned LPB's insolvency and not the entirety of Sun Park or every owner?
  5. What documentation did Romera receive from Laura Acosta Matos/CAM, and what did he receive from Meeting Point?
  6. Can the 17 January contradiction about whether Meeting Point commercialised the hotel be authenticated against the original interlocutor records?
  7. How do Meeting Point/FTI explain the 2020 corporate brochure, the description of Club Sei Lanzarote as a 315-room hotel and the sonnenklar.TV offer at Calle Janubio 3; what company, contract, inventory, bookings, commissions, invoices and payments sat behind those materials?
  8. What check was made of the relationship —or absence of relationship— between the classification judgment and the RIC/RICPE, funding/incentive and extraconcursal-ownership questions in the pitch?
  9. Are the original inbound transmission, headers, attachments, voice notes and call records needed to reconstruct the sequence preserved?
  10. Does elEconomista wish to correct, complete or reopen its investigation in light of these documentary clarifications?

This page alleges no judicial impropriety from that chronology; it calls only for allocation, signature and document-custody traceability where relevant.
