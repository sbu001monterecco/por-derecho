The story elEconomista was investigating in January 2025 was not “a dispute about a hotel”

It was a public-interest story concerning hotel commercialisation, Meeting Point/FTI, RIC investment, public finance, support measures, European funds, and the risk that a disputed patrimonial position could continue producing economic and institutional consequences. Sun Park was the asset where those questions could be tested; it was not the whole story.

What the 16 January 2025 pitch asked the newsroom to investigate

The press release sent to elEconomista was titled “Multimillion-Euro Fraud in the Commercialisation of Hotels in the Canary Islands during Covid”. It was a party-authored journalistic pitch and some of its language was more categorical than our present evidential standard permits. We do not use it as proof of its own allegations. It does establish what story was being put to the newspaper.

  • commercialisation of a hotel asset during insolvency proceedings;
  • possible involvement of Meeting Point and FTI Touristik;
  • an investment architecture linked to RIC;
  • public financing and support associated with the relevant business perimeter;
  • European funds and regional incentives;
  • use of the asset, its refurbishment and operation as an economic basis for later transactions;
  • and the risk that investors, regulators and public authorities could be making decisions on patrimonial premises requiring independent verification.

Scope correction: describing the proposed publication simply as “a Sun Park article” materially understates it. Sun Park was the evidential node of a broader public-interest story about public money, investment, commercialisation and institutional traceability.

What has changed since then

RIC / RICPE

The current repository documents the investment structure, governance, related persons and corporate links around the project with much greater precision. That does not retroactively turn every January 2025 assertion into fact, but it shows that several questions then raised were capable of documentary investigation.

FEDER and incentives

The existence of a FEDER component is no longer merely a formulation from the old press release: the controlled record identifies FEDER expressly on the project's permanent plaque. Exact amount, certifications, eligible expenditure and controls remain open.

Financial continuity

Later research has documented the RICPE→AGM→Orion continuity and the financing architecture described in issue materials. The narrower open question is what identifiable Sun Park/MYND/LPB/Matkator value did—or did not—enter those channels.

Title and authority

The record now distinguishes formal title, material possession, operational control, units, dates and legal limits more carefully. That distinction is central to evaluating what could properly be represented to investors, public bodies and operators at each point.

The temporal point is the important one: the story elEconomista was verifying did not disappear after 20 January 2025; its underlying questions became more documentable.

What changed between 17 and 20 January 2025?

17 JANUARY 2025

Investigation and verification remained open

Javier Romera, then editor-in-chief, expressly rejected our contemporaneous characterisation of “censorship”:

“There is no attempt at censorship and of course we would not allow it…”

He explained that before publication they needed everything properly tied down and all documents. He then added:

“I have only been asked to wait until Monday for them to send us some court orders.”

That is a legitimate editorial position: verify before publishing.

→

20 JANUARY 2025

Order received and non-publication communicated

Romera forwarded a file titled AUTO CONCURSO CULPOSO.pdf and wrote that they had been sent an order concerning the culpable insolvency classification and Gil's disqualification. He then wrote, in substance:

“I am sorry, but with this we cannot publish it.”

The preserved email establishes receipt of the document and the decision communicated. It does not by itself identify the original sender.

The point that should not disappear: the Meeting Point responses were materially relevant

Meeting Point/FTI commercialisation was one of the central propositions being investigated. Our contemporaneous record of what Romera relayed after verification calls records versions that are difficult to reconcile.

According to that party record, the Laura/CAM side was said to have acknowledged that Meeting Point had commercialised the hotel, albeit “like any other tour operator” and no longer did so. The same record says that, in calls to Meeting Point, the relationship was denied or minimised and Club Sei Lanzarote was described as a commercialisation project that was not ultimately carried out or was later passed to another operator.

Evidential classification: this point comes from our contemporaneous email recording what we understood Romera had told us after his calls. It is not yet an authenticated written statement by Laura Patricia Acosta Matos or Meeting Point. The three identified WhatsApp audio files still require native preservation, transcription and authentication.

Its journalistic relevance remains clear: if one account attributed to CAM acknowledged commercialisation while another attributed to Meeting Point denied, minimised or reframed it, that contradiction was part of the story requiring verification, not a side issue.

An insolvency-classification order did not automatically answer the public-interest story

The press pitch was not simply asking who was right inside Insolvency 36/2012. It asked who commercialised the asset, from when and with what authority; what role Meeting Point and FTI played; what investments and funding were represented; what RIC, incentives, support and public funds could converge; what was said about ownership or availability of the asset; and what risks existed for investors and public resources.

An insolvency-classification ruling, even one materially adverse to Gil Marer, does not by itself answer all of those questions. The relevant question is therefore not merely whether the ruling existed, but how it was transmitted, characterised and used in the verification process.

elEconomista may itself have been harmed

We do not automatically place the newspaper on the wrong side of this episode. If a newsroom was investigating a public-interest matter, had carried out verification, had received potentially inconsistent responses and then abandoned publication because a court ruling was presented as decisive, there is a hypothesis that should be capable of testing:

that the newspaper's legitimate verification process may itself have been used to induce abandonment of the story through an incomplete, decontextualised or materially misleading representation of the document.

That is not established today. If established, however, the newspaper would not be the beneficiary: it could have lost a potential exclusive, an opportunity for earlier scrutiny, and the chance to report sooner on risks that later continued to materialise.

We likewise cannot know what public harm would have been avoided by publication in January 2025. We can legitimately ask whether earlier scrutiny might have enabled investors, regulators, public administrations, creditors or other journalists to identify certain risks sooner.

Suppression by unlawful means?

In January 2025 we used words such as “censorship”. Today we narrow that formulation. What is documented is that publication stopped after time was allowed for judicial material to be supplied and after elEconomista received the order.

If it were established that this result was obtained by knowingly using a non-final ruling, presenting it outside its true scope, materially mischaracterising it, transmitting it unlawfully, or using it to create a false appearance regarding matters it did not decide, then the issue would go beyond ordinary right of reply. There would be a separate question about the means used to neutralise a journalistic investigation.

Any civil, criminal, insolvency, professional or other legal characterisation would depend on facts and sources still to be completed. This page does not convert that hypothesis into a judicial finding.

The March 2025 querella

A later Madrid querella attributed the transmission to Laura Patricia Acosta Matos and alleged that the ruling was non-final and supplied in circumstances intended to prevent publication. It also sought Javier Romera's evidence and referred to documentary exhibits.

The querella is a party pleading, not a judgment. Until the original transmission or an equivalent primary record is obtained and authenticated, we do not present the sender's identity or an unlawful purpose as established fact.

Por Derecho: the rule is traceability

When a document produces an important consequence, it is not enough to ask whether it exists. We should be able to reconstruct who created it, who transmitted it, how it was characterised, what it actually proved, what it did not prove, what the recipient checked and what decision followed.

That applies to courts, prosecutors, public administrations, regulators, investment vehicles and listed companies. It also applies when a company supplies material to a journalist investigating it.

17 JANUARY 2025: investigation open, verification under way, Meeting Point versions in issue, judicial material awaited.

20 JANUARY 2025: “we have been sent this order…” → “with this we cannot publish it”.
