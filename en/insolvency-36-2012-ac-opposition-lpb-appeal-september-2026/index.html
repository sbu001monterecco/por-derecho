Checkpoint 1

Non-finality acknowledged; adverse first-instance judgment used as a narrative premise

The AC says conclusion of the insolvency depends solely on the appeal against the 28 September 2023 judgment and, in the same sequence, reproduces the €3,032,010.34 award plus interest when explaining the supposed background to the removal application.
Documented fact

The appeal remains pending

The filing itself acknowledges that the 2023 classification judgment is appealed and that closing the insolvency depends on the appeal. The first-instance outcome is therefore not the final procedural result.

Inference to test

Narrative asymmetry

The non-final judgment is nevertheless used as an adverse premise when interpreting motive and credibility. That is not inherently unlawful, but it requires a strict distinction between an existing first-instance judgment and a final outcome.

Checkpoint 2

The 2011 origin may pre-date the AC. The 2016 knowledge does not.

The AC responds to the February and June 2011 Community records by saying he could not have participated in conflicts that pre-dated his June 2012 appointment. That temporal limitation should be preserved. But his filing then reproduces Gil Marer’s 29 April 2016 email to the AC.

According to the text the AC chose to reproduce, the email directly notified him that: LPB disputed the Community debts as unreal; Community accounting documentation was said to be absent; legal and administrator costs were said to be the material costs serviced since 2011; and audited operating-company material was to be reconciled against the disputed quotas/debt.
AC position

The words of thanks

The AC relies on Gil’s thanks for his authorisation/assistance as evidence that he cooperated with the debtor.

Evidence incorporated

The same email fixes substantive notice

Its evidential value does not stop at the thanks. It fixes receipt, by 29 April 2016, of a concrete controversy about debt, arrears, accounting and reconciliation of common expenditure, linked to LPB’s hoped-for composition.

MOTIVE / COUNSEL

The proposed common explanation for why different lawyers ceased acting needs lawyer-by-lawyer proof.

The filing moves from one documented 2020 withdrawal to a general explanation for successive professionals using expressly inferential wording. The correct test is individual: date, contemporaneous reason, scope of withdrawal and what each professional actually said could or could not be maintained.

ANNUAL ACCOUNTS

The AC acknowledges the duty and attributes impossibility to missing documentation.

The test does not end with that attribution: what was requested, what was actually received, what remained missing, what further production measures were taken and what accounting, tax and quarterly-report material was later used. A specific missing item is not automatically proof of global non-cooperation.

ARTICLE 1535

Failure of one legal route does not itself reveal the economic assignment price.
