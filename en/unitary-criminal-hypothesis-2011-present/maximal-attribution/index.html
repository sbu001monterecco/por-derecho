25 August 2026 · attributed position of Gil Marer

Maximal attribution of criminal causation.

Gil Marer alleges that the 2011 reconfiguration of the Sun Park Owners' Community organ was the origin of a mechanism through which identified private actors obtained and maintained practical control over debt, voting, documentation, litigation and, later, assets and decisions connected with Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. inside and outside Concurso 36/2012.

Boundary: this page sets out Gil's maximal attributed theory. It does not declare a crime, conspiracy, de facto administration, court deception or knowing criminal conduct by the insolvency administrator to be established.

The allegation in its fullest form.

Gil alleges that the private actors involved in the 2011 takeover or reconfiguration of the Community organ later continued to exercise or accumulate practical capacities that, in his view, were functionally comparable to de facto or shadow administration of LPB-related assets, rights and decisions. He alleges this occurred without formal appointment as administrators of the insolvent estate and without sufficiently independent defence of the estate against that perimeter.

Gil uses the expression “estafa concursal” as an umbrella description of the alleged sequence. The repository does not treat that phrase as an autonomous offence or an established legal conclusion. It decomposes the theory into possible fraud, procedural fraud, disloyal administration, insolvency offences, documentary falsity or other offence families only where their specific elements can be proved.

Position concerning the insolvency administrator. Gil alleges that Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte materially enabled continuation of the private-actor perimeter through acts and omissions, silence, inaction and failures to investigate or defend. Gil simultaneously keeps alternative knowledge states open: the administrator may initially have been deceived; later failure to inquire may, in Gil's characterisation, have become reckless; or future evidence may establish actual knowledge or deliberate facilitation.

Possible deception of the court: a separate proof track.

Gil alleges that acts or omissions by private actors and/or the insolvency administrator may have allowed the court to receive or retain a materially incomplete or misleading picture. That proposition can only be upgraded by identifying the exact representation or omission, who produced it, what contrary information existed, what the judge actually received, what decision depended upon it and what each actor knew.

No inference is made that the judge was deceived, knew of falsity or participated in unlawful conduct merely because an adverse judicial decision was issued.
