Right of defence · Reform and governance

Specialisation without fragmentation.

Towards genuinely effective legal representation: advice, litigation and independent review must work for the rights-holder, not the service provider’s organisation chart.

Editorial analysis · 10 November 202610 November 2026Explore the analysis ↗
In one idea

We need not choose between lawyers who advise and lawyers who litigate. We need specialists who share case knowledge, an identified accountable lead and safeguards that protect the client.

01 · Our thesis

Specialise functions, integrate knowledge, demand accountability.

Effective representation does not require every lawyer to do everything. Nor does it arise from dividing a case between departments that barely communicate. It requires technical competence, practical dispute experience, documentary continuity, professional independence and a strategy leader answerable to the person whose rights are at stake.

PRINCIPLE 01

Genuine specialisation

Those drafting a contract, bringing a claim or examining a witness must understand the particular risks of their work.

PRINCIPLE 02

Whole-case understanding

Every specialist needs to understand the client's objective, evidence, alternatives and impact on other proceedings.

PRINCIPLE 03

Independent challenge

A consequential decision should be capable of review by someone who did not propose it and is not invested in defending it.

The useful separation is of responsibilities; the dangerous separation is of knowledge.

Proposed Por Derecho editorial principle · not a rule of existing law

The measure of success is not a law firm's organisation chart. It is whether a person can understand their choices, maintain their defence when counsel changes and receive diligent, verifiable representation.

02 · Why the debate matters now

What actually changed in Madrid — and what did not.

On 5 November 2026 the Community of Madrid announced that its Assembly had approved legislation reorganising its public Abogacía General. It announced a Legal Advice Directorate, a Litigation Directorate and a Technical Secretariat. The announcement concerns the regional government's legal service, not a general reclassification of lawyers in private practice.[1]

On 9 November Rodrigo Corchero asked in Economist & Jurist whether functional differentiation could foreshadow a specialist qualification for appearing in court. That is a prospective question in the article, not an established legal consequence of Madrid's reform.[2]

VerifiedOrganisational differentiation

The official statement identifies separate legal-advice and litigation functions within Madrid's regional legal office.

InterpretationA wider specialisation debate

Specialised teams may sharpen capability, but structure alone does not establish improved quality, lower costs or greater independence.

Not establishedExclusive private advocacy

These sources do not establish a general requirement for private lawyers to divide into advisers and courtroom advocates. The reform's commencement date requires confirmation from its official publication.

An anonymised comment supplied privately for this discussion adds an essential challenge: preventive legal advice improves when informed by the real consequences of disputes and hearings. We recognise that insight, while noting that trial experience alone does not replace specialist corporate, insolvency, tax or regulatory expertise.

03 · Legal foundations

Start with the rights-holder, not the professional category.

Article 24 of the Spanish Constitution protects effective judicial protection and the right of defence. Organic Law 5/2024 on the Right of Defence provides a particularly relevant framework for judging legal-service quality from the recipient's perspective.[3][4]

Advice and advocacy form a continuum

Article 3 encompasses pre-proceedings legal advice and defence through the legally available procedures. Article 8 links advice, courtroom defence, quality, accessibility and continuing specialised education.

The client must understand and be able to change counsel

Articles 5 and 6 address choosing and replacing counsel, transferring information needed for continuity of representation and receiving comprehensible information about the dispute.

Independence, loyalty and conflict prevention

Articles 13, 14, 19 and 20 place independence, good faith, professional ethics and the avoidance of conflicts of interest among the profession's guarantees and duties.

Costs, case updates and institutional protection

Spain's General Statute of the Legal Profession addresses information about fees, risks and case progress; Organic Law 5/2024 recognises professional-body protection and disciplinary functions subject to applicable procedures.[5]

An essential distinction: Spanish civil procedure already distinguishes, subject to statutory exceptions, representation by a procurador from technical legal defence by an abogado. This does not create two separate adviser-versus-litigator professions among Spanish lawyers.[6]

04 · Comparative perspectives

Compare safeguards, not merely professional labels.

Comparative analysis only helps when we distinguish professional classification, rights of audience, practical experience and the quality of assistance. They are not the same thing.

SystemHow it worksPotential lessonImportant limit
Spain: private practiceLawyers may advise and act in proceedings, subject to the field of law and relevant procedural rules.Allows direct continuity between prevention and litigation.A broad professional role does not itself prove specialist competence.
Madrid: public legal officeSeparate advice and litigation directorates have been announced inside the regional legal service.Can clarify team organisation and specialist responsibility.It is not a compulsory model for the entire legal market.
England and WalesSolicitors and barristers are distinct professional categories, but solicitors can qualify for higher rights of audience.Advanced advocacy skills can be separately assessed.The split is not absolute and does not automatically establish superior results.[7]

The comparative lesson is not to impose an exclusive new litigation profession without evidence. It is to specify demonstrable competences by function, protect client choice, preserve legal aid and measure the consequences of change. Spain’s legal-aid framework is set out in Law 1/1996.[8]

05 · Proposed architecture

A client-centred model with three capabilities that can challenge one another.

For materially or procedurally complex matters, Por Derecho proposes a functional structure. It does not require retaining three law firms: one organisation can cover multiple functions if competence is shown, conflicts are managed and independent review is used when risk warrants it.

The rights-holderSets objectives · chooses counsel · receives information · authorises decisions
↓ ↑
Identified lead legal professionalCoordinates strategy, instructions, deadlines, risks, budget and accountability
Specialist adviceContracts · substantive law · regulation · prevention
Specialist litigationProcedure · evidence · hearings · appeals · enforcement
Independent reviewChallenge · conflicts · contrary hypotheses · quality
All three functions operate from a documented record of facts, sources, decisions and deadlines, with access controls that preserve professional confidentiality.

The safeguard: independent review should be proportionate to risk, transparently funded and free to disagree with the principal team. It should never become a marketing certificate lacking genuine scrutiny.

06 · Por Derecho standard · policy proposal

Ten practical safeguards for an effective defence.

These are proposals for good governance, not ten newly enacted legal obligations. Some draw on existing rights and duties; others would require professional adoption, piloting or reform.

One accountable lead, explicit roles

Record who is responsible, the precise scope of each engagement and who decides what.

Early litigation stress-testing

Ask how any consequential piece of advice could be defended, challenged or enforced.

One timeline, multiple hypotheses

Separate proven facts, source documents, competing accounts and unresolved questions.

Verifiable procedural calendar

Allocate deadlines and use second checks when missed action could extinguish rights.

Meaningful informed decisions

Explain alternatives, prospects, cost, uncertainty and implications before material choices.

Transparent fee arrangements

Record engagement terms, scope changes, updated budgets and third-party fees when relevant.

Continuous conflict screening

Review independence, competing interests and material relationships at intake and as facts change.

No gap when counsel changes

Use a compliant handover of the file and pending issues when one professional replaces another.

Documented internal challenge

Test the strongest opposing case before a decisive filing, appeal, settlement or withdrawal.

Outcome review without spin

Measure diligence, clarity, procedural security and cost without equating victories with professional quality.

08 · Implementation agenda

Reform through measurable pilots, not premature monopolies.

Stage 1 · Immediate

Voluntary good practice

  • Engagement terms and responsibility map.
  • Deadlines list and handover protocol.
  • Conflict checks and cost transparency.
Stage 2 · Pilot

Experiment and listen

  • Mixed teams on selected matters.
  • Feedback from clients, the profession and legal aid.
  • Independent assessment of accessibility and cost.
Stage 3 · If evidence supports it

Consider tailored regulation

  • Demonstrable advanced litigation competence.
  • Recognition without disproportionate barriers.
  • Protection of access, competition and client choice.

Evaluation should track, using comparable and protected data, procedural incidents, quality of client information, transitions between counsel, time and total cost, upheld complaints and the experience of vulnerable users. A raw litigation win rate, divorced from case mix, is not an adequate standalone measure.

09 · Open debate

Four objections that must stay visible.

Risk 01 · Cost

“More specialisation means more invoices.”

It can. Our model requires proportionality, an integrated budget and evidence that additional involvement adds value.

Risk 02 · Access

“An exclusive category would close the market.”

A serious concern, especially in underserved areas and legal aid. Restrictions require public justification and impact assessment.

Risk 03 · Experience

“An adviser without courtroom experience misses reality.”

The criticism has force when practice knowledge is absent. The answer is integration, continuing education, case review and competence appropriate to the function.

Risk 04 · Independence

“One coordinator can accumulate too much power.”

The client therefore retains control; files must be transferable, and the lead professional cannot monopolise evaluation of their own decisions.

10 · Frequently asked questions

What this approach does — and does not — mean.

Should Spain simply copy the barristers' model?

No. Importing labels without assessing existing rights of audience, professional arrangements and access to justice would be a mistake. A more proportionate first step is to improve competence, coordination and independent review.

Must clients retain multiple lawyers?

No. A qualified lawyer may fulfil several functions. External review becomes more useful when decisions are irreversible, conflicts may exist or extraordinary personal or financial risks arise.

Does Madrid's reform require private firms to separate advisers and litigators?

No such general requirement is established by the official material reviewed. The announcement concerns the Madrid regional government's own legal office. Commencement of the new law should be checked against official publication.

Does losing a case show that a lawyer was negligent?

No. Courts resolve uncertain disputes. Professional quality calls for assessment of conduct, capability, duties, communication and case-specific facts; legal liability requires proof of the relevant legal elements.

Can a client's case file be published to showcase practice standards?

Not without an appropriate legal basis and specific review. Transparency must respect confidentiality, legal professional privilege or secrecy, third-party rights, personal data and procedural rules.

11 · Editorial position

The most important reform is not professional. It is institutional and civic.

The challenge is to prevent a person's defence from being lost between changes of law firm, disconnected specialties, incomplete information, opaque fees or decisions for which no one takes responsibility. A more specialised structure may help. A more fragmented defence may do the opposite.

Specialise tasks. Integrate judgment. Make review independent. Keep accountability with the rights-holder.

Por Derecho · a proposal for debate, testing and improvement

Por Derecho calls for public discussion involving clients, professional bodies, legal-aid advocates, universities, courts, dispute lawyers and preventive advisers. The priority is to test what genuinely improves access to high-quality representation — not to protect a professional label.
