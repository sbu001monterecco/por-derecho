Case
Prism

One operating park. Several legal perimeters.

Twelve units function as one logistics platform. Eight belong to an insolvent company and four to a non-insolvent third party. An offer for the whole promises more value and continuity. Before approving it, the decision-maker must establish what may be sold, who may consent, and what would happen if the premise were wrong.

Exercise rule

Do not search for “the right answer”. Observe which question reaches each professional, which sources are available at that point, what competence is being exercised, and which consequences are preserved.

Everything is fictional. Prism Park, its companies, documents, figures, communications and decisions neither correspond to nor anonymise any real matter.

The proposal

A reasonable transaction under real pressure

The first view should explain why a diligent person may genuinely wish to act quickly.

Discipline: do not confuse commercial urgency with legal authority; equally, do not turn a legal question into an automatic instruction to stop.

Prism Park · fictional record

Initial position

  • Prism Operations Ltd is in liquidation and owns Units 1–8.
  • Nexus Property Ltd, which is not insolvent, owns Units 9–12.
  • One operating agreement coordinates access, security, energy, customers and brand.
  • The secured creditor’s mortgage covers Units 1–8.
  • A buyer offers €18.4 million for the complete operating park.
  • The separate-sale estimate is €12.1 million and may destroy contracts and employment.
Proposed action: approve a sale of the whole as one operating unit, with a rapid closing and continuity for 86 jobs.

The record

Twelve documents, three knowledge points

The event chronology is not the knowledge chronology. A decision should be assessed using what was available at that time.

No hindsight rewrite: evidence received at T2 may change future action, but must not be presented as if it had been available at T0.

The role

The same record does not confer the same competence

Select a viewpoint. The second review must adapt to what that person may decide, supervise or refer.

No super-authority: a cross-record tool may preserve context, but does not turn prosecution into an appellate court or professional oversight into a judicial remedy.

Information · proposal · diligence

Insolvency practitioner

Must maximise value within the legal estate perimeter, identify third-party rights and place a sufficiently reconciled proposal before the court.

May doInvestigate, inventory, negotiate, report, propose conditions and seek approval.
Must not assumeThat operational unity automatically converts third-party assets into estate assets.
Core questionWhat document allows the proposal to reach U9–U12 and the separate rights?

Contradiction · reasons · protection

Judicial decision-maker

Must decide within jurisdiction, identify the precise object and consider whether a later remedy could be effective after a difficult-to-reverse transfer.

May doApprove, refuse, narrow, condition, request clarification or preserve rights under the applicable law.
Does not automatically receiveEvery commercial conversation, unfiled document or external knowledge held by others.
Core questionIs the bridge between the insolvency and each third-party asset or right identified?

Separate criminal competence

Prosecutorial review, where applicable

Only enters the simulation if a separate communication alleges knowingly false representations or another potentially criminal issue. It does not review the civil correctness of the sale as an appeal.

May examineReported facts, intent, documents used, contradictions, competence and lawful alternative explanations.
Must not convertA perimeter dispute, without more, into proof of crime or guilt.
Core questionWhat was represented, who knew what, what effect was sought and what lawful explanation fits?

Conduct · organisation · learning

Professional or institutional review

May consider diligence, conflict, custody, response, file organisation and protocols without replacing judicial remedies or attributing responsibility from the result alone.

May produceAn identifiable response, recommendation, correction, training or referral to the competent route.
Must not confuseAn adverse decision with an automatically established professional breach.
Core questionWho had the specific duty to verify title and what did that person record?

Outcomes and record

The methodology does not have one ending

Explore three outcomes. None automatically characterises a person’s conduct; they show how the position changes when evidence or structure changes.

Principle: the visible outcome must not rewrite what was known earlier. The report preserves when each document was received.

Outcome A

Authority is reconciled

At T2 an earlier notarial mandate is produced. Nexus authorised a combined sale subject to minimum price, allocation, guarantees and final confirmation.

New status

Authority question reconciled. The hypothesis of complete absence of consent is no longer supported by the completed record.

  • The sale must still satisfy the mandate conditions.
  • Later evidence is not pretended to have been available at T0.
  • The tool records why the alert was cleared.

Outcome B

The transaction is redesigned

The available instrument does not cover all shared rights. Instead of losing the offer, the insolvency approval and the third party’s private agreement are separated.

New status

Perimeter narrowed and closing conditioned. U1–U8 follow the insolvency route; U9–U12 and common rights require a separate agreement.

  • Part of the unified value is preserved.
  • The buyer knows what is acquired through each route.
  • The third party controls its consent and price.

Outcome C

The sale proceeds without an identified bridge

The twelve-unit sale proceeds using operational unity and urgency as the principal justification, without adding the mandate or narrowing the object.

Risks that become embedded

The simulation does not declare the sale unlawful. It shows that the unresolved question becomes harder to repair after closing.

  • Possible third-party challenge and estate contingency.
  • Buyer uncertainty over title, access and warranties.
  • Cost of later separating customers, energy, possession and investment.
  • Need to explain who knew of the warning and why it was overridden.

The false positive is cleared

Schedule D12 establishes that the 1,700 m² difference consists of roads and common loading areas included in the operating valuation but not in private registered floor area. The numerical contradiction changes to “terminology and measurement reconciled”. Not every discrepancy is a title problem.
