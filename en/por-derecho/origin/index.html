Declared origin · designed independence

Public origin statement

Born from a case.
Independent of its outcome.

Por Derecho arose from a prolonged personal experience of complex records, fragmented decisions and consequences that were difficult to reverse. That experience explains the initial questions. It cannot decide institutional answers, replace evidence or turn a founder’s conviction into a conclusion of the method.

The decisive test

The method must be capable of weakening, correcting or closing a founder’s concern when the source, competence, professional explanation or contrary evidence requires it.

If it cannot do that, it is not a second review: it is advocacy.

Four layers that must not be confused

From experience to a verifiable question

The personal origin is published so that no one has to infer it and every inferential step can be challenged.

O-01

Lived experience

Explains why an issue appeared important and what harm was feared.

It does not itself prove a disputed fact, intention, breach or responsibility.
O-02

Research hypothesis

Frames a question that may be confirmed, qualified, displaced or rejected.

It must include the strongest lawful explanation and the evidence capable of changing the view.
O-03

Documentary record

Separates native source, date, authorship, version, knowledge available and inference.

“Filed”, “received”, “opened”, “reviewed”, “verified” and “sufficient” are different states.
O-04

Public-interest method

Turns the question into reusable checks of source, authority, perimeter, contradiction, consequence and reversibility.

The rule must work in the same way whether it assists, harms or does not affect the founder’s position.

Founder-related matters

Experimental application, not independent validation

Transparency requires the first real uses to be described with their correct status.

ICALPA DIP 80/2026 and DIP 79/2026

These are experimental research applications related to the founder. They test whether published rules can organise a real record without concealing uncertainty, defences, competence or the next human decision.

They are not external validation, institutional adoption, ICALPA endorsement or a disciplinary conclusion.

What they may demonstrate

They may demonstrate traceability, correction capacity, separation between matters, time-sensitive treatment of evidence and visibility of human gates. They cannot by themselves demonstrate general neutrality or institutional effectiveness.

The next legitimate test is an independent red-team exercise on synthetic cases under pre-declared criteria.

Independence tests

Independence is not declared once. It is exercised in every decision.

These rules move from the public narrative into the governance draft and the external-review protocol.

1

Prior rule

The control is fixed before anyone knows which result it will favour.

2

Real contradiction

The strongest contrary explanation is published, not a weakened version.

3

Recusal

The founder does not decide the conclusion of a personal or closely related matter.

4

Visible correction

A new source may change the state and leaves a version, date and reason trail.

Public layer

  • method and limits;
  • correct status of each application;
  • declared conflicts and recusal rules;
  • corrections and changed conclusions;
  • review and adoption criteria.

Protected layer

  • personal data and confidential material;
  • legal strategy and privileged advice;
  • protected-source identities;
  • acquisition negotiations and diligence;
  • information an institution has not authorised for publication.
