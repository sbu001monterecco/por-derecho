Oversight of oversight

How a legal question travels—or is lost—across distinct institutional routes.

The Second Review does not treat every institution as one hierarchy. It separates judicial remedies, prosecutorial involvement and professional controls, then tests whether the material question and its sources survived the transfer.

It does not review judicial merits. It examines traceability, context, competence, information received, identifiable response and blind spots.

Synthetic guiding question: which assets truly belonged to the insolvency estate at the relevant date, and which document permitted third-party assets or rights to be affected?
1

Insolvency and judicial route

Educational model. Concrete competence and remedies depend on the applicable law and procedure.

Insolvency practitioner

What was receivedInventory, title documents, contracts, submissions and operating data.
What was decided or reportedA proposed asset perimeter and patrimonial action were defined.
What remainedReconcile the 40% attributed to a non-insolvent third party.

Competent court

What was receivedThe practitioner’s report, parties’ positions and incorporated documents.
What was decidedThe request was resolved within the court’s competence.
What remainedIdentify the individual legal bridge between the insolvency and the third-party asset.

Competent judicial remedy

What must surviveThe real ground, contrary sources and alleged irreversible effect.
What it may doAffirm, reverse, vary or remit under the applicable route.
Narrowing riskThe issue arrives as a valuation disagreement rather than a perimeter question.
2

Prosecutorial route, where applicable

The prosecution service is not an appellate court. Its role depends on legal competence and the facts communicated.

Receipt and classification

What was receivedA communication, annexes and a hypothesis of criminal or legality relevance.
What must be distinguishedCivil conflict, procedural irregularity, criminal indicator and matter outside competence.
What remainedWhether the episodes should be examined separately or as a connected chain.

Identifiable prosecutorial decision

What should be recordedSources examined, competence, lawful alternative explanation and action taken.
What may occurClosure, inquiries, territorial or specialist referral, or procedural action.
Loss riskA referral preserves the submission but not the documentary context needed to understand it.

Internal coordination or review

What it testsCompetence, integrity of referral and existence of contradictory decisions.
What it does not doIt does not replace judicial remedies or automatically determine responsibility.
Persistent questionWho assumed responsibility for reconciling the economic and asset perimeter.
3

Professional and institutional route

Professional, disciplinary, insurance and administrative controls have distinct objects and competences.

Engagement oversight

What was receivedA concern about diligence, conflict, custody, information or professional conduct.
What must be separatedJudicial merits, professional duty, file organisation and possible liability.
What remainedIdentify who had the concrete duty to verify third-party title.

Disciplinary or ethical control

What it may examineConduct, conflicts, truthfulness, independence and professional duties.
What it cannot presumeAn adverse decision does not itself prove a breach.
Atomisation riskEach document is treated separately without reconstructing cumulative effect.

Institutional learning

What it producesBetter education, escalation protocols and pre-decision controls.
What it preservesWho knew what, when, what they did and which explanation they gave.
Required resultAn identifiable response, including that the concern is not supported.

What the Second Review adds

Memory between institutions

It does not create competence where none exists. It makes visible the exact point at which a question was answered, referred, narrowed or left without an owner.

Preserves context

Keeps the guiding question, sources and lawful alternative explanation attached to each transfer.

Identifies missing bridges

Shows where a conclusion depends on authority, a document or reconciliation that has not been identified.

Separates allegation from evidence

Distinguishes what is asserted, documented, inferred, disputed and officially decided.

Shows who knew what and when

Orders receipt, decision, referral, alleged omission and later response without presuming culpability.

Constitutional and institutional boundary

Testing traceability is not replacing the decision-maker

Judicial independence, prosecutorial functional autonomy and professional responsibility require each actor to decide within competence. The tool creates no “super-authority”; it creates an auditable memory of the question, sources and response.

Express boundaries

  • The prosecution service is not presented as an appellate body.
  • Professional oversight does not replace a judicial remedy.
  • A remedy does not automatically decide disciplinary or criminal responsibility.
  • An alert is not a breach.
  • The absence of a visible response does not itself prove misconduct.
