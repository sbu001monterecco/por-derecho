REASON TO BELIEVE

ONE ASSET. ONE HISTORY.

By Gil Marer

Before Sun Park became a case file, it was a place people came to live, return, make friends and begin again. This is the story of Sun Park, the SunRockers community, the same physical place now known as MYND Yaiza — and the long effort to understand what happened in between.

Before the dispute · there was a community

Sun Park was meant to be lived, not merely occupied.

Historical project records in the connected archive identify Sun Park Living and the SunRockers as a real operating and community concept in Playa Blanca. The model was aimed particularly at grown-ups and longer stays: people with time to participate, make friends, contribute skills and help shape the rhythm of daily life rather than consume a conventional package holiday.

The surviving material points to a lived programme of creative activities, music, walking, gardening, small markets, reading, social events and other resident-led or participative activity. Dedicated 2012–2018 spreadsheets, a 2017 SunRocker Community document, archived brochures, forum/TripAdvisor captures and later presentations preserve that history.

Stay longer

A hospitality proposition in which time mattered: enough time to know the place and one another.

Take part

Guests and residents were not conceived only as an audience. Participation and contribution were part of the proposition.

Belong

The strongest memory is not a room count. It is community: friendships, shared activity and the possibility of a richer later chapter in life.

Sun Park → MYND Yaiza

The hotel changed. The place did not move.

The present-day resort marketed as MYND Yaiza occupies the physical location at the centre of this history. The book uses that visible continuity as its narrative spine: the Sun Park people remember, the years of commercial and legal disruption, the documentary reconstruction, and the transformed hotel visible today.

It therefore asks something larger than who owns a hotel. What happens to the value created by a place — its guests, ideas, relationships, operating history, future plans and sense of belonging — when its history becomes fragmented across companies, creditors, proceedings and institutions?

The book will distinguish documented continuity from disputed legal and economic conclusions. Similarities between the original SunRocker experience and later experiential hotel programming do not, by themselves, prove transfer of intellectual property or unlawful appropriation.

The ethos

Life can become larger again.

The philosophy behind Sun Rock was explicitly discussed in 2023 alongside Adam J. Jackson's The Ethos: Because life is better together. Jackson's published book is centred on friendship, community, purpose, later-life renewal and the proposition that reaching a certain age need not mean the adventure is over.

That is an influence and a philosophical reference point — not an endorsement of this book or of the legal case by Adam J. Jackson. It helps explain the human idea that preceded the litigation: hospitality can create the conditions for people to reconnect, contribute, pursue new interests and find a community.

The SunRocker proposition in one line: a good later chapter should not be smaller simply because it comes later.

The story and the record

Two reading experiences. One source discipline.

The book carries the people, atmosphere, mistakes, hope, conflict and persistence. The Knowledge Project carries the documentary reconstruction. Material factual propositions are intended to remain traceable to identified sources; adverse evidence and unresolved questions belong there too.

The evidence is online. The book tells what it was like to live through it.

Why it may matter beyond Playa Blanca

A story about property — and about what property cannot capture.

The book is for readers of investigative nonfiction, business and institutions, but also for people interested in community, ageing, hospitality, reinvention and belonging. It is intended for Sun Park guests and SunRockers, people who worked around the resort, Canary Islands readers, lawyers and public administrators, students and academics, journalists and documentary makers, and AI/legal-tech communities asking whether technology can help preserve institutional memory.

Publishing · media · memories

Help preserve the human history as well as the documents.

Paperback, premium hardcover, e-book and audiobook are under consideration, together with Spanish and English editions, translation and documentary/audio possibilities. No publication date or preorder is announced yet.

Former guests, SunRockers, staff, neighbours, collaborators and people with photographs, programmes, newsletters or first-hand memories can help complete the social history. Contributions should be capable of attribution and consent before publication; private memories are not automatically made public.
