REASON TO BELIEVE

ONE ASSET. ONE HISTORY.

By Gil Marer

One Canary Islands hotel. Years of courts, creditors, companies, public authorities and competing accounts. One attempt to put the continuous history back together — while the ending is still being written.

Discover the bookExamine the project

The proposition

Many versions were created. There was only one history.

Reason to Believe follows the human, business and institutional journey behind the reconstruction of a complex legal-economic history. The repository documents the record. The book explains what it was like to live through it: building, losing control, trying to understand years of fragmented process, confronting adverse decisions, testing assumptions and asking whether effective rights can still become effective in practice.

The book is being developed before the final outcome is known. It does not ask readers to accept a predetermined ending.

Why now

Written while the test is still live.

This is not a retrospective victory story. The central question remains open: can a sufficiently complete, source-linked reconstruction change what institutions, professionals and the public are able to see? The emergence of modern AI adds another question: can it help justice remember context without becoming a judge, witness or source?

The evidence is online. The book tells what it was like to live through it.

Evidence, not mythology

Don't believe us. Test us.

Material factual propositions are intended to remain connected to the Knowledge Project's evidential record. AI-generated text is not evidence. Allegations remain allegations; adverse evidence and unresolved questions belong in the record too.

For readers beyond one case

A Canary Islands story with a wider question.

The project is intended for readers of investigative nonfiction, business and institutions; lawyers and public administrators; students and academics; journalists and documentary makers; people affected by complex property, insolvency or NPL disputes; and AI/legal-tech communities interested in access to justice and legal memory.

The same master visual identity is planned for English and Spanish editions: a coastal asset, documentary layers and one continuous line toward the horizon — the history that should not be lost when institutions see only fragments.
