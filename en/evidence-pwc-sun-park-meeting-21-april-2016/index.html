Contemporaneous and hindsight reading

Gil Marer’s position, preserved as a legal allegation: the refusal of PwC, the warning to Carlos/Miguel/Elena and the debt-to-vote linkage formed part of conduct he considers civilly, insolvency-law and criminally relevant.

The current documents justify a formal criminal-law examination, but the email alone does not establish an offence. In 2016 an Insolvency Administrator could control new estate claims and require justification of need and fees. Article 15.2 of the Horizontal Property Act also removed voting rights for overdue debts unless judicially challenged or deposited. The real question is whether those powers were exercised on true premises, within authority and without deliberate patrimonial harm.

With hindsight, the email becomes more significant if the full evidence proves a consciously false debt; falsified or concealed records; manipulation of evidence in the insolvency case; deliberate excess in administering another’s property causing harm; recognition of fictitious claims; or coordinated use of vote exclusion to produce a patrimonial result. Each bridge still needs a document, actor, knowledge, act, causation and loss.

Insolvency separation and perimeter

The former Insolvency Act separated the debtor’s estate from the right to recover third-party property held by the debtor. The later removal applications and third-party-property allegations therefore provide relevant context, but do not automatically convert this 2016 email into a crime. The test remains what the IA knew, verified, reported and caused, asset by asset.
