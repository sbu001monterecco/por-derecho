Insolvency Administrator: from functional neutrality to the loyalty test

FUNCTIONAL_DELIVERY_CAM_FAVOURABLE_EFFECT

The strongest formulation supported today does not require an allegation of secret intent. It measures outcome direction: the Administrator's report supported CAM recognition; the Court expressly relied on that verification; and at later points the Administrator remained the continuous institutional actor with powers over information, standing, liquidation, accounts and practical implementation.

Estate-interest test

For each CAM-favourable decision: what quantified or identifiable benefit did LPB receive? Debt reduction, settlement, lower enforcement risk, savings, higher value, asset preservation or an equivalent advantage.

Role-boundary test

Were there moments when the Administrator moved from administering/supervising the estate into effective control of an LPB procedural position, cause of action, authorisation or information source? If so, what authority, conflict analysis and estate-interest rationale governed that step?

Central question: when an LPB protection disappears and the CAM perimeter preserves or improves its position, where is the contemporaneous document showing that this distribution of advantage was best for the estate?

Commercial Court: active intervention + passive deference

ACTIVE_AND_PASSIVE_CAM_FAVOURABLE_EFFECT_PATTERN

The analysis does not merely count adverse rulings. It compares intensity of intervention. In the retracto episode, a separate court required production so LPB could inspect the assignment economics; for CAM recognition, the Commercial Court accepted that full title support need not be required because the Insolvency Administrator had reported favourably and verification could be presumed.

Active intervention

Recognition, adjudication, implementation or decisions producing an identifiable material advantage for CAM.

Passive/deferential intervention

Reliance on the Administrator, absence of an additional neutral check, or no equivalent response where LPB seeks protection, restitution, disclosure or material competition.

The responsible public formulation is intervention asymmetry, not “the judge always ruled for CAM.” There are rulings adverse to CAM or protective of LPB that must remain visible. Moving from asymmetry to improper bias or prevaricación requires additional legal and subjective elements.

Coordination? The optics are strong; proof of agreement is not yet there.

The recurrence creates an appearance of functional convergence: repeated beneficiary, recurring institutional nodes and repeated loss of LPB positions. That appearance justifies a targeted search for coordination evidence.

What would raise the grade

Communications around decision points, cross-instructions, timing unexplained by ordinary procedure, undeclared conflicts, private benefits, common planning documents or corroborated witness evidence.

What is not enough

Parallel conduct, prior professional relationships, an adverse decision, benefit to a creditor, recurring presence or temporal coincidence do not by themselves prove an unlawful agreement.

Accountability ladder: five levels that must not be collapsed

1 · Repeated effect

Supported. The CAM perimeter repeatedly appears as beneficiary of material transitions.

2 · Possible breach / disloyal insolvency administration

Investigative hypothesis. Requires proof of duty, authority, act or omission, departure from estate interest, harm and causation.

3 · Criminal administración desleal

Not a finding. Requires legally relevant powers, abusive/disloyal exercise, patrimonial damage and actor-specific causation.

4 · Improper influence / judicial prevaricación

Not a finding. Requires proof of an influence act or a judicial decision meeting the criminal threshold of injustice plus the required knowledge/intent. Asymmetry alone is insufficient.

5 · Concerted action / conspiracy

Highest-threshold hypothesis. Requires communication or agreement around a specific criminal plan, not merely aligned outcomes.

The simplest sentence remains the hardest to answer

“How does killing the retracto route help LPB?”

It does not assume the retracto was a winner. It asks why losing an already-admitted information, price and optionality route was better for LPB than preserving it. The same test should be applied to standing, control, restitution, exit alternatives, competition, asset perimeter and final accounts.

Mandatory counter-record

The strength of this dossier depends on keeping visible the facts that may support a legitimate explanation: the Commercial Court rejected Article 1535; it corrected part of the amounts in LPB's favour; there was a 2018 suspension and a 2019 refusal of validation; insolvency can lawfully result in creditor acquisition; and later companies are legally distinct.
