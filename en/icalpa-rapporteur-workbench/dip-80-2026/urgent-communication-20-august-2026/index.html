Subject: DIP 80/2026 – urgent institutional requirement for a reasoned decision, evidence preservation and containment of professional risk

TO THE ILLUSTRIOUS BAR ASSOCIATION OF LAS PALMAS
For the attention of the Deontology Commission, competent body and appointed rapporteur in DIP 80/2026
With a request for immediate routing to the Dean and Governing Board

Dear Sirs and Madams,

This communication concerns DIP 80/2026 exclusively and is institutionally urgent. It does not seek a predetermined result or attribute established criminal or professional-conduct responsibility to the Bar, the rapporteur or the professional concerned. It seeks a human, competent, traceable and reasoned route, together with proportionate preservation and professional-risk containment measures.

This email is formulated as immediate notice. Its content will also be prepared for electronic-registry presentation so that formal evidence of receipt and treatment exists.

I. Elapsed time and absence of a communicated route

On 27 May 2026 preliminary information was opened concerning Mr Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte in relation to his professional conduct as Insolvency Administrator of Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. Opening was communicated on 28 May.

As at 20 August 2026, 85 days have elapsed from the opening agreement. During that period clarifications, contextual material and express requests for procedural progress were sent. The communication of 13 July requested a reasoned decision or, alternatively, identification of the precise fact, document, custodian and verification still outstanding.

I have not located any later substantive ICALPA communication identifying:

  • the current procedural phase;
  • the date and nature of the latest act;
  • whether opening was notified to the professional and on what date;
  • whether he filed representations or documents;
  • which concrete check remains outstanding;
  • whether there is a reasoned suspension or coordination with criminal proceedings;
  • or which route is proposed.

I do not assert an automatic three-month deadline for completing preliminary information. I do note that the Bar's Statutes contemplate a finite transition to archive, the minor-infringement route where appropriate or formal disciplinary proceedings, and connect limitation consequences to the date on which opening was notified to the professional concerned. That date, the hearing status and the next act are indispensable to understanding the clock and procedural legality.

II. Why the matter now requires a unitary criminal and professional-conduct reading

The documentary reconstruction no longer permits the matter to be reduced to technical mistakes, adverse court decisions or isolated insolvency disagreements.

The hypothesis requiring proof or rejection is whether professional and court-entrusted powers of the Insolvency Administrator — LPB representation, control of its defence, reporting to the Court, estate preservation, verification of credit and value, assessment of alternatives, liquidation, implementation and accounting — may have been used, by act or omission, as a conversion layer through which disputed private premises acquired procedural, patrimonial, registry, financing and operating effect.

The sequence under examination is:

disputed credit, Community authority and control → use or non-use of powers exclusive to the office → weakened or lost LPB defence and information routes → uncontrolled material and economic control → valuation and liquidation → award and dation → financing, HNT and MYND normalisation → increasing irreversibility and third-party participation.

I do not present this as proved fact or ask ICALPA to decide criminal responsibility. The principal criminal hypothesis put before competent authorities is provisional and concerns possible disloyal administration under article 252 of the Criminal Code: powers over another's property, possible infringement or excess, and patrimonial loss. Any hypothesis of procedural fraud, professional harm, document falsity or use, disclosure of protected information or third-party cooperation would be sustainable only if all elements, knowledge, participation and causation were separately proved.

The professional-conduct question is autonomous and prior: whether the professional acted with the diligence, independence, impartiality, loyalty, integrity, prudence, transparency and traceability required of an office that operated as the legal gate of the estate.

III. The source-of-power test

CAM could defend its credit, submit documents, make applications, bid and seek judicial decisions.

CAM could not itself:

  • terminate or condition the lawyer acting for LPB;
  • withdraw LPB proceedings;
  • decide which information, defence or recovery routes LPB maintained;
  • select which alternatives reached the Court from the estate side;
  • report to the Court for the administered property;
  • protect or fail to protect estate property, possession, access and income;
  • implement liquidation from LPB's position;
  • or render the insolvency accounts.

Those capacities came from the office. Each episode must therefore be reconstructed through a uniform test:

OFFICE POWER → LEGAL SOURCE → INFORMATION AVAILABLE → DUTY AND PURPOSE → ACT OR OMISSION → PRIVATE OBSTACLE REMOVED → ESTATE BENEFIT, IF ANY → PATRIMONIAL CONSEQUENCE → CONTEMPORANEOUS REASON → CONTRARY EXPLANATION → DECISIVE MISSING EVIDENCE.

IV. Concrete gates requiring a professional explanation

1. LPB defence and information — 25 January 2018

The published documentary record identifies that the Insolvency Administrator ended the formal mandate of the lawyer acting for LPB in proceedings seeking information about the PH122→CAM assignment. LPB later withdrew and that route ended.

This does not prove dishonesty, identify by itself who drafted or ordered withdrawal, or establish that the proceedings would have succeeded. It does require production of authority, instructions, estate-interest analysis, alternatives, the reason for withdrawal and measured consequence.

2. Community, authority, debt and voting

The minutes, title, liquidations, accounts and checks relied upon by the Administrator in dealing with disputes over offices, quotas, debt, access and exploitation must be identified, together with contradictions reported to the Court.

3. Material control in June 2018

The preservation, inventory, restitution, protective, court-reporting or separation measures taken concerning property, access, keys, works, income, operation, Matkator units and third-party property must be explained. The Administrator did not physically control every actor, but should be able to show which legally available measures he requested or adopted and with what result.

4. Funded exit and operator route in 2018

It must be clarified whether the route intended to pay liabilities, conclude the insolvency and preserve the hotel unit received a fair, rapid, quantified and documented assessment; which working creditor figure was provided; what objections existed; and why it did not reach an operational decision. The proposal is not presumed binding, funded or superior.

5. Deed of 28 November 2018 and approximately €400,000

The chain title→authority→payment→account→non-validation→restitution or retention→later inclusion→final accounts must be reconstructed. An unreconciled figure does not prove appropriation, but the insolvency accounts should permit its treatment to be reproduced completely.

6. Credit, valuation, award and dation in 2021–2022

Principal, interest, valuations, instructions, methodology, objections, property perimeter, alternatives, award conditions, dation, extinguished debt and any surplus must be reconciled. A low valuation or creditor award is not itself an offence; the question is what the Administrator independently checked and what verifiable estate benefit or loss followed.

7. Accounts and later normalisation

The HNT/RICPE/MYND position makes fruits, income, costs, third-party property, recovery rights, LPB claims, Matkator assets, debt extinction, fees and surplus indispensable. Current operation does not establish unlawful origin; it makes every prior bridge materially traceable.

V. The professional perimeter has widened

The controlled formulation is not that three firms are criminally involved. It is that three professional or legal-professional perimeters with Canary relevance now hold potentially decisive records and face conflict, secrecy, independence, custody and preservation questions.

1. PwC/Landwell

The file preserves detailed Sun Park work and an email acknowledging a telephone conversation with the Insolvency Administrator without a contemporaneous detailed email or report; the client immediately asked for a written account of what had been said and answered.

This does not establish improper contact, coordination, unlawful information transfer or conflicted later work. It justifies identifying participants, date, content, consequence and record.

2. Grant Thornton/Cuyás/Grant Thornton Canarias

In 2020 a conflict and independence process was communicated in which the Administrator and CAM were identified, together with a confidentiality framework. The institutional channel later acknowledged a professional or commercial relationship with a Canary partner company. Internal file 2024_04 was archived on 10 December 2025.

This does not establish failure of the check, cross-file access, scope over the acts under review or responsibility of a Grant Thornton entity. It requires reconciliation of entity, dates, capacity, access, barriers and conflict memory.

3. San Telmo/RSM

A public source records the statement that “the firm” introduced clients into the first investment; separate sources connect the relevant chronology with Sun Park. RSM has confirmed an Ethics & Independence review under reference NNR4-1025C2F66.

This does not itself identify particular clients or establish Administrator knowledge, information transfer, unmanaged conflict, coordination or criminal responsibility. It justifies preserving matter opening, conflicts, referrals, KYC, remuneration, billing, access, barriers and independence decisions.

VI. Institutional consequence of not communicating a route

I do not assert that ICALPA caused the historical events under investigation. Nor that absence of a response proves protection, concealment or complicity.

The proposition is narrower and more serious:

Timely, finite and reasoned Bar action could have reduced foreseeable risks of evidence loss, continuing contested representation, widening professional involvement, continued use of disputed premises and normalisation of unexplained practices. The absence of a communicated route has not contained that risk.

The problematic signal to the Las Palmas profession would not be a reasoned archive or a decision adverse to the complainant. Archive after proportionate and adversarial checking is legitimate. Risk arises when opened preliminary information, repeatedly supplemented and expressly pressed for progress, remains without a visible explanation of what was checked, what remains and by what route it will be resolved.

This creates moral-hazard, public-confidence and institutional-reputation risk. It is particularly sensitive where private firms have undertaken or continue internal reviews, issued institutional positions or received concrete preservation requests, while the route of the competent professional body remains without communicated explanation.

This communication does not presently quantify or attribute additional economic loss to ICALPA's handling. That issue is expressly reserved. Current identified risk concerns evidence preservation, professional governance, institutional confidence and reputation.

VII. Immediate and finite institutional requirement

  1. Acknowledgment and confirmation of routing to the competent body, rapporteur, Dean and Governing Board by 14:00 on 21 August 2026.
  2. By 27 August 2026, confirmation of the current procedural state of DIP 80/2026, date and nature of the latest act, responsible body and expected date of the next decision.
  3. Confirmation of the exact date on which opening was notified to the professional concerned, whether he filed representations or documents and whether that stage is complete, without requesting protected contents that cannot lawfully be disclosed.
  4. Official index of submissions and annexes actually incorporated into DIP 80/2026, separated, classified and deduplicated from material shared with other files.
  5. Identification of the proposed route: reasoned archive; minor-infringement procedure; formal disciplinary proceeding; additional finite checks; or reasoned suspension/coordination with a concrete criminal matter.
  6. For every further check: exact fact, indispensable document or explanation, custodian, intended request and completion deadline.
  7. Within the Bar's competence, agreement, request or encouragement of preservation of relevant records in the PwC/Landwell, Grant Thornton/Cuyás/Grant Thornton Canarias and San Telmo/RSM perimeters; or, if ICALPA considers it lacks direct competence, identification of the custodian and correct route.
  8. Dean and Governing Board institutional oversight of status, preservation and route if the Commission is not yet able to take a decision.
  9. Recording as a navigation index — without replacing primary sources or the official file — of the independent and unofficial review at:

    Criminal and institutional review of DIP 80/2026

    Specific landing page:

    DIP 80/2026 · independent index

The review expressly separates documented fact, institutional statement, allegation, inference, open question, contrary explanation and missing evidence. It does not presume guilt, attribute a position to ICALPA or remove any right of reply and correction.

Where ICALPA cannot meet a request, I ask for a brief explanation of the legal or procedural limitation and the correct route. If a route decision cannot yet be taken, I request a dated and reasoned programme of checks leading to it.

Absence of a response will not be presented as an admission or proof on the merits. It will require a request for certification and institutional review through the competent professional, administrative and, where appropriate, judicial routes.

The purpose is not improper pressure on the rapporteur or a demand for sanction. It is that, after the elapsed time and objective widening of professional risk, the Bar visibly exercises its function through a reasoned decision or a finite, dated and accountable plan.
