1. Instrumentalisation hypothesis requiring proof

Falsifiable hypothesis. Powers entrusted to the Insolvency Administrator to represent LPB, report to the Court, preserve the estate, verify credit and value, assess alternatives, liquidate, implement and account may have operated — through individualised acts or omissions — as a conversion layer through which disputed private premises acquired procedural, patrimonial, registry, financing and operating effect.

Disputed credit, Community authority and control → use or non-use of office powers → weakened LPB defence and information → uncontrolled material/economic control → valuation and liquidation → award/dation → HNT, financing and MYND normalisation → increasing irreversibility.

The sequence does not itself prove a criminal plan. Its value lies in asking whether each decision had a lawful basis, reasonable estate benefit, sufficient information, independence and a reproducible contemporaneous record.

4. Criminal modules and limits

Primary provisional hypothesis

Disloyal administration · Criminal Code art. 252

Requires authority over another's property, infringement or excess, mental element, patrimonial loss and causation, act by act.

Conditional

Procedural fraud

Requires a deliberate deceptive mechanism or manipulated evidence, judicial error, patrimonial decision and causation. Error, non-deceptive omission or adverse result is insufficient.

Conditional · validate

Criminal Code article 467

The professionally entrusted interest, manifest harm and intentional or serious-negligence route must be identified for the dual lawyer/administrator function.

Conditional

Documents, secrecy and participation

Require the exact document or information, author, knowledge, use or disclosure, contribution and effect. Professional association and final benefit do not replace the elements.

Criminal-control rule. Where evidence capable of excluding the innocent explanation is absent, the correct conclusion is “pending verification”, not “proved”.
