4. Why the chain could extend beyond the insolvency

Evidence and court decision

Inspection, expert evidence and the physical condition

The CAM/Community account entered the adversarial record in which the requested inspection and expert process was not then carried out. No sole causation is alleged; the exact contribution requires reconstruction.

Access and possession

Entry control, title checks and exclusion

The Community certificate could influence the legal characterisation of security, custody and access by owners, technicians, experts, operators and potential participants.

Works and value

Transformation, damage, valuation and bidders

Characterising works as internal or safety-related could become a premise for measuring loss, comparing alternatives, valuing and deciding liquidation.

Title and third parties

LPB, CAM, Matkator and other owners

The RICPE certificate itself described fragmented title. A unitary project narrative could not replace property-by-property title or transfer extra-insolvency assets.

Investment and financing

RICPE, due diligence and documentary trust

A judicially presented account might circulate as a legitimacy or risk signal in investment, supervisory, financing and advisory settings without legally binding those third parties.

Later operation

HNT, MYND, income and irreversibility

Later operation does not prove unlawful origin, but makes the traceability of documentary bridges affecting access, works, value, title, financing and operation increasingly material.

5. Mandatory separation from DIP 80/2026

DIP 79: what CAM's lawyer verified, qualified, presented, maintained or corrected.

DIP 80: what the Insolvency Administrator independently verified, adopted, reported, protected, implemented and accounted for from the estate side.

Requested institutional rule: shared documents justify coordinated preservation, indexing and reconciliation; they do not justify merged culpability, automatic transfer of evidence or a common merits decision.
