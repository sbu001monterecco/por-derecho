PRESCRIPTION ≠ CADUCITY · CRIMINAL ≠ CIVIL · INTERIM ≠ FINAL JUDGMENT

There is no single clock.

Recovery strategy cannot rest on the phrase “the criminal case stops everything”. Each claim has its own claimant, defendant, act, period, trigger, interruption or suspension mechanism and remedy.

Control rule: preserve and interrupt what the law makes interruptible; protect each still-live caducity period through the mechanism specifically available; correctly judicialise supported criminal hypotheses; and seek proportionate measures before assets, evidence, income or value become harder to restore.

1. Five clocks that must not be merged

CRIMINAL

Criminal Code arts. 131–132

The period depends on the maximum statutory penalty. Computation and interruption follow their own criminal rules. A complaint or private prosecution filed before a judicial body against a sufficiently identified person can produce the maximum six-month suspension mechanism in article 132.2 while the qualifying judicial resolution is awaited.

CIVIL

Civil Code arts. 1964, 1968, 1969 and 1973

A claim accrues when it can be exercised, and prescription can be interrupted by judicial exercise, an extrajudicial creditor demand or acknowledgment. The actual period depends on the cause of action.

CADUCITY

Strict short periods

Article 1535, article 1524 and Horizontal Property Act article 18.3 are examples of short periods that must not be treated as ordinary civil prescription.

IA / STATE

Special regimes

Insolvency-administrator liability and State liability for judicial error or abnormal functioning have their own legal routes and periods.

Never publish “still alive” or “time-barred” without identifying the exact clock, Day Zero and the act said to interrupt or suspend it.

2. Criminal prescription: what can actually stop the clock

Criminal Code article 131 sets the periods by maximum penalty. Article 132 governs commencement and interruption.

Judicial resolution

When a reasoned judicial resolution attributes sufficiently identified suspected participation in potentially criminal facts to a person, the proceeding is directed against that person for these purposes.

Court-filed complaint / private prosecution

Filing before a judicial body against an identified person suspends computation for up to six months. If the required judicial resolution arrives within that window, interruption operates retroactively from filing.

Do not confuse: an ordinary report to the Public Prosecutor, police or an administrative authority is relevant for alerting, preservation and evidence, but it must not be described as automatically interrupting article 132. The record must establish when, if at all, the matter became judicialised and against whom.

For a legally established continuing, permanent or habitual offence article 132 contains special commencement rules. But ongoing hotel operation, continuing economic benefit or continuing effects of an earlier loss do not by themselves make the offence continuing or permanent.

3. Criminal proceedings can change the civil clock — but only when the legal bridge exists

Articles 111 and 114 of the Criminal Procedure Act can prevent a separate civil action concerning the same facts from proceeding while the criminal case is pending.

The Supreme Court Civil Chamber has repeatedly held that where criminal proceedings concern the same facts and create a legal obstacle to the civil claim, they can delay the start of civil prescription. Judgment 1599/2023 also summarises the line of authority that a criminal complaint can amount to exercise of the civil action for article 1973 purposes and that, for a personated party, the civil clock can begin after the criminal judgment/dismissal/archive is final and correctly notified.

Required bridge: same material facts + civil claim actually affected + status of civil liability in the criminal case + final resolution + notification/finality. Without that bridge there is no presumed tolling or interruption.

For the special Sun Park periods it remains COUNSEL VALIDATION REQUIRED: it assists only if the right remains legally alive and must not be presented as automatic revival.

5. Insolvency administrator: four years, but not one universal expiry date

Historical Insolvency Act article 36 and current TRLC articles 94–98 contain a special liability regime. The estate-damage action has a four-year framework linked to knowledge of the damage and, in any event, cessation in office; direct individual actions remain separately preserved.

IA ACT/OMISSION → DUTY → ESTATE OR DIRECT HARM → KNOWLEDGE → CESSATION → INTERRUPTIONS → CRIMINAL LINK → QUANTUM → REMEDY

The loyalty-breakpoint, conflict or adverse functional-agency hypothesis can be relevant to duty, knowledge, causation and possible criminal characterisation. It does not by itself extend the statutory period.

6. Judge / Administration of Justice: a special route

Organic Judiciary Act articles 292–293 govern State liability for judicial error and abnormal functioning. Harm must be effective, economically assessable and individualised; reversal alone does not automatically create compensation.

The judicial-error declaration has its own strict three-month period and the compensation claim a one-year period from when it could be exercised. Abnormal-functioning claims use their own State-liability route.

Do not merge: State compensation, judicial error and any personal criminal allegation against a judge are distinct routes with different predicates and clocks.

7. The criminal route is also about restitution and damages

Criminal Code articles 109–112 include restitution, repair and compensation for material and moral harm. Article 111 addresses restoration of the same asset where possible, subject to legal third-party protection. Article 121 provides for subsidiary public-entity civil liability under its statutory conditions, and article 122 reaches a participant for profit in the effects of an offence up to the value of that participation.

Sun Park consequence: the criminal matrix needs a recovery column. The question is not only “what offence?”. It is also “what property, income, value or loss could be restored or compensated if the constituent elements are proved?”.

8. Interim relief: preserve the ability to recover

Criminal

Criminal Procedure Act article 589 permits security and attachment to secure possible pecuniary liabilities where the legal predicates exist; article 764 permits patrimonial precautionary measures in abbreviated proceedings; Criminal Code article 127 octies permits seizure/attachment/deposit from the first investigative steps to secure confiscation.

Civil

Civil Procedure Act articles 721, 727 and 728 allow measures to secure effective final relief where delay risk, a provisional merits basis and normally security are shown. Horizontal Property Act article 18.4 permits interim suspension of challenged resolutions.

Operational question: what asset, title, income stream, evidence, Registry position or economic value will be harder to restore if it is not preserved before final judgment?

9. “Interim damages” is not automatic advance compensation

The interim priority is to preserve the ability to obtain final relief: attachment/security where available; restrictions on disposal or Registry measures where legally supportable; evidence preservation; deposit/accounting of disputed income where there is a legal basis; and interim suspension where the governing statute permits it.

Final relief can include, depending on cause of action and standing: restitution of property/rights, invalidity or ineffectiveness, specific repair, accounting, patrimonial damages, lost profits, moral damages where available, interest, costs and special IA/State/third-party liability.

10. The economic denominator must eliminate double counting

ASSET VALUE + LOST INCOME + USE OF THIRD-PARTY PROPERTY + LOST EXPLOITATION DISTRIBUTIONS + CAUSALLY PROVED DAMAGES + TRACEABLE VALUE + INTEREST − RECOVERIES − LAWFUL CONSIDERATION − OVERLAPS = CLAIMANT-SPECIFIC NET DENOMINATOR

Aweswell, LPB, Matkator, CEXP and any third-party owner must remain separate for standing and quantum before economic consolidation. The entire present hotel value is not automatically an additional damages head on top of every historic loss.

11. Four control ledgers that should now exist

Criminal judicialisation

Complaint/private prosecution → judicial body → identified person → article 132 resolution → suspension/archive → restart.

Civil interruption

Claim → defendant → court action/demand/acknowledgment → receipt proof → scope → new computation.

Caducity / Day Zero

Right → trigger → strict period → Registry/knowledge/demand → MASC → legally recognised effect.

Damages and remedy

Harm → right-holder → causal act → counterfactual → amount → support → overlap → interim preservation → final remedy.

12. Public boundaries

  • Do not say an ordinary Fiscalía report automatically interrupts criminal prescription.
  • Do not say a criminal case freezes every civil deadline.
  • Do not equate continuing harm with a legally continuing offence.
  • Do not say retracto/tanteo rights remain alive merely because criminal proceedings exist.
  • Do not publish one prescription date for every insolvency-administrator act.
  • Do not say a criminal allegation automatically freezes CAM/HNT/MYND assets.
  • Do not add the entire hotel value, all historic income and every damages head as independent losses.

Conservative internal action dates belong to counsel/strategy work and should not be published.

Conclusion

The correct strategy is anti-forfeiture, not deadline denial. Determine each clock; document each interruption; distinguish prescription from caducity; use the criminal route only for effects the law actually recognises; seek proportionate preservation where risk is real; and keep restitution, damages, IA liability, State liability and civil liability arising from crime open in parallel where their elements are available.
