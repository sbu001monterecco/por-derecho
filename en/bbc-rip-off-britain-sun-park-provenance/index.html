BBC RIP OFF BRITAIN · SOURCE PROVENANCE · UPDATED 22 AUGUST 2026

Who supplied the story, what did the BBC verify, and who later used the resulting narrative?

This page reconstructs the January 2019 Sun Park item without assuming that Construcciones Acosta Matos, an Acosta Matos-related person, Lanzarote Information, Camel Travel, New Horizons or another private actor caused, commissioned or coordinated the BBC programme.

Important limit. A contemporaneous BBC programme page, production correspondence and later BBC responses have been located. A certified master recording and complete verbatim transcript of the Sun Park segment have not yet been obtained. The broadcast is therefore reconstructed only to the extent the primary source set permits.

PROVENANCE

The known source path starts with customers. One earlier journalist/group link is now a material lead.

Before 30 Nov 2018

Four named customers

The BBC's first located formal approach says four named customers had contacted the programme about bookings, payments, closure and refunds. Their allegations are source material, not proof of the underlying propositions.

→
30 Nov 2018 onward

Rip Off Britain production

Philip Jeffery put the complaints to Sun Park, received detailed rebuttal and later said the programme intended to proceed because customers were confused and disappointed while still inviting a broadcast response.

→
22 Jan 2019

BBC One broadcast

The preserved BBC episode page describes a sunshine resort turning away guests who had booked and paid. In 2025 the BBC said the programme also carried Sun Park's explanation that activities had ceased because of alleged illegal activities by third parties.

New provenance lead · 8 October 2018

New Horizons / Ian Griffiths / Phil Lee

A preserved former-customer-group capture records an Ian Griffiths seeking access. A group member described him as a journalist investigating Sun Park; Phil Lee said he had been in contact with him and recommended that he be admitted. Separate later public credits identify an Ian Griffiths as a Rip Off Britain producer. The present record does not prove that the October 2018 account was the same person acting for the BBC. The identity, employer and assignment bridge remains open.

No CAM causation finding.

No primary email, payment, production note, instruction, contributor agreement, call record or witness statement has been located showing that CAM, JDAM, Hotel New Trend, Canarian Hospitality or MYND initiated, commissioned, supplied, funded or directed the BBC programme.

DATED RECONSTRUCTION

The important dates

7 June 2018

Material-control rupture

The repository documents a material change in physical and operational control. Project Sun Rock disputes its legality. This page does not convert that documented event into a final judicial finding of an unlawful takeover.

8 October 2018

Journalist enters the provenance picture

New Horizons capture: journalist named Ian Griffiths seeks access; Phil Lee says he has been in contact and favours admission. Professional identity/capacity remains to be proved.

30 November 2018

First formal BBC approach located

BBC identifies four named customers who had contacted it and asks Sun Park for detailed response.

3 January 2019

Formal provenance challenge before broadcast

Sun Park's legal notice disputes the customer narrative, raises source-provenance issues and places ownership, operation, possession and litigation distinctions before BBC Legal and production. This proves notice, not the truth of every allegation in the letter.

8–16 January 2019

BBC decides to proceed

The BBC cites customer confusion/disappointment, invites further response and confirms the intended 22 January broadcast.

22 January 2019 · 09:15

BBC One

Rip Off Britain: Holidays, Series 7, Episode 12, 45 minutes. The complete Sun Park segment remains an archive-production request.

2019 onward

Own-side preservation

BBC materials appear in Hamlins, AlasLegal, KMT and later recovery packages. This is documented use by the affected side; it does not prove adverse-party reliance.

2023

Separate successor-publication path

Lanzarote Information's adverse predecessor material remained public while Camel Travel promoted MYND Yaiza, identified it as the former Sunpark and invited quotation requests. New Horizons later shared Camel's MYND post. BBC causation of that later contrast is not proved.

BBC AFTER NOTICE

The BBC's later position is procedural and finite.

12 February 2025

No plan to revisit

Series producer Lizz Martin said the programme had been made under BBC Editorial Guidelines, said it had reflected Sun Park's position at the time, and said there were no plans to revisit the story.

24 February 2025

Internal discussion promised

The BBC acknowledged further material and said it would be discussed internally. This is an acknowledgement, not acceptance of the allegations.

4 March 2025

Programme no longer viewable

The BBC said the programme was no longer available to view and was out of time for an editorial complaint, directing the matter to the Complaints Framework.

10 March 2025

Complaints / Legal routing

Editorial complaints were directed to the Complaints Framework and legal complaints to BBC Legal. No complete institutional merits response to the later documentary reconstruction has been located.

The present institutional question: after receiving materially expanded documentary evidence, did the BBC preserve the production file, test source provenance, compare later evidence with the original editorial basis, and make a reasoned decision on correction, contextualisation or archive annotation?

LANZAROTE INFORMATION · CAMEL TRAVEL · NEW HORIZONS

Connected narratives, but no proven common commissioning chain.

The existing record proves a former commercial relationship between Lanzarote Information and Sun Park; a later adverse Sun Park publication; favourable Camel Travel promotion of the refurbished same property as MYND Yaiza; and third-party sharing of that MYND post within New Horizons. The October 2018 New Horizons capture also creates a significant BBC-provenance lead through Phil Lee and a journalist named Ian Griffiths.

Still not proved: that Mike or Julie founded or controlled New Horizons; targeted former customers; caused or instructed the MYND repost; diverted bookings; received MYND commission; acted on CAM instruction; introduced or caused the BBC programme; or coordinated the BBC's editorial decision.
