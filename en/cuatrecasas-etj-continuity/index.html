Evidence correction: invoicing is not judicial pursuit

Controlled-corpus finding as at 3 September 2026. The reviewed record documents invoices, proformas and extrajudicial payment discussions with Aweswell/Gil. It does not presently identify a judicial fee-recovery action brought by Cuatrecasas against Aweswell in Spain or the United Kingdom. The identified judicial route was brought against Matkator through Cambiario 1048/2019 and ETJ 163/2020.

This is a finite corpus finding, not universal proof that no other proceeding could exist. It also does not establish that Matkator lacked an autonomous or guaranteed obligation, that the promissory notes were invalid, that all invoiced sums were due, or that any procedural impropriety occurred.

service → professional client → invoice recipient → alleged debtor → promissory note → issuer/signatory → corporate authority → proceeding → asset enforced

Do not use either inaccurate shorthand. The record does not support “Cuatrecasas never invoiced or asked Aweswell to pay.” Nor should extrajudicial correspondence be described as “Cuatrecasas judicially pursued Aweswell for the fees.”

Finite title-formation question

The principal open issue is not merely whether professional fees existed. It is how the claim became an enforceable Cambiario title against Matkator.

Promissory notesRecover complete front/back, endorsements, guarantees, issue and custody trail, invoice allocation, signatory and authority.
Matkator capacityIdentify its registered office, administrator/representative and the legal capacity of each possible contact at every material date.
Service chronologyRecover every address enquiry and attempt, the application for service by public notice, what was represented to the court, the authorising order, publication and title chronology.
Direct communicationsCompare any direct communications with Gil against the exact service dates. Contact is relevant only if it could lawfully and practically have assisted service on Matkator.
Testing rule. A service/title hypothesis requires primary evidence of useful location information known at the material time, a materially relevant representation or omission to the court, resulting lack of effective participation before title formation, and a causal route into ETJ 163/2020. Direct contact alone is not proof that edictal service was defective.

Article 569 / Article 40.1 control

Cuatrecasas contests the strict conditions for suspending enforcement on grounds of criminal prejudiciality under Article 569 LEC. A separate question, reserved for counsel and the court, is whether any concrete facts appearing in the civil record engage the distinct communication mechanism under Article 40.1 LEC.

No conclusion is published here. The website does not state that either article applies. It records that they are different procedural questions and that failure to meet a suspension test does not, by itself, decide every possible treatment of concrete facts appearing in the record.
