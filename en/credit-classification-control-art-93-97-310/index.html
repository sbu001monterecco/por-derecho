LEGAL CONTROL · 21 AUGUST 2026

The question is not “Article 93: yes or no?” It is which rule governed each creditor substitution.

The Bankia/SAREB → Promontoria Holding 122 B.V. → CAM chain must be reconstructed by date, order, creditor list, security and classification. The legal answer depends on the timing of the assignment and whether the later creditor truly met the statutory test for a specially related person.

Immediate correction of approach

The published chronology places CAM's entry as assignee in 2017, after the 2012 insolvency declaration.

Accordingly, historic Article 93.3 should not be used as a shortcut: its presumption concerned acquisitions of claims belonging to specially related persons made within the two years before the declaration. For a substitution after the declaration, the specific control is Article 97.4.4 of Law 22/2003, now TRLC Article 310.2.4, if the later creditor was a specially related person.

Historic Art. 93.2.2

Potentially relevant only if evidence establishes that the person/entity actually fitted the applicable statutory category — for example de jure/de facto administrator, liquidator or general attorney — at the legally relevant time. Economic influence, creditor status, asset purchase or benefit does not replace that functional proof.

Historic Art. 93.3

A rebuttable presumption tied to acquisitions within the two years before declaration and to claims belonging to a specially related person. It is not the natural rule for a 2017 acquisition in a 2012 insolvency.

Art. 97.4.4 of Law 22/2003

Where the initial creditor is replaced in the list and the later creditor is a specially related person, classification must take the outcome less burdensome for the insolvency among those corresponding to the initial and later creditor.

TRLC Art. 310.2.4

The current recast rule: in that situation the Insolvency Administrator must reclassify the claim by choosing the lower-ranking classification. It confirms the system; the temporally applicable rule must still be fixed for each act.

Supreme Court Judgment 22/2026 clarifies the timing divide

The Civil Chamber's Judgment 22/2026 of 14 January distinguishes assignments made before the insolvency declaration from those made afterwards. As a general rule, a pre-insolvency assignment to a related person does not automatically degrade a claim that arose without that relationship. But the judgment expressly identifies the special Article 97.4.4 rule for an assignment after the declaration to a specially related person: upon modification of the creditor list, classification can mutate to the less favourable classification.

That makes this a potentially important Sun Park control, but a conditional one: primary evidence must first establish that CAM legally qualified as a specially related person at the relevant date. The judgment does not allow that prerequisite to be presumed.

Immediate action

Request the certified creditor-substitution and classification matrix.

  1. Application and instrument for each assignment/acquisition of the claim.
  2. Date on which each assignee was entered/substituted in the final creditor list.
  3. Amount, security and classification before and after.
  4. Insolvency Administrator report/submission addressing substitution and treatment.
  5. Any judicial order authorising, recognising or deciding the modification.
  6. Contemporaneous corporate and functional evidence needed to test Article 93: shareholding, group status, directors, general powers, instructions and acts of de facto administration.
  7. If no reclassification occurred, identify the document explaining why Article 97.4.4 was considered inapplicable.

How this fits the June filing lanes

Daily intake reference 22 — Insolvency Administrator: a direct evidential question: what was known, what classification was retained or changed, on what basis and with what effect. The handwritten 22 is a Decanato intake reference, not an official proceeding number, and it does not by itself prove disloyal administration.

Private-actor lane / DP 1901: relevant to status, powers, representations and benefit, but it does not turn an assignment or disputed classification into a crime.

Daily intake reference 24 — judicial conduct: belongs to a distinct filing and is relevant only if an exact judicial act, the material then on the record, the legal issue actually raised and the response can be identified. It too is not an official proceeding number. An adverse or erroneous interpretation is not by itself judicial criminality.

Evidence capable of falsifying the hypothesis

This line must be abandoned or narrowed if the documents show that CAM met no statutory specially-related-person category, there was no legally relevant post-declaration creditor-list substitution, or the classification applied was the one required by the law and procedural posture. That falsifiability is part of the control.
