60-SECOND ORIENTATION

Eight questions before any conclusion.

1 · COMPETENCE

What can each body decide?

Discipline, inspection and court functioning, jurisdictional merits, LAJ/court-office acts, appeals and criminal routes are different questions.

2 · ADVERSE FIRST

What has the CGPJ already rejected?

DI 169/2026 was archived on 14 May 2026. The Promotor maintained the archive on 10 July 2026. That reasoning should be read before our objections.

3 · STATUS

What happened later?

Appeal 286/2026 and later submissions exist, but filing or registry routing is not the same as incorporation, examination, acceptance or truth.

4 · BRIDGE A

How was the number fixed?

The party corpus identifies a quantitative jump that should be reconstructed order by order before being described as judicially fixed fact.

5 · BRIDGE B

How did 2019 become 2022?

The 24 October 2019 non-validation point must be reconciled with the 2021 sale process, 26 January 2022 adjudication, deed and registry effects.

6–8 · PROOF

What is missing, what alternative exists, and which document resolves it?

Every issue ends with a finite verification step and the strongest lawful alternative explanation that should also be considered.

TWO DOCUMENTARY BRIDGES

The question is not “who is right?” but “which act connects A to B?”.

BRIDGE A · QUANTITATIVE

≈ €9.052m → ≈ €13.166m → adjudication / finality

Status: working sequence identified in the party corpus; not presented here as a judicially fixed amount.

The check is to identify the exact act fixing principal, interest, period, rate, classification and any amount used for tender, set-off, adjudication or deed mechanics.

  1. Which ruling finally fixed each component?
  2. Which part was a party or insolvency-administrator calculation?
  3. Which later act, if any, converted a provisional figure into a final one?
  4. Which amount did each 2021–2022 document use?

Record that would close the bridge: complete primary ruling(s) + accepted liquidation/calculation + traceability of the figure used in adjudication and deed.

BRIDGE B · TITLE

24/10/2019 → 2021 → 26/01/2022 → deed / Registry

Starting point: the public record already identifies a 24 October 2019 ruling refusing later authorization/validation concerning an operation performed without the required prior judicial authorization.

The reconstruction should explain, without jumps:

  1. the legal position after 24 October 2019;
  2. what was offered and under what perimeter in the 2021 tender;
  3. what the 26 January 2022 order actually approved;
  4. what testimony/court-office act enabled the deed;
  5. what title and premises reached the Registry and produced external effects.

Record that would close the bridge: full tender/adjudication record + testimony + deed + registry entry/note and any reconciling judicial decision.

THE STRONGEST ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION

A serious review must be capable of concluding that no irregularity occurred.

Alternatives that remain visible include: the issues correctly belonged to appeals rather than discipline; a later operation was lawfully enabled by acts not yet in our corpus; the figures were validly fixed in rulings or liquidations we have not yet reconciled; an act belonged to the LAJ or another authority; an alleged interaction was ordinary professional courtesy; or the material was insufficient, time-barred or irrelevant to disciplinary competence.

Correction rule: if a primary record establishes any of those explanations, the page and classification must change.
