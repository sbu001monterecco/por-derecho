Why this matters to the judge/LAJ analysis

The primary chronology demonstrates that the plan-compliance and review-access issues were raised repeatedly before the eventual outcome. That strengthens a knowledge/notice inquiry. It does not prove that the judge or LAJ deliberately concealed anything, or that every submission was legally well-founded. The next question is what the court and LAJ actually did with each filing and whether the available appellate route ever produced merits review of the definitive-adjudication objections.

Former counsel: neither blanket excuse nor scapegoat

An adverse outcome is not proof that counsel failed. A filed pleading is not proof that counsel exhausted the best available strategy. Every team must be audited using the same chain: mandate → knowledge → advice → draft → filing → receipt → evidence supplied → remedy → court/LAJ response → further challenge → outcome → strategic alternative.

Accordingly, possible shortcomings by former lawyers remain open, but they must be measured against the filings they actually made and the procedural environment they encountered.
