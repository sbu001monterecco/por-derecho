E.G. 745/2026 — continuity, errors, omissions and possible misclassifications

Control date: 31 August 2026. This page prepares continuity of the wider Ministerio Fiscal work, using E.G. 745/2026 as a controlled case. It does not declare illegality, criminal guilt or coordination. It separates primary-source facts, source-proved discrepancies, review grounds, omissions requiring explanation or production, and unresolved evidential questions.

What the controlled sources establish

The 26 August Decree archives E.G. 745/2026, describes the communication as confusa e incoherente, invokes Article 66.1(c) of Law 39/2015 and states that no sufficiently concrete irregularity attributable to a particular prosecutor in a particular matter was identified. The Inspection registration REGAGE26e00070235775, however, contains a substantive Expone, 12 numbered requests, and expressly names DI 248/2018, DI 113/2022, DI 22/2026, DIP 2/2026, EG 49/2026 and DP 1901/2026. It also asks Inspection to reconstruct from institution-held records who received, allocated, examined, reported, approved, signed, incorporated, excluded, referred or archived relevant material.

Inventory of discrepancies, omissions and review points

  1. Source–Decree discrepancy: a global characterization as “confusa e incoherente” versus a registered source with defined scope and 12 numbered requests. The particular fact, passage or request said to be unintelligible should be identified.
  2. Article 66 / cure: because Article 66 is invoked, review asks whether Article 68 cure/subsanación had to be addressed and, if not, what rule displaced it. This is a review argument, not an existing judicial holding.
  3. Specific matters: the Decree says there is insufficient concreteness as to prosecutor/matter, while the source names six proceedings. Naming them does not itself prove personal liability, but the reasoning should be reconciled.
  4. Functional attribution: the filing asks Inspection to reconstruct institutional attribution from records under institutional control. Absence of final applicant-supplied personal attribution should be distinguished from a request that the institution identify who performed the relevant acts.
  5. Twelve requests: the located Decree does not expose an individualized disposition of each numbered request.
  6. Six proceedings: the located record does not expose what was examined in each expressly named proceeding.
  7. Corpus examined: no source inventory of the material actually examined before archive has been located.
  8. Particularized reasoning: the located act does not identify which proposition, request or fact failed the stated clarity/concreteness threshold and why.
  9. Preservation: no express disposition has been located for requests to preserve institutional emails, headers, attachments, allocation/routing and relevant system traces.
  10. File and index: no complete E.G. 745 file or controlled/certified index has been located as produced.
  11. Actor-by-actor reconstruction: no specific response has been located addressing who received, allocated, examined, reported, approved, signed, incorporated, excluded, referred or archived material.
  12. DP 1901/2026: report author, allocation, instructions, review/visado, exact corpus available and what was transmitted to court remain open production questions.
  13. Referrals: no complete list of remissions/referrals to another unit or authority, or reasoned confirmation that none occurred, has been located.
  14. Native/verifiable Decree: the received PDF is hash-controlled, but production of the native/searchable/verifiable act and signature/production metadata remains outstanding.
  15. 2/3 August route: the REG-AGE registration is fixed on 2 August while the Decree refers to an email received on 3 August. The internal routing trace should reconcile the two paths; the date difference alone does not prove alteration or suppression.
