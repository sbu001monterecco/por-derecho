Minutes 2011–2022: how authority was produced and circulated

Sun Park functioned physically as a hotel across divided properties; the operator’s identity, title and perimeter changed or were disputed over time. This page reconstructs how debt, removal of voting rights, offices, custody, certificates, litigation, security and works appear connected in the records—and how those records reached Insolvency 36/2012, other courts and the Acosta Matos project.

The issue in this case—not a residential abstraction

A Community office was not title to the hotel.

Horizontal division did not turn Sun Park into a residential complex or erase its hotel reality. At the same time, the hotel’s economic unity did not erase private titles or merge the Owners’ Community, the Comunidad de Explotación del Complejo Sun Park (CEXP), LPB and the insolvency estate.

Bounded conclusion specific to Sun Park: No located instrument empowered minority Owners’ Community office-holders, by virtue of that office, to bind LPB/Matkator properties, take the whole undertaking or dispose of others’ rights. This must not be confused with the signed CEXP–Monterecco notice of 1 February 2012, which did purport to assign or subrogate the activity. The open questions are competent-body authority and scope, not the document’s non-existence; later CEXP minutes do not by themselves prove reversal.

Adverse evidence disclosedThe 2008 records show voluntary participation; the 1 February 2012 notice names Monterecco as new operator; Monterecco/Pink obtained registration for 159 units and genuinely operated; AP 89/2014 found possession/operation over claimant properties, receipt of keys from CEXP and applied veil-piercing; and AP 804/2018 was adverse to CEXP’s asserted possession in 2018. The 2011 resolutions were not set aside then.
Why it does not resolve the authority jumpThose facts defeat “no cession,” “Pink never operated” and “CEXP indisputably remained operator.” They do not establish competent CEXP-body authority or scope across every owner, third-party property or common element.

Reading framework: Articles 3, 13–15, 18 and 24 of the Horizontal Property Act separate private property, common elements, organs, voting and Community scope. Articles 38–39 of Canary Islands Law 7/1995, as amended by Law 5/1999, require a single operator and a documentary enabling title granted by the owners; operational unity does not manufacture that title.

Actual knowledge of the insolvency administrator

By 2016 the dispute could no longer be treated as unknown.

The evidence supports receipt and discussion of the allegation’s core. It does not automatically turn notice into acceptance of its legal correctness or criminal intent.

  • 12 July · version described as a burofaxGil Marer states that he sent it personally after LPB’s then insolvency counsel declined to adopt it. No independent Correos acceptance or delivery certificate for that exact version has been located.
  • 21–27 July · package receivedCounsel sent four files to the administrator. On 27 July he replied “after analysing the documents received”, rejected the Community criminal-law theory and addressed a possible civil challenge.
  • 11 August · direct personal noticeGil emailed a signed letter and the 26 April minutes to the administrator’s professional address. It alleged absent authority, manufactured debt, indications of crime and requested insolvency-administration action.
  • 15 September · receipt confirmedThe administrator quoted the message, referred to “what was discussed at today’s meeting”, proposed awaiting a possible agreement with the board, and requested updates on the personal complaint.
Evidential conclusion: The administrator’s actual knowledge of the authority, debt, voting and possible criminal-law dispute is documented, not merely constructive. The exact July postal route remains independently unproved, but the package analysed in July, direct August notice and September response/meeting make a later claim of unawareness of those allegations inconsistent with the located record.
Documented bottleneckThe first lawyer declined to send as his own a client-prepared draft; exercising LPB claims also depended on the insolvency allocation of powers and the administrator’s authority/action. Gil Marer interprets that sequence and later delay as functional blockage of LPB’s voice.
Limit of the inferenceProfessional refusal, insolvency dependence and a convergent outcome do not by themselves prove a secret instruction or improper influence by the administrator over that lawyer. Communications, mandate, conflict or direct benefit would be required.

Three lanes that must not be collapsed

Community, insolvency administrator and judge: distinct acts, connected outcome.

A unitary theory still requires individual attribution. The administrator administered or represented LPB’s estate; the judge supervised the insolvency. Neither thereby becomes a participant in every private act—or falls outside scrutiny of their own acts and omissions.

Administrator · documented affirmative contribution

Notice, voting and access

The administrator received the dispute in 2016. In 2018 he acted for LPB to request security and later testified that he authorised access to LPB premises for maintenance, accepting lock-breaking when required within that mandate. The question is whether he verified the voting basis, identified each unit and prevented that authority becoming broader hotel control.

AP 804/2018 · adverse evidence

Provisional coercion dismissal

The Provincial Court affirmed dismissal, treated the dispute as predominantly civil and relied on the administrator’s account/authority. It treated Pink’s formal possession as delivered on 22 May 2017 and was adverse to the criminally protectable possession asserted by CEXP. Its conditional wording that Pink “would already have abandoned” compressed an email whose original distinguishes the end of new commercialisation from services, supplies, maintenance, preservation and transition. The Order contains 2017/2018 and 2012/2013 inconsistencies. It did not adjudicate civil/tourism title or give CAM all Sun Park, but neither did it recognise CEXP as operator in 2018.

24 October 2019 · judicial control

Non-validation

The Court described its 26 June 2018 pronouncements as erroneous, recorded the absence of prior judicial authority and refused to validate the separation/sale of 31 LPB properties. This is contrary evidence to a theory of uniform judicial cooperation and requires following the operation through Registry, restitution and accounts.

24 February 2021 · supervisory decision

Knowledge and no inspection

The order recorded the positions on works and units, rejected inspection/expert evidence in that procedural setting and left estate preservation to the administrator, noting a potential liability route. It proves knowledge and a reasoned decision; it does not prove clandestine agreement or criminal intent.

Production-and-propagation theory

Not a pile of anomalies—a testable chain.

Every arrow requires an author, record, date, knowledge, competence, effect and benefit.

  1. 01DebtFees, services and balances produced inside the structure.
  2. 02VotingLPB attends but is excluded from decision-making.
  3. 03Organs and archiveThe remaining voters ratify office, custody, banking and litigation.
  4. 04CertificateThe resulting record circulates as authority of “the Community”.
  5. 05Access and controlLimited authority is invoked in material control of the hotel.
  6. 06Works and valueThe later project combines cost, title, finance and operation.

Functional law: during the relevant period the 2003 Insolvency Act governed diligence, supervision, liability, removal, patrimonial powers, preservation and continuation in Articles 35–37, 40, 43–44 and 54. From September 2020 the Consolidated Insolvency Act maintains duties of diligence, independence, supervision, liability and removal. Those provisions define duties and competence; they do not themselves prove agreement or intent.

Attributed unitary criminal-law theory

Limited authority allegedly converted into patrimonial displacement.

Gil Marer alleges that debt produced within the structure was used to remove LPB’s vote; that the remaining minority ratified offices, custody, certificates, litigation, security and access; and that the resulting records were later used as apparent authority over LPB property, operation and the hotel project. If knowing falsity or concealment, causation, dispositive acts and benefit are proved, the sequence may carry criminal-law significance. It is not published as adjudicated guilt.

Legal text: Spanish Criminal Code, current and historical versions. The date of each act determines the applicable offence and limitation period; a global label of “insolvency fraud” is no substitute for that analysis.
