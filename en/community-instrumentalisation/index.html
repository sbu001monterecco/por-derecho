Plain-language explanation

The Community did not own the hotel.

It administered common elements and common expenditure. Repetition could not make it owner of 220 registered units, the hotel operator, the transferor of private units or an unlimited authority over LPB, Matkator and third parties.

The central point If the same architecture helps calculate debt, decides who may vote, certifies the debt, holds the records, brings claims, appoints security and signs communications, an outside recipient cannot treat its output as self-proving. Every link requires verification.

The alleged mechanism

Instrumentalisation does not require every resolution to be false. It means using limited community powers to produce consequences outside the Community.

  1. Debt is attributedCharges and costs are presented as each unit’s community liability.
  2. Debt removes voting rightsThe majority owner is represented but prevented from deciding.
  3. A minority ratifiesAdministrator, accounts, certificates, lawyers, banking, security and access.
  4. The record travels“The Community resolved” is used before the court, insolvency administrator or authorities.
  5. The appearance expandsAccess, possession, works, licence, operation, finance, RIC and incentives.
  6. The result back-validatesThe later consequence is cited as proof that the original authority was sound.

Opinion attributed to and assumed by Gil Marer

The unit-by-unit sale created a vulnerability; Gil places the alleged root cause in non-completion or breach and the later conduct of the perimeter associated, in specific acts, with Montelanza, S.L. and Molina-linked owners.

The hotel operated as one economic unit but had been horizontally divided into approximately 220 registered units. In June 2008 JSP and other owners transferred units individually. The located documents then place LPB as the new property company—the PropCo—and the Sun Park Operating Community as the operational vehicle—the OpCo—while units and premises remained outside LPB.

The causal distinction: selling the shares in Montelanza, S.L., while keeping title and the hotel mechanism within one company, would have reduced the fracture points. The chosen route separated title, Community, operation, costs and minorities and created a vulnerability. Gil does not now present that architecture, by itself, as the root cause: he attributes that causal position to the alleged non-completion or breach of the 2008 transaction by the perimeter associated, in specific acts, with Montelanza, S.L. and Molina-linked owners and to the relevant later acts or omissions which, he says, exploited the vulnerability. The 22 June 2011 minutes are the first located Community matrix joining debt, voting exclusion, Pamanil, custody and litigation; Bankia’s enforcement and scheduled auction were later the documented immediate timing trigger for LPB’s insolvency filing. This is Gil’s causal position, not a judgment or collective attribution of liability: seller, property, obligation, act, person, knowledge and effect must be proved individually. Gil Marer’s public conclusion

Gil Marer relies on this named representation as an indication supporting his extra-insolvency criminal-law theory.

The continuity of named actors materially weakens an innocent-confusion explanation for those specific acts. The minutes evidence that separate ownership was presented within the team; each person’s actual knowledge and use must be established individually. The alleged later documentary, possessory, registry, hotel or financing integration of those units into an appearance of CAM ownership is the extra-insolvency property core Gil Marer asks authorities to investigate.

The Community could not convert administration into ownership

Community representation administers participation, expenses and common elements. It does not transfer title to the represented apartment. Community debt, denial of a vote, a minority-produced majority, an insolvency award of LPB assets and repetition of “complete hotel” do not place privately owned units into the estate or CAM’s property. The leap from Community authority to ownership of another’s property is the patrimonial instrumentalisation documented by this dossier.

Criminal-law characterisation advanced by Gil Marer — an express theory, not a conviction

Section 15.2 of Spain’s Horizontal Property Act governs debt and voting; it transfers no private title. Named ownership, production of Community authority, transport of that authority into the insolvency and later exploitation of an appearance of ownership are advanced as a unitary patrimonial theory of possible criminal-law significance, pending judicial determination.

  • Possible fraud and procedural fraud: only if sufficient deception, error, a causal disposition or order, loss, benefit and intent are proved (Criminal Code art. 248 and art. 250.1.7).
  • Possible disloyal administration: requires identification of administered property, authority, knowing excess, causal loss and intent (art. 252).
  • Possible document offence, knowing use or participation: requires a specific record, statutory mode, authorship, knowledge and use. A disputed minute or invalid resolution is not enough by itself.
  • Alleged extra-insolvency dimension: every act of assertion, disposal, possession or operation without title must be proved property by property.

Criminal-law language applied: the dossier describes the alleged conduct as assertion, disposition or operation without title over property outside the insolvency estate. It forms part of the procedural-fraud, document-offence, disloyal-administration and participation theory Gil Marer asks authorities to investigate.

Located documentary basis: named owners and representatives and a record of separate title. Open question: property-by-property investigation of later acts concerning those units and the other non-estate assets of Matkator/Aweswell.
