The “Community” and its instrumentalisation

How a body designed to administer common elements could become—on Gil Marer’s allegation—a machine for debt, voting, access, certification and authority whose output then travelled through the insolvency, hotel project, financing and public files.

Plain-language explanation

The Community did not own the hotel.

It administered common elements and common expenditure. Repetition could not make it owner of 220 registered units, the hotel operator, the transferor of private units or an unlimited authority over LPB, Matkator and third parties.

The central point If the same architecture helps calculate debt, decides who may vote, certifies the debt, holds the records, brings claims, appoints security and signs communications, an outside recipient cannot treat its output as self-proving. Every link requires verification.

The alleged mechanism

Instrumentalisation does not require every resolution to be false. It means using limited community powers to produce consequences outside the Community.

  1. Debt is attributedCharges and costs are presented as each unit’s community liability.
  2. Debt removes voting rightsThe majority owner is represented but prevented from deciding.
  3. A minority ratifiesAdministrator, accounts, certificates, lawyers, banking, security and access.
  4. The record travels“The Community resolved” is used before the court, insolvency administrator or authorities.
  5. The appearance expandsAccess, possession, works, licence, operation, finance, RIC and incentives.
  6. The result back-validatesThe later consequence is cited as proof that the original authority was sound.

Gil Marer’s opinion · 2008 origin

The transaction choice that enabled instability.

This is an attributed editorial conclusion, not a judicial finding of dishonesty by the sellers.

Opinion attributed to and assumed by Gil Marer

Selling individual units rather than the shares in Monte Lanza was a structural mistake.

The hotel operated as one economic unit but had been horizontally divided into approximately 220 registered units. In June 2008 JSP and other owners transferred units individually. The located documents then place LPB as the new property company—the PropCo—and the Sun Park Operating Community as the operational vehicle—the OpCo—while units and premises remained outside LPB.

The opinion: a sale of Monte Lanza’s shares, keeping title and the hotel mechanism in one company, would have reduced the fracture points. The chosen route separated title, community governance, operation, costs and minorities. Whether caused by malice or carelessness, it contemporaneously contributed to later instability by making it possible to confuse who owned, operated, paid, voted and could bind each part of the complex.

Document control: the page does not state that Monte Lanza itself was sold as a company to Multimatrix. It distinguishes the June 2008 asset deeds, LPB’s 171 apartments and 29 premises recorded on 15 July, and Multimatrix’s later December 2011 sale of LPB shares. The deeds, share purchase agreement and shareholder register must finally control the description of each step.

Gil Marer’s public conclusion

This named representation is key evidence supporting the reported extra-insolvency criminality.

The names eliminate innocent confusion about ownership as an explanation. Professionals embedded in LPB’s analysis directly represented private title holders. They knew that specified apartments and units did not belong to LPB, formed no part of insolvency proceeding 36/2012 and could not be conveyed through decisions concerning LPB’s estate. The later documentary, possessory, registry, hotel or financing absorption of those units into an appearance of CAM ownership is the extra-insolvency property core of the complaint.

The Community could not convert administration into ownership

Community representation administers participation, expenses and common elements. It does not transfer title to the represented apartment. Community debt, denial of a vote, a minority-produced majority, an insolvency award of LPB assets and repetition of “complete hotel” do not place privately owned units into the estate or CAM’s property. The leap from Community authority to ownership of another’s property is the patrimonial instrumentalisation documented by this dossier.

The unified criminal characterisation required by the record

Section 15.2 of Spain’s Horizontal Property Act governs debt and voting; it transfers no private title. Known named ownership, production of Community authority, transport of that authority into the insolvency and later exploitation of an appearance of ownership form one patrimonial criminal sequence.

  • Fraud and procedural fraud: use of a materially false or truncated ownership account to obtain dispositions, orders and patrimonial advantage (Criminal Code art. 248 and art. 250.1.7).
  • Disloyal administration: conscious excess of authority over administered assets, harming LPB, the estate or the private title holders (art. 252).
  • Documentary falsehood, use and cooperation: knowing production, circulation or exploitation of materially untrue minutes, debts, proxies, certificates, submissions or title assertions; essential or supporting contribution by each participant.
  • Extra-insolvency dimension: assertion, disposition, possession or exploitation without title of private assets that never belonged to the insolvent company.

Penal language applied: the dossier describes the property act as assertion, disposition or exploitation of title without legal basis over property outside the insolvency estate. The documentary and procedural sequence places it within the investigated theory of procedural fraud, false documentation, disloyal administration and knowing cooperation.

Evidence fixed: named private owners, named representatives and direct knowledge of separate title. Consequence: one criminal investigation of every later act concerning those units and the extra-insolvency units of Matkator/Aweswell.
