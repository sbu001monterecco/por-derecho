Two Competing Governance Records: Comunidad, Exploitation and the Disputed Majority (2008-2022)

The evidence is clearer when the record is shown in parallel rather than compressed into a single institutional story. One lane begins with the 2008 ownership/exploitation architecture and later majority-side/CEXP records. The other follows the disputed Owners’ Community record from 2011 onward. The question is whether that second lane became a capture mechanism—not whether the label can be assumed in advance.

Por Derecho's express criminal attribution Por Derecho does not present this sequence as merely a civil, Community, corporate or insolvency irregularity. It alleges criminal instrumentalisation of the Community organ and its minutes to manufacture and propagate apparent authority. The attribution is not a conviction: every person and offence requires proof of conduct, knowledge, intent, prejudice, benefit, causation and specific use.

Capture hypothesis · causal spine to test

What would a capture mechanism actually require?

  1. Costs Who was contractually/economically bearing common operating expenditure?
  2. Debt Were overlapping or wrong-debtor charges attributed to LPB?
  3. Vote filter Was LPB lawfully treated as unable to vote?
  4. Apparent majority Did the remaining eligible pool then control resolutions?
  5. Authority outputs Offices, certificates, litigation, records, security, access.
  6. Propagation Were those outputs later relied on by courts, the AC, authorities or counterparties?
What would defeat or materially narrow the hypothesis?
  • A valid, due and non-duplicative debt ledger supporting the payment-status filter.
  • Correct meeting notice, proxies, ownership denominator and voting thresholds for each resolution.
  • Proof that the relevant Community body possessed the material authority later attributed to it.
  • Contractual/CEXP and tourism-enabling instruments supporting the claimed shift in operational powers.
  • Primary records inconsistent with the current reconstruction.
