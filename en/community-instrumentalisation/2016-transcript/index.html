What it records—and what remains to be proved

The derivative transcript attributes a direct discussion of the complex’s economic and governance architecture to LPB advisers, two PwC professionals and people described as acting for the Community. It is a 2021 transcript of 2016 audio; source authenticity, speaker identity, attendance, capacity, timestamps and wording still require verification against the native files.

Identity and date control: Las Palmas, not the Princesa Yaiza

The contemporaneous scheduling chain, the 10 June 22:45 reference to “today’s meeting” and the original 10JUN2016 AMR filenames place this working meeting on Friday 10 June 2016 in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. The later derivative transcript is labelled 11JUN2016; that label and the established route are retained as version history, not as the occurrence date. The transcript attributes participation to Carlos Saavedra, Miguel Hernández, Cristo Pimentel, Jonathan Simó, FMMM and Juan Carlos Prieto, but authentication and the attendee/capacity map remain open. This was separate from the Community General Meeting held on 26 April 2016 at the Hotel Princesa Yaiza in Playa Blanca. The minutes, attendees, private-owner proxies and voting result belong to the Princesa Yaiza General Meeting, not to this recording.

Attributed by the transcript

The derivative transcript attributes participation to Carlos Saavedra and Miguel Hernández and describes them as acting for PwC Tenerife in representation of LPB. It attributes discussion of operator payments, owner fees, debt, account access, voting and the need to reconstruct each category. Native-audio authentication and exact capacity remain open.

Inference requiring an answer

PwC UK and PwC Spain’s later receipt of the CNMV/RIC package makes it difficult to present the conflict as institutionally unknown. The live question is how prior knowledge, independence and conflicts were handled.

Not yet proved

The public record does not prove Carlos joined any later Acosta Matos/RIC engagement or that PwC or Carlos deliberately performed a “whitewash”. Internal records are needed before that can be stated as fact.

Gil Marer’s conclusion, stated plainly

Gil Marer alleges that PwC’s later professional assistance, legitimating effect and public proximity helped “normalise” or “whitewash” the Acosta Matos perimeter despite accumulated knowledge. That is an attributed allegation, not a judicial finding. The chronology makes it testable; engagement files, conflict checks, staffing, billing and work product will determine whether it is correct.
