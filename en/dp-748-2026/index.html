Executive digest

The clearest 90-second reading

The 4 February complaint advanced two central propositions: ETJ 163/2020 was said to have been pursued against Matkator even though Aweswell was alleged to be the true professional-services client/debtor, and enforcement was said to have continued over a registered unit after substantial physical transformation within MYND Yaiza. The 16 March enlargement expanded that theory through evidence concerning the hotel refurbishment, functional integration, physical/registry/cadastral divergence, a corporate transfer and public financing. After the 24 March provisional dismissal, the 17 April reform and subsidiary appeal added eight documents and identified dated notice and later enforcement acts. The Fiscal asked for partial allowance because the first order lacked reasons, but criticised the presentation as complex and demanded clearer facts. The 25 June filing responded with eight factual blocks and eight finite enquiries. The 16 July order accepted the original reasoning defect but maintained dismissal, saying the exact act, concrete properties and minimum criminal indicium were not sufficiently specified and treating much of the dispute as belonging in civil enforcement. The order is not final, expressly provides a five-day appeal from the last notification, and preserves reopening with further indicia.

Controlled procedural lineage

The relationship is documentary and functional, not a merger of jurisdictions. Cambiario 1048/2019 is the civil origin; ETJ 163/2020 contains the enforcement acts; DP 748/2026 asks whether independently investigable criminal conduct occurred in connection with that enforcement.

Evidential boundary. Refurbishment, financing, tourism registration or administrative records do not by themselves prove deception, knowledge or criminal intent. Their stated purpose was to enable objective verification of the properties’ physical and formal correspondence.

What the 16 July order actually decided

Original defect acceptedThe judge accepted that the complainant and Fiscal were right that the 24 March order lacked reasons.
Dismissal maintainedReform was allowed only on that issue; reasons were added and provisional dismissal ratified.
Civil/criminal boundaryOrdinary ETJ objections were treated as matters for the civil enforcement court rather than general criminal review.
Insufficiency findingThe exact act, concrete property and minimum indicium of criminality were considered insufficiently specified.
Not finalThe operative part provides a five-day appeal from the day after the last notification.
Reopening preservedThe order expressly records that provisional dismissal may be reopened with other indicia.

The decision does not make a final finding that no offence could exist; it holds that the presentation before it did not sufficiently justify continuing the investigation.

  • It did not decide Cambiario 1048/2019 or ETJ 163/2020 as a civil appellate court.
  • It did not permanently close the criminal route: appeal and reopening remain expressly relevant.
Boundary. Recording unresolved questions is not a finding that the complainant’s theory is true; it only defines the scope of a provisional order.

Appeal and reopening are different routes

A · Appeal

The 17 April subsidiary appeal is verified and the served order again expressly provides a five-day appeal. The core task is to cite the existing file precisely and show that acts, assets, indicia and finite enquiries were already sufficiently identifiable before 16 July.

Structure: identity; admissibility/time; reasoning defect; filed particularisation matrix; unaddressed finite enquiries; civil/criminal separation; proportionate relief.

Deadline: no final date is published because the order counts from the last notification. Counsel must verify the complete service record.

B · Potential reopening

If dismissal becomes final, the safer route is not repetition of the same corpus but genuinely new or distinct indicia closing the links identified by the order.

Minimum package: certified ETJ record; updated deeds/registry notes; expert finca-to-room overlay; signed filings and dates; proof of receipt/knowledge; later act; judicial effect; adjudicatee/assignee identity.

A “new” document must be material and legally usable, not only a rhetorical restatement.
