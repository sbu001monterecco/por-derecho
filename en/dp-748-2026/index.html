What the 16 July order actually decided

Original reasoning defect acceptedThe judge expressly accepted that the complainant and Ministerio Fiscal were correct that the 24 March dismissal lacked reasoning.
Dismissal nevertheless maintainedThe court supplied new reasoning and ratified the provisional dismissal and archive.
No criminal review of the civil executionThe order treats ordinary objections to the execution as matters for the civil enforcement court and refuses to use the criminal proceeding as a general mechanism to review ETJ decisions.
Reopening expressly preservedThe order states that the provisional dismissal could lead to reopening if further indicia are supplied.

The court records that the provisional dismissal “could lead to the reopening of the proceeding with contributions of other indicia.”

What the order did not decide

  • It did not order a sobreseimiento libre.
  • It did not acquit Cuatrecasas or any individual.
  • It did not hold that falsedad or estafa procesal could never arise from conduct associated with an enforcement proceeding.
  • It did not determine as a proven fact that the physical, cadastral or registry reality of the relevant properties was correctly represented at every stage.
  • It did not determine that every reported participant lacked prior knowledge of any material discrepancy.
  • It did not close the case permanently: the judge expressly preserved reopening with further indicia.
Boundary. These are descriptions of the scope of the judicial decision, not findings that criminal conduct occurred.

The judge's three stated deficiencies

1 · Exact actThe court says the exact conduct attributed to the reported party was not sufficiently specified.
2 · Concrete propertyThe court says the particular assets/fincas potentially affected were not sufficiently identified.
3 · Minimum criminal indiciumThe court says a minimum indicium of criminality attributable to the reported conduct was not presented with sufficient specificity.
Fourth practical condition: autonomous criminal conduct. Any appeal or reopening package must show why the issue is an independently investigable alleged act of deception, falsity or knowing procedural use—not merely disagreement with a civil ruling or an attempt to relitigate ETJ 163/2020.

Two distinct remedial routes

A · Appeal now

The 17 April subsidiary appeal is source-verified. Under Article 766.4 LECrim, where an appeal was filed subsidiarily with reform and the reform is totally or partially rejected, the appellant is given five days to formulate allegations and, where appropriate, submit supporting documents before transfer to the other parties.

Appellate question: were the supposedly missing exact acts, properties and minimum indicia already identifiable in the complaint, ampliaciones, June synthesis or annexes; and were finite investigative measures available before closing the case?

The exact post-reform processing of the subsidiary appeal, any Article 766.4 traslado, admission/remission and any appellate roll remain under primary-source verification. No appellate roll is asserted here.

B · Reopening

The judge expressly preserved reopening with further indicia. Once a provisional dismissal is final, the safer legal architecture is to rely on genuinely new or distinct relevant indicia, rather than merely asking the court to reassess the identical evidential set.

Reopening question: what new document, registry material, certified ETJ act, property evidence, witness evidence or knowledge evidence now supplies the missing connection between actor, act, property, knowledge and procedural effect?

Reopening and appeal are related but not interchangeable. Evidence developed now can support the appeal if it was already effectively before the court, or a later reopening if it is genuinely new and legally usable.

Appeal-standard audit

The appeal analysis is different from the reopening analysis. It must first test the judge's factual premise against what was already before the court on 16 July 2026.

  1. Exact act audit: identify every passage in the complaint/ampliaciones/June synthesis that already attributed a dated act to a particular reported participant.
  2. Exact property audit: identify every finca/property reference and every annex that made the affected asset objectively identifiable.
  3. Minimum-indicia audit: identify every contemporaneous source offered to prove knowledge, inconsistency, continued procedural use or material effect.
  4. Finite-diligence audit: identify each concrete investigative step proposed before dismissal and whether the court gave a specific answer to it.
  5. Criminal/civil boundary audit: isolate the alleged autonomous act from the separate question whether the civil enforcement court decided correctly.

Current legal framework

Article 641 LECrimProvides for provisional dismissal where commission of the offence is not duly justified, or where an offence appears to have occurred but there are insufficient grounds to accuse identified persons.
Article 766 LECrimAllows reform and appeal against appealable investigating-court orders; appeal may be filed subsidiarily with reform. Article 766.4 governs the allegations stage after total or partial rejection of reform where appeal was already filed subsidiarily.
STC 3/2019Constitutional doctrine explains that reopening after a sufficiently substantive final provisional dismissal is conditioned by new relevant indicia, rather than mere reconsideration of the same evidential set.
Investigation sufficiencyConstitutional case law also examines, case by case, whether a provisional dismissal was premature where concrete, useful and relevant investigative steps had been proposed but not reasonably addressed.
