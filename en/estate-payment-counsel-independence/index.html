Who paid LPB’s lawyer?

01 / SEQUENCE

Exit → sale → defence → fee

The ONA/Clubotel operating terms were signed on 6 June 2018. The owner side had developed buyer and finance routes, with closing steps still open. The administrator separately sold facilities to CAM on 28 November 2018 and reported a buyer cheque into an LPB account he controlled. LPB sought the deed through a filed request on 10 January 2019, and the court refused convalidation on 24 October. The administrator’s 21 July 2021 paid-credit report lists €26,750 for former LPB counsel Juan Tomás Parrilla as paid in July 2020.

Gil’s unitary allegation: a functional adverse mechanism, not legal identity. Gil alleges that, in the decisive 2018–2020 window, private-purchaser interest, the AC’s control of estate information, assets and payments, and the separate judicial protection/remedy gate operated together in practical effect to neutralise LPB’s independently instructed representation during the owner-funded exit and its immediate defence after 7 June 2018. This does not say that the actors became one legal person, that the judge was a payer or payee, or that association alone proves culpability. It is a joined causal case: each actor’s notice, duty, power, act or omission, knowledge and effect must be tested individually, and any coordinating mechanism must be proved with primary sources. Counsel’s protective work remains part of the record; the allegation concerns material windows and mechanisms of effect.
02 / PROSECUTORIAL AND PROFESSIONAL INQUIRY

Why the payment relationship matters

The concern would remain if the amount were smaller or larger. Each question below requires its own evidence and legal assessment.

Client authority

Client-side correspondence asked whether this was the same insolvency-cost claim, requested the AC–counsel record and raised continuity of work. Determine its legal effect, who held the claim and who directly authorised any allocation to counsel.

Claim-holder versus payee

Contemporaneous advice described the insolvency-cost claim as belonging to client-side funders rather than automatically to the lawyer or procurador. Reconcile that position with the fee allocation, invoice, IGIC treatment, service and authority.

Loyalty to LPB

A lawyer’s instructions and judgment should serve LPB. Determine whether payment discussions with an actor adverse to LPB influenced the advice, litigation choices or silence.

Administrator’s dual position

The AC controlled estate payments while LPB and the AC had opposed positions in litigation. That structure calls for scrutiny of his legal basis, motives and safeguards.

Estate property and creditor priority

A charge to the estate affects LPB and creditors. Identify the services, invoice, classification as a claim against the estate and approval or challenge route.

Incentive and dependence

The prospect of payment can matter before cash changes hands. Fix each proposal and decision date and test whether counsel expected money controlled by the AC.

Timing and protective work

Compare payment discussions with each dated request, appeal, warning and missed opportunity concerning the ONA exit and hotel facilities; retain counsel’s protective work.

Adverse beneficiary

CAM bought the facilities; the AC reported receipt into LPB’s controlled account. Investigate any connection among buyer benefit, administrator decisions and counsel fee without assuming a direct CAM-to-lawyer payment.

Confidentiality and instructions

Check whether privileged strategy, client instructions or settlement positions reached a party whose interests diverged from LPB’s; no breach is established by a payment entry.

Conflict disclosure and consent

Test what counsel told LPB about the payer, fee basis and potential conflict, whether any informed consent was available and whether withdrawal or independent advice was required.

Causal loss

Identify how a specified act or omission changed a defined opportunity to protect the hotel as a unit, challenge the sale or pursue the developed exit; do not assume the exit was guaranteed.

Judicial treatment

Judicial protection and remedy are a distinct causal gate. Test exact notice, power, request, signed act or omission and effect. The judge’s own acts have a separate evidence lane; no record here shows that he ordered or received the fee.

Criminal and professional inquiry

If evidence shows dishonest diversion of estate funds, a bought change of loyalty, collusion or purposeful concealment, the elements and individual knowledge should be tested. A fee entry itself does not establish bribery or a crime.

Proof boundary

The 2018 buyer cheque and the reported July 2020 fee are distinct entries. No executed fee allocation, direct authorisation or full AC–counsel communications record is supplied in this reader. The matched bank debit and credit, invoice, fee mandate, IGIC treatment, actual services, earlier incentive, each actor’s knowledge and causal effect remain to be verified. LPB could in principle lawfully pay its own lawyer from estate funds; the issue is whether this particular arrangement met the duties and benefited LPB.
