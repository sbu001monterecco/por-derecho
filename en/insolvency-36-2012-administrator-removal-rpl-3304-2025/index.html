SEVEN PLEADED BLOCKS · DETAILED SUMMARY

What the application alleged and why each block was advanced as a removal ground

This is a detailed public-safe synthesis of material already controlled in the specialist Administrator file. Each block describes Aweswell's allegations, not findings made by a court.

PARTY ALLEGATION

Accounts / rendition of accounts

What was allegedThe application alleged prolonged failures to prepare and file annual accounts after LPB's powers had been suspended. Among the materials cited was a 20 March 2025 court-office record that did not locate an exemption order or the accounting documents at the point searched.

Why it was advanced as a removal groundAweswell framed this as an ongoing accountability and traceability issue affecting the estate's ability to know its accounting position, and coupled it with a request for rendition of accounts and related institutional communications.

PARTY ALLEGATION

Financial risk / swap

What was allegedThe application alleged inaction and later obstruction concerning the 2008 financial-risk-management contract. According to the pleading, that contract had caused more than €500,000 of loss and could support an estate claim or recovery route.

Why it was advanced as a removal groundThe point was not merely that a swap existed, but that the Insolvency Administration allegedly should have examined and, where appropriate, protected a potential estate claim. That remains an allegation requiring confrontation with the contract, decisions taken and contrary evidence.

Credit-assignment price / retract

What was allegedAweswell alleged obstruction or insufficient access to the true price and full terms of the credit assignment. The pleading linked that information to assessing remedies, litigation, negotiation and estate strategy, including analysis of retract where legally applicable.

Why it was advanced as a removal groundThe application argued that failure to obtain or disclose the full economics of the assignment could prevent informed decisions about the liability and alternatives to liquidation. Dación en pago / fiscal valuation

What was allegedThe application challenged what it characterised as insufficiently critical acceptance or defence of a transfer/adjudication transaction, raising valuation, perimeter, authority and estate-protection questions.

TWO ADVERSE DECISIONS · MERITS NOT RESOLVED BLOCK BY BLOCK

What the first-instance decisions actually decided

Order 1377/2025 · 12 September 2025

The court accepted the Administrator's standing objection: it treated the former Monterecco/Aweswell credit entered at €0 as an exclusion and considered that Aweswell had not challenged the provisional list. The application was rejected expressly without needing to enter the substantive grounds.

It decided: Aweswell's standing in that application.

It did not decide: a comprehensive merits adjudication of accounts, the swap, assignment price, dación, the hotel unit, remuneration or conflicts.

Decision of 11 November 2025

It confirmed the refusal, addressed reconsideration arguments from LPB and Aweswell and left open the non-suspensive appeal route. It added an ex officio assessment that remains relevant contrary material, but it likewise did not contain an act-by-act documentary adjudication of the seven pleaded blocks.

Methodological consequence: “dismissal/refusal” describes the first-instance procedural outcome; it does not by itself prove every alleged fact false. Conversely, the absence of a block-by-block merits decision does not prove the allegations true.

LPB ALSO APPEALS

The insolvent company is not “on the sidelines” of the removal issue

LPB filed its own challenge to the refusal. Its appeal is RPL 3304/2025. That means the insolvent company itself is challenging the first-instance decision. It does not automatically validate every Aweswell allegation, but it prevents the matter from being portrayed as an extinguished unilateral Aweswell initiative.

The Master Register distinguishes GC-APP-005 / PD-SP-R-0011^ for LPB and GC-APP-006 / PD-SP-R-0012^ for Aweswell. GC-APP-007 / PD-SP-R-0013 is a continuity family reference, not a third appeal.

SCRUTINY OF JUDICIAL CONDUCT

The judicial organ is also part of the contested procedural history

This cannot be presented as a dispute isolated between Aweswell and the Insolvency Administrator. Judicial decisions, alleged omissions, timing and treatment of the removal application form part of the appealed procedural itinerary. They must be separated into: what the Administrator did or allegedly failed to do; what Aweswell requested; what LPB challenges; what the court decided; what reasoning or acts are challenged; and what remains for the Provincial Court.

Legal qualification: including judicial conduct does not presume criminal guilt or replace a judicial determination of responsibility. It prevents treating the court as an external observer when its decisions and procedural treatment form part of the documentary sequence under appeal.
