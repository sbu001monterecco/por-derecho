THE BRIDGE THAT MUST BE PROVED

8 February 2018 → 4 June 2018: €9.052.251,69 versus €13.165.832,36

8 February 2018 · formal modification

Following an IA request and opposition by LPB and Yaiza Town Council, the judge ordered the definitive texts amended to substitute Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. for Promontoria Holding 122 B.V.

Identified specially secured claims: €857,373.81 + €8,194,877.88 = €9,052,251.69, plus contingent enforcement costs without a self-standing fixed amount.

Decisive judicial rule: the assignment changes the holder, not the amount; the amount must remain what was recorded in the definitive texts.

Route: former Ley Concursal Article 97 bis. The order stated a 20-day appeal period.

4 June 2018 · liquidation-plan clarification

LPB asked for the IA first to calculate the updated specially secured claim. Instead, the order relied on two 17-May CAM balance-fixing instruments and stated those amounts should be used without first requiring an IA calculation.

Operative amount: €11,887,314.33 + €1,278,518.03 = €13,165,832.36, plus later default interest within the limit of the respective real security.

The order itself presents the act as a clarification of the 16-April order and says no appeal lies against the clarification under LEC 214.4. It is not verified as an insolvency incident or as a formal order amending the definitive texts.

Declared Gil Marer/Aweswell position. It is categorically disputed that the 4-June clarification was legally capable, by itself, of converting the approximately €9.052m definitive-text amount into an approximately €13m definitive claim or insolvency payoff/set-off amount.

The competing lawful route must also be tested

Former Ley Concursal Article 59 is a serious counterargument: interest on claims secured by real security could continue to the extent covered by that security. The audit therefore must not assume that every difference is necessarily unlawful. It must reconcile, mortgage by mortgage: principal, ordinary interest, default interest, mortgage-liability ceiling, exigibility, calculation date, payment/set-off and — separately — whether the definitive creditor-list amount was formally amended.

Definitive-text gate. Por Derecho will not record a lawful change merely because a different figure appears in a balance certificate, IA calculation, plan/clarification order, deed, testimony or accounting entry. Required: application + legal ground + IA report + transfer/opposition + incident where the applicable route requires it + signed modifying decision + exact before/after entry + notification/remedy/finality + corrected list/inventory or equivalent implementation binding.

Owners’ Community: important, but the label is not forced

The primary order currently controlled is the 20 January 2017 definitive-text modification under Article 97 bis: LPB sought correction/objected, the IA reported favourably, the report was transferred and the located order records no opposition; the judge expressly amended the treatment of the Community's contingent ordinary pre-insolvency fee credit. This is an important comparator because it shows a formal modification route. We do not relabel it an “insolvency incident” without a separate primary incidental piece, claim/admission and decision proving that route.

Why the other material acts are not automatically put in the incident list

Former Article 192 LC made the incident a residual channel for questions for which the Ley Concursal prescribed no other procedure. Thus: 4 June is clarification/plan; plan approval is liquidation; reposición/appeal/nullity are remedies; qualification has its own statutory section; ordinary filings and IA reports are section acts; the 2021–22 improvement/adjudication chain is realisation/implementation; testimonies are certification/finality; later clarifications follow their own route. Excluding them from the incident list does not reduce their criminal, evidentiary or causal importance.

PERMANENT GOVERNANCE

Black-letter law + adversarial review of judge, IA and private perimeter

Declared position / evidentiary limit. Gil Marer/Aweswell alleges multiple potentially unlawful acts and omissions by the insolvency judge across the proceeding — not one isolated incident and not only the 4-June order — including lack of neutrality, asymmetric treatment, possible institutional shielding/neutralisation and potential judicial prevaricación. Complaint/communication history also exists before the CGPJ and other routes. That history is linked as allegation context; it does not itself prove guilt.

1. Dry law

First identify the exact legal route, jurisdiction, prerequisites, contradiction, remedies, limits and effect of the act.

2. Adversarial lens

Then test: knowledge, route substitution, consistency with prior decisions, selection of evidence/calculations, equality of arms, loss of review, beneficiary/harm, estate/third-party protection, causation, repetition, competing lawful explanation and subjective element.

A prevaricación hypothesis requires far more than an adverse or erroneous decision: the analysis must address the alleged injustice of the resolution and the knowledge required by the criminal offence. The chronology must also preserve protective/corrective decisions that may weaken that hypothesis.
