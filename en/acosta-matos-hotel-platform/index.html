PUBLIC-INTEREST VISUAL · QUESTIONS, NOT CONCLUSIONS

12 hotels. 2,500 rooms. More than €100m in hotel sales.

The headline attracts attention. The accounting and corporate distinction matters more: commercial volume generated by hotels under management is not the same metric as the management company’s own turnover.

Two different metrics

COMPANY SELF-DESCRIPTION

12 · 2,500 · +€100m

Canarian Hospitality states that it moved from launch and its first hotel in 2021 to 12 hotels, 2,500 rooms and more than €100m in sales in 2025.

CEO COMMENTS REPORTED BY CINCO DÍAS

44 → ~50 → 60m

Francisco Fernández reported the management company’s own turnover as €44m in 2024, around €50m in 2025, with a forecast of €60m in 2026.

Not the same thing

The +€100m figure is presented as sales generated by the managed hotel portfolio. The €44m/€50m/€60m series is presented as the management company’s turnover. Adding them would create double counting or, at minimum, combine different accounting perimeters. This page keeps them separate.

A “platform” does not mean common ownership

The term is used here for an ecosystem of differentiated relationships. Every arrow requires its own source and date.

Ownership / equity

Asset title, owner SPV, shareholder or co-investor. A governance role does not automatically become ownership.

Management / operation

Management agreement, lease, hotel operation or service provision. Managing does not mean owning.

Brand / franchise

MYND, Sholeo, Radisson, Marriott or another brand may add distribution and reputation without owning the property.

Capital / RICPE / RIC

Equity, debt, structured finance, subscription and tax-incentive materialisation must be distinguished by instrument and beneficiary.

Construction / development

Designing, developing, refurbishing or building a hotel is distinct from title and operation.

Mandate / advice / media

An adviser, introducer, authority, journalist or outlet may provide access or visibility. Association does not prove knowledge or agreement.

Credibility, “reputation whitewashing” and instrumental inclusion

Accumulated credibility

Trading hotels, international brands, professionals, capital, mandates and media coverage may create a credibility stack capable of opening further doors. Each use and later benefit requires proof.

“Reputation whitewashing”

This is a project-side analytical and rhetorical lens: it asks whether positive associations can displace or obscure a disputed history. Concealment intent is not attributed without independent evidence.

Predatory inclusion

This describes the hypothesis of including reputable third parties in order to borrow their authority. The presence of a brand, adviser, investor or journalist does not establish knowing participation.

Authority ratchet

The sequence to test is: initial asset or foothold → credibility → access → mandate → capital → further asset or opportunity → traceable later benefit.

Coverage / cover

The first meaning is verifiable media reporting. The second is a critical metaphor for reputational cover. No media coordination is alleged without specific evidence.

Right of reply

People and entities referred to may provide contracts, accounts, ownership structures, sources of funds, corrections or contrary context for inclusion in the record.

Questions, not conclusions

  1. Which hotels or properties were owned by the perimeter at each date, and which were only managed, built, financed or associated?
  2. What exactly does “more than €100m in sales” mean: hotel revenue, gross volume under management, consolidated revenue or another metric?
  3. Who were the investors, subscribers, RIC taxpayers/materialisers, co-investors, financiers and intermediaries in each project?
  4. How was the credibility of Sun Park/MYND Yaiza, later hotels, brands, advisers or media used to obtain access, mandates, capital or further assets?
  5. Is there a financial trace from the originating asset through fees, operating flows and financing to later acquisitions?
