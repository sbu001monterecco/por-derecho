NewLawWhistleblowers · judicial protection · insolvency

A protected report and a court filing are different; the duty to react remains verifiable

The review separates two legal planes. The 2021 filings entered the insolvency case, met opposition and were later held judicially irrelevant: there was a procedural response, not simple silence. That does not identify a substantive reconciliation of title, third-party units, promotion and estate risk, or locate any Article 40.1 LEC referral to the Public Prosecutor or Article 82 TRLC supervision report.

The current Law 2/2023 regime protects qualifying reports made with reasonable grounds to believe the information true, prohibits obstructing or delaying follow-up and protects public disclosure in defined circumstances, including a low prospect of effective treatment because an authority may be in collusion. Canary Islands Decree 91/2024 permits up to six months only for special complexity and requires immediate referral to the Spanish or European Public Prosecutor where appropriate. Failure to respond within that regime would be a verifiable procedural failure; it would not prove collusion by itself.

NewDocumentary chainNotice · title · public exposure

Prior notice, surviving Matkator title and current public exposure

The 13 January 2021 communications to CNMV and AEAT, the 11 February insolvency filing and the 28 February public warning document that objections concerning Sun Park–RICPE title, control and marketing predated adjudication. In 2026 CNMV confirmed supervisory actions and that its material did not contain two contradictory July 2021 records: CAM acknowledged only 54 units and RICPE broke down 262 properties, including 18 third-party properties, with no letter of intent or full due diligence.

Matkator’s 2015/2016 deeds and January 2025 Land Registry notes place full title to properties 8584 and 8588 in Matkator; the 2022 insolvency deed did not transfer them. Hotel operations and the official reference to EU co-financing continue. Without an audited property–use–consent–income map, the proprietary, tax, investor and public-funds exposure is current. Bounded conclusion: the available record requires a “no” answer to whether the integrated 2020 representation gave a complete and truthful title/control account; it does not by itself determine intent or criminal liability.
