ICAM / CCACM 2026: what was alleged, what was archived, and what actually remains open.

This page does not present bar complaints as proof of misconduct. It reconstructs the procedural sequence and separates what ICAM decided from what it did not decide.

Reading rule: a complaint is an allegation. An archive based on limitation or territorial competence is not a merits ruling in favour of the respondent. A pending appeal likewise does not mean the complaint will succeed.

Proceeding boundary

434/26 and CCACM 193/2026 are linked phases of one professional-conduct chain: the first-instance ICAM complaint/archive and its administrative appeal. ETJ 163/2020, DP 748/2026 and other court proceedings remain separate judicial files. Their facts may be evidential context, but they are not stages of 434/26 or 193/2026. Likewise, the later autonomous complaint 1487/26 and its technically generated 1566/26 registration must not be collapsed into the first 434/26 → 193/2026 chain.

The most important contribution of complaint 1487/26

The second complaint separates client · instructor · service beneficiary · invoiced entity · expected payer · promissory-note issuer · judicial debtor. That prevents formal existence of a promissory note from automatically answering the distinct professional-conduct question.

It also frames, as a hypothesis rather than a proven fact, a “progressive inversion of the professional mandate”: adviser with strategic knowledge of the perimeter → own-account recovery → current enforcement/adjudication/possible assignment steps against Matkator. The requested evidence sits largely inside the firm's own records: engagement files, billing, cause of the notes, conflict barriers, DMS access, recovery approvals and the ETJ decision chain.

Limit: the reviewed documents do not prove actual misuse of confidential information, coordination with CAM/HNT/MYND/RIC/Acosta Matos, existence of a connected assignee, or that Matkator could never have been a debtor in any sense.

The immediate procedural traceability gap

The 6 July appeal against archive 1487/26 was submitted through ICAM's portal, but the system registered it as a new complaint under file 1566/26. The same day, written correction was requested so filing RE-007622/26 would be treated as the appeal in 1487/26 and forwarded to CCACM while preserving the filing date.

The present scan has not located a later ICAM confirmation or a CCACM procedural order assigning a number to the second appeal.

The accurate status is therefore: appeal filed 6 July; technical registration as 1566/26; correction/forwarding requested; effective CCACM receipt still to be certified.
