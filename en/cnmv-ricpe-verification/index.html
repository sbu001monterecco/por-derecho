COMPETENCE

What CNMV can review and what remains in separate legal lanes.

Supervisory core

  • Project and investor information.
  • Governance, related parties, conflicts and recusals.
  • Due diligence, valuation, conditions, waivers and re-entry.
  • Approval, funding, monitoring, preservation and correction.
  • Reconciliation of new evidence with the earlier supervisory corpus.

Context, not substitution

  • Title, Registry, Community and insolvency: factual premises, not matters for CNMV to adjudicate.
  • RIC, Regional Incentives and ERDF: relevant reconciliation, but under separate authorities and rules.
  • Criminal law: acts, knowledge and intent require a separate route and elements.

OPEN KIMONO · INSPIRATION AND WARNING

Credible supervision shows the pathway; its opposite creates a black box.

Opening the file does not mean disclosing supervisory secrets or protected data. It means being able to explain what entered, what was checked, what was missing, what contrary evidence was considered, which unit acted and what real status the matter reached.

Best practice

A supervisable file

  • A question limited to CNMV competence.
  • Primary source and boundary for each proposition.
  • Knowledge attributed to specific records and people.
  • Any change in position explained contemporaneously.
  • Conflicts, recusals, versions and corrections visible.
  • Receipt, analysis and decision kept distinct.
Warning

Signs of the opposite practice

  • A preliminary presentation later treated as settled truth.
  • The final outcome used to justify the past.
  • Inconsistent figures silently normalised.
  • Referrals between bodies used as mutually validating seals.
  • Secrecy or silence converted into a merits conclusion.
  • No trace of who decided what.

Boundary: this list does not assert that CNMV or RICPE engaged in every negative practice. It offers a public, documentary and falsifiable test for the real file.

Series F/G control: Series F/G · suitability → separates the 2022–2023 administrative authorisation, source-specific amounts and use-of-funds questions without treating them as a fraud finding.
