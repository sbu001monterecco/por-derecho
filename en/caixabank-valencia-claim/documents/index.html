FILEDAWESWELL POSITION

Claim — dated/sent 11 October 2023

Aweswell Limited v CAIXABANK, S.A., JPI No. 27 Valencia, ORD 1859/2023-9. CaixaBank's later expert report describes filing on 13 October 2023.

The claim is procedurally unusual and important: Aweswell asserts creditor standing to pursue subsidiarily an estate claim associated with LPB where the Insolvency Administrator did not bring it. The case therefore cannot be understood without Concurso 36/2012.

Economic package alleged

  • 17-Jun-2008: €8.6m mortgage financing, later E12m +1.50%, 3.50% floor.
  • 17-Jun-2008: €5m financial-risk-management swap to January 2012.
  • 27-May-2010: refinancing/novation, second €850k facility and €405k pledge.
  • 2011–2012: account treatment, swap settlements, alleged arrears, acceleration and Bankia enforcement.
  • 4-Jun-2012: the claim links the resulting liquidity pressure to LPB's defensive insolvency filing.

CaixaBank's expert records total claimed loss of €1,124,445.84. Liability, causation, quantum and limitation are disputed.

The wider thesis is that this was an integrated economic package whose later legal components travelled down different routes: personal/product responsibility through Caja/BFA/Bankia→CaixaBank, and the mortgage asset through Bankia→SAREB→PH122→CAM.

FILED 29-JAN-2024CAIXABANK POSITION

CaixaBank defence

CaixaBank raises threshold defences before reaching the merits:

  1. Aweswell lacks sufficient standing for the subsidiary action;
  2. the insolvency treatment of the swap-related credit is final/preclusive;
  3. the nullity claim is out of time;
  4. contractual damages are prescribed; and
  5. the 2023 claim exceeds the approximately €596,123.75 damages action the Insolvency Administrator had accepted in 2021.

On the merits, CaixaBank says the contract warned of negative settlements, the LPB management perimeter was sufficiently sophisticated, consumer standards do not apply, and the structure served a hedging purpose.

Why the Insolvency Administrator matters to CaixaBank's defence

The defence does not merely mention the insolvency. It files and relies upon the Administrator's 2013 report and the 2021 authorization/position to support standing, preclusion/finality and scope arguments. That source dependence is a legitimate evidential reason to ask how the defence was built and why CaixaBank itself requested the Administrator's testimony in Valencia.

Boundary. Reliance on a filed insolvency record is entirely compatible with independent advocacy. It is not proof of coordination or collusion. The evidential question is provenance, communications, selection of material, expected testimony and consistency with the contemporaneous record.

FILED 18-OCT-2024CAIXABANK EXPERT

PKF ATTEST expert report — 17 October 2024

PKF argues that the swap was a standard hedging mechanism, that the 17-Jun-2008 forward curve did not predict the later rate collapse, and that under its methodology the expected annualized differential was slightly favourable to LPB. It disputes causation and documentation, and says the €405k pledge must be reconciled against loan balances if enforced.

It also repeats the important procedural boundary: the 2021 action accepted by the Insolvency Administrator was approximately €596,123.75, whereas the 2023 pleading claims a higher total and broader relief.

Aweswell's response position is that percentage or forward-curve analysis is insufficient without a single cash-flow ledger joining mortgage, floor, E12m, swap/E3m, refinancing, pledge, accounts, payments, enforcement and later recoveries.

INSOLVENCY COURT FILING

25 January 2021 — Administrator accepts a Bankia damages action

In a filing seeking extension of liquidation operations, Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte told the Las Palmas insolvency court that LPB and Aweswell had asked the Administration to bring a Civil Code art. 1101 damages action against Bankia in relation to the 17-Jun-2008 swap.

He identified an approximate claim of €596,123.75 and stated that after receiving a viability report the Administration had accepted bringing the claim because, if successful, it could obviously benefit the insolvency estate. Aweswell was to fund the process, including a possible adverse costs order, and lawyers/procurador were to be retained by the Administration.

DOCUMENTARY RECORD

13 September 2023 — Administrator refuses procedural authority for the new filing

In an email later filed as documentary material, the Administrator said he had authorized the action almost three years earlier despite believing it legally weak, but in September 2023 refused authority to the new lawyers/procurador. He cited limitation/preclusion concerns, different counsel, absence of the bank guarantee he regarded as essential, lack of the full pleading and the advanced state of liquidation.

Aweswell's lawyers answered that the Administration had accepted the action in January 2021 but had not retained counsel to file it during the intervening period. This chronology explains why timing and subsidiary standing are live issues; it does not decide them.

2013 AC REPORT

The insolvency report CaixaBank brings into Valencia

CaixaBank uses the 2013 report to support arguments about a predecessor/Aweswell-related creditor position, LPB/group structure and the insolvency treatment of the bank's swap credit. One relevant entry records that the Administration did not recognize a predecessor credit because supporting documentation had not been delivered at that time.

The temporal question is important: a 2013 statement about documents then available is not automatically a complete description of Aweswell's later procedural position. Conversely, later developments do not erase what the Administrator recorded in 2013. A lawyer should ask what changed and what legal consequence follows.
