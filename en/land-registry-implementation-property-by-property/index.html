PRESENTATION → QUALIFICATION → ENTRY → CANCELLATION → CAM→HNT → LATER RIGHTS

Requesting an entry is not the same as obtaining it: the chain must be reconstructed property by property.

The Registry can publicise and protect rights satisfying the statutory conditions, but it does not remove the need to identify the causal title, the affected properties, the charges actually cancelled and the persons whose rights would be altered.

Evidential limit. A registered entry is legally significant and may produce strong effects. It does not by itself prove that every upstream economic or procedural fact was true, nor permit cancellation of later rights without consent, sufficient title or a decision against the necessary parties.

Finite questions for the Registry and title-holders

  1. Which exact title was presented for each property?
  2. Which presentation entry, qualification and cure exist?
  3. Which mortgages were cancelled, when and under which title?
  4. Which premises or unencumbered properties followed a separate chain?
  5. Which Matkator or third-party properties now appear within the HNT perimeter and under what title?
  6. Which later rights invoke independent protection and which remain legally or economically dependent on the earlier entry?
  7. Which parties must be joined to any rectification claim?

Strongest Registry defence

The Registry may maintain that it qualified formally sufficient judicial, notarial and corporate documents within its competence; that it could not reopen the merits determined by the Court; and that substantive rectification requires title-holder consent or a final judgment made with all affected parties participating.

What this page does not establish

It does not establish unlawful Registry qualification, knowledge of falsehood, automatic loss of HNT or third-party rights, or that upstream invalidity automatically cancels every later entry.
