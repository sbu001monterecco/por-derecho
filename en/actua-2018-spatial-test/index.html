Why this matters to the €400,000 disposal

The insolvency plan used an ACTÚA-derived figure of €489,361.96 for the 31-property branch later conveyed to CAM for €400,000. Those properties included reception/main access, premises and pools/solarium. GESVALT’s contrary methodology treated the 262 properties as one hotel operation. That does not make the 31 properties legally worth the whole-hotel €26.4 million figure; it does make hotel-wide operational loss a necessary part of any prejudice analysis.

Gil Marer’s criminal allegation: he says ACTÚA was materially false or non-representative and that the Insolvency Administrator knew, or consciously disregarded obvious reasons to know, that it was unsafe as a basis for the disposal. Gil characterises the latter possibility as wilful and criminal recklessness. The methodological defects and contrary material are documented. Knowledge, deliberate disregard, the applicable offence elements, causation and criminal purpose are not adjudicated and are submitted for prosecutorial investigation.

The Administrator later reported receipt of the €400,000 cheque, opening the LPB account and paying his own remuneration. The court then refused convalidation of the deed for lack of judicial authority. Gil now characterises the sale as criminal and the money as illicit proceeds; the dedicated page traces the later €26,750 former-counsel payment, the fee mismatch and the effect on defence continuity.
