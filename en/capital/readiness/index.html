PAP-aligned risk overlay

Readiness does not remove credit, recovery, project or legal-system risk.

The public site summarises the risk architecture; the controlling PAP and issue-specific materials must be read before any investment decision through a lawful private route.

Material risk

Issuer / repayment / recovery concentration

NewCo may initially depend on Sponsor support, project cashflow, refinancing or recoveries. Claims may fail or be delayed because of ownership, standing, limitation, merits, causation, quantum, procedure, appeal or collectability. Overlapping remedies cannot be counted twice.

Material risk

Historic institutional-treatment and spillover scenario

The Group has made formal, source-controlled allegations concerning historic Canary/Spain treatment. They remain unresolved allegations, not findings of corruption, collusion, prevaricación or institutional capture. Spillover, selective scrutiny, procedural friction or retaliation are risk scenarios to be mitigated, not predictions of unlawful conduct.

Material risk

UK / Spain jurisdiction and foreign-investor limits

Foreign-investor records strengthen provenance and cross-border analysis; they do not create immunity, State backing, an assumed UK–Spain BIT/ISDS route or automatic foreign jurisdiction. English-law financing cannot displace mandatory Spanish property, security, insolvency, corporate or public-law rules.

Total-loss risk

Capital may remain unpaid

Notes are not bank deposits. Private securities may be illiquid. Principal, cash return and PIK can remain unpaid; refinancing, sale, recovery proceeds or contingent participation may not materialise within the required time.

Mitigation architecture. Ring-fenced entities and cash, independent governance/counsel, deadline and service controls, rapid interim-relief preparedness, comparator and reliance evidence, permit-defensibility files, Registry protection where available, contract-specific forum/service/enforcement planning, evidence preservation, insurance/security testing, direct agreements/step-in rights, public-aid defensibility and institutional aftercare are designed to reduce risk and improve evidential/remedial position. None guarantees an outcome.
