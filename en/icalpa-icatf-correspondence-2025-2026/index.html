2. What was already said about rapporteurs, independence and pressure

In DIP 80 on 20 August 2026, Gil Marer expressly wrote that he did not seek improper pressure on the rapporteur or a predetermined sanction, but a human, competent, traceable and reasoned route. The same communication framed diligence, independence, impartiality, loyalty, integrity, prudence, transparency and traceability as core concerns.

In DIP 79 on the same date, he sought a general standard and wrote that the best protection for the independence of the profession and the right of defence is to have a clear rule before the situation arises.

The Tenerife record separately contains a documented allegation that the threat of using a Bar complaint may have been used as pressure against counsel in 2018. That supports institutional caution; it does not prove that pressure will be attempted against the Canary Council or a future rapporteur.

Proposed Canary Council rule

Request that any rapporteur/instructor be conflict-cleared in advance and have sufficient independence, availability, continuity and institutional backing to carry the matter to a reasoned outcome. If any call, informal approach, representation or material pressure outside the file is attempted by an interested or connected person, it should be logged and channelled into the formal record, avoiding non-traceable decision paths. The request does not presume such contact will occur.
