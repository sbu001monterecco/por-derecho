ONA does not appear in the record merely as an isolated bidder: the documents place it inside a coordinated funded-exit structure

This page reconstructs the documentary sequence before and after 7 June 2018 as one evidential chain. The central proposition is that ONA / Clubotel La Dorada, S.L.'s proposed operating and acquisition role was embedded in a wider strategy to determine the amount properly payable, finance payment or consignation to creditors and enable Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. to exit Insolvency Proceeding 36/2012.

Evidential limit: documented facts, attributed contemporaneous statements, party allegations and criminal-investigation hypotheses are kept separate. This is not presented as a conviction or as a jointly adopted witness statement by all named persons.

Before 7 June

  1. April 2018: the project record contains an ONA letter of intent and business plan.
  2. 26 April 2018: Carlos Sanz contacted the Insolvency Administrator directly about a proposed offer and requested the applicable terms, amount to improve and deadline.
  3. 27 April 2018: Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte did not provide the requested substantive commercial information and directed Carlos to seek formal appearance and request the information through the court.
  4. 6 June 2018: the record contains ONA industrial-lease documentation. The operating/acquisition and financing architecture was therefore materially developed before the following day's material-control event alleged by the project side.

The stronger evidential proposition is not that 7 June created the problem from nothing, but that it occurred against an already-developed exit structure that depended on control, access, income, due diligence, security and possession assumptions.

After 7 June

  1. Financing and exit work continued; the evidence does not support saying that 7 June automatically extinguished all financing.
  2. In September 2018 Carlos Sanz, Daniel Irigoyen, Cuatrecasas, Juan Tomás Parrilla and the LPB/Aweswell side worked on quantifying what was required to exit the insolvency process.
  3. Daniel Irigoyen contemporaneously reported that he had told the judge that financing was approved to exit by payment/consignation to creditors and sought a coordinated route with the court and Insolvency Administrator.
  4. Cuatrecasas correspondence discussed removing a subtle ONA reference from one filing. The proper inference is not that ONA was absent, but that its reference was deliberately omitted from that particular filing.
  5. In 2019 Clubotel La Dorada, S.L. sought formal appearance in Proceeding 36/2012 through Daniel Irigoyen and challenged the refusal. Its formal interest was framed as acquisition of Sun Park and access to the procedural information needed to participate.

Judge

The judicial role must be analysed decision by decision and omission by omission. Third-party accounts of conversations are not substitutes for court orders or minutes.

Criminal-investigation lens

The test is act or omission → knowledge → duty or transaction dependency → causal effect → intent indicators → benefit/downstream effect → contrary evidence → proof gap. For private adverse actors, the issue is whether any specific person knew of the funded exit and intentionally interfered with control, access, income, due diligence or closing for an unlawful purpose. For the Insolvency Administrator and judge, adverse outcomes or disputed omissions are not enough: duty, knowledge, causation and legally relevant mens rea must be proved actor by actor.
