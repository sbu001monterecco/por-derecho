Unitary dossier · 7 June 2018

Material takeover, exclusion and alleged insolvency overreach.

The evidence describes Sun Park as occupied and registered as active shortly before 7 June, with guests and service and maintenance activity. That does not determine the lawful operator at that date or prove that Pink or CEXP retained operation or possession. This record explains how authority limited to LPB spread materially over a complex of fragmented titles.

The decisive distinction

LPB was the debtor in Insolvency 36/2012. The whole Sun Park complex was not part of its estate.

The right question is not “who controlled the insolvency?” It is which property and powers were within Insolvency 36/2012, and which independent title permitted interference with each non-estate unit, the hotel operation, contracts, guests and common elements.

The alleged overreach begins where administration and supervision of LPB property became a practical bridge to a hotel that legally remained composed of different patrimonies and powers.

The insolvency did not absorb the hotel.

The estate could contain LPB properties and rights identified in the inventory. It did not sweep in Matkator’s or other owners’ properties, third-party rights or any surviving rights of the Comunidad de Explotación del Complejo Sun Park (CEXP); nor did it turn the Owners’ Community into a universal owner or operator. The existence and scope of CEXP rights in 2018 must be reconciled with the 2012 cession, Monterecco/Pink’s real operation and later CEXP minutes.

01 · insolvency estate

LPB and its assets

The insolvency administrator represented and administered LPB’s patrimony according to the procedural phase; the judge supervised and decided matters brought before the court. That authority required preservation and inventory, but did not create title to others’ property.

02 · operation

CEXP / provenance and scope

CEXP had its own collective provenance. But a signed CEXP–Monterecco notice of 1 February 2012 said it subrogated the activity and named Monterecco as the new operator; Monterecco/Pink obtained registration for 159 units and genuinely operated. No competent CEXP-body resolution or complete chain has been located proving transfer of the entire owner-derived mandate, third-party units or common elements. The 2014/2017 CEXP minutes support organisational continuity, not by themselves recovery of operation or possession in June 2018; Order 804 was adverse to CEXP’s asserted possession then.

03 · non-estate

Matkator and third parties

Their properties did not belong to LPB and could not pass through LPB’s liquidation. Credit, Community majority, security, keys or unitary operation do not replace a deed, consent and property-specific title.

04 · common elements

Owners’ Community

It administered expenses and common elements within its powers. No title has been located making it owner of the apartments or permitting it to dispose of the hotel business; a security mandate was not by itself an eviction, refurbishment or whole-site control mandate.

The 2008 origin explains the problem; it did not authorise a forcible solution.

The 2008 asset sale—which Gil Marer characterises as a breach of the agreed transaction architecture—left one hotel operation spread across multiple titles. Certain properties remained within the dissident perimeter connected to Montelanza/Molina. Their later proceedings were property-specific: they did not by themselves decide extinction or succession of every operating right or turn restitution of 18 units into authority over the whole hotel. Who held each right after 2012 is a separate question.

The continuity Gil alleges between those owners, representatives or successors and later acquisitions within the Acosta Matos perimeter is a proprietary hypothesis to be proved, not automatic criminal succession. Gil Marer says the 2018 exit proposal was intended to address fragmentation through an operator, financing, agreements and owner-by-owner treatment—not manufacture ownership through the Community or insolvency.

Contemporary control

The record does not show an empty or inactive complex on 7 June.

It needed investment and modernisation. That did not make it available for possessory self-help. This conclusion concerns the complex’s condition and activity; it is not a finding that Pink or CEXP was then the lawful operator.

2014 · technical programme

Integral redevelopment already studied

A Las Palmas architect developed measurements, three-/four-star refurbishment and expansion options, technical studies, and a basic/execution-project route conditional on licensing. The plan was staged to preserve operations.

2017 · paid execution

An earlier model apartment

A specialist Tenerife contractor prepared designs and renders and received a 30% payment—€1,923.43—with the transfer reference “Reforma Piloto SPL”. Works photographs and emails connect it to commercial preparation and a tour-operator filming visit.

2017–March 2018 · strategy

Phased works, without sacrificing a season

The contemporary instruction was explicit: refurbish “in phases” and avoid losing another tourism season. By early 2018 the objective was framed as a €1.5m repositioning to four-star level. That was a refurbishment objective, not a category already obtained.

23 May 2018 · Gesvalt inspection

Completed, occupied and registered as active

The independent ECO valuation issued on 7 June described the property as completed and occupied, in good condition without apparent structural deterioration, registered as operational, and with some rooms already updated. Mortgage value: €25,647,179.50.

Exact scope: the 2014 and 2017 materials prove technical planning, expenditure and partial execution; they do not prove the entire scheme was licensed or financed. The independent valuation rebuts the premise of an empty or inactive property fifteen days after inspection, but does not identify the operator or determine its authority.

How it worked

Operate, pay or deposit, refinance or sell—not stop the hotel.

The ONA contract contemplated continuity of staff, reservations and maintenance. Bridge finance was to lead to bank funding or the integrated Elaia/Lagune sale/takeout route. Preparation, authority and execution

Security for LPB was not a title to take the hotel.

On 18 May the insolvency administrator, representing LPB, asked that the Community retain security to control access and preserve areas. The minutes do not deliver possession to CAM or authorise exclusion of the onsite manager, guests, staff or persons asserting operational or possessory rights, entry into others’ property or complex-wide transformation.

7 June 2018 · material takeover by force at access points

No possession order appears. A forced lock, chains, replaced cylinders, uniformed control and exclusion do.

Documentary conclusion: an eyewitness testified under a duty of truth that he saw the coded lock broken, chains and padlocks fitted, cylinders replaced and entrances closed. He attributed operational instructions to Laura Acosta Matos and made clear that José Daniel Acosta Matos was not present that day.

16 April · Commercial Court 1

Future realisation, not possession

The order approved a transfer/sale route for LPB assets with publicity and competing offers. It did not order handover of the hotel, eviction, broken locks or operator replacement.

7 June · contemporary report

Entry formally contested

The same-day email reports that those arriving said they came to “take possession”, demanded keys, identified guests and told the onsite manager it was over. It is a contemporary account; police, CCTV and security logs must corroborate each act.

25 June · judicial testimony

Continuing exclusion

The onsite manager testified that access control kept him from his workplace from the 7th; he entered only with a notary and for a sanitation intervention. He said he could not retrieve tools and machinery.

8 June · contrary account

CAM allegedly entered only its units

A contemporaneous party note of a call attributed to the administrator said CAM entered only its own units and common areas. It was challenged immediately. That conflict requires a map of keys, properties, cameras and security incident reports.

Guests and residents

Access under private guards

Independent resident emails from June and July describe permanent guards, gate-level access decisions, lock removal, contacts routed to Acosta Matos and residents leaving. The complete guest, booking, luggage and refund record remains missing.

Staff and operation

De facto displacement, not judicial eviction

Judicial evidence supports exclusion of the onsite manager. Other communications report staff or operators absent or told to move. A universal expulsion should not be stated without payroll, rosters, statements and termination records person by person.

Maintenance

Pools, gardens and operation

Residents and the ONA visit described non-operational pools and unmaintained gardens. The 9 July images establish visible condition, not authorship. They must be tested against the earlier valuation, incident reports, orders, suppliers and exact control period.

Business and value

Bookings, goodwill and insolvency exit

The documented access control affected apartment access and due diligence by the proposed operator ONA; the alleged effect on bookings, goodwill and continuity requires PMS, banking and accounting evidence.

Fiduciary bridge and protection

Insolvency authority was critical precisely because it was limited.

The administrator had to preserve and represent LPB’s patrimony; the judge had to supervise the insolvency and decide applications. Neither was Sun Park’s universal manager. But that limit does not make them spectators where authority or a decision concerning LPB produces whole-hotel effects.

03Institutional result

Criminal dismissal did not create CAM title

Preliminary Proceedings 1132/2018 were provisionally dismissed on 20 August. Provincial Court Order 804/2018 rejected the appeals, treated the dispute as essentially civil and relied centrally on the administrator’s account and authority. Its reasoning was adverse to the criminally protectable possession CEXP asserted and treated Pink’s formal possession as delivered on 22 May 2017. It was not a civil or tourism-title trial and did not create CAM title.

“I allege that authority over LPB was converted into a bridge to non-estate control: private actors executed; the administrator enabled or failed to correct the overreach; and judicial protection did not stop it in time.” — Gil Marer

This is an attributed accusation subject to proof. No direct evidence has been located of a clandestine agreement, illicit benefit to the administrator or judge, or a decision establishing criminal intent.

Decisive evidence: original instructions, notice timeline, property-by-property inventory, measures requested and decided, ONA communications and a reconciled loss-and-benefit account.

From control to project

After the disputed 2018 control, evidence of pilot rooms emerged before LPB title passed in 2022.

The pilot does not establish by itself who commissioned it or with what authority. Its potential evidential significance arises from placing it between the 2018 material control, later party accounts of a whole-hotel proposal under “Lava Verde”, proposed complex-wide rollout and later investor representations.

What is not alleged

What does the pilot prove—and what does it not? Its installation was not unlawful merely because it occurred during a dispute. If it was wholly within a property validly owned and possessed by CAM, confined to its interior and supported by proper consent and planning authority, it could have been lawful. A connection to overreach can be made only by proving the unit and its 2019 owner, order, invoice and payer, plans and licence, access and keys, impact on common elements, and relationship to the whole-hotel project. Until then, Atilano’s emails are direct evidence that he stated a pilot had been manufactured and installed before LPB’s 2022 adjudication; by themselves they do not prove unlawfulness, the commissioning party or a connection to the whole-hotel project.

Unitary reading

From partial access to a financeable, operable hotel asset.

The allegation is not an assortment of incidents. It is a continuing conversion: credit and partial units → Community and insolvency authority → physical control → project and pilots → image of hotel availability → adjudication, refurbishment, finance and operation.

The alleged displacement includes property, possession, business and opportunity.

Gil Marer alleges that LPB lost value and a competitive insolvency exit; that CEXP or its participants lost the residual or reconstituted operating rights they assert, as well as customers, reservations and goodwill; that Matkator and other owners lost control or fruits of non-estate properties; and that Aweswell lost the opportunity to execute the exit platform. The later record documents, through different entities linked to the Acosta Matos project, material control, later LPB title, redevelopment, RIC finance, a regional incentive award and operation as MYND Yaiza. Attribution of each benefit requires an entity-by-entity account.

That does not permit asset value, credit face value, loans, incentive and revenue to be added as one gain. Net enrichment requires entity-by-entity consolidated accounting: actual credit price, debt extinguished, assets received, own capital, loans and repayment, incentive actually paid, related margins, rent, EBITDA, refinancing and distributions.
