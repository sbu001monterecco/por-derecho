7 JUNE 2018 · DIRECT CRIMINAL-ATTRIBUTION RECORD · UPDATED 24 AUGUST 2026

From secured creditor to alleged physical controller: who instructed, which acts enabled, and which rulings or omissions consolidated the result.

Gil Marer and Aweswell expressly allege that identified CAM / Acosta Matos and connected private actors—the five are Francisco Mario Matos Matas, Antonio Cogolludo Rojas, Shaila María Cogolludo Ramos, José Daniel Acosta Matos and Laura Patricia Acosta Matos—formed an actor-specific chain of instruction, coordination, authorisation, adoption or knowing implementation of the 7 June 2018 Sun Park control operation; that the insolvency administrator committed affirmative acts of request, selection, authorisation, enablement, approval or ratification together with legally equivalent omissions; and that Judge Alberto López Villarrubia issued resolutions and refusals or caused evidential closures, delays and omissions that Gil alleges knowingly frustrated or sabotaged a developed, finance-backed exit. These are direct criminal allegations supported by a multi-layer evidential corpus. They are not judicial findings.

The allegation is not confined to civil or insolvency irregularity and is not merely an investigative question.

It concerns the alleged deliberate conversion of secured credit, isolated-unit title and Community/security machinery into physical possession, management, whole-hotel project availability and later economic benefit. Gil further alleges that institutional acts and omissions enabled and consolidated that result and frustrated the exit alternative. The record supports a developed, financeable exit architecture; it does not establish unconditional cash, certain closing or guaranteed success.

Controlling boundary.

The attribution is direct. Evidence strength, contrary material, missing proof and adjudication remain separate. The record supports publishing and demanding investigation of the allegations, while the 2018 provisional dismissal and appellate confirmation remain prominent. Missing native instruction or mental-state evidence does not erase the allegation; the allegation does not establish guilt.

CREDITOR IN MATERIAL POSSESSION

“Lender in possession” is a functional comparison. Liability follows from acts, control and benefit.

Project Sun Rock alleges that CAM crossed from protection and enforcement of a secured credit into possession, direction, use and management of assets before formal adjudication. Spanish-law consequences must be derived from the actual acts—not from the English label.

Authority search result.

No instrument has been located in the reviewed corpus authorising CAM to take interim administration or possession under Article 690 of the Civil Procedure Act or equivalent insolvency authority. This is a corpus finding, not a categorical claim that no such instrument could exist. Mortgage security subjects an asset to enforcement; it does not itself transfer possession or permit appropriation merely by virtue of the security.

De facto / shadow administration

LPB company functions

The hypothesis is strongest where CAM-perimeter persons actually performed administrator functions or formal decision-makers habitually followed their instructions: access, operators, works, revenue, insolvency strategy, disposals or use of LPB assets in project promotion.

Estate-asset control

A narrower route may still carry liability

Even if no one replaced LPB’s corporate board, practical control of particular insolvency-estate assets may require restitution, accounting, damages or criminal examination.

Hotel-platform control

Operation is distinct from title

Hotel control must be proved through booking, reception, staffing, merchant, maintenance, utility, insurance and commercial records.

Non-LPB property

Not “LPB shadow administration”

Control of Matkator, CEXP or third-party rights is analysed as unauthorised possession, interference, use, works or exploitation—not as administration of property LPB did not own.

Spanish company law extends administrator liability to de facto administrators, including persons who actually perform administrator functions without title or under whose instructions formal administrators act. That statutory concept is an evidence test, not an automatic label arising from creditor influence.

The accounting period begins with the first date on which material access, decision-making, use or economic benefit is proved—not only later adjudication or registration. It must separate revenue, reservations/deposits, occupation value, charged expenses, deterioration, avoided costs, related-party payments, rescue obstruction, the 31-finca route and later MYND value. Each head must be assigned to the legally correct claimant—LPB/the estate, Aweswell, CEXP, Matkator, Pink, another owner or another person—without double recovery.

INSOLVENCY ADMINISTRATOR

The allegation encompasses affirmative authority and enabling acts, as well as later ratification or omissions.

Gil Marer’s direct allegation.

Gil alleges that the insolvency administrator criminally enabled, approved or ratified the 7 June operation and was not a passive observer: he acted affirmatively by advancing the security route, selecting a provider, seeking Community ratification and authorising limited access to LPB property, and those acts enabled or were used for the wider operation. Gil further alleges knowing approval or ratification and, after the alleged excess became known, legally equivalent omissions by failing to delimit, supervise, recover control, protect estate and non-estate assets, demand an account, reverse the effects or present the control change accurately to the court.

The controlled treatment of the administrator's 31 July 2018 declaration records selection of a security provider, pursuit of Community ratification, authority to open doors to identified LPB premises where necessary and limited authority for a Laura Acosta actor to enter LPB premises for maintenance or supervision. Read in full, this is both an affirmative authority-chain node and a narrower, potentially exculpatory account: the administrator denied authorising use, occupation or the principal takeover.

Level 1 · lawful narrow mandate

Exculpatory possibility

A valid, limited security or maintenance mandate was exceeded without the administrator’s knowledge. No criminal approval follows from that fact alone.

Level 2 · negligent design/supervision

Civil, professional or insolvency route

A foreseeable risk was created or inadequately controlled. Duty, breach, causation and damage must be proved.

Level 3 · knowing tolerance/ratification

Potential criminal omission

After actual knowledge, the administrator had legal and practical power to intervene but consciously retained the result. The specific offence and mental element remain essential.

Level 4 · affirmative common purpose

Strongest criminal allegation

The administrator intended the wider control transfer or coordinated it with private actors. Direct or compelling circumstantial proof is required.

Why the narrower-authorisation explanation matters.

The administrator’s denial of the main lock takeover is important contrary evidence. The reference to narrower authorised access simultaneously places the administrator inside the authority chain. The decisive questions are scope, foreseeability, first notice, response, recovery, accounting and later use of the result.

Criminal-omission boundary.

Silence, passivity, professional breach or negligent supervision does not alone prove criminal cooperation. Criminal Code Article 11 requires a particular protective duty, knowledge, practical capacity to intervene, causal equivalence and the mental element of the underlying offence. Negligence is punishable only where the Code expressly provides it. This proof limit does not dilute Gil’s direct allegation.

AFFIRMATIVE JUDICIAL ACTS AND OMISSIONS · PREVARICATION ALLEGATION

Gil alleges direct judicial intervention through resolutions and refusals, as well as delays and omissions. Each conduct requires its own criminal-law test.

Gil directly alleges omissionary judicial prevarication. That is one dimension of the unitary accusation, not its limit: he also attributes affirmative resolutions and refusals alleged to have been knowingly unjust.

Gil Marer’s direct allegation.

Gil alleges that Judge Alberto López Villarrubia’s role did not consist only of omissions. He attributes a unitary sequence of affirmative judicial acts—resolutions, refusals and evidential closures concerning protection of the hotel and estate, control and works, bidding/adjudication and, ultimately, adoption of accusatory propositions in Judgment 163/2023—together with delays and omissions of protection, restoration, investigation and accounting. Gil alleges that, with knowledge of a developed, finance-backed exit architecture, that sequence preserved or legitimised the private-control result and knowingly frustrated or sabotaged LPB’s exit from the insolvency. This is his direct criminal allegation, not an adjudicated criminal fact.

Financeable exit, one controlled meeting and contrary evidence.

The record documents the convenio, viability plan and payment plan filed on 27 April 2017 and, by June 2018, signed hotel-operation instruments, an operator plan, due diligence, an ECO valuation, staged security and parallel finance routes. The controlled record identifies one direct meeting with the judge: the meeting Daniel Irigoyen reported contemporaneously on 13 June 2018, followed by a separate meeting with the insolvency administrator. His account says the judge allowed the route to be explored and asked that the application be pursued, which is contrary evidence against a simplistic continuous-obstruction theory. The June finance was conditional, and a later route reached an executed term sheet that remained subject to conditions. This supports a developed, financeable architecture; it does not establish multiple judge visits, unconditional funds, certain closing or guaranteed success, or that every annex was received or personally read by him.

Gil Marer’s direct allegation on condition allocation and destruction of the security route.

Gil directly alleges that Aweswell had fulfilled, or was ready and able to fulfil, the sponsor-side and counterparty conditions within its lawful control. He alleges that the operative blocking conditions instead depended on preserving and delivering collateral and staged security, maintaining access for inspection, valuation and hotel operation, obtaining the court and insolvency-administrator outputs required by the finance documents, and preventing the adverse private/Community perimeter from disabling those inputs. In Gil’s account, the alleged private-control or lender-in-possession mechanism destroyed or made unavailable the security package, obstructed payment or consignment of the insolvency liabilities and prevented refinancing. This is Gil’s attributed causation and sabotage allegation, not a finding that every condition was satisfied, that closing was certain or that any identified actor caused the non-closing.

Spanish criminal law separately addresses knowingly issuing an unjust judicial resolution, grossly negligent issuance of a manifestly unjust resolution, refusal to judge without legal cause and malicious delay intended to achieve an illegitimate purpose. Any commission-by-omission theory also requires a legally equivalent duty and the necessary subjective element. “The judge did not grant the requested remedy” is not by itself prevarication.

Every alleged act must be classified under the criminal provision in force when it occurred, subject to retroactive application of a later law more favourable to the accused under Criminal Code Article 2.2. The current consolidated Code is a research entry point, not authority to project later wording backwards.

The identified allegation concerns Judge Alberto López Villarrubia. Controlled notice/decision nodes include a 27 April 2017 filing, a reported 13 June 2018 discussion, the 4/17 February 2021 filing/registration, the signed 24 February 2021 order and Sentencia 163/2023. Contrary or corrective acts—including the 26 June 2018 suspension, 24 October 2019 non-validation and partly estimatory aspects of the 2021 order—must remain in the same matrix. Informal or draft material is not attributed to the judge without receipt proof.

Exceptional case law concerning omission in administrative prevarication cannot be transferred automatically to jurisdictional conduct. The allegation must be classified through the exact resolution, refusal or deliberate delay under Criminal Code Articles 446–449.
