## 1. Controlling proposition

**The concurso was LPB’s concurso. It was not a judicial insolvency proceeding over “the hotel as a whole”.**

Sun Park was a functioning hotel/operating platform built across legally distinct rights and properties. LPB held a majority asset perimeter, but Matkator and other third parties held separate property/interests, and common areas / Community powers / operating rights had their own legal sources. Therefore:

- LPB’s creditor position did **not** make CAM owner or legal possessor of the whole hotel;
- the Administración Concursal’s duties over LPB’s estate did **not** by themselves create authority to transfer, surrender or place under private-creditor control property or rights belonging to Matkator, other owners or separate rights-holders;
- a Community/security decision did **not** by itself become a universal mandate over private units, the hotel business, reception, keys, operating data or every common/technical interface;
- formal title, legal possession, de facto/material control and effective hotel-wide control must remain separate.

This is the controlling frame for Calificación causation after 7 June 2018.

## 2. Historical insolvency-law perimeter

For the 2018 period, Ley 22/2003, Concursal supplied three important controls:

- **Art. 76 — masa activa:** the estate comprised the property and rights integrated in the **debtor’s** patrimony, plus later reintegrated/acquired property. It did not absorb third-party property merely because it formed part of the same hotel environment.
- **Art. 80 — separación:** third-party property in the debtor’s possession, where the debtor lacked a right of use, guarantee or retention, was subject to delivery to its legitimate holder on request. This reinforces, rather than erases, the distinction between the debtor’s estate and property belonging to others.
- **Art. 43 — conservación:** administration/disposition of the estate had to attend to its conservation; the AC could request judicial assistance. A preservation power over the estate is not, without an additional legal source, a power to confer whole-complex possession on a creditor.

Official source: BOE, Ley 22/2003, historical text applicable before the 2020 recast. Use the exact historical version when quoting in a filing.

## 5. Extraconcursal harm / overreach theory

The core harm is not exhausted by loss to LPB’s masa activa.

If a security/access/control regime organised around the insolvency or an LPB creditor materially affected:

- Matkator property;
- other third-party units;
- reception/common/technical areas whose authority did not belong exclusively to LPB;
- hotel operating rights, reservations, guest access, maintenance systems, staff, suppliers, data or commercial platform;
- Aweswell’s rescue/financing/investor position;

then those effects must be analysed as **extraconcursal / third-party harm** as well as, where appropriate, effects on LPB’s estate.

This is the alleged overreach: **using a legal process whose debtor was LPB as a practical bridge into control of a wider mixed-ownership hotel ecosystem.** Whether particular acts were unlawful, void, tortious, criminal or compensable requires the specific title, duty, actor, knowledge, authority, causation and remedy to be proved.

## 6. Calificación culpable — mandatory causal correction

The Calificación cannot coherently ask only what Gil/LPB/Pink should have done after June 2018. It must also ask **what they were still materially capable of doing**.

For every allegation based on post-7-June deterioration, non-delivery, lack of operation, lost income, maintenance, access, records, hotel condition, financing failure or inability to rescue the company, use this test:

1. What asset/right was involved?
2. Was it LPB estate property, third-party property, common infrastructure or an operating right?
3. Who held formal title?
4. Who held legal possession?
5. Who held keys/security/access/material control?
6. Who could actually perform the act attributed to Gil/LPB/Pink?
7. What did CAM, Community/security and the AC each do or know?
8. What evidence of that control shift was before the Fiscal and the Judge at the relevant date?
9. What loss belongs to LPB and what loss is extraconcursal?
10. What contrary explanation was considered and rejected?

A culpability narrative that attributes deterioration to debtor-side actors while omitting a proven or seriously evidenced external control shift risks a **causal asymmetry**: blaming one actor for consequences during a period in which another actor may have had the practical capacity to cause, prevent or document those consequences.

## 9. Mandatory contrary case

Any public or institutional presentation must preserve these possibilities until disproved:

- the security measure may have been genuinely intended for preservation/safety;
- the Community may have validly approved some security measures within its competence;
- CAM may have confined particular acts to units it owned and lawful common-area access;
- LPB may have retained more practical control than the project alleges;
- operational problems may have had independent causes;
- later judicial/court records may contain authority not yet located in the controlled corpus.

Those possibilities do not remove the duty to identify the actual authority, perimeter and effects of the June 2018 control shift.
