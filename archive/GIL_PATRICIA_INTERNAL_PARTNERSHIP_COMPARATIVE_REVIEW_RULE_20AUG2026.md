For each material issue or decision, answer as precisely as the evidence permits:

1. What was the shared objective?
2. What did Gil know at the time, from whom and on what source?
3. What did Patricia know at the time, from whom and on what source?

5. What did each understand the legal, financial, operational and personal consequences to be?
6. What did each say, recommend, resist, accept, sign, communicate or implement?
7. Which external actor originated, framed, recommended, pressured, documented or implemented the decision?
8. Was any person excluded from the communication or given a materially different version?
9. Was the apparent difference genuine, or explained by time, audience, role, language, evolving information, strategy, stress, adviser reliance or source quality?
10. What happened next, what harm or lost opportunity followed, and how strong is the causal link?
11. What evidence would confirm, weaken or reverse the current assessment?
12. What can now be corrected, clarified, recovered, preserved or prevented from recurring?

## 4. Source and custody controls

Read and apply `archive/EVIDENCE_CUSTODY_AND_PRESERVATION_PROTOCOL_16AUG2026.md` before handling native messages or audio.

### 4.1 Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. native email/message/audio and complete metadata;
2. complete contemporaneous thread and attachments;
3. same-day or near-contemporaneous notes or memorialisation;
4. later statements tied to identifiable contemporaneous sources;
5. recollection unsupported by a contemporaneous source;
6. generated transcript, translation or summary.

A transcript or translation is a derivative. It does not replace the native audio or original-language message.

### 4.2 Context requirement

Do not compare isolated quotations. Recover, where available:

- the full thread;
- quoted earlier text and whether it was edited;
- attachments and versions;
- surrounding messages before and after the item;
- date, time and time zone;
- sender, recipients, copied persons and omitted persons;
- channel used and whether the same matter moved between email, WhatsApp, calls and voice notes;
- the decision or event to which the communication relates.

### 4.3 Voice-note controls

For every material voice note record:

- native filename and source location;
- date/time and surrounding conversation;
- speaker identity and confidence;
- original language;
- transcript method and confidence;
- uncertain words, interruptions, irony, colloquial usage or inaudible portions;
- translation status and material alternative translations;
- what the audio directly establishes and what tone-based inference remains uncertain.

Tone may support context but must not carry a material finding by itself.

### 4.4 Data minimisation

Exclude private relationship material that is not necessary to reconstruct a project decision, external influence, evidential contradiction or corrective action. Do not broaden the review into personal surveillance.

## 5. Mandatory search and matching method

### Phase A — Corpus inventory

Create a private source inventory for Gil and Patricia across authorised Gmail, Drive, uploaded files and other lawful source systems. Search both names, known email addresses, aliases, Spanish and English terminology, project entities, advisers, decision subjects and relevant date ranges.

Record searches that produced no result and retry with alternative spellings, names, filenames, quoted phrases, entities and narrower date windows. Absence from a search result is not proof that a communication did not exist.

### Phase B — Event and decision clustering

Do not compare people globally. Group sources by a finite material event or decision, for example:

- engagement or replacement of an adviser;
- acceptance of legal or financial advice;
- signing, filing or withholding a document;
- ownership, operation, financing or litigation decision;
- response to a threat, deadline or institutional communication;
- decision not to act;
- implementation of a jointly understood plan by a third party.

Assign stable private IDs:

- `GP-DEC-YYYY-NNN` — material decision/event;
- `GP-COM-YYYY-NNN` — material communication;
- `GP-INF-YYYY-NNN` — external influence/pressure episode;
- `GP-GAP-NNN` — evidence or coordination gap;
- `GP-FIX-NNN` — corrective/preventive action.

IDs organise the review; they do not establish truth.

### Phase C — Matched comparison

For each event, place the strongest contemporaneous source for Gil beside the strongest contemporaneous source for Patricia. Compare equivalent time windows and the same decision stage. Do not compare one person's early uncertainty with the other's later informed position without stating the temporal difference.

### Phase D — Contrary and exculpatory search

For every proposed inconsistency, mistake or pressure finding, search actively for:

- a message showing alignment;
- a later correction or clarification;
- missing advice or attachment;
- a lawful reason for urgency;
- a reasonable alternative interpretation;
- evidence that both partners received the same information;
- evidence that the external adviser disclosed risks and alternatives;
- evidence that the decision was rational on the information then available;
- evidence that implementation, rather than the decision itself, caused the problem.

## 7. Difference and contradiction classification

Every apparent divergence must receive one of the following classifications:

- `ALIGNED` — materially the same position.
- `COMPLEMENTARY` — different emphasis or expertise, but compatible.
- `APPARENT — TIMING` — positions differ because knowledge evolved.
- `APPARENT — LANGUAGE/TRANSLATION` — wording differs but substance may not.
- `APPARENT — AUDIENCE/ROLE` — communication adapted to recipient or responsibility.
- `APPARENT — STRATEGIC PRESENTATION` — different external framing of a shared position; verify that it was deliberate and lawful.
- `INFORMATION ASYMMETRY` — different source material or advice was available.
- `MEMORY/RECORD MISMATCH` — later recollection conflicts with contemporaneous material.
- `EXTERNALLY INDUCED DIVERGENCE` — evidence indicates materially different advice, pressure or framing from a third party.
- `GENUINE UNRESOLVED CONTRADICTION` — incompatible propositions remain after timing, language, role, source and evolution are tested.
- `SOURCE-INTEGRITY ISSUE` — transcript, edit, attribution, completeness or metadata problem prevents comparison.

A contradiction is not dishonesty. It may reflect incomplete memory, changed understanding, ordinary error, different information, stress, strategy, external influence or source defects. Deliberate deception requires separate actor-specific evidence and must never be inferred merely from inconsistency.

For each material divergence state:

1. the two exact propositions;
2. the best source for each;
3. whether they concern the same date, issue and meaning;
4. the classification;
5. the most plausible reconciliations;
6. what would change the assessment;
7. whether any corrective statement or record amendment is needed.

## 8. External influence and pressure test

The system must examine whether external advisers or other actors communicated differently with Gil and Patricia, especially where Patricia was approached in Spanish or through local relationships and Gil was absent, copied later or given a summary rather than the original communication.

Do not treat ordinary professional disagreement, firm advice or a genuine deadline as improper pressure. Classify the evidence as:

- `NO EVIDENCE OF PRESSURE`;
- `CONTEXTUAL STRESS` — surrounding financial, legal, operational or personal urgency;
- `PERSUASION / PROFESSIONAL ADVICE` — strong recommendation without evidence of improper leverage;
- `DOCUMENTED PRESSURE` — repeated insistence, threat, exclusion, material urgency framing or authority-based leverage supported by contemporaneous evidence;
- `POTENTIALLY UNDUE OR COERCIVE — COUNSEL REVIEW` — evidence of materially constrained choice, misrepresented necessity, threatened consequence, concealment, conflict or exploitation of dependency. This label is an investigative flag, not a legal conclusion.

### Indicators to test, not assume

- an explicit threat, ultimatum or claimed unavoidable consequence;
- assertion that an action was legally mandatory without written basis;
- artificial or unexplained urgency;
- communicating only through one partner on a joint material decision;
- directing that the other partner not be copied or consulted;
- withholding the original document, translation, assumptions, alternatives or downside;
- materially different advice to each partner;
- repeated insistence after an expressed objection;
- reliance on local status, professional authority or social pressure;
- adviser conflict, self-interest, fee interest or benefit to another client/counterparty;
- later denial, recharacterisation or omission of the earlier advice;
- implementation that exceeded what either partner authorised.

## 9. Critical decision or mistake analysis

Do not begin by labelling an outcome a “mistake.” First distinguish:

- a reasonable decision that produced a bad outcome;
- a legal or technical error;
- an information deficit;
- unequal or incomplete advice;
- a translation or terminology failure;
- an internal coordination failure;
- adviser reliance that was reasonable or unreasonable at the time;
- pressure or urgency distortion;
- authority or mandate ambiguity;
- implementation error by a third party;
- external misrepresentation or withheld information;
- later failure to correct or document the record.

For each possible critical mistake reconstruct:

`GOAL → OPTIONS THEN KNOWN → INFORMATION MISSING/WITHHELD → ADVICE → PRESSURE → DECISION → IMPLEMENTATION → CONSEQUENCE → COMPETING CAUSES → REVERSIBILITY → CURRENT REPAIR`

Apply a strict hindsight-bias test: ask what a reasonable person in that role could have understood at that time, not what is obvious after later events.

Allocate causal responsibility only after separating proposal, advice, decision authority, signature, implementation and benefit. Use graded language:

- `DIRECTLY CAUSED`;
- `MATERIALLY CONTRIBUTED`;
- `POSSIBLY CONTRIBUTED`;
- `TEMPORALLY ASSOCIATED ONLY`;
- `NO PROVED CAUSAL LINK`.

## 12. Repair and prevention protocol

The analysis is incomplete unless it converts each material gap into an action. Possible repairs include:

- recover the full native thread, attachment or voice note;
- correct a transcript or translation;
- obtain the advice, mandate, engagement letter, invoice or conflict disclosure;
- identify whether both partners received the same information;
- create a jointly reviewed chronology or decision memorandum;
- clarify a past statement without suppressing uncertainty;
- update a private legal chronology, witness preparation note or evidential ledger;
- seek independent legal, financial or technical review;
- correct a public or institutional statement only where justified and authorised;
- distinguish a decision error from an implementation error;
- preserve the original source and record the chain of custody.

### Future high-impact decision safeguards

For material legal, financial, ownership, litigation, filing, settlement, financing, adviser-engagement or public-communication decisions:

1. both partners should receive the same core documents and advice;
2. oral advice should be confirmed in writing with assumptions, alternatives, risks and deadlines;
3. the adviser must identify who the client is, who has authority and any conflict;
4. language or terminology material to the decision should be checked in the original and translated form;
5. the record should distinguish recommendation, decision, signature and implementation;
6. disagreement or uncertainty should be recorded rather than erased;
7. irreversible or high-impact action should receive an independent second review where practicable;
8. any claimed urgency should be documented and tested;
9. native evidence should be preserved before external escalation or publication;
10. later corrections should be propagated to all affected private ledgers and authorised public statements.

## 13. Privilege, consent and publication boundary

This protocol is for private alignment, evidence reconstruction and remediation. It does not authorise publication of comparative personal findings.

No detailed comparison, private excerpt, voice-note transcript, personal decision-style conclusion or allegation of pressure/coercion should be placed on the public website, sent to a third party, used in a filing or converted into a witness statement without explicit human authorisation and appropriate legal/editorial review.

Preserve legal privilege, common-interest protections and confidentiality where applicable. Do not describe any item as privileged merely because it is private; record the actual basis and obtain counsel validation where material.

## 14. Completion standard

A review is complete only when:

- both partners' strongest contemporaneous sources have been searched with equivalent effort;
- the same decision windows have been compared;
- supporting, contradictory and exculpatory material have been tested;
- apparent contradictions have been classified rather than merely listed;
- external pressure has been traced actor-by-actor and source-by-source;
- every possible mistake has a hindsight-bias and causation analysis;
- the analysis preserves the partnership and does not manufacture blame;
- every material gap has a concrete retrieval or repair step;
- sensitive outputs remain outside the public repository;
- a fresh authorised reviewer can reproduce the method without relying on conversational memory.
