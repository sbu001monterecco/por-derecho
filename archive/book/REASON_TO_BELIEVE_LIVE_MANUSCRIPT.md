## Editorial north star

**Before Sun Park became a case file, it was a place people came to live, return, make friends and begin again.**

# PROLOGUE — THE SAME PLACE

There is a particular cruelty in seeing a place you knew intimately continue without you.

The pool is still there. The Lanzarote light is still there. The volcanic landscape has not moved. Guests still arrive with luggage, plans and expectations. The same physical place that once carried the name Sun Park is now presented to the world as MYND Yaiza.

A hotel can change its name. Companies can change. Creditors can change. Proceedings can multiply. Files can be opened, closed, transferred and reopened. A judge can see one slice of time, a prosecutor another, a tax authority another, a registry another. But the asset does not acquire a new history each time somebody changes the folder in which it is discussed.

There was one place. One economic history. One chain of people, money, decisions, authority, control and consequences.

This book is an attempt to put that history back together.

It is also an attempt to remember what existed before the dispute became the dominant story.

Before there were case numbers, creditor substitutions, insolvency arguments and allegations, there were people arriving in Playa Blanca and deciding to stay a little longer.

They were the SunRockers.

# PART II — THE SUNROCKERS

## Chapter 3 — Stay Longer

[Living draft]

Time changes hospitality. A guest who stays two nights sees a product. A person who stays for weeks begins to see a place, its routines and its people.

The SunRocker proposition depended on that difference. Longer stays created the possibility of friendship, habit and contribution.

## Chapter 4 — Take Part

[Living draft]

The archive points to creative activities, music, walking, gardening, small markets, reading, social events and other participative activity. The detail must be rebuilt from contemporaneous brochures, schedules, photographs, newsletters and memories rather than from later summaries alone.

The important distinction is between **animation delivered to guests** and **community partly made by the people who were there**.

## Chapter 5 — Belong

[Living draft]

What survives most powerfully in a hospitality project is often not the room or the tariff. It is the relationship. People remember who they met, what they did together and whether a place made them feel temporarily less alone.

That is why the SunRockers matter to the larger story. They are evidence of value that cannot be captured by a title deed alone.

## Chapter 6 — The Ethos

[Living draft]

Years later, when trying to explain the philosophy behind Sun Rock, Gil explicitly paired the Sun Park Living material with Adam J. Jackson's `The Ethos: Because life is better together`.

The relevance is philosophical rather than proprietary. Jackson's published themes — friendship, community, purpose and later-life renewal — offer a vocabulary for something Sun Park was trying to make practical: a later chapter of life need not become smaller merely because it comes later.

**Publication boundary:** do not imply endorsement by Adam J. Jackson of this book, the legal case or any allegation.

## Chapter 14 — Sun Park → MYND Yaiza

[Living draft]

The strongest visual fact in the story is also the simplest: the place did not move.

The current hotel marketed as MYND Yaiza occupies the physical location at the centre of the Sun Park history. That continuity permits a reader to look at the same place across time while asking what changed in title, authority, financing, operation, brand, income and beneficiary.

The book must not convert experiential similarities into proof of intellectual-property copying or unlawful appropriation. Those are separate propositions requiring separate evidence.

[Living draft]

The larger institutional question is not whether every individual decision was wrong. It is whether a continuous history can become functionally invisible when responsibility is distributed across enough offices, proceedings, years and legal categories.

# PART VI — RECONSTRUCTION

## Chapter 21 — One Asset. One History.

[Living draft]

Fragmentation is an administrative fact. It is not a historical truth.

A mortgage proceeding may end. An insolvency proceeding may continue. A tax file may examine a different entity. A criminal complaint may be archived. A corporate transaction may appear in the registry. None of those events, in isolation, reconstructs the whole economic history of the same place.

## Chapter 24 — Reason to Believe

[Living draft]

This book is being written before the ending is known.

That is not a weakness. It is the test.

A reason to believe is not certainty that an institution will agree with you. It is the proposition that rights, evidence, persistence and institutional capacity for self-correction are still worth invoking even after years of disappointment.

The book therefore cannot end by pretending that the outcome has already been achieved.

Its ending must remain capable of changing as the real history changes.
