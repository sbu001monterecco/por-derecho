## 1. Reader rule

The 4 February ACTA, the later LPB dación/adjudication sequence, a Community levy, a works project and later hotel operation are related chronology. They are **not** a single legal act and do not prove one another.

In particular:

- LPB was the debtor in Concurso 36/2012; Matkator was not thereby absorbed into that estate;
- an amount appearing in an ACTA assessment table is a documented **claimed assessment/charge in that record**. It remains material even if it was never collected or later abandoned; it does not by itself prove an enforceable debt, a payment, a work performed, a loss, a benefit or a valid resolution;
- a court or AC act later in time does not prove that the ACTA was filed, read, relied upon or known beforehand;
- a document defect, conflict question or suspicious chronology does not itself establish falsity, deception, intent, collusion or a criminal offence.

The public question is therefore: **for each named actor and each asset, what document shows authority, notice, use, reliance, payment, benefit and resulting effect?**

### Documentary entry is not the end of the enquiry

The record supports a four-stage audit, not an either/or choice between “debt proved” and “nothing happened”:

1. **ACTA/document stage:** what assessment or claim the minutes record or purport to create;
2. **ledger/certificate stage:** whether it was booked, certified, invoiced, demanded, contested or otherwise treated as a Community claim;
3. **legal-effect stage:** whether a competent route made it valid, enforceable, contingent or assumed within a defined perimeter; and
4. **downstream stage:** whether any recipient received, relied on, paid, collected, presented or used it in the insolvency, title, project, investor or public-support track.

An absence of collection evidence does not erase the documentary entry. Conversely, the entry alone does not prove any later stage, purpose, fraud or common intent.

## 6. The accounting and authority test

The central documented tension is not “a fee exists, therefore fraud is proved.” It is the need to reconcile all of the following, property by property and invoice by invoice:

1. the original budget, project, licences and scope of works;
2. the ACTA’s debt table and works-assessment table;
3. the narrative that CAM would carry out conversion works at its own cost;
4. each owner/unit coefficient, notice, proxy, vote and any debt-based voting restriction;
5. Community accounts, bank statements, invoices, certificates, demands, payments, credits and litigation treatment;
6. the LPB estate inventory/accounts and the treatment, if any, of Community claims;
7. Matkator’s separate title, rights, possession/use, payment and revenue position; and
8. any later document that presented the ACTA, assessment, works or title position to the AC, court, Registry, investor, authority, financier or operator.

The 25-January and 25-April AC reports add a precise continuity question: whether the 4-February assessment was ever classified as a pending Community due recognised in the definitive creditor lists, connected to the plan’s purchaser-assumption clause, entered in LPB/Community accounts, communicated to the AC/Court, demanded or paid. Neither report names the ACTA or its LPB/Matkator figures. Their silence does not prove knowledge, intent or an omission; it creates a finite record-reconciliation request.

Only this ledger can distinguish, for example, a valid but disputed Community assessment, an unpaid claimed balance, a duplicated cost, a misallocated charge, a document error, a civil/insolvency challenge, or a possible deception. None of those outcomes may be presumed from chronology alone.

## 7. Criminal and public-official attribution boundary

The source family justifies an **investigative test**, including possible documentary, authority, accounting, insolvency and procedural-reliance questions. It does not establish a shared criminal plan.

Any alleged private-actor offence requires the specific representation/document, author or adopter, knowledge, intended recipient, reliance, causal consequence, harm and benefit. Any alleged AC omission requires a specific duty, actual information, capacity and intentional/causally equivalent failure. Any alleged judicial offence requires an act-by-act legal and evidential basis; an adverse decision, a lack of inspection, a later benefit to another actor, or a contested order is not proof of knowing judicial participation.

If a court or Judge was deceived by a document, that is analytically different from proving the Judge knowingly participated in the deception. The latter requires independent evidence.
