# 1. EXECUTIVE CONCLUSION

The institutional chronology should no longer begin in 2018 or with the Acosta Matos phase. It must be divided into two earlier layers.

1. **2011–2012 — causal and insolvency prehistory.** Disputes over Community authority, debt, voting, hotel exploitation and access were already live. Concurso 36/2012 opened on 6 June 2012 and Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte `PD-SP-P-0010` became insolvency administrator. Contemporaneous private material shows that he received significant background on the 2008/Multimatrix history and pre-insolvency conflict. That establishes contextual knowledge, not collusion. [PD-MF-HIST-EVT-2012-06-06]

2. **2013 — first verified prosecutorial frame.** The earliest controlled Public Prosecution Service act is not 2012 but 2013. The insolvency administrator reported matters to the prosecution service on 18 September 2013. On 27 December prosecutor Tomás Fernández de Páiz `PD-SP-P-0114` signed the DI 273/2013 criminal complaint against Gil Marer `PD-SP-P-0001` and Patricia Domínguez `PD-SP-P-0002`. [PD-MF-SRC-002][PD-MF-SRC-003]

The significance of the first frame is not that every accusation was necessarily false. Important conduct—especially the 6 February 2012 operating agreement and later payment/collection conduct—occurred after Gil's arrival and required independent examination. The evidential question is different: **which propositions supplied by the insolvency administrator were independently verified, which uncertainties became official accusatory hypotheses, and how institutional credibility was allocated from that point onward.**

Within months, Gil/Patricia had advanced an extensive counter-model concerning the Montelanza/Molina perimeter, Community governance, debt, voting, exploitation, access and economic harm. Fiscalía 39/2014 was archived on 8 May 2014, with parts characterised as generic/insufficiently concrete and parts as civil/horizontal-property matters. [PD-MF-SRC-004][PD-MF-SRC-005][PD-MF-SRC-006]

The contrast becomes objectively stronger in 2015. A 24 February court order admitted **Roque Prieto, S.L. `PD-SP-O-0012`, Amenem, S.L. `PD-SP-O-0009` and Inversiones Salinetas, S.L. `PD-SP-O-0016` as private prosecutors in DP 332/2014**. [PD-MF-SRC-007] That does not prove that those companies caused or controlled the 2013 prosecutorial act. It does establish that a criminal proceeding born from the AC→prosecution route later included entities from the earlier private-owner/Molina-side perimeter on the accusing side.

A distinct later convergence appears in 2018. On 27 February the insolvency administrator asked the Owners' Community presidency to convene a meeting to hire security to prevent unauthorised access to common and privately owned areas. [PD-MF-SRC-009] On 18 May the same governance environment included CAM/Laura Patricia Acosta Matos `PD-SP-P-0012`, LPB represented by the insolvency administrator, and an AMENEM/Shaila `PD-SP-P-0008` voting line. [PD-MF-SRC-010] Between 23 and 31 May, a controlled deed/Registry chain records Finca 8718 passing from the Tengolf/Amenem perimeter to CAM. [PD-MF-SRC-011] The material access/control pivot occurred on 7 June. Criminal character, authority and personal participation remain separate questions for each act.

On 12 September 2018 the procedural roles reversed: Gil/Aweswell complained to prosecutors about AC/CAM, generating DI 248/2018. [PD-MF-SRC-012] Yet in February–March 2019 the insolvency administrator filed his classification report and the Public Prosecution Service adopted a severe classification position seeking culpability, 15-year disqualification and patrimonial consequences, expressly identifying the AC report, annexes, accounting and AC testimony among its evidence. [PD-MF-SRC-013][PD-MF-SRC-014]

The **most important direct institutional bridge** appears on 7 May 2019. When archiving DI 248/2018—the complaint against AC/CAM—the prosecution service expressly invoked the fact that “this Ministry” had already taken an adverse position in the insolvency classification against Gil, including alleged non-cooperation with the insolvency administrator. [PD-MF-SRC-016] This establishes a documented return path:

```text
AC / adverse source frame
        ↓
prosecutorial classification position
        ↓
institutional adverse position
        ↓
reuse of that position when assessing a complaint against AC/CAM
```

This may be described analytically as **institutional self-referential reinforcement** or **adverse-frame feedback**. The reuse is documented. Whether it was legally improper, motivated by institutional self-protection or criminal is not established and must be assessed separately.

Sentencia 163/2023 prevents any simplistic “rubber stamp” theory. It rejected or narrowed some AC theories while retaining other materially adverse findings; RPL 2523/2025 `PD-SP-R-0002` is identified as the appeal. [PD-MF-SRC-017] Neither “everything the AC said was true” nor “the court rejected the entire case” is accurate.

A new traceability problem develops in 2026 through DI 22/2026 `PD-SP-R-0026`, DIP 2/2026 `PD-SP-R-0006`, DP 1901/2026 `PD-SP-R-0005` and ultimately E.G. 745/2026 `PD-SP-R-0034`. DIP 2 is a clear example: the 6 March archive relied on an appeal-status premise contradicted by primary judicial appeal records; a correction was registered on 11 March, but the institutional treatment of that correction has not been located. [PD-MF-SRC-018]

DP 1901 adds another gap. The 14 September 2026 court order proves the existence of a 29 July prosecutor's report seeking dismissal, yet the signed report, author, assignment, exact corpus, reasoning and supervisory/visado history remain uncontrolled. [PD-MF-SRC-019]

Fiscal Inspection archived E.G.745 on 26 August. A **separate** DP1901 identification/preservation communication was registered on 20 September and expressly stated that it was not the reposición. [PD-MF-SRC-021] The reposición was filed on 21 September through ten linked registrations containing 27 PDFs, 1,272 pages and 22 illustrations, with seven requests and three additional requests/otrosíes. [PD-MF-SRC-022][PD-MF-SRC-023][PD-MF-SRC-024]

The 29 September decree, received by email on 1 October, dismissed the reposición in full. Its significance is that **the appeal was not unintelligible as to the files and issues it raised**: the decree itself identifies DIP2, DP1901, the 29 July report, DI22, DI248, the classification position and other historical matters. It then largely reframes the request as an attempt to turn Fiscal Inspection into an appellate body over prosecutorial decisions. [PD-MF-SRC-025]

The core Supreme Court issue should therefore not be expressed as “Should Inspection have changed what other prosecutors decided?” It should be:

> **Did the reposición identify separable questions about institutional functioning, concrete processing practices, receipt, corpus, allocation, authorship, remittal, preservation and post-correction handling that fall within Article 13 of the Public Prosecution Service Regulation, and were those questions lawfully and sufficiently answered without incorrectly collapsing them into a merits appeal?**

Article 13 of Royal Decree 305/2022 expressly gives Fiscal Inspection functions to verify how the prosecution service operates and to review general and concrete practices followed in processing and handling proceedings. Articles 165 and 167 provide for archive, opening a governmental file, remittal to the competent body and narrowly tailored information-gathering to test plausibility and identify a presumed author when the statutory threshold is met.  
Official source: https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2022/05/03/305
