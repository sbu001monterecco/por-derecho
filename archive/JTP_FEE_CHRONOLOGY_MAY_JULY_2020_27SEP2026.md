# JTP fee chronology — May–July 2020

**Updated:** 27 September 2026
**Purpose:** neutral documentary chronology. This record does not determine entitlement, professional misconduct, criminality, source of funds, or liability.

- **21 May 2020:** Juan Tomás Parrilla explained in an email that, while the challenged sale remained unresolved, he had previously considered it inappropriate to take his fee from money that might have to be returned. In the same email he stated that he believed the procedural position had since changed and argued that payment of debtor counsel as an estate claim was legally ordinary.
- **22 May 2020:** the client side told Parrilla in writing that Gil preferred to pay the additional fees from another source rather than through the insolvency proceeding. The email recorded concern about the funds, CAM and possible return/impact on the client's position.
- **31 May–1 June 2020:** the client substituted Parrilla. Successor-counsel correspondence treated him as no longer LPB's lawyer and disputed aspects of the proposed fee route.
- **11 June 2020:** Parrilla proposed a final fee of €50,000 + IGIC, recorded €32,250 previously received, described €17,750 + IGIC as remaining, proposed two possible routes involving the Insolvency Administrator, and requested Gil/Patricia's written approval before proceeding.
- **12 June 2020:** incoming counsel said the proposal could not be accepted. The client side agreed, ended the discussions and referred to proceeding by insolvency incident.
- **July 2020:** the Insolvency Administrator's later 21 July 2021 report records €26,750 paid for debtor-lawyer fees to Parrilla.

## Open reconciliation

The operative invoice/minuta, complete payment or recognition authority, matching estate-account debit, recipient credit and complete bank/ledger reconciliation have not been established in the current source close. No completed CAM-to-Parrilla bank route is stated here.

## Reading rule

The 21 May statement and Parrilla's explanation that he believed payment had become legally ordinary must always be presented together. Chronology is not causation. The later reported payment must be distinguished from proof of its exact banking source or legal authority.
