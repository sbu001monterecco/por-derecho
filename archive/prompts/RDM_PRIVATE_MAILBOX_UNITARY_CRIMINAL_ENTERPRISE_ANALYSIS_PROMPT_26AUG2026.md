## 4. Successive adoption and divided functions

The model does not require every actor to have been present at the beginning or
to have performed the same act. For each actor and period, test separately:

1. precise identity and capacity;
2. source and limits of power or duty;
3. earliest verified knowledge and each later warning;
4. act, instruction, document, certification, omission, assistance, adoption,
   implementation, ratification, concealment or benefit alleged;
5. whether the actor encountered an existing mechanism innocently, investigated
   it, rejected it, adopted it, escalated it or supplied an independent lawful
   basis;
6. dependency between that conduct and earlier/later acts;
7. objective effect and quantified benefit, burden or loss;
8. evidence of intent, purpose, recklessness, gross negligence or innocent
   explanation under the exact offence being tested;
9. contrary and exculpatory evidence; and
10. offence-specific participation, temporal and limitation status.

Later conduct cannot retroactively make a person an author, inducer, necessary
cooperator or accomplice in an already completed offence under CP 28–29. Test
later adoption only against an offence that was still executing or permanent at
that date, or against a legally distinct later offence such as concealment,
handling/receiving, obstruction, falsity/use, procedural fraud, disloyal
administration or another properly identified route. Do not infer an original
pact from continuity, relationship, professional role or shared benefit alone.

For every alleged handoff create:

`PREDECESSOR ACT → TRANSMISSION BRIDGE → MATERIAL RECEIVED → RECIPIENT KNOWLEDGE
→ RECIPIENT CHOICE → RECIPIENT ACT/OMISSION → EFFECT → BENEFIT/HARM`.

Label any missing bridge. A relationship, family connection, client relationship,
public office, shared adviser or chronological proximity never transfers
knowledge, intention, control or liability by itself.

### A. Participation, omission and legal persons

- **CP 27–29:** identify author, co-author, inducer, necessary cooperator or
  accomplice act by act; no collective attribution or retroactive participation.
- **CP 11:** use commission by omission only for a result offence where omission
  is legally equivalent to causing the result. Identify a specific legal or
  contractual duty, or prior creation of the risk, the actor's knowledge and
  practical capacity to act, the required subjective element and whether timely
  performance would have prevented the result with the causal equivalence
  required by the offence. Do not treat a merely possible improvement in outcome
  as the statutory equivalence. Appointment or supervision alone is insufficient.
- **CP 31 bis:** first identify an offence for which the Code, in the version in
  force on the act date, expressly provides legal-person liability and identify
  the exact legal person. Because the record crosses 1 July 2015, do not import
  the current attribution wording or organisational-model exemption into earlier
  conduct; set out the then-current 31 bis text and any favourable later law.
  Under the current framework, keep the two attribution routes separate. Under
  **31 bis.1(a)** test the physical actor's status as legal representative,
  authorised decision-maker or holder of organisational/control powers, conduct
  in the entity's name or on its account and direct or indirect benefit. Under
  **31 bis.1(b)** test conduct in the exercise of corporate activities, on the
  entity's account and for its direct or indirect benefit, by a person subject to
  the authority of a 31 bis.1(a) person, made possible by that person's **serious**
  breach of supervision, vigilance and control duties in the concrete
  circumstances. Apply the temporally relevant organisational-model exemption or
  mitigation rules rather than treating absence or presence of a compliance
  document as conclusive. CP 129 permits motivated accessory consequences for a
  non-personified entity only where the Code expressly provides them or for an
  offence that permits legal-person liability; it is not automatic corporate
  guilt. Apply CP 31 quinquies public-entity exclusions and public-company limits
  exactly. Do not assume that CP 31 bis automatically applies to a Community,
  CEXP or other non-personified perimeter.

### B. Fraud, procedural fraud and patrimonial administration

- **Historical/current CP 248–249:** use the version applicable on the act date.
  Do not silently place current computer/data/payment-instrument fraud in old
  Article 248. For ordinary fraud prove sufficient prior or concurrent deception,
  induced error, dispositive act, patrimonial loss, causation and profit intent.
- **CP 250.1.7:** procedural fraud requires manipulation of evidence or analogous
  procedural fraud that induces judicial error and produces a decision harmful
  to another's economic interests. Identify the exact false/manipulated material,
  its actor, knowing use, the tribunal's actual reliance/error, dispositive or
  prejudicial decision, causation, benefit and loss. Keep this distinct from an
  allegation that a judge knowingly decided unjustly.
- **CP 250.1.2:** analyse abuse of signature and the subtraction, concealment or
  disabling of a file, record, protocol or public/official document under this
  separate aggravated-fraud route when the facts and temporal text fit. Prove
  the applicable fraud elements; the listed conduct is not, by itself, a
  standalone offence under Article 250. Do not relabel those acts as the
  manipulation-of-evidence/analogous-fraud limb of 250.1.7.
- **CP 251:** test its three alternatives separately: (1) falsely claiming a
  power of disposition over a **movable or immovable** thing and transferring,
  encumbering or leasing it to another's prejudice; (2) disposing while
  concealing an encumbrance, or encumbering or disposing again after presenting
  the thing as free and before definitive transfer; or (3) entering a simulated
  contract to another's prejudice. Identify the exact thing, power, charge,
  transaction, actor and prejudice; do not use Article 251 as generic fraud
  shorthand.
- **CP 252:** identify entrusted powers over another's patrimony, excess in their
  exercise, patrimonial prejudice, causation and the applicable subjective
  element; a bad commercial result or disputed accounting is not enough.

### C. Frustration of enforcement and insolvency offences

Keep CP **257**, **258/258 bis**, **259/259 bis**, **260** and **261** separate.
Apply the version in force on the act date and, at minimum, distinguish:

- **CP 257:** concealment, disposition or obligation-creation intended to
  prejudice creditors or defeat an initiated or foreseeably initiated judicial,
  extrajudicial or administrative execution, and the distinct criminal-civil-
  liability avoidance route. A later concurso does not supply an element and is
  not required for Article 257.
- **CP 258:** in a judicial or administrative execution, an incomplete or false
  asset declaration that delays, hinders or prevents creditor satisfaction, or a
  required debtor's failure to provide it. Test the pre-discovery truthful and
  complete rectification rule in 258.3; do not treat it as a generic accounting
  offence.
- **CP 258 bis:** unauthorised use of property embargoed by public authority and
  constituted in deposit, subject to its subsidiarity clause.
- **CP 259/259 bis:** actual or imminent insolvency plus one exact listed act, or
  causation of insolvency by such an act. Map concealment/damage of estate assets,
  disproportionate and unjustified transfers or debts, unjustified below-cost
  transactions, fictitious credits, accounting/books/document conduct and the
  residual serious breach of economic-management diligence to the exact numbered
  limb. Apply the prosecution gate in 259.4 and the no-need-to-await-concurso-
  conclusion rule in 259.5. Test the separate imprudence route in CP 259.3. Apply
  CP 259.6 correctly: the civil/concursal classification of insolvency does not
  bind the criminal jurisdiction.
- **CP 259 bis:** apply the aggravated route only if Article 259 is first
  established and one of its own thresholds is proved: actual or potential
  patrimonial harm to a generality of persons or grave economic jeopardy to
  them; harm exceeding EUR 600,000 to any one creditor; or at least half of the
  insolvency claims being held by the relevant public treasury and Social
  Security. Do not use the scale of the wider alleged enterprise as a substitute.
- **CP 260.1:** a debtor in actual or imminent insolvency favours a creditor by an
  economically or commercially unjustified disposition or obligation intended
  to pay a non-due credit or provide security to which the creditor had no right.
  Keep it separate from **260.2**, which applies after admission of the concurso
  application to unauthorised dispositions or obligations paying one or more
  creditors while postponing the rest, outside cases permitted by law.
- **CP 261:** prove knowing presentation of false accounting-state data in an
  insolvency proceeding for the purpose of improperly obtaining the declaration
  of concurso; do not use it for every incorrect figure or omission.

### D. Documentary falsity and use

Separate CP **390.1.4** false narration by an authority or public official in the
exercise of functions from CP **392** private commission concerning public,
official or commercial documents, and from CP **395** falsity in a private
document. Articles 392 and 395 incorporate only the falsity modes in **390.1.1–3**;
a private actor's merely untrue narration is not, without alteration, simulation
or false attribution of intervention/declarations fitting one of those limbs,
documentary falsity under 392 or 395. Article 395 also requires the specific
purpose of prejudicing another. Analyse knowing presentation or use of a false
public/official/commercial document under **CP 393** and of a false private
document under **CP 396** separately, applying each provision's knowledge,
in-court/use and purpose requirements. For each alleged document identify author,
capacity, document class, exact false component, authenticity versus truth,
materiality, intent, use, recipient reliance, prejudice and temporal version.

- For **1 July 2015–11 January 2023**, test the then-applicable CP 432 framework,

  under CP 435.4 specifically in relation to the insolvency estate or creditors'
  economic interests. Verify the historical text rather than importing the later
  reform.
- From **12 January 2023**, do not describe the rewritten chapter as a generic
  disloyal-administration offence. Test separately **CP 432** appropriation of (or
  consenting to a third party's appropriation of) entrusted public patrimony with
  the required profit intent; **CP 432 bis** temporary private use without intent
  to appropriate and its reintegration rule; and **CP 433** application of
  administered public patrimony to a different public use. Address CP 433 bis and
  433 ter only where their own public-entity accounting or public-patrimony text
  fits. Then test the CP 435.4 extension to the insolvency administrator, estate

  order of credit payments. Apply `lex mitior` where required.
- For every temporal route prove the qualifying capacity, exact entrusted
  property or creditor interest, personal act, authority, statutory mental
  element, prejudice or other required result, benefit where required and causal
  accounting bridge. Court appointment alone does not make every act a public-
  official offence.
- Keep **CP 440** separate. For an insolvency administrator, prove conduct of the
  kind described in CP 439—using the required intervention to force or facilitate
  direct or interposed participation in the transaction—specifically concerning
  rights or assets integrated in the insolvency estate. A conflict, fee, later
  benefit or adverse estate decision alone is not Article 440.

### F. Judicial and administrative decision-making

- **CP 404** concerns an authority or public official who, **knowing its
  injustice**, issues an arbitrary resolution in an administrative matter; it is
  not the judicial-prevarication provision.
- **CP 446:** identify the exact judgment or resolution, why it was objectively
  unjust in the criminal-law sense, and proof that the judge or magistrate knew
  it was unjust when issuing it.
- **CP 447:** require a **manifestly unjust** judgment or resolution caused by
  gross negligence or inexcusable ignorance; do not mix it with knowing injustice.
- **CP 448:** identify a judge or magistrate's refusal to judge without alleging
  legal cause, or under pretext of obscurity, insufficiency or silence of the law.
- **CP 449:** identify the delayed act, responsible actor and a delay deliberately
  caused to achieve an illegitimate purpose. Record duration and procedural
  effect as evidence, but do not invent a causal-result element the statute does
  not contain; ordinary delay or backlog alone is insufficient.
- Keep the actor classes separate: judge/magistrate, LAJ and other judicial
  official. Do not assign a judge an office act without proof of personal
  adoption or knowledge.

Where one theory says private actors induced a court's error and another says a
judge knowingly issued an unjust decision, treat them as alternatives unless
different stages, materials and actors allow both without logical contradiction.

### G. Concealment and later assistance

For **CP 451**, prove knowledge of the predicate offence, non-participation in it
as author or accomplice and conduct after its execution, then select exactly one
statutory mode:

1. assisting authors or accomplices to benefit from the proceeds, product or
   price of the offence, **without personal profit intent**;
2. concealing, altering or disabling the body, effects or instruments of the
   offence **for the purpose of preventing its discovery**; or
3. helping presumed perpetrators evade official investigation, search or capture
   only where the predicate is one of the offences listed in 451.3(a), or where
   the helper abused public functions under 451.3(b).

The no-personal-profit condition belongs to mode 1, not to every mode. An ordinary
economic predicate does not enter mode 3 merely because it is serious; absent a
listed predicate, prove abuse of public functions. Apply the CP 452 sentencing
cap, CP 453 even where the predicate author is irresponsible or personally exempt,
and CP 454 family exemption with its mode-1 exception. Do not use `encubrimiento`
as a generic label for silence, professional representation, an adverse ruling or
later commercial benefit.

### H. Criminal organisation/group and the factual enterprise

Test **CP 570 bis** and **CP 570 ter** independently and identify each person's
exact statutory conduct. For **570 bis**, prove a grouping of more than two people,
stable or indefinite character, concerted and coordinated distribution of
different tasks or functions and the purpose of committing offences; distinguish
promotion, constitution, organisation, coordination or direction from active
participation, membership, economic cooperation or other cooperation. For
**570 ter**, prove a union of more than two people which lacks one or more of the
570-bis organisational characteristics but has the purpose or object of the
concerted perpetration of offences; distinguish constitution, financing and
integration. Do not import the 570-bis stability and task-division requirements
into 570 ter. CP 570 ter is a residual alternative; do not classify the same
grouping and period cumulatively under both except as expressly stated
alternatives.

The factual phrase **one continuing economic-criminal enterprise** is an
attributed description of alleged continuity, successive adoption and divided
functions. It does not itself prove CP 570 bis/ter, an original common pact, any
person's participation, a `delito continuado` under CP 74, a permanent offence,
consummation, a changed participation window or a changed prescription date.
