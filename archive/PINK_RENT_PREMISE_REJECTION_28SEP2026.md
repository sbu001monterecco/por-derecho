## 1. The rejected characterisation

Gil Marer expressly rejects as false the characterisation **“Pink owed rent but could not afford to pay it.”** That sentence must not be used as Por Derecho's neutral account, as Gil's position, as an admission of an enforceable debt, or as the starting premise of the operating-cost analysis.

Gil describes it as a self-serving, bad-faith inversion advanced in the adverse-party interest and as inconsistent with the principles on which the integrated hotel operated. These are his allegations. Public text must state them clearly without falsely certifying that a court has found the characterisation dishonest or the contract void.

## 2. Gil's position, in its proper order

1. **Authority and validity from inception.** Gil maintains his primary position that the 6 February 2012 LPB–Monterecco/Pink agreement was incorrectly entered into and wholly void from inception. He disputes the authority and structure through which LPB alone purported to grant rights within the integrated hotel/CEXP arrangement. He is not conceding a valid rent debt and pleading inability to pay it.
2. **Advice and professional failures.** Gil alleges that Garrigues misadvised the project at the time on multiple aspects of the arrangement and that Juan Tomás Parrilla Suárez also failed in relevant professional duties. The exact engagement, instruction, act, omission, date, knowledge and causal contribution must be assessed separately for each professional. This statement does not establish that the two professionals had the same mandate, that either knowingly participated in a crime, or that one person's act is attributable to the other.
3. **Operating contributions.** Gil's position is that the operating side carried the integrated hotel's expenses and costs, including costs benefiting owners, and that there was no contemporaneous justification under that operating model for treating additional direct rent or owner payments, including to LPB, as an admitted obligation. A cost-bearing account is not a concession on validity, nor is it an assertion that every invoice or proposed credit has already been independently reconciled.
4. **Alleged affirmative conduct and criminal instrumentalisation.** Gil alleges more than mere failure or inaction by Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte, the insolvency administrator. He alleges that failures surrounding advice, contracting and defence were criminally instrumentalised through acts within the historical Montelanza/Molina perimeter and the later Acosta Matos perimeter, together with relevant AC conduct, to displace the operating side and consolidate or defend control and value. The alleged continuity of a mechanism is not a merger of legal persons, proof of a single unchanged membership, or attribution of knowledge or intent by association.
5. **Truth-inversion allegation.** The alleged inversion is that the side sustaining the hotel and its owners' property was recast as an ordinary non-paying tenant, while disputed authority, costs borne, interference and the subsequent use of the resulting positions were omitted or subordinated. This is the causal allegation to test, not a statement of collective criminal guilt.
