## 1. Controlling evidential rule

This bundle contains several different source classes. They must never be flattened into one level of proof.

- **SIGNED COURT ORDER / P1 PRIMARY:** establishes the procedural act, the positions the order records and the court's stated reasoning. It does not establish the truth of every allegation summarised in the order.
- **REGISTERED OR COURT-HEADED PARTY SUBMISSION:** establishes that a party formulated the identified allegation or request, subject to proof of actual filing/receipt where the copy does not itself contain the receipt. Its allegations are not findings.
- **DIRECT CORRESPONDENCE COPY:** establishes a communication and notice event to the extent sender, recipient, date and text are visible. A PDF printout is not a substitute for the native RFC822 message, full headers or attachments.
- **PARTY-AUTHORED VISUAL RECONSTRUCTION:** useful for chronology, diagrams, room/finca mapping and source discovery. Its legal characterisations, attribution and quantum remain allegations unless independently verified.
- **REGISTRY / NOTARIAL / CORPORATE ANNEX:** may establish the specific recorded corporate, notarial or registry proposition within its scope. It does not validate every surrounding allegation in the pleading that annexed it.

Use the repository labels:

`VERIFIED FACT` · `VERIFIED PROCEDURAL FACT` · `PARTY ALLEGATION` · `EVIDENCE-BASED INFERENCE` · `UNRESOLVED` · `CONTRADICTED/CORRECTED`.

No item in this bundle proves, merely by being pleaded or recorded, that any person committed usurpation, coacciones, daños, estafa, falsedad, administración desleal, fraude procesal or any other offence.

## 4. Procedural chronology and source-faithful effect

### 4.1 2 May 2018 — Arrecife DP 1132/2018 opening order

The signed order of Juzgado de Instrucción nº2 de Arrecife:

- records receipt of a complaint by Gil Marer against Construcciones/Constructora Acosta Matos and José Daniel Acosta Matos, together with other possible participants;
- records pleaded theories including usurpation, damages, extortion, fraud, procedural fraud and disloyal administration;
- summarises alleged site-presence/access episodes on **31 October 2017, 8 January 2018 and 1 March 2018**;
- states that at that initial stage it could not exclude that the alleged conduct might fall within coercion analysis;
- opens preliminary proceedings concerning those presence/access episodes;
- provisionally dismisses two specific alleged damage events of 6 and 19 March 2018 for lack of an identified perpetrator;
- inhibits/transfers the distinct alleged `maquinación engañosa` directed at the Mercantil nº1 concurso to the Las Palmas judicial deanery; and
- orders statements from the complainant, identified witnesses and José Daniel Acosta Matos, plus information from Concurso 36/2012.

**DECIDED:** the identified procedural opening, provisional partial dismissal, transfer and investigative measures.  
**NOT DECIDED:** guilt; the truth of every complaint allegation; whole-hotel title or lawful possession; the scope of any 7 June 2018 event; civil, tourism or registry authority; or the later DP/appeal result.

This order is a formal judicial bridge proving that access/control allegations pre-dating 7 June 2018 were already placed in a criminal-investigation route. It must not be misdescribed as a finding that the alleged acts occurred or were criminal.

### 4.2 12 May 2020 — Mercantil nº1 order

The signed Auto dismissed LPB's reposición against the 24 October 2019 order. Its principal legal treatment concerned res judicata and the interest figure. It also recorded that:

- common areas sold to CAM were alleged to have been demolished;
- a damage/revaluation expert report and judicial inspection were requested; and
- CAM was alleged to impede access through security personnel.

The court declined to address those requests in that reposición because they were introduced as new facts, were considered extemporaneous in that procedural vehicle and did not identify a legal error in the appealed order.

**DECIDED:** rejection of the requests within that reposición and confirmation of the appealed order.  
**NOT DECIDED:** whether demolition occurred as alleged; whether LPB/third-party property was affected; whether access was unlawfully blocked; quantum; responsibility; or the need for protection in another procedural vehicle.

The correct knowledge proposition is therefore **institutional notice by May 2020 plus procedural non-adjudication**, not a merits finding that the allegations were false.

### 4.3 18 December 2020 — Aweswell estate-conservation filing

Aweswell's filing said prior quarterly AC reports did not adequately address the state of the active estate and repeated/expanded allegations concerning:

- demolition and deterioration of LPB assets and common areas;
- security preventing inspection;
- possible occupation/intervention in LPB apartments;
- consequences for value, free competition and third-party offers; and
- the AC's conservation duty under the then-cited TRLC provisions.

It requested:

1. access for experts paid by Aweswell;
2. a prohibition/warning against further CAM works affecting LPB or non-convalidated assets;
3. judicial inspection;
4. testimony/remission to Fiscalía; and
5. an AC report on the state of the active estate and relevant works.

**Classification:** PARTY ALLEGATION + VERIFIED REQUEST. The filing does not by itself prove the alleged condition, attribution or loss.

### 4.4 16 February 2021 — CAM's contrary party position

CAM's opposition contains several important admissions/positions that future threads must preserve alongside the accusation:

- CAM said it was **exclusively a concursal creditor** in relation to LPB and did not own LPB apartments at that date;
- CAM said it did hold full title to **54 apartments** acquired from third parties in Sun Park;
- CAM said 2018 interior works were limited to its own units, were undertaken under municipal licence and were later paused;
- CAM said security/access measures rested on Community resolutions intended to protect the complex; and
- CAM said the AC participated in Community meetings in representation of LPB.

**Classification:** PARTY POSITION / COUNTERCASE. The filing does not independently prove the 54-unit schedule, exact works perimeter, licence scope, validity of Community authority or absence of impact on LPB/Matkator/third-party rights. It is nonetheless a material source against any undifferentiated proposition that CAM itself claimed whole-hotel legal ownership in February 2021.

### 4.5 24 February 2021 — Mercantil nº1 estate-conservation order

The signed Auto:

- accepted that the earlier providencia's motivation was too brief and partially upheld Aweswell's appeal only to replace that motivation;
- recorded the AC's position that it had not delivered LPB assets to CAM, that CAM was another unit owner, that the AC had requested information from the Community/CAM and that no prospective bidder had requested inspection after publication of the direct-sale authorisation;
- recorded CAM's position concerning 54 owned apartments, own-unit works and Community security measures;
- said the photographs showed interior works without establishing whether the units belonged to LPB or CAM;
- treated debris in common areas as producing no demonstrated damage beyond temporary aesthetic effect;
- noted common-area works connected with a sale later not convalidated, while reasoning that their commencement had initially been supported by a public deed and appeared to have stopped;
- declined judicial inspection as not useful because the photographs already showed the condition;
- declined an expert damage valuation in the ongoing sale/improvement context;
- reaffirmed that estate conservation was a function of the AC; and
- expressly observed that a request of this type might have meaning in a possible liability action against CAM or the AC, while concluding that it would delay liquidation in the then-current procedural context.

**DECIDED:** the motivation correction and continued denial of inspection/expert/prohibition measures in that liquidation-stage application.  
**NOT DECIDED:** that no LPB or third-party property had been entered or changed; that no compensable damage existed; that the exact photographed units were CAM's; that Community/security authority was valid as to every space; that the AC had fulfilled every conservation duty; or that liability claims were unfounded.

This is a high-value `KNOWN → REQUESTED → RESPONSE → WHAT HAPPENED NEXT` node. It narrows both sides:

- the allegation side cannot say the court judicially found the alleged destruction proved;
- the defence side cannot use the denial as a merits exoneration of all works, access or conservation issues.

### 4.6 8 March 2021 — direct notice/request to the AC

Gil Marer's email printout requests written authority for Gil, experts and a notary to enter LPB assets, inspect them and quantify alleged damage. It invokes the 24 February order's treatment of the AC's conservation function and asks the AC to instruct persons controlling access to permit entry.

The email also contains extensive allegations concerning 7 June 2018, Community actors, CAM, pre-title control, investor material and the effect on LPB and other owners.

**Directly established:** a detailed request/notice was sent to the AC at the visible address/date in the printout.  
**Not established:** the truth of the embedded allegations; whether the email and attachments are complete; whether the AC responded, authorised or refused; whether experts attended; whether the email was filed in court; or any resulting damage calculation.

The native email, full headers, attachments and complete reply chain remain high-priority evidence.

### 4.7 20 February 2025 — initial Matkator complaint

The first page bears a 20 February 2025 Juzgado de Instrucción nº2 de La Laguna stamp and digital signature. The complaint was directed initially against Hotel New Trend, S.L. and Canarian Hospitality, S.L. It alleges that Matkator's Sun Park units were:

- structurally changed without Matkator authorisation;
- reconfigured/fused or divided into hotel rooms;
- fitted with digital locks that excluded the owner;
- marketed and exploited within MYND Yaiza; and
- used without accounts or payment of generated exploitation income to Matkator.

The accompanying 17-page `Documento Uno` is a party-authored visual reconstruction. It uses Registry/Catastro extracts, Gesvalt plans/images, GrafCan aerial views, RICPE project material, BORME and hotel/OTA/public images to advance the following source-derived claims:

- original Sun Park consisted of 262 registered elements, described as 220 apartments, 40 commercial premises and 2 solariums;
- the later project is described as 324 rooms and 6 commercial premises;
- former apartments 511 and 507 correspond to Matkator fincas 8584 and 8588;
- the reconstruction maps apartment 511 to room 1503 plus part of room 1515, and apartment 507 to room 1507 plus part of room 1517;
- original approximately 48.62 m² apartment layouts with living room/kitchen/bathroom/bedroom/terrace/patio were allegedly divided or fused into smaller hotel-room configurations; and
- ownership quotas and the original title configuration were allegedly altered in practical/project presentation without the required owner consent/registration.

These propositions are **PARTY ALLEGATION / SOURCE-LED RECONSTRUCTION** unless the exact project, cadastral, Registry, physical-survey and consent chain proves them unit by unit.

The bundle also contains corporate/notarial/Companies House material relevant to Matkator's incorporation, shareholding/administration history and the UK Monterecco Sun Park Limited → Aweswell Limited name continuity. Apply CR-030 and do not reproduce unnecessary personal data.

### 4.8 Damages slide in the 20 February bundle

The visual annex calculates alleged lost profit of **€13,432 annually and €94,024 over seven years per apartment**. The slide uses assumptions including a €184 room-night figure, 80% occupancy, 50% operating cost and a 50/50 profit allocation with the operator.

**Classification:** PARTY QUANTIFICATION / NOT AN INDEPENDENT EXPERT REPORT. Do not cite it as verified loss, audited revenue, judicially accepted quantum or a substitute for actual room mapping, opening dates, rates, occupancy, costs, contracts, tax records and accounts.

### 4.9 6/7 March 2025 — amplification / DP 552/2025

The later version expands the complaint to:

- identify Laura Patricia Acosta Matos and José Daniel Acosta Matos as additional reported persons;
- add Matkator's asserted interest in fincas 8497 and 8498 and the allegation that later notes simples showed Hotel New Trend as registered owner from December 2022 without a transfer known to Matkator;
- plead possible usurpation, alteration of boundaries/terms, coercion, damages and real-estate fraud; and
- request statements, documentary evidence and a precautionary prohibition on disposal or preventive annotation.

**Classification:** VERIFIED PROCEDURAL/PARTY ALLEGATION. The legal labels and responsibility propositions are not findings. The 8497/8498 discrepancy may reflect a valid instrument, Registry error, identity/perimeter issue or another explanation; falsity is not established without the title instrument, Registry presentation/inscription basis and complete chain.

### 4.10 13 March 2025 — DP 552 → ETJ 163/2020 bridge

The ETJ filing says:

- the 20 February complaint generated DP 552/2025;
- the amplification was presented on 7 March;
- the criminal allegations were a new fact relevant to the earlier September/October 2024 suspension/nullity applications;
- the physical asset no longer matched the apartment description used in the execution/appraisal;
- the appraisal was said to be stale and not to reflect current physical/possessory condition; and
- the criminal investigation could create prejudicialidad penal.

It asks the civil court to:

1. take the new facts into account under article 286 LEC;
2. consider nullity of the auction;
3. appoint a judicial expert/tasador to inspect and update valuation; and
4. permit Matkator's personation/access to the originating main file.

**Directly established:** the party made the identified transmission and requests.  
**Not established:** that the auction was void; that the original asset legally ceased to exist; that a criminal-prejudiciality threshold was met; that the appraisal was legally unusable; that a judicial expert was appointed; or that the ETJ court granted any requested relief. The court's response to this filing remains missing.

## 6. Cross-proceeding significance

### A. DP 1132/2018 — pre-7-June notice, not a whole-hotel merits finding

The 2 May 2018 opening order now supplies a primary procedural anchor for attributed access/owner-like conduct before 7 June. The complete docket, statements, later 7 June material, archive order and Auto 804/2018 remain necessary. Do not use the opening order as a conviction or use the later archive as civil/title authority broader than its actual scope.

### B. Concurso 36/2012 — knowledge and procedural non-adjudication

The 12 May 2020 and 24 February 2021 Autos show that alleged demolition, access restrictions, uncertain ownership of photographed works and requests for inspection/expert protection were in the court record. The court rejected or denied the measures in the particular procedural contexts described above. That is materially different from either:

- a judicial finding that all alleged damage occurred and was attributable to CAM/AC; or
- a judicial finding that no damage, exclusion or conservation breach occurred.

### C. AC notice and conservation route

The 24 February order identifies the AC as responsible for conservation in the court's reasoning. The 8 March email is a direct follow-on request to the AC. The absence of a located reply is a **response gap**, not proof that no response occurred. Recover the native thread, any written authority/refusal, expert attendance attempts and AC/court reports.

### D. DP 552/2025 — extraconcursal Matkator track

DP 552 is legally distinct from DP 1901/2026, DP 1956/2026, DP 748/2026 and the LPB concurso. It concerns alleged conduct affecting Matkator rights and identified HNT/Canarian Hospitality/individual actors. Common facts or documents create possible transmission bridges; they do not merge the proceedings.

### E. ETJ 163/2020 — civil execution consequence remains open

The 13 March filing proves an attempted transmission of the DP 552 allegations into the civil execution and an application for nullity/expert inspection/personation. It does not prove that the civil court accepted the criminal premise, suspended execution or invalidated the auction. Later ETJ orders and current title/remate consequences must be read separately.

### F. Three-plane consequence discipline

For every later analysis maintain:

`LPB concursal estate effect | Matkator/third-party extraconcursal effect | Aweswell cross-border/shareholder effect`.

A work, lock, court order, deed, operating representation or hotel revenue may affect more than one plane, but the owner, legal basis, mechanism, causation and remedy must be established separately.
