## 1. Executive verdict

The 2008 Montelanza accounts **materially strengthen the governance-capture investigation**, but in a specific and bounded way.

They do not prove that the Owners' Community, CEXP, FMMM, AAS/Molina actors, CAM, the insolvency administrator or any public/professional actor committed an offence. They do something more foundational:

> **They provide a contemporaneous “before” record showing that Sun Park was an operating tourist business with service income, staff, substantial fixed assets and an expressly planned transfer of assets for continued exploitation, while legal title was being fragmented through parallel unit sales.**

The accounts therefore sharpen the central transition question:

> **What exact deed, Community/CEXP act, ownership schedule, operating contract and representation chain carried Montelanza's 2008 operating/property position into the governance structure that existed by February–June 2011?**

That transition is the hinge. If it was complete, properly documented and lawfully implemented, the later governance-capture hypothesis weakens materially. If it was incomplete, selectively represented, documentarily manipulated or used to turn limited Community powers into control over operation, debt, voting, access and private property, it becomes the earliest structural bridge in the alleged capture-and-conversion sequence.

## 3. What the 2008 accounts directly establish

### 3.1 Montelanza was an operating tourism company, not merely a passive shell

Page 11 states that Montelanza's objects included:

- purchase, sale, leasing and exploitation of immovables;
- promotion, construction and sale of bungalows, apartments and tourist premises;
- operation of hotels, residences, apartments and bungalows under hotel/tourism regimes.

The 2008 profit-and-loss account records:

- turnover: **€1,304,632.79**;
- sales: **€411,450.94**;
- services: **€893,181.85**;
- personnel expense: **€368,746.75**;
- external services: **€512,944.18**.

**Controlled conclusion:** Montelanza had a real operating/business function and a service structure in 2008. The accounts are inconsistent with describing the historical Sun Park platform as merely a collection of unrelated residential units.

### 3.2 A major asset/business transition occurred on 17 June 2008

Page 12 states that a sale for **€1,368,000**, parallel to apartment sales by a number of owners:

- meant cessation of Montelanza's activity; and
- placed the transferred assets at the purchaser's disposal for exploitation.

Page 17 records tangible-fixed-asset disposals of **€3,064,576.00** from an initial cost of **€3,232,654.69**, leaving final net book value of **€119,820.01**.

**Controlled conclusion:** the 17-June transaction was represented in the company's own accounts as an operating transition, not only a financial disposal. The precise purchaser, assets, fincas, title and legal operating succession require deeds and Land Registry proof.

### 3.3 Fragmentation remained after the principal sale

Page 12 records an option, on the same conditions, over **11 unsold premises**, expiring 31 December 2010.

**Controlled conclusion:** the 2008 transition did not necessarily eliminate all residual Montelanza title or all owner-by-owner fragmentation. This is directly relevant to later Community/CEXP voting, debt, representation and operating authority.

### 3.4 Montelanza's corporate exit was financially stressed

The accounts record:

- total assets: **€360,192.84**;
- negative equity: **€321,742.56**;
- result for the year: **-€1,550,210.37**;
- impairment/result on fixed-asset disposals: **-€1,222,514.83**;
- shareholder support approved: **€704,000**;
- contributions made by 30 March 2008: **€497,000**;
- refund still pending at 31 December 2008: **€53,486.53**.

The board intended to realise financial investments, pay liabilities and dissolve/liquidate the company after realisation of assets.

**Controlled conclusion:** Montelanza was transferring the operating/asset platform while moving toward liquidation. That made correct succession of title, contracts, operator authority, books, liabilities and residual rights especially important.

### 3.5 Historic RIC/DIC existed — but must be kept separate from later RICPE/HNT

Page 18 records historical DIC/RIC assets and materialisations, including RIC 2000/2001 tables.

**Mandatory separation:**

`MONTELANZA HISTORIC RIC/DIC` **≠** `RIC PRIVATE EQUITY / HNT / MYND YAIZA SERIES F/G`.

An actual bridge requires source proof of the same asset, tax obligation, successor, eligible cost or legal continuity. Mere use of the letters “RIC” is not evidence of continuity or wrongdoing.

## 5. The pre-capture baseline

The accounts, read with the current repository, support this baseline:

1. Sun Park operated economically as one tourist/hotel platform.
2. Legal title was unitised and capable of owner-by-owner transfer.
3. Montelanza carried an operating role, personnel, service revenue and substantial fixed assets.
4. CEXP was approved in April 2008 by a substantial participating-owner majority, according to the current Community dossier, and was authorised to contract with Montelanza.
5. Montelanza transferred assets in June 2008 for continued exploitation and ceased its own activity.
6. Parallel apartment sales and 11 unsold premises meant title remained fragmented.
7. Therefore the new operator/PropCo/Community architecture required exact documentation of:
   - who owned what;
   - who operated what;
   - what CEXP received;
   - what LPB received;
   - what Montelanza retained;
   - what the Owners' Community administered;
   - what costs each organ/entity assumed;
   - who could vote, certify debt, hold records and control access.

## 7. Governance-capture hypothesis

### 7.1 Offence-neutral formulation

Por Derecho alleges that limited Community/CEXP functions may have been intentionally used to create practical power outside their legal perimeter:

`control of owner register / debt / voting`

→ `control of appointments, records and representation`

→ `control of operating narrative and expense attribution`

→ `control of access, security and keys`

→ `documents treated externally as “the Community decided”`

→ `acceptance by AC/court/authorities/professionals`

→ `CAM entry and later hotel-wide project`.

### 7.2 Why Montelanza strengthens this inquiry

The accounts show that the original operating capacity and assets were deliberately transitioned for continued exploitation while title remained fragmented. The later Owners' Community could not acquire the sold/unsold units, hotel operation or owner-derived rights simply because it administered common elements.

Therefore any later claim by a Community officeholder or private perimeter to represent the hotel as a whole must identify its legal bridge from:

- Montelanza;
- the purchaser/LPB;
- CEXP;
- each participating owner;
- and each residual/non-participating owner.

The absence or defect of that bridge would not itself prove crime. It would identify the point at which documentary appearance may have replaced underlying authority.

## 12. Strongest lawful explanation

A serious defence may establish that:

- the 2008 sale and CEXP/LPB transition were fully documented and lawfully implemented;
- participating owners validly created CEXP and assumed the agreed operating/common-cost architecture;
- residual owners retained only the rights shown by their deeds;
- AAS/FMMM appointments, proxies, coefficients and debt calculations were valid;
- voting exclusions complied with law and were not manufactured;
- Community litigation and security were genuine preservation measures;
- CAM acquired valid credit and units and used ordinary creditor/owner rights;
- the 2018 security/access acts stayed within authorised spaces or were reasonable responses to a deteriorating asset;
- later Community, judicial, administrative and financing acts had independent lawful foundations;
- ONA/finance would not have closed for unrelated reasons;
- HNT/MYND and protected third parties acquired autonomous later rights.

This defence must explain the whole chain, not merely one meeting, one invoice or one unit.
