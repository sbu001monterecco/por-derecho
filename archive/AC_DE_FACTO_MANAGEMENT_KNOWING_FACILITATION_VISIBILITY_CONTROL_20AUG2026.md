## Controlling public allegation

> **Por Derecho alleges a serious de facto-management and knowing-facilitation architecture:** private actors performed recurring management functions over Community authority, debt, voting, access, security, maintenance and practical control, while the Insolvency Administration requested, authorised, adopted, relied upon or insufficiently delimited important parts of that architecture. The investigation asks whether the documented functions crossed the threshold from lawful protection, creditor conduct or professional error into de facto administration, knowing facilitation, essential cooperation or coordinated participation.

This is an attributed allegation and investigative question. It is not a criminal judgment, a finding against every participant or a conclusion that every later user of the project knew the upstream facts.

## Reverse-engineering scan findings

### What was already strong

- A canonical ES/EN forensic page already separated documented functions, Por Derecho’s allegation and unproved criminal agreement.
- The controlled dataset already recorded stable private functions over debt, voting, access, security and maintenance and AC request/authorisation/reliance.
- The Textos Definitivos incident answer was already source-controlled.
- A cross-site runtime module already placed update and context panels on home, updates, Case Control Room, criminal investigation, Community, AC, takeover, adjudication, definitive-text, Acosta Matos, Calificación, judge and LAJ routes.
- Dedicated validation and public-edge verification were already live and deletion-safe with open evidence.

### Visibility gaps identified

1. The exact allegation—**serious de facto management and knowing facilitation**—was not the dominant headline on the home, updates, control-room or criminal-investigation surfaces.
2. The existing wording described “new reconstruction” and “functions/dependency” but did not state clearly enough that Por Derecho alleges an architecture affecting the entire case.
3. The causal dependency chain from Community authority to downstream title/finance was not shown in one compact visual block.
4. The route coverage stopped too early. Lender, Article 1535, insolvency, notarial, Registry, same-asset/multiple-funding, RICPE, CNMV and public-support routes did not all receive the allegation’s route-specific relevance.
5. The downstream-finance boundary needed to be explicit: upstream seriousness requires due-diligence and reliance answers, but does not transfer knowledge or guilt automatically.
6. The judge/LAJ boundary needed to be explicit: repeated favourable effects make notice, evidence and reasoning material but do not establish prevarication.
7. The public interface needed a finite “what would prove or disprove it” block so seriousness did not become rhetorical inflation.

## Por Derecho’s articulated allegation

Por Derecho alleges that:

1. the private Community/CAM-linked perimeter exercised unrecognised functional management over critical LPB/estate and wider hotel functions;
2. those functions were not adequately appointed, delimited, independently verified, accounted for or supervised;
3. the AC knowingly requested, enabled, adopted, used, relied upon or allowed important parts of that architecture to operate;
4. disputed authority and debt were thereby capable of becoming control, bid burden, liquidation advantage, adjudication, title and downstream operating/financial reality;
5. the conduct may amount—depending on actor-specific knowledge, intent, duty, benefit and causation—to de facto administration, knowing facilitation, essential cooperation or coordinated participation.

## What remains unproved

- criminal agreement or shared unlawful purpose;
- full administrator-in-fact status for every private actor or function;
- habitual obedience by the AC to private instructions;
- knowing falsity of a specific debt, minute, certificate or calculation;
- procedural fraud, falsity, disloyal administration, blanket double charging or prevarication;
- automatic knowledge or responsibility of later investors, advisers, operators, supervisors or funding bodies.
