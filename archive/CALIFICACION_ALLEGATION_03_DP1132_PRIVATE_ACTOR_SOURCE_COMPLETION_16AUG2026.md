## 1. AC declaration — direct authority chain now verified from the preserved court scan

The 31-Jul-2018 AC declaration materially strengthens the unitary analysis because it records the AC's own account of his role after liquidation.

### Community knowledge and governance

The AC said, in substance:

- he had known of the problems/conflict involving the Community and LPB since accepting the insolvency appointment;
- he knew the Community owed money to LPB because LPB held a high ownership quota;
- he did **not know whether unity of exploitation existed** at the time of his declaration; and
- he did **not know the cost of maintaining the complex**, saying that this did not correspond to the insolvency company but to the Community.

Those answers are important to Allegation 03 because the later calificación reasoning treated the hotel operation/rent as a central income and insolvency-causation mechanism. The source record therefore contains, within the same actor's knowledge, both a powerful rent theory and admitted limits concerning the hotel-wide operating/maintenance system.

### Security and the Community

The AC said that after liquidation, once he had effective control, he sought security because unauthorised persons were entering. He described Community owners proposing security providers; he selected one and asked the Community meeting to ratify payment.

He also stated that from January to June 2018 the principal thing he asked of the Community was to put a security service in place, and characterised the task of limiting entry after liquidation as a Community responsibility rather than an LPB function.

### Doors, LPB premises and Laura Acosta Matos

The preserved declaration records the AC saying, in substance:

- he authorised the Community to break/open doors of **LPB-owned premises where necessary**, identifying local 8 and expressly distinguishing this from apartments;
- Laura Acosta Matos called him concerning entry to an LPB local because of an alleged fault and he authorised entry;
- he authorised the Community to enter LPB premises for maintenance/access purposes; and
- he authorised **Laura Acosta Matos to enter any LPB-owned local** for supervision or maintenance of installations/common areas.

The AC expressly drew a limit: he said this was **not authorisation to use or occupy** the property, but to supervise/maintain.

### Controlled conclusion

This is stronger than the generic proposition `the AC knew the Community was involved`.

It establishes a direct, actor-specific chain:

`AC control after liquidation → AC security initiative → Community implementation channel → AC authorisation for entry into LPB premises → specific Laura Acosta Matos access authorisation for supervision/maintenance`.

It does **not** establish:

- authority to take possession of the whole hotel;
- authority over Matkator or third-party units;
- authorisation to exclude lawful occupants beyond the AC's stated LPB perimeter;
- authorisation to undertake structural works;
- criminal agreement between AC, Community actors and CAM; or
- that every later access/control act implemented those authorisations lawfully or within their limits.

**Classification:** VERIFIED FROM PRESERVED COURT DECLARATION; legal scope/effect and later implementation require act-by-act analysis.

## 2. Francisco Mario Matos Matas — Community administrator and debt/voting/security mechanics

The 20-Jul-2018 witness declaration records Francisco Mario Matos Matas as administrator of the Comunidad de Propietarios Sun Park since February 2011, acting through Pamalexsha/Pamalexsha S.L.

Material points include:

- he said Gil/LPB attended Community meetings but LPB had not voted because the Community treated LPB as owing more than €1 million;
- he said the debt had once been around €3 million, but a 2016 Community resolution retroactively reduced community charges, bringing the asserted debt to around €1 million;
- critically, when asked about the origin of that debt, he said **he did not know its origin**;
- he said the unit charge had moved from about €189/month to €22/month, retroactive to 2010, because the prior level was not justified by actual Community expenditure;
- he said LPB was denied a vote because the Community meeting decided so, and also referred to the AC having communicated limits on Gil's authority over economic matters;
- he said the 18-May-2018 security measure was adopted at the AC's request;
- he said CAM later supplied liquidity/paid amounts against Community debt that were used for urgent costs including security/insurance; and
- he said only Shaila voted at the May/June meetings because, on his account, she was the only owner then current with Community charges.

### Why this is central to the alleged Community capture thesis

This testimony directly joins:

`asserted debt → voting exclusion → Community decisions → AC-requested security/access → later CAM liquidity for urgent Community expenses`.

It also contains an internal verification issue: the person administering the Community since 2011 said he did not know the origin of the large debt that was being used to exclude LPB from voting and that had itself been dramatically reduced retroactively.

That is a **strong verification issue**. It is not proof that the debt was fabricated, nor proof that the voting exclusion was unlawful. Original ledgers, invoices, certifications, minutes, resolutions and legal basis remain necessary.

### Other limiting evidence

Matos Matas also gave evidence consistent with a lawful protective explanation:

- security was aimed at controlling access and safety;
- Community owners could enter on showing title;
- he described unsafe/uncertain utilities and unlicensed activities;
- he said the Community did not intend to exploit the hotel; and
- he denied a family relationship with José Daniel Acosta Matos.

**Classification:** VERIFIED TESTIMONY; debt validity, voting legality, financial influence and improper purpose remain unresolved.

## 3. Shaila Cogolludo Ramos — implementation link between Community, AC rules and CAM access

Shaila's 20-Jul-2018 investigada declaration records that she worked for the Community administrator, served as Community vice-president and had been an owner.

Material points include:

- the Community adopted security on 18-May-2018 because the AC requested access control in light of his responsibility for LPB units;
- after that meeting, access codes/locks were changed as needed;
- the Community had not previously undertaken hotel-wide maintenance and said it could enter the complex only from 18 May 2018 onward;
- the Community allowed José Daniel Acosta Matos and CAM personnel to enter because CAM owned units;
- she said the Community remained in contact with the AC and that **Community decisions were communicated to and had the AC's approval**;
- she said lock changes occurred by Community agreement with the approval of the AC and attending owners; and
- she personally transmitted to the security guards an AC document/rule concerning which occupants could enter LPB apartments, stating that the restriction came from the AC.

### Evidential significance

This supplies a direct implementation chain from AC decision/rule to Community office to private security at the physical entrance.

It also connects that access-control system to CAM access as an owner.

This still does not prove that CAM controlled the Community, that the restrictions were unlawful, or that CAM's access exceeded its title. But it makes the Community an operational transmission mechanism rather than merely background governance.

**Classification:** VERIFIED INVESTIGADA DECLARATION; legality, authority limits and improper coordination unresolved.

## 4. Laura Isabel Acosta Matos — timing, Community meetings, access and liquidity

Laura's 20-Jul-2018 declaration records that she was Consejera Delegada of Construcciones Acosta Matos S.A., with administrative/legal functions, and that CAM had acquired units and a creditor position.

Material points include:

- CAM was acquiring units from late 2017 and had a credit position in LPB's insolvency from October 2017;
- she attended the Community meetings in May and June 2018;
- she said she knew from the 18-May meeting that security would be placed at the complex and went on **7 June 2018** because she wanted to access the interior;
- she said she arrived when the guard was already there and denied ordering lock changes;
- she said CAM was paying Community arrears/charges and that the liquidity it provided would presumably fund the most urgent costs;
- she said she was concerned about electrical installations and possible owner liability; and
- she denied causing damage or presenting CAM as owner of the entire complex.

Read together with the AC's 31-Jul statement, the source record therefore contains **both sides of a direct access-authority relationship**: Laura says she attended the Community security meeting and came on 7 June to access; the AC separately says he authorised Laura to enter LPB-owned locals for supervision/maintenance.

That is a material coordination/authority fact. Its lawful scope remains disputed.

**Classification:** VERIFIED INVESTIGADA DECLARATION + AC CROSS-CORROBORATION; unlawful takeover/occupation not established.

## 8. Why this source completion changes Allegation 03

The calificación rent branch treated the economic result primarily as a failure by Gil/Pink to collect/pay rent and inferred aggravation of insolvency.

The expanded primary record requires the decision-maker to confront a wider system:

`Community debt/voting → availability of units/governance → operating costs and set-offs → actual hotel cash → liquidation → AC security/access decisions → Community implementation → CAM-linked access and liquidity → effect on occupation/operation/value → realistically recoverable rent → insolvency aggravation`.

The point is **not** that the private actors automatically caused the earlier rent non-payment. Much of the CAM-linked access evidence is 2017–2018, later than the 2012–2015 rent period. The chronology must remain honest.

Its importance is instead threefold:

1. the pre-existing Community/minority conflict was already an asserted operating constraint during the rent period;
2. the AC had direct notice of the Community/debt/voting explanation before his calificación report; and
3. after liquidation, the AC himself used the Community/private-owner/security architecture in ways that make his later account of hotel operation, possession, access and causation subject to a reciprocal consistency audit.

Do not collapse those three periods.
