This addendum makes the PH122 → CAM assignment, DP 1041/2017 and the linked creditor-recognition sequence **material events in the Sun Park storyline**. They must not be treated merely as banking footnotes.

## Material-event insertions

### 12 July 2016 — pre-assignment quantified banking dispute

LPB had already communicated a quantified challenge concerning elements of the banking liabilities to the insolvency administration. The figures remain party allegations pending full reconciliation, but the existence of the dispute is material to the later assignment/retracto analysis.

**Storyline significance:** the 2017 transfer occurred against a pre-existing debtor-side controversy, not a documentary blank slate.

### 20 October 2017 — PH122 assigns the mortgage-credit position to CAM

Promontoria Holding 122 B.V. is identified as assignor and Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. as assignee of the relevant mortgage credits against LPB.

**Storyline significance:** CAM moves from the broader Sun Park perimeter into the banking-credit position itself. This credit acquisition must be read separately from later unit ownership, control, liquidation and hotel-operation developments.

### 6 December 2017 — LPB demands deed, price and transaction economics

After learning of the assignment, LPB sought the deed and price and asserted that it needed the information to preserve potential rights, including its Article 1535 position.

**Storyline significance:** the assignment economics become an express debtor-side legal issue.

### 18–19 December 2017 — two judicial tracks run in parallel

**Track A — CAM creditor substitution:** CAM asks the Commercial Court to amend the final insolvency texts so it replaces the prior creditor. The Commercial Court refers the request to the insolvency administration.

**Track B — LPB information/retracto:** on 19 December, JPI nº 2 admits DP 1041/2017, finds just cause and legitimate interest and orders production of the assignment deed and the price/payment/cost information.

**Storyline significance:** the same underlying assignment now produces two parallel legal pathways — one toward CAM recognition, the other toward LPB disclosure and potential Article 1535 optionality.

### 10 January 2018 — AC reports favourably on CAM substitution

The insolvency administration gives a favourable report on the requested creditor change, subject to the qualification later recorded in the Commercial Court order.

**Storyline significance:** the AC becomes a material validating node in the CAM creditor-recognition route.

### 12 January 2018 — DP 1041 disclosure route becomes operational

JPI nº 2 sets the opposition/appearance timetable and preserves procedural consequences for unjustified non-appearance.

**Storyline significance:** LPB's route to the deed/economics is live and procedurally operational.

### 26 January 2018 — CAM documentation completed in Commercial Court route

The later Commercial Court order records that documentation was completed and the matter awaited decision.

### 7 February 2018 — LPB opposes CAM recognition terms

LPB challenges amounts and maintains that the transferred credit is litigioso.

### 8 February 2018 — Commercial Court recognises CAM and rejects LPB's Article 1535 theory

The controlled primary order is body-dated **8 February 2018**, Judge-signed **9 February** and LAJ-signed **14 February**. `15FEB2018` is a source filename/notification alias, not the operative decision date. It:

- partially corrects amounts in LPB's favour;
- rejects LPB's litigated-credit position;
- recognises CAM as assignee creditor; and
- expressly treats complete production of CAM's supporting title documentation as unnecessary for the substitution because the AC has reported favourably and is presumed to have verified the requirements.

**Storyline significance:** this is a convergence point between private credit acquisition, insolvency-administration verification and judicial recognition. It also creates a notable comparative-treatment question because a separate court was requiring production of the deed/economics to LPB.

### February–March 2018 — fate of DP 1041 remains a P0 primary-source gap

The project record indicates that DP 1041/2017 subsequently ceased/was archived, but the current 19 August re-scan has not yet rebound the exact primary withdrawal/archive act, signatory, instruction chain and date.

**Storyline significance:** do not publish “the AC personally withdrew/killed the claim” as verified until the cessation act is recovered. Publish the stronger presently supportable effect question instead: **what measurable LPB estate benefit was obtained if a live information/retracto route was surrendered or lost?**

Javier and Estefanía Sixto later reviewed archived material from the Promontoria/retracto/AC sequence in the course of subsequent work. No later Sixto communication currently located gives a definitive merits conclusion that the Article 1535 claim was viable or non-viable.

**Storyline significance:** the episode remained relevant as historical evidence of credit-transfer, AC conduct and procedural-control questions even after the original DP was no longer live.

### 2026 — current forensic position

The present Por Derecho position is not that LPB possessed a certain winning Article 1535 claim. It is that:

1. LPB obtained a judicially admitted route to the assignment economics;
2. CAM separately obtained insolvency recognition with express reliance on favourable AC verification;
3. the Commercial Court rejected LPB's technical litigated-credit theory but did not itself require all supporting title documents before recognising CAM;
4. no measurable LPB estate benefit from the later loss/termination of the information/retracto route has yet been identified; and
5. the exact termination act and Cristo handover remain priority evidence targets.
