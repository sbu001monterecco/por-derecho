### Evidential classifications

- **PF — verified primary-document fact:** explicit in a signed report, pleading, judgment, formal notification or contemporaneous source.
- **PS — verified procedural status:** established by a formal procedural record or finite retrieval check.
- **EI — evidence-based inference:** a reasoned conclusion from identified facts, not an express primary-source statement.
- **GA — Gil/LPB/PINK allegation:** a disputed allegation or legal characterisation expressly attributed to that side.
- **OA — opposing allegation:** a proposition advanced by the AC, Fiscalía or another opposing actor.
- **UC — unresolved contradiction:** materially inconsistent propositions not yet reconciled.
- **EG — evidential gap:** decisive source or actor-specific knowledge proof still missing.

## 3. Legal frame — normal purpose, not a criminal conviction

Under the former Ley Concursal applicable to this section, the calificación process examined whether the debtor company’s insolvency was generated or aggravated by legally specified conduct, identified affected persons or accomplices, and could impose disqualification and patrimonial consequences. A culpable classification is not, by itself, a criminal conviction. Sentencia 163/2023 itself applied the former Article 164.1 because of the date on which the sixth section was opened.

Gil’s separate thesis is that this mechanism was instrumentally used to invert and divert the factual record. That is a documented allegation, not an adjudicated criminal finding. Its strongest support is not rhetoric but the source chain: a broad accusation package; contrary material inside the AC’s own report; a two-page Fiscal adoption; major judicial rejections and narrowing; retained findings contested for causation, proof and procedural fairness; and the wider control chronology.

## 5. What the Ministerio Fiscal actually signed

The 12-Mar-2019 opinion is two pages. It requested culpable classification on five headings:

1. the literal text says `situación de insolvencia agravada por dolo o culapa del administrador concursal`;
2. relevant accounting irregularities;
3. alzamiento de bienes;
4. failure to request the concurso within the legal period; and
5. failure to collaborate with the AC.

It identified Uri Omid and Gil Marer as responsible administrators and Patricia Domínguez and PINK as accomplices. It requested 15-year disqualification for natural persons, loss of rights, damages and deficit coverage.

### 5.1 Facial role contradiction

The first bullet literally attributes aggravation to intent/fault of the **insolvency administrator** and then identifies the social administrators as responsible. This may be a drafting error. The verified fact is the text. It must not be silently corrected into a different accusation, nor may a drafting defect alone prove bad faith.

### 5.2 Adoption depth

The opinion does not set out a debtor-by-debtor receivables analysis, a transaction-by-transaction alzamiento tracing exercise, an actor-specific knowledge analysis, or a proposition-by-proposition reconciliation of the contrary evidence reproduced in the AC report. It requests severe consequences and identifies the AC report and proceeding material as evidence. The publication-safe conclusion is:

> **PF:** the Fiscal adopted a five-heading accusation package and maximum-severity consequences in a two-page opinion.  
> **GA/EI:** Gil alleges that this was not an independent evidential audit and that the institutional adoption converted the AC’s narrative into a public-prosecution accusation without confronting its internal contradictions.  
> **EG:** proof of the prosecutor’s personal reading and knowledge of each contrary document remains actor-specific and incomplete.

## 7. Person-by-person outcome and sanctions

### 7.1 LPB — Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U.

**First-instance finding:** its concurso was declared culpable on four operative bases in the fallo:

1. the general clause, but in substance only the PINK rent/non-collection branch survived;
2. substantial breach of accounting duties;
3. late filing, allocated personally to Uri Omid rather than Gil; and
4. narrowed non-collaboration concerning support for identified CEXP/third-party receivables.

LPB is the debtor company whose concurso was classified. It is not accurate to describe LPB as criminally convicted.

### 7.2 Gil Marer

**Adverse grounds personally attributed:**

1. failure to claim PINK rent or terminate the arrangement;
2. substantial accounting breach; and
3. narrowed failure to collaborate over receivable support.

**Express exclusion:** late filing was **not** attributed to Gil.

**Consequences in Sentencia 163/2023:**

- 10-year disqualification, to run from finality;
- loss of any creditor rights against the concurso/estate; and
- €3,032,010.34 damages to the active estate plus statutory interest described in the fallo.

These findings and consequences are appealed. Do not present them as final while the appeal remains pending.

### 7.3 Pink Canary Services, S.L.

**Only surviving role:** accomplice in the rent non-payment/non-collection branch.

**Consequences:** loss of creditor rights. The calificación judgment did not duplicate the €3,032,010.34 award against PINK because PINK had already been condemned and was the enforced party in the rent proceeding.

PINK was **not** held liable in either alzamiento branch, accounting, late filing or collaboration. Its appeal challenges complicity, profitability/ability-to-pay inferences, causation, operating-cost evidence and procedural fairness.

### 7.4 Uri Omid

**Only ground:** late filing.  
**Consequences:** 2-year disqualification from finality and loss of creditor rights.  
The AC expressly said it lacked information to justify damages/deficit against Uri.

### 7.5 Patricia Domínguez

The AC and Fiscalía sought complicity and major consequences. The judgment rejected complicity because the AC report did not contain sufficient argumentation to attribute the required conduct personally. No unnecessary private identifiers may be published.
