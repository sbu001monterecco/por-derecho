# Project Sun Rock / Por Derecho — Maximum-Reach Outbound Campaign Layer

**Control date:** 23 August 2026  
**Status:** mandatory campaign/exposure supplement to `archive/OUTBOUND_EMAIL_COMMUNICATIONS_PROTOCOL_23AUG2026.md`  
**Send-control rule:** `EMAIL_SEND_FINAL_AUTHORIZATION_RULE.md`

## 1. Purpose

This layer governs how Project Sun Rock / Por Derecho converts safe recipient-specific email preparation into a controlled exposure and engagement programme.

The media-distribution objective is maximum credible coverage across all suitable verified named professionals and useful official newsroom routes. Recipient count is pursued through role-based personalisation, not blind duplication. Optimise:

`RELEVANT REACH × RECIPIENT CREDIBILITY × MESSAGE COMPREHENSION × EVIDENTIAL CREDIBILITY × DELIVERABILITY × CHANCE OF MEANINGFUL ACTION × FOLLOW-UP TRACEABILITY`

while minimising:

`DUPLICATION × CORRECTION RISK × RECIPIENT FATIGUE × ATTACHMENT OVERLOAD × CROSS-AUDIENCE CONFUSION × PROCEDURAL INVALIDITY × LEGAL/PUBLICATION RISK`.

This supplement does **not** authorise any email to be sent. If any part conflicts with `EMAIL_SEND_FINAL_AUTHORIZATION_RULE.md`, the final-authorisation rule controls.

## 2. Seven audience lanes

Every material prospective recipient must be classified before drafting.

### A. Journalist / editor / media gatekeeper
Goal: independent examination and wider public exposure.

### B. Investigative / cross-border media
Goal: jurisdiction-specific investigation, collaborative reporting or specialist cross-border interest, including tourism, finance, insolvency, public funds and FTI/Meeting Point themes where relevant.

### C. Public authority / regulator / prosecutor / court-related office
Goal: a finite institutional act, routing, preservation, incorporation, reasoned decision or formal investigation within competence.

### D. Professional body / auditor / law firm / compliance / custodian
Goal: knowledge, records, preservation, conflicts, instructions, due diligence, professional responsibility or right of response.

### E. Local / political / public-institutional actor
Goal: public-interest understanding, constructive dialogue, tourism/local-governance response, policy action or working meeting.

### F. Investor / financial-market / transaction actor
Goal: asset provenance, disclosure, funding, source-and-use, due diligence, governance and economic consequence.

### G. Witness / source / historical participant
Goal: preserve and obtain the person's own records or recollection without leading or contaminating it.

Do not use one audience's email merely by changing the salutation.

## 3. Recipient priority matrix

Score each potential recipient from 0–5 for:

- relevance to the specific story module;
- ability to act or amplify;
- audience / institutional reach;
- documentary connection;
- likelihood of understanding the material;
- language / jurisdiction fit;
- previous engagement;
- channel reliability;
- duplication / fatigue risk; and
- legal / reputational sensitivity.

Then classify:

- **TIER A — bespoke high-value gatekeeper**;
- **TIER B — specialist audience**;
- **TIER C — wider awareness**;
- **RESEARCH ACTIVE — fit, identity or route not yet sufficiently verified; do not guess**.

A high-recipient-count list is not by itself evidence of high reach; under `archive/MAXIMUM_MEDIA_DISTRIBUTION_MULTI_RECIPIENT_RULE_23AUG2026.md`, every suitable verified named media lane nevertheless remains active and no same-outlet hold or spacing rule applies.

## 4. Controlled exposure waves

Default campaign architecture:

### Wave 1 — Tier A
A small number of carefully personalised gatekeepers. Each must receive a strong, specific reason why the story matters to them.

### Wave 2 — specialist clusters
Sector-specific journalists, regulators, professional bodies, investors, investigators or cross-border actors using audience-specific versions and asks.

### Wave 3 — wider awareness
Broader circulation only once the controlling factual package is stable and the earlier waves have not identified a material correction or better route.

Do not place materially different audiences into one large CC distribution merely to increase recipient count.

## 5. Channel-preflight gate

Before drafting, verify:

1. current recipient identity;
2. current professional role;
3. address from a reliable source;
4. pagination-complete Gmail history for the person **and**, independently, the outlet/employer/organisation under `archive/PRE_SEND_GMAIL_PERSON_OUTLET_HISTORY_GATE_23AUG2026.md`;
5. previous bounce or delivery failure;
6. existing ticket/reference/expediente/thread;
7. whether a specialist mailbox or channel exists;
8. whether email is legally/procedurally sufficient for the requested act;
9. whether the intended message should remain in an existing thread; and
10. whether a prior exclusion or correction affects the route.

If a previous address bounced, do not reuse it without fresh verification.

If either the person or organisation scan is incomplete, a continuation token remains, a relevant thread is unread or a colleague/fallback/other-channel collision is unclassified, the channel preflight fails closed. Do not describe the target as untouched merely because no direct `to/from` domain email was located.

If a specialist working channel has already produced a response, prefer it over a generic information address unless there is a documented reason not to.

## 6. Exposure is not formal filing

For every authority-facing communication classify the channel internally as one of:

- **INFORMATION / AWARENESS**;
- **FORMAL NOTICE**;
- **EVIDENTIAL SUPPLEMENT**;
- **PRESERVATION REQUEST**;
- **FORMAL FILING**;
- **PROCEDURAL SUBMISSION**.

Never assume an email is a legally effective complaint, appeal or filing.

Where signature, electronic register, portal submission or another formal channel is required:

`EMAIL = notice/supporting communication`

`FORMAL CHANNEL = legally operative submission`.

Record the two separately.

## 7. Exposure-package levels

### Level 0 — link-led
No attachment unless necessary. Best for relationship-based or introductory approaches.

### Level 1 — journalist first look
Normally:
- two strongest source maps / explanatory visuals;
- exact website or dossier link;
- one controlled audiovisual or primary public source.

### Level 2 — evidential
Two visuals plus a small number of decisive primary documents. Best for regulators, compliance, professional bodies and serious follow-ups.

### Level 3 — indexed dossier
A fuller primary-source package with an index. Use when requested or procedurally necessary.

Do not send the largest package by default.

## 8. Media first-screen rule

For journalists/editors, the first approximately 100–150 words should answer:

- **WHY YOU?**
- **WHAT IS THE STORY?**
- **WHY DOES IT MATTER NOW?**
- **WHAT CAN YOU VERIFY QUICKLY?**

Then provide one strong public-interest thesis, three to five documentary anchors, one finite ask, limited links and a proportionate attachment package.

The first email should normally seek interest in examining the evidence rather than reproduce the whole repository.

## 11. Controlling version rule

Every important outbound package must have:

- `COMMUNICATION_ID`;
- controlling version;
- source cutoff date;
- recipient set;
- audience lane;
- tier;
- campaign wave;
- language;
- Attachment Manifest;
- Link Manifest;
- channel status; and
- send status.

Only one version may be designated **CONTROLLING**.

If a substantive error is found before send, replace/correct the draft.

If found after send, identify exactly which recipients were affected, assess materiality, prepare a targeted correction, and obtain new user authorisation. Do not automatically broadcast another correction.

## 12. Anti-accidental-send rule

Never use a real Gmail send action to:

- test connector behaviour;
- test draft creation;
- test attachment handling;
- verify an address;
- verify a subject;
- test deletion;
- test threading;
- test recipient formatting; or
- test whether a send function works.

No placeholder, dummy, `x`, `DO NOT SEND`, invalid-address or test message may be sent from the real account.

Testing must remain draft-only or use non-mail tooling.

The only permitted workflow is:

`RESEARCH → DRAFT → READ-BACK → OUTBOUND PACKAGE MANIFEST → USER EXPLICIT FINAL AUTHORISATION → SEND EXACT REVIEWED DRAFT → READ SENT COPY → SENT + VERIFIED`.

## 13. Thread-first follow-up

If a recipient or institution generated a:

- ticket number;
- expediente;
- case/reference number;
- acknowledgment;
- substantive reply; or
- explicit request to add material by replying,

future evidence should normally stay in that existing thread/reference unless a documented reason justifies a new communication.

Avoid parallel submissions that force the recipient to reconstruct chronology.

## 14. Response-based follow-up

- **SUBSTANTIVE RESPONSE:** answer the actual issue raised.
- **PROMISED REVIEW PERIOD:** do not chase before the promised period expires.
- **ACKNOWLEDGMENT / TICKET:** preserve the reference and use it for later supplements.
- **OOO:** record the return date; any alternative address is a candidate only and never authorises automatic resend.
- **BOUNCE:** stop the route and re-verify before proposing another send.
- **NO REPLY:** do not interpret as refusal or non-receipt; consider proportionate follow-up based on recipient type and elapsed time.
- **REQUEST FOR FORMAL FILING:** separate the operative filing route from the awareness email.

## 15. Exposure intelligence

Measure useful exposure by observable outcomes, not recipient count.

Track where evidenced:

- verified delivery;
- acknowledgment;
- ticket/reference creation;
- internal routing;
- evidence incorporated into file;
- request for additional evidence;
- substantive journalistic engagement;
- right-of-reply engagement;
- formal institutional response;
- correct specialist contact identified;
- bounce / dead channel; and
- no response yet.

Do not claim opens, readership, review or substantive consideration unless evidenced.

## 16. Journalist/media register boundary

Use `archive/JOURNALIST_MEDIA_REGISTER.md` as the neutral identity/media register.

The register may contain name, organisation, role/beat relevance, geography/language and public-source verification notes.

It must not automatically publish:

- whether the person was contacted;
- the email address used;
- whether they replied;
- whether they did not reply; or
- private Gmail/message identifiers.

Contact/publication history requires a separate source-controlled record and a separate editorial decision before website publication.

## 17. Tier-A red team

Before any Tier-A package ask:

- What will this recipient understand in 30 seconds?
- Why should they care specifically?
- What is the strongest primary/source anchor?
- What is the strongest adverse or contrary fact?
- What is the most likely reason they would ignore the email?
- Is the ask realistic?
- Could the subject line look like bulk campaigning?
- Are we giving them too much material?
- Is any attachment or link stale?
- Is there anything here that would require an immediate correction?
- Would a shorter message increase engagement?

## 18. Website-link selection rule

Do not default automatically to the homepage or the material-updates page.

For each recipient choose the page that best answers their first question:

- homepage / 60-second chain for first orientation;
- 7 June 2018 dossier for material-control/possession issues;
- lender-of-record for credit/title/standing;
- institutional records for regulator/public-authority status;
- Community/minutes dossier for authority and unit-consent questions;
- RICPE/funding dossier for investment, incentives and funding;
- legal notebook for lawyers, academics and technically oriented journalists; or
- another exact current route where it provides a cleaner first read.

Verify live content and correction state immediately before drafting.

## 19. Seven-day campaign review

At least weekly during active outreach, review the previous seven days of external sent/received mail and record:

- messages that created substantive engagement;
- tickets/references created;
- evidence actually incorporated into files;
- formal-channel requirements discovered;
- corrections/duplicates;
- bounces/dead addresses;
- OOO return dates and suggested routes;
- attachment/link patterns that worked or caused confusion;
- recipient clusters that were too broad;
- current journalist/media names to add neutrally to the register; and
- the next Tier-A/B/C priorities.

Self-archive/preservation mail should not be counted as external exposure.

## 20. Final principle

The objective is not:

> send the story to everybody.

The objective is:

> put the right, verifiable part of the story in front of every person who can meaningfully investigate it, amplify it, preserve it, answer it, regulate it or act on it — in the form most likely to make them engage.
