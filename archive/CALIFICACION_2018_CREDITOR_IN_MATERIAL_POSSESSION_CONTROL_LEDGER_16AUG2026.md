## 1. Controlled proposition

The controlled proposition is **not** that Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. (CAM) became legal owner of the whole hotel on 7 June 2018.

The proposition is narrower, more important and evidence-testable:

> By 7 June 2018 CAM was already a recognised secured-credit holder in the LPB insolvency and the controlled record places a **de facto material-control threshold** at Sun Park on that date. No whole-hotel judicial delivery or order authorising CAM to assume that practical control on or before 7 June 2018 has been located in the controlled corpus.

For comparative explanation, this may be described as:

> **a secured creditor in de facto material possession/control — functionally comparable to a “lender in possession”.**

`Lender in possession` is **not presented as a formal status created by Spanish insolvency law**. It is a functional description of the combination of creditor status plus practical asset/control power. The Spanish-law issue is whether there was legal authority for each act of possession, access, exclusion, security, operation, use, works or control.

## 3. Why this is structurally important to the calificación

The calificación cannot be analysed only by asking what Gil/LPB formally owned or what obligations were attributed to the debtor. It must ask **who had the practical capacity to do the thing in question at the relevant time**.

The required causal grammar is:

> **FORMAL POWER / DUTY → ACTUAL CONTROL / CAPACITY → KNOWLEDGE → ACT / OMISSION → CONSEQUENCE → CAUSATION → CALIFICACIÓN / REMEDY**

From 7 June 2018 onward, every allegation concerning operation, preservation, access, accounting, experts, rents, repairs, commercialisation, rescue or collaboration must be tested against the actual-control baseline.

### A. Collaboration allegations

If LPB/Gil lacked practical access to parts of the hotel, keys, records, staff, security interfaces or physical areas because another creditor/private-control actor held them, a later allegation that LPB failed to cooperate must distinguish:

1. information or acts genuinely within Gil/LPB's control;
2. information or acts dependent on the AC;
3. information or acts dependent on CAM/security/operators/Community actors;
4. information or acts impossible or materially obstructed because of exclusion from the asset.

This does **not** erase documented delays or missing documents. It changes the causal and capacity analysis.

### B. Pink / rent / operating-causation allegations

The adverse Pink/rent finding cannot be evaluated as a single uninterrupted economic period if material control of the hotel changed. The audit must split at least:

- pre-7 June 2018 operating/control period;
- 7 June 2018 material-control event;
- post-event control, access and revenue conditions;
- later operator/commercialisation periods;
- formal adjudication/conveyance period.

Who could generate revenue, admit guests, maintain rooms, contract operators, incur costs, collect benefits or obstruct those activities is a causal fact, not background colour.

### C. “Aggravation of insolvency” attribution

Under the former Ley Concursal, culpable classification under Article 164.1 required the relevant causal attribution to generation or aggravation of insolvency. A serious intervening control event therefore matters if it affected value, revenues, rescue, costs, deterioration, access, valuation or the ability to operate.

The controlled argument is:

> **Any post-7 June deterioration or lost opportunity attributed to Gil/LPB requires a counterfactual that first identifies what Gil/LPB could still control after 7 June and what had moved into the practical control of CAM or other separately proved actors.**

Failure to perform that split can create an incomplete causal attribution. It does not automatically make the judgment void, criminal or prevaricatory.

### D. AC knowledge and administration of the estate

The AC's position becomes especially important because the former Ley Concursal placed intervention/administration, preservation and continuation functions within the concursal structure. The finite questions are:

- What did the AC know before 7 June about intended CAM/security access or control?
- What exactly did the 18 May Community/security node authorise?
- Did the AC authorise, consent to, tolerate, oppose or seek to reverse the 7 June control event?
- Did the AC demand a key/access inventory?
- Did the AC demand an account of occupation, revenues, fruits, expenses or works?
- Did the AC ask the court for protective or restorative measures?
- How was the event presented in the later calificación report?
- If it was omitted or minimised, why was an event changing actual control not incorporated into causation?

These are evidence questions. They do not establish collusion merely because AC and CAM appear in the same chronology.

### E. Judge / court causation

For judicial-accountability analysis the controlling rule remains **evidence before actor**. The question is not whether the event is now known in 2026, but what evidence of it was demonstrably before the judge at each relevant decision point.

Where the event and its consequences were before the court, the audit should ask whether the signed causal findings in Sentencia 163/2023 adequately accounted for the practical controller of the asset. A failure to do so may support appellate error, incomplete causal reasoning or an institutional-accountability question. It is **not**, without the separate knowledge/arbitrariness proof required by criminal law, self-proving prevaricación.

### F. Article 166 — possible third-party complicity question, not an automatic conclusion

Former Ley Concursal Article 166 treated as accomplices persons who, with intent or gross negligence, cooperated with the debtor/its relevant representatives in an act grounding culpable classification.

Therefore CAM's creditor/control status does **not** automatically make CAM a `cómplice`. The correct question is narrower:

> If a specific culpability-grounding act involved third-party cooperation, was that cooperation proved, was the required subjective threshold proved, and was the actor individualised?

The asymmetry to audit is whether private actors' conduct was relevant to the same causal events but escaped equivalent individualisation while culpability was concentrated on Gil/Pink.

## 4. Implications for the rest of Concurso 36/2012

### 4.1 Preservation and administration of the active estate

A material-control shift affects who could physically preserve, inspect, repair, operate and protect assets. The pre/post-7 June inventory, condition, access and custody record becomes essential to any later allegation of deterioration or lost value.

### 4.2 Collective enforcement / secured-credit discipline

CAM's recognised secured-credit status gave it creditor rights; it did not by itself answer the separate question of authority to assume whole-hotel practical possession/control. The 2018 statutory regime channelled debtor powers, estate preservation, enforcement and real-security realisation through the insolvency framework and, for relevant questions, the insolvency judge.

The core verification question is therefore:

> **What judicial or other legally sufficient authority, if any, empowered each concrete act of taking possession/control on or before 7 June 2018?**

### 4.3 Valuation integrity

The file should treat the 23 May / 7 June Gesvalt inspection/valuation sequence together with the control event. A controller can affect access, information, observable operating condition and later investment/work choices. This does not invalidate a valuation by itself; it makes access/control provenance material to interpreting it.

### 4.4 Rescue / financed exit / continuation

If a creditor/private-control event impaired access, continuity, investor diligence, hotel operation or a financed route out of the concurso, it becomes a causal variable in assessing why rescue failed and whether insolvency worsened.

The correct test is counterfactual and evidence-based: identify the concrete rescue instrument, what remained to be done, who had power to permit it, what the control event changed, and whether that change caused measurable loss.

### 4.5 Information and bidding asymmetry

Long-term practical control before a later competitive sale/adjudication can create possible informational, operational or access asymmetries. That is a matter for audit, not a presumption of invalidity.

Required comparison:

- who had keys/access;
- who had plans, technical information and condition data;
- who could inspect;
- who could perform works;
- who knew operating demand and costs;
- who had the ability to maintain or change the asset;
- what equivalent information/access other bidders received.

### 4.6 Accounting for use, revenues, fruits and expenses

If an actor possessed or controlled assets before formal adjudication, a complete accounting is required to determine what economic value was generated, received, spent or foregone during that period. The legal consequence depends on title, authority, good/bad faith, asset ownership and the precise cause of possession; it cannot be inferred from the word `possession` alone.

The evidence package should include revenues, rents, reservations, operator payments, utilities, security, repairs, capex, taxes, insurance and any benefits credited against the debt or retained outside the estate.

### 4.7 Later adjudication does not answer the earlier-authority question

The 2021–2022 adjudication/dación path may establish later rights from its legally effective date and within its lawful asset perimeter. It does not logically answer whether practical control in June 2018 was authorised at that earlier date.

This temporal separation is mandatory in every public and internal narrative.

## 5. Multiple parallel lives outside the concurso

The 7 June event cannot be collapsed into LPB because Sun Park was a mixed-ownership / multi-rights environment and the user alleges multiple legal and economic lives outside the debtor estate.

### 5.1 LPB concursal life

Questions: estate preservation, creditor treatment, secured-credit enforcement, operation, rescue, valuations, sale/adjudication, accounting and calificación causation.

### 5.2 Matkator / third-party property life

A creditor of LPB does not acquire title to a Matkator or other third-party asset merely because the asset sits within the same hotel platform or is practically controlled together with LPB units.

Every unit/finca/right must be mapped separately:

> `OWNER → TITLE → POSSESSION → ACCESS → USE → WORKS → INCOME → TRANSFER / CURRENT STATUS`.

Where a third-party unit was taken into practical control, the claim/remedy may be extra-concursal even though the physical event arose in the same hotel.

### 5.3 Community / security life

Community resolutions, security mandates, locks, guards, common areas and unit access have their own authority chain. A Community/security decision cannot be assumed to transfer ownership or unlimited possessory authority. The exact minutes, notices, titles/proxies, vote, contract and instructions must control.

### 5.4 Operator / commercialisation life

Lava Verde, Club Sei, Meeting Point and any other operator/commercialisation layer must be tested against the exact period, contracting entity, inventory, authority, booking platform, revenues and payments. Later operation can be downstream evidence of practical control but is not proof of the original legal basis for the 7 June event.

### 5.5 Works / technical / administrative life

Building works, licences, professional appointments, tourism records and administrative permissions have their own public-law validity questions. An administrative permission or professional document does not, without more, cure a private title/possession defect or establish authority over third-party units.

### 5.6 Financing / RICPE / public-support life

Later financing, RICPE materials, incentives, FEDER or other public-support records can prove how the asset/control platform was represented to financiers or public authorities. They do not retroactively prove title or lawful possession in 2018. They are downstream provenance/benefit and due-diligence evidence requiring their own source chain.

### 5.7 Aweswell / investor-loss life

If the control event impaired the ability to rescue LPB, exploit separately owned assets, attract finance or realise value, Aweswell/investor loss may exist on a different legal plane from losses of the LPB estate. Standing, duty, causation and double-recovery rules must be analysed separately.

## 6. Civil possession / fruits boundary

The Civil Code possession rules reinforce the need to separate physical possession from ownership and to identify legal process where possession is disputed. Articles 441 and 446 are relevant to the proposition that disputed possession cannot simply be reduced to `the creditor had a claim`. Articles 451–455 show why good/bad faith and accounting for fruits/expenses can materially affect consequences.

**Publication rule:** do not state a bad-faith possessor conclusion unless a competent legal analysis establishes the factual and legal predicates. Use the Civil Code as an issue-identification framework, not as an automatic judgment against CAM.

## 7. Strongest defence / what remains

### Strongest reasonable defence for CAM / associated control actors

CAM could contend, depending on the evidence, that:

- access or control was consensual;
- it arose from a valid Community/security mandate;
- it was limited to protection/security rather than possession;
- creditor/guarantee rights justified the conduct;
- the hotel was not in the operational state alleged;
- LPB/Gil retained meaningful access/control;
- later judicial decisions recognised or regularised the relevant rights;
- any works/operation were undertaken by legally distinct entities with their own authority;
- no measurable insolvency aggravation was caused by the June event.

### What remains even if those defences are taken seriously

1. Identify the exact authority operative **on 7 June 2018**, not a later order.
2. Define the asset perimeter covered by that authority.
3. Identify who physically held keys, guards, access and operating capability.
4. Determine whether LPB, Matkator and other owners consented and to what.
5. Determine what the AC knew and did before and after the event.
6. Account for revenues, use, costs, works and benefits during the control period.
7. Determine whether the event changed rescue, valuation, operation or later bidding conditions.
8. Re-run every calificación causation finding across the pre/post-control divide.

## 10. Non-overstatement locks

- **Credit holder ≠ owner.**
- **Material possession/control ≠ legal title.**
- **No authorising order located ≠ proof no such document can exist.**
- **Later adjudication ≠ retroactive authority.**
- **Association/perimeter ≠ collective liability.**
- **Chronology + benefit ≠ conspiracy.**
- **Appellate/legal error ≠ prevaricación without separate proof.**
- **Third-party complicity under former Article 166 is not automatic; conduct and subjective threshold must be proved.**
