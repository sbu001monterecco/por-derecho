## 3. Central legal question created/sharpened in this thread

The controlling research question is:

> **Did the Arrecife court decide possession of an ordinary autonomous apartment, or did it decide possession of a hotel/tourist accommodation unit after material parts of its juridical identity, exploitation structure, rights and obligations had been omitted or misrepresented?**

The issue is not whether the word `apartamento` appeared in a title or pleading. The analysis must distinguish:

1. physical accommodation unit;
2. registered finca;
3. apartment under horizontal division;
4. residential dwelling;
5. tourist accommodation unit;
6. unit within an operating hotel;
7. ownership;
8. civil possession;
9. contractual possession;
10. tourist exploitation rights;
11. operator rights;
12. Community/CEXP authority;
13. planning/tourism restrictions.

## 5. Mandatory legislation scan

Build date-specific legal snapshots for 2008, 2011, filing of JV 1260/2011, 23-Feb-2012, hearing/judgment/enforcement, commencement of Concurso 36/2012, and current remedies.

### Spanish criminal law

At minimum analyse:

- Código Penal Articles 248–251;
- **Article 250.1.7 CP — estafa procesal**;
- what qualifies as manipulation of evidence or `otro fraude procesal análogo`;
- whether materially deceptive omissions can qualify;
- judicial error, causation and patrimonial prejudice;
- attempt versus consummation;
- prescription;
- actor-specific intent and participation;
- documentary offences Articles 390–396 where factually justified;
- false testimony / false accusation only where the procedural role fits;
- lawyer/representative duties and offences only where elements are independently supported.

Do not convert legal error, incomplete pleading, aggressive interpretation, procedural irregularity or civil invalidity automatically into criminal fraud.

### Spanish civil procedure

- Article 24 CE;
- Article 11 LOPJ;

- Article 250 LEC and the exact cause of action used;
- Article 444 LEC where applicable;

- Articles 509–516 LEC;
- **Article 510 LEC — revisión / maquinación fraudulenta**;
- **Article 512 LEC — time limits**;

### Property / contracts / horizontal property

At minimum examine historically applicable versions of:

- Código Civil Article 348 and possession provisions;
- Articles 1091, 1255–1258, 1281–1289;
- lease/use/mandate/management/exploitation structures;
- Ley de Propiedad Horizontal;
- Registry and mortgage-law consequences;
- difference between ownership and immediate possession;
- opposability of exploitation rights;
- LAU in force in 2011–2012.

### Canary tourism law

At minimum conduct a historical and current scan of:

- **Ley 7/1995 de Ordenación del Turismo de Canarias**;
- historical versions of Articles 38–42;
- Ley 5/1999;
- Ley 14/2009;
- Decreto 142/2010;
- Decreto 10/2001 where relevant;
- predecessor hotel/extrahotel accommodation rules;
- Ley 2/2013;
- later tourism-use reforms only insofar as they affect current interpretation/remedies, not retrospectively;
- transitional provisions;
- planning/tourism authorisations applicable specifically to Sun Park.

The scan must determine whether Sun Park and the relevant unit were subject in 2011–2012 to `unidad de explotación`, what that principle did and did not mean for private ownership and possession, whether units could lawfully leave the common exploitation, and whether public tourism regulation itself created or defeated a private possessory title.

## 9. Mandatory adverse/exculpatory testing

Search actively for evidence and law supporting the claimant, including whether:

- registered title carried an ordinary right to exclude;
- the operator lacked a valid or opposable title from that owner;
- CEXP/operator authority was invalid, expired or limited;
- the owner had lawfully withdrawn the unit from common hotel exploitation;
- tourism law regulated public operation but did not confer private possession;
- `apartamento` accurately described the registered asset;
- hotel/exploitation context was actually disclosed;
- the defendant appeared and could fully defend;
- the court considered and rejected the hotel argument;
- any omission was immaterial;
- the claimant reasonably relied on advice;
- the case involved an arguable legal interpretation rather than deceit;
- no patrimonial prejudice was caused;
- remedies are time-barred.
