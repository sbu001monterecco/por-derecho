## 1. Executive finding

The whole-matter scan confirms that the Sun Park dispute is best understood as a **single hotel-economy and control chain with legally distinct components**, rather than as a collection of unrelated proceedings. The principal alleged private-actor chain remains the progression from CAM/Acosta Matos’s creditor and minority-owner position, through practical control and Community-linked authority, into later adjudication, HNT succession, refurbishment, financing and MYND operation. Nothing in the Cuatrecasas redigest exonerates, replaces or reduces that primary alleged Acosta Matos benefit chain.

The backward reconstruction from the present MYND/HNT outcome and the forward reconstruction from the earlier ownership, Community/CEXP, lender and insolvency architecture converge on seven decisive gates:

1. **authority and possession precursor**;
2. **physical-control gate**;
3. **ONA/financing/concurso-exit gate**;
4. **credit and economic-calculation gate**;
5. **restoration and implementation gate**;
6. **adjudication, funding and downstream-operation gate**;
7. **professional and Matkator execution gate**.

The unitary reconstruction materially changes the Cuatrecasas analysis in both directions.

It **strengthens and expands** the protection case because Cuatrecasas’s knowledge of possession, access, keys, security, mixed title, investor access and the distinction between LPB and other Aweswell property did not begin on 7 June 2018. The contemporaneous record shows that by January and February 2018 the firm knew that the insolvency administration was seeking physical possession and key/code control; that the client alleged prior Acosta Matos entries and ownership claims; that private security and investor exclusion were contemplated; and that Aweswell owned additional Sun Park property outside LPB. The June event was therefore not a wholly unforeseeable surprise. It was the culmination of a documented possession-and-authority risk architecture.

It **narrows one earlier allegation**. Cuatrecasas did give reasoned early advice on the economic condition for concluding the concurso, including the need to address all recognised or contingent liabilities and the strategic risk that payment could prejudice later dispute. The sustainable allegation is not that the economic gate was never analysed. It is whether that analysis was later updated, reconciled and preserved as the credit, interest, improvement-threshold, cash/credit-equivalence, Community and AP4 issues evolved—and whether the resulting model was implemented and handed over.

The **strongest professional-failure case** is now:

> Cuatrecasas accepted and performed an integrated rescue mandate for Aweswell that joined LPB’s insolvency exit, mixed hotel title, Matkator’s separate property position, ONA operation, financing, security, investor access, minority consolidation and eventual refinancing or sale. It had pre-June notice of the possession and authority risks. When the June 2018 crisis materialised, it took some protective steps, but the available record does not yet show one integrated, entity-correct and evidence-preserving response capable of securing the physical, legal, operational and financing status quo. The later October 2019 opening was accompanied by a detailed client implementation programme, but the firm declined to assess the proposed next steps until fees were regularised. The completeness of urgent protection and handover remains unresolved.

The later execution track is independent and cumulative. The 3 July 2019 Cuatrecasas receivables table attributed issued invoice balances to Aweswell and LPB and did not list a Matkator invoice. That does not disprove a valid autonomous pagaré, guarantee or security obligation. It does make the original instruments, cause, authority, service, accounting and debtor presentation indispensable. The 2024–2026 requests for adjudication, reservation of cession and opposition to suspension or fresh expert verification were affirmative later choices, not the passive continuation of a 2019 matter.

DP 748/2026 remains a **finite prima facie investigative hypothesis**, not a proved estafa procesal. It becomes a viable procedural-fraud case only if the complete certified files establish an exact material deception or equivalent omission, knowledge and intent, judicial error, a prejudicial patrimonial resolution or attempted disposition, loss and improper benefit. No actual Acosta-linked remate cessionary or common plan with Cuatrecasas is presently proved.

## 5. Origin-first forward reconstruction

### 5.1 Mixed ownership and operating unity

Sun Park was never adequately reducible to one owner or one legal layer. LPB held a majority position within the horizontal division and was in concurso. Matkator held separate, extraconcursal property. Other owners retained units. Common elements, Community authority, CEXP/exploitation rights, tourism operation, physical possession, keys, clients, licences and revenue were distinct layers requiring reconciliation.

**Control rule:** unity of operation does not itself transfer title; title does not itself establish practical possession or operating authority.

### 5.2 Insolvency and lender chain

Concurso 36/2012 initially operated as a protection against enforcement and later became the central procedural environment through which LPB title, creditor claims, liquidation, offers, control and final adjudication were mediated. The lender/credit chain and CAM’s acquisition of the credit and additional ownership positions altered the balance of power but did not, by themselves, collapse every legal role into one.

### 5.3 Community/CEXP authority and minority position

The Community and exploitation structures became a practical gateway for access, keys, security, voting, alleged debt, representation and operation. The unitary model requires transaction-by-transaction proof of notice, proxies, voting rights, debt status, authority and the distinction between protection of common elements and control of private fincas or the hotel business.

### 5.4 January–February 2018 precursor

The contemporary record shows a possession and security problem before June:

- the insolvency administration sought keys and access-code changes for the stated purpose of taking physical possession;
- Cuatrecasas was working on Aweswell’s appearance and insolvency strategy;
- the client alleged earlier Acosta Matos entries, damage, ownership claims and access pressure;
- Cuatrecasas recommended rapid financing and Aweswell’s court appearance to prevent measures harmful to estate value;
- the insolvency administration later contemplated private security and exclusion of unconsented entry;
- the client expressly told Cuatrecasas that Aweswell held other Sun Park property outside LPB and that LPB did not own the entire hotel;
- investor access and live hotel operation were already relevant.

This is a decisive correction to any chronology that begins the professional-protection problem only on 7 June 2018.

### 5.5 ONA, financing and formal DD

The April–May 2018 mandate and billing joined:

- lender-facing DD;
- financing and security;
- LPB insolvency monitoring;
- title and property analysis;
- litigation/public-law questions;
- ONA lease/management/purchase documentation;
- investor and manager meetings;
- coordination with LPB counsel.

The ONA package made possession, deliverability, authority, unit availability, common operation, funding and insolvency exit interdependent. It was a real lawful alternative but remained conditional.

### 5.6 June 2018 control event

Cuatrecasas was promptly informed of the reported entry, keys/access demands, security and Community-control assertions. It considered complaint/evidence, Matkator’s owner route, Arrecife/civil measures and a Mercantile-Court communication. The 13 June record shows an explicit timing question linked to the positive court impression concerning the ONA route.

The evidence disproves ignorance and absolute inaction. It leaves unresolved whether the chosen sequence was a reasonable, informed strategy or an under-reaction that failed to preserve the indivisible transaction and asset baseline.

### 5.7 2018–2019 protection and economic work

Cuatrecasas continued insolvency, Registry, finance and rescue work and took protective action. It gave reasoned early advice concerning full satisfaction of liabilities and the risks of payment. Later developments required continuous reconciliation of recognised credit, contingent claims, interest, mortgage liability, third-party improvement thresholds, cash versus credit and estate outcome.

The sustainable allegation is therefore one of **update, integration, implementation and handover**, not complete absence of analysis.

### 5.8 October 2019 opening

Cuatrecasas reported two favourable results, including non-convalidation of a CAM-related local-sale route and preservation of Aweswell’s position, and said the work might help save the complex. The client then sent a detailed eleven-part programme covering protection, implementation, court and Registry measures, finance, possession and recovery. Cuatrecasas declined to assess the proposed next steps until fees were regularised.

This creates a concrete, document-testable issue:

- which steps were urgent;
- which fell within the existing mandate;
- which required a new fee arrangement;
- what the client was warned would occur if they were not taken;
- and what was transferred to successor counsel.

### 5.9 Transition to recovery and later execution

The matter moved to claims/collection in 2020. Venia was granted promptly; obstruction of venia is contradicted. The complete transfer index remains unresolved. The later fee enforcement cannot be characterised as automatically improper, but it must be reconciled with the entity, invoice and instrument records.

The later ETJ choices are distinct acts:

- adjudication at 70%;
- reservation of cession to a third party;
- opposition to suspension;
- opposition to fresh expert verification;
- treatment of the alleged physical transformation and hotel value;
- renewed adjudication requests.
