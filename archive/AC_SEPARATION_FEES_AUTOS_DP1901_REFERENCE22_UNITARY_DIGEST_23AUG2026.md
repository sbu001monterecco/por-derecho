## 3. The 23 April 2025 removal application — complete structured digest

Aweswell's 58-page application sought removal under article 100 TRLC. Its opening thesis alleged a persistent pattern of omission, patrimonial harm and opacity and argued that alternatives to liquidation were not properly explored. The seven pleaded factual blocks were:

1. **Annual accounts.** Alleged failure to formulate and deposit LPB's annual accounts after suspension of the debtor's powers, said to have continued for more than seven years. The application relied particularly on a 20 March 2025 procedural order stating that the file contained neither an exemption order nor annual-account documentation. It alleged resulting impairment of estate supervision, creditor information and Aweswell's own consolidated reporting.
2. **Financial-risk-management contract.** Alleged inaction and obstruction concerning a 2008 financial-risk/derivative contract said to have caused more than EUR 500,000 harm. The application alleged failure first to analyse/pursue a viable patrimonial response and later obstruction of that response.
3. **Price of the credit assignment.** Alleged obstruction of access to the real consideration and complete terms of the PH122/credit transfer, despite its significance to litigation, negotiation and estate strategy.
4. **Transfer in payment / dación.** Allegedly uncritical support for or acceptance of a transaction said to be harmful to the estate, including valuation, perimeter and authority concerns. This was framed as an estate-protection and informed-recommendation question, not merely disagreement with the eventual judicial outcome.
5. **Fragmentation of the operating unity.** Alleged failure to protect the hotel as an integrated economic unit and to respond effectively to fragmentation, third-party property, access and exploitation issues.
6. **Remuneration.** Alleged accrual and receipt of remuneration beyond statutory liquidation limits and alleged interest charges. This block overlaps factually with, but does not replace, the separate professional-liability action.
7. **Structural opacity and conflicts.** Alleged failure to disclose or manage relationships, information asymmetries and conflicts said to compromise independence, transparency and confidence in the office.

The application reproduced these blocks as legal grounds under diligence, loyalty, transparency and article 100 TRLC. The AC opposed the application. Both the application and the complete located opposition are now published as redacted full-text transcriptions (`R01` and `R03`).

### Relief requested

The application asked the court to:

- remove the AC;
- make a new appointment under article 101 TRLC;
- require the removed AC to render accounts;
- notify the Public Insolvency Register;
- transmit relevant annual-account material to AEAT; and
- transmit relevant facts to the Public Prosecutor for assessment of any possible criminal significance.

### Publication decision

The repository preserves the original file hash and a complete digest. The raw pleading is not published because it contains unnecessary personal/professional data and references to a large annex set. This is a data-minimisation decision, not a retreat from the allegation. Any public quotation must remain subordinate to the pleading's actual text and procedural status.

## 4. The two removal orders

### Auto 1377/2025 · 12 September 2025

The court accepted the AC's objection that Aweswell lacked active standing. It treated the listing of the former Monterecco Sun Park Limited/Aweswell credit at EUR 0 as exclusion and relied on the absence of a challenge to the provisional creditor list. It dismissed the removal request **“sin necesidad de entrar en los motivos de fondo.”**

Consequences:

- the order is an adverse standing decision;
- it is not a merits finding on accounts, risk management, assignment price, dación, operating unity, remuneration or conflicts;
- it expressly identified reconsideration and then appeal as available remedies.

Text-control note: after quoting article 3 TRLC, the order refers to “apartado tercero del artículo 100” when discussing partners personally liable for company debts. This appears to be an internal citation slip. It is recorded neutrally and carries no inference about intent or outcome.

### Order of 11 November 2025

The court rejected the reconsideration appeals of LPB and Aweswell, confirmed the 12 September order, imposed no costs for the reconsideration stage and stated that appeal lay to the Provincial Court without suspensive effect.

Its material reasoning was:

- a EUR 0 creditor listing was treated as exclusion;
- article 427 TRLC was rejected as a new basis because it had not appeared in the 58-page original application;
- once the standing objection was upheld, the court considered no merits analysis was required under article 218 LEC;
- on the articles 82/100 argument, the court said it did not see indications of breach that would require ex-officio removal or an article 82 report;
- LPB's reconsideration was rejected by reference to the earlier reasoning.

That limited ex-officio statement is contrary evidence and must remain visible. It is not the same as an act-by-act evidential adjudication of the seven pleaded blocks.

Text-control note: in the section concerning LPB, the final reasoning sentence names Aweswell, while the operative part unambiguously dismisses both LPB and Aweswell appeals. This is recorded as an internal naming inconsistency, without an inference of motive.

## 6. The remuneration / professional-liability action

The 21-page pleading dated 1 September 2024 asserted a collective estate-damage action against the AC and the liability insurer. The pleading's caption says `ORD 134/2024`; later procedural records identify an originating/transfer chain involving `582/2024` and the merits court's ordinary proceeding `641/2024`. A certified docket should be obtained to reconcile every intermediate reference.

### Pleaded theory and amount

The claim alleged that liquidation opened on 19 December 2017, that remuneration continued beyond the statutory ordinary liquidation period and that interest was charged on common/convenio-phase remuneration. It sought **EUR 110,956.97** for return to the insolvency estate—or the amount produced by the pleaded calculation basis—plus legal interest and costs, with the insurer jointly liable. The pleaded components were:

| Component | Pleaded amount |
|---|---:|
| Liquidation-phase remuneration said to be improper, including IGIC | EUR 65,217.76 |
| Legal interest on that component | EUR 9,504.77 |
| Interest said to be improper on common/convenio remuneration | EUR 31,107.46 |
| Interest on the latter component | EUR 5,126.98 |
| **Total claimed** | **EUR 110,956.97** |

The pleading also identified EUR 29,208.88 plus IGIC described in the underlying account as “Intereses honorarios fase común y convenio” and alleged inconsistencies between quarterly reports as to the last paid remuneration period.

### Statutory-source correction

The controlling statutory label is **Disposición Transitoria Tercera of Ley 25/2015**, not “Disposición Adicional Tercera.” The provision states, in summary, 10% for the first six liquidation months, 5% from month seven, and no remuneration from month thirteen unless the court grants a reasoned extension after hearing the parties, with quarterly extensions subject to the statutory total limit. Any earlier site or pleading shorthand using `DA 3ª` must be read as corrected to `DT 3ª`; the correction does not decide whether the pleaded calculation succeeds.

### Sentencia 4/2026 · 21 January 2026

The court dismissed the action for lack of Aweswell's active standing under the collective estate action. It did not reach the remaining defences or determine whether the remuneration or interest was legally due. Costs were imposed on the claimant, and appeal was available.

### RPL 421/2026

The appeal was filed; the appellate deposit was paid on 10 March 2026 and the filing receipt is dated 11 March 2026. Later provision-of-funds material exists. No signed merits decision was located through the control date. The safe formulation is therefore: **fee legality and quantum remain disputed; first instance decided standing, not fee merits.**

## 7. Criminal complaint associated with Decanato daily intake/reference 22

The 48-page complaint dated 18 June 2026 is an AC-focused criminal complaint/notitia criminis. The controlled intake sources associate the physical presentation with handwritten daily intake/reference **22**. That handwritten number is a reception locator, not an official Control, NIG, Diligencias Previas number or proof of admission.

The complaint's main provisional hypothesis is possible administration disloyal under article 252 CP. It expressly says that not every irregularity, adverse decision, omission or disagreement is criminal and distinguishes negligence—even serious negligence—from intentional criminal conduct. Its analytical sequence is:

> entrusted power → verifiable information/knowledge → act, decision or failure to use a reasonably available measure → patrimonial harm → possible corresponding third-party advantage.

The principal episodes it asks to investigate are:

- Community representation, accounts and debt certificates;
- LPB's capacity to defend and protect its estate;
- the funded 2018 exit and the information needed to execute it;
- security, access, keys, works, demolition, exploitation and displacement of material/economic control;
- the 28 November 2018 `ob rem` transaction, approximately EUR 400,000, its accounting and later non-validation;
- credit liquidation, competitive conditions, adjudication and any surplus;
- fruits, rents, revenues, treasury, recovery claims and complete accounts.

The complaint does not ask the criminal court to re-decide the removal application or insolvency orders. It asks for act-specific investigation of the AC's own administration, information, protection, disposition, liquidation and accounting conduct. It identifies the removal orders and appeal family as sources and explains that the removal merits were not comprehensively adjudicated.

The raw complaint is not public because it contains the complainant's NIE, address, email and telephone. The structured digest preserves its complete analytical architecture.

## 8. DP 1901/2026, additions and cross-evidence

DP 1901 is primarily the private-actor/CAM lane. Its relevance to AC analysis is evidential and finite:

- who instructed and paid security/locksmith activity;
- what authority was represented and what title existed;
- who held/received keys and controlled access;
- what the AC was told and when;
- what post-entry protection, recovery, accounting or court application was available;
- what benefit or project continuity followed.

The 9 July 2026 `ampliación` is treated as belonging to the CAM/JDAM/LPAM/private-actor lane. It is not represented as an amendment to the AC complaint or as proof that every person shared a criminal plan. NEXUS 36 and other private-actor filings likewise remain separate unless a signed procedural act joins them.

DIP 80/2026 and its later submissions may supply evidence about professional duties, notice and preservation, but disciplinary material does not prove criminal intent. DP 1956/2026 remains a separate AC/concurso criminal route with a controlled provisional-dismissal state. Evidence may overlap; procedure and legal effect do not.
