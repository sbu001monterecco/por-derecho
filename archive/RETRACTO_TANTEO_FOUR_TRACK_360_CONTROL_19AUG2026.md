## 1. Controlling rule: there is not one generic “retracto”

The Sun Park record now requires four separate tracks.

### RTA — CREDIT ASSIGNMENT / ARTICLE 1535 / DP 1041

Object: **credit**, not hotel units.

`PH122 → CAM assignment of LPB mortgage credits → DP 1041/2017 → deed/price/payment/cost disclosure → possible Article 1535 CC position → AC control of LPB representation → claimant-side withdrawal → archive.`

This is the existing Article 1535 / PP 1041 workstream.

### RTB — 2018 UNIT-TRANSFER PREFERENCE WORKSTREAM

Object: **individual Sun Park property interests / units** reportedly acquired from minority owners by a third party, contemporaneously identified by counsel as potentially CAM.

The existence of this separate legal workstream is **DIRECTLY DOCUMENTED** by May–June 2018 lawyer communications, including the contemporaneous formulation:

> `tanteo/retracto legal de LUCHY contra unidades adquiridas por un tercero (CAM?) a la minoría`

The `CAM?` question mark must be preserved where title evidence has not independently closed the purchaser identity for the specific transaction.

**Critical legal correction:** counsel investigated, among other possible bases, paragraph 4 of the sole transitional provision of Canary Law 5/1999. That paragraph was later described by the Constitutional Court as having been repealed by Canary Law 2/2000, and STC 28/2012 declared it unconstitutional and null. It must **not** be presented as an operative 2018 statutory basis.

Status:

`VERIFIED_SEPARATE_LEGAL_WORKSTREAM — RIGHT_ITSELF_REQUIRES_TRANSACTION_SPECIFIC_VALIDATION`

### RTC — RETRACTO DE COMUNEROS / ARTICLES 1522–1524 CC

Object: sale to an outsider of an **undivided share in the same common property**.

Article 1522 CC gives a co-owner of a common thing a statutory retracto when another co-owner's share is sold to an outsider. Article 1524 sets a nine-day exercise period from Registry registration or, failing that, actual knowledge of the sale.

This route must be tested only where the pre-sale structure was genuine `pro indiviso` ownership of the same finca/right.

**Do not generalise it to horizontal property.** Article 396 CC expressly states that, when a separate flat or local is sold, the owners of other flats/locals do not obtain tanteo/retracto merely by virtue of that horizontal-property ownership.

### RTD — CONTRACTUAL / STATUTORY / TOURISM / EXPLOITATION RIGHTS

Object: any independent consent, notice, first-refusal, substitution, adherence, exploitation or transfer restriction arising from:

- the registered Sun Park statutes;
- the horizontal-division title;
- the 2008 exploitation/sale architecture;
- CEXP instruments;
- owners' resolutions;
- purchase contracts;
- operator agreements;
- Canary tourism / unidad de explotación rules;
- insolvency/liquidation instruments.

The registered Sun Park statutes reviewed in the 360° scan strongly support the historic **single-unit tourist exploitation architecture**, continuity into successive titles and the distinct role of the Comunidad de Explotación. They do **not**, in the passages reviewed, create a generic first-refusal/retracto over every sale of an independent unit.

## 3. Law-in-force correction — Ley 5/1999

The original paragraph 4 of the sole transitional provision of Law 5/1999 created a preferential-acquisition/retracto mechanism linked to the principle of `unidad de explotación`.

However:

1. STC 28/2012 records that the paragraph had been **repealed by Law 2/2000**; and
2. STC 28/2012 declared the provision **unconstitutional and null**.

Therefore the repository must not describe the May–June 2018 lawyer investigation as proof that this particular statutory mechanism was legally available in 2018.

Correct formulation:

`HISTORICAL_LEGAL_THEORY_INVESTIGATED_IN_2018 — NOT_AN_OPERATIVE_2018_RIGHT`

The next task is to determine whether each transfer engaged **another** valid right: Art. 1522/1524 co-owner retracto, a contractual/exploitation right, an insolvency-estate right or another transaction-specific legal mechanism.

For each unit/property right run a fresh causation analysis:

2. whether the AC controlled it;
3. when the AC knew of the transfer/right;
4. what advice or request was put to the AC;

7. whether a measurable estate/perimeter loss resulted;
8. whether a contemporaneous estate-interest analysis exists;
9. whether the outcome benefited CAM or a predecessor/successor adverse perimeter;
10. what neutral explanation exists.

Only then assign C0–C5.
