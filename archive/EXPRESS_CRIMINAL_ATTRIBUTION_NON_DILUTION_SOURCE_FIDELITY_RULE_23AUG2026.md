## 1. Controlling position

Where Gil Marer or Aweswell has expressly and actor-specifically attributed criminal conduct, the repository must preserve that position as an **express criminal allegation**.

It must not convert or reduce the allegation into a mere:

- concern;
- question;
- possible issue;
- possible involvement;
- investigative lead;
- matter warranting examination;
- request to determine whether something may have happened; or
- neutral chronology from which no accusation is apparent.

Those formulations may describe the **evidential or procedural status** only after the direct allegation has been stated faithfully and with equivalent or greater prominence.

The controlling distinction is:

> **The attribution may be direct and unequivocal while the proof, adjudication, procedural outcome and individual legal responsibility remain separately classified.**

## 2. Four independent axes — none cancels another

Every material publication must keep four questions separate:

1. **Attribution:** what Gil Marer / Aweswell directly alleges each actor did or omitted to do.
2. **Evidence:** what is documented, witness-supported, inferred, disputed, exculpatory or still missing.
3. **Adjudication and procedure:** what a court, prosecutor, regulator or other competent body has or has not decided, including dismissal, appeal, finality and scope.
4. **Source literal:** what the underlying document, email, pleading, recording or image actually says or shows.

An evidential gap does not erase or soften the party attribution. A direct party attribution does not establish guilt. A dismissal does not rewrite the historical source. A canonical identity correction does not permit alteration of a quotation or source description.

## 5. Prohibited dilution

The following are prohibited when used as the only or controlling description of an express criminal attribution:

- “there are questions concerning CAM”;
- “CAM may have been involved”;
- “the material supports a possible lead”;
- “the evidence warrants investigation of whether CAM acted”;
- “the matter raises concerns”;
- “the record does not establish wrongdoing” without first stating the attributed wrongdoing;
- “no finding has been made” as a substitute for identifying the allegation;
- replacing an actor-specific accusation with a perimeter-wide abstraction that obscures the named actor and alleged function; or
- moving the direct attribution to a less visible location while leaving only limiting language in the first-read surface.

“Supports investigation”, “requires examination”, “remains unproved” and similar language remains valid only as a separate statement of evidence or adjudication status.
