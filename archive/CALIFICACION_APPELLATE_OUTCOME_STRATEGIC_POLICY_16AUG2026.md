## 2. Gil Marer's binary strategic rule

Gil's position is deliberately binary at the strategic level.

### Bucket A — CLEAN EXONERATION

The Audiencia Provincial is excluded from Gil's adverse institutional-accountability perimeter only if its judgment produces a substantively clean exoneration.

For this policy, a `clean exoneration` means, in substance and not merely in rhetoric, an appellate result that removes the materially adverse culpability case against Gil rather than redistributing, narrowing or re-labelling it.

The exact judgment must be analysed before applying the label, but the expected indicators include:

- reversal of the materially adverse culpable-classification findings attributable to Gil;
- removal of the personal/patrimonial consequences based on those findings;
- no preservation through alternative reasoning of materially equivalent factual or causal blame;
- no ambiguous formulation that purports to grant relief while retaining the adverse narrative as an institutional fact;
- no fragmentation that leaves core disputed propositions standing without deciding the documentary contradiction; and
- a disposition/reasoning combination capable of being described fairly as substantive exoneration rather than merely procedural or quantum relief.

A clean exoneration would not erase historical questions concerning the lower court, AC, Fiscalía or private actors, but it would materially change the present strategic treatment of the Audiencia Provincial itself.

### Bucket B — ALL OTHER OUTCOMES

For Gil's strategic purposes, **every outcome short of clean exoneration is treated as insufficient and institutionally adverse**.

This includes, without limitation:

- full dismissal / confirmation of Sentencia 163/2023;
- partial confirmation and partial reversal;
- reduction of damages, disqualification or other consequences while preserving materially adverse findings;
- reversal of some grounds while preserving one or more core culpability/causation findings;
- procedural nullity or remand without substantive exoneration;
- a mixed result designed or structured so that each side can claim some success;
- reasoning that fragments the allegations so that the central contradiction is never confronted as a whole;
- an outcome that avoids deciding actor-specific knowledge, causation or documentary inconsistencies through jurisdictional or procedural compartmentalisation; or
- any formally favourable language that leaves a materially equivalent adverse institutional narrative intact.

Gil may describe such an outcome internally as **strategically equivalent to ratification** of the lower-court adverse frame.

### Mandatory wording control

`Strategically equivalent to ratification` does **not** mean that the Audiencia Provincial has legally affirmed every finding, order, rationale or consequence of the first-instance judgment.

Every future filing, public statement and repository analysis must separately state:

1. what the appellate court legally affirmed;
2. what it reversed;
3. what it modified;
4. what it annulled or remitted;
5. what it did not decide; and
6. how Gil chooses to treat that result strategically.

Do not use the shorthand `the AP ratified the lower court` if the operative judgment is mixed, partial, remittal-based or otherwise legally different from full affirmance.

## 3. Consequence for the Audiencia Provincial accountability perimeter

Gil is presently prepared either to **exclude** or to **include** the Audiencia Provincial in later accountability work depending on the outcome.

### If Bucket A applies

The AP is not treated as an adverse institutional actor in the Calificación outcome chain, absent separate evidence unrelated to the merits/outcome itself.

The judgment becomes important corrective evidence and may materially strengthen:

- the challenge to Sentencia 163/2023;
- the critique of the AC/Fiscal accusation package;
- causation/damages analysis concerning use of the first-instance judgment before correction; and
- requests for correction or reconsideration by institutions that relied on the first-instance adverse frame.

### If Bucket B applies

Gil intends to continue the dispute and is prepared to scrutinise the Audiencia Provincial itself within every legally competent route potentially available.

This does **not** create an automatic civil, criminal, disciplinary or regulatory allegation against the appellate judges.

Before the AP or any member of the panel is included in a specific liability allegation, the same evidence-first threshold applies:

`exact appellate proposition → contrary primary evidence/law → proof it was procedurally before the court → reasonable alternative explanation → material effect → applicable jurisdiction/remedy → actor-specific knowledge or other required mental element where relevant`.

An adverse or unsatisfactory appellate judgment is not, by itself, evidence of prevaricación, corruption, collusion, regulatory breach or civil liability.

The strategic decision is only that Gil will **not treat a mixed/partial/fragmented result as institutional closure or vindication** and will preserve all competent further routes for independent assessment.

## 4. Civil, criminal, regulatory and further-remedy separation

Gil's stated preparedness to fight on civil, criminal and regulatory grounds must remain jurisdictionally separated.

For every proposed next action after the AP judgment, future work must answer independently:

### Appellate / extraordinary judicial remedies

- What further judicial remedy is legally available under the law applicable on the date of the appellate decision?
- Which grounds survive and what deadlines apply?
- What parts of the AP judgment are final, remitted, modified or still challengeable?

Never allow the binary strategic classification to cause loss of a useful partial legal victory or a procedural deadline.

### Criminal

- What exact judicial proposition or conduct is alleged to meet a criminal offence?
- What proves the required knowledge/intent rather than disagreement, error or adverse interpretation?
- Which body has competence over the specific judicial actor and alleged conduct?

### Civil / patrimonial

- What legally recognised cause of action or state-liability route exists?
- What is the actionable act, damage, causal bridge and exhaustion/finality requirement?
- Which losses flow from the first-instance judgment, the appellate judgment, private-actor use, or another independent act?

### Regulatory / institutional

- Which regulator or governance body has competence over which actor?
- Is the complaint about regulated private conduct, professional conduct, court functioning, judicial discipline, public funding, securities/investor disclosure or another defined remit?
- Do not use a regulator as a substitute appellate court.

The existence of several routes does not permit them to be collapsed into a single allegation.

## 5. Middle outcomes — how to analyse them

A middle outcome is strategically in Bucket B, but must still be harvested precisely for every favourable legal or evidential proposition.

Example analytical rule:

> **Strategically adverse does not mean legally worthless.**

If the AP:

- rejects one culpability ground;
- removes a damages award;
- finds indefensión;
- criticises the AC or Fiscal reasoning;
- rejects a causal proposition;
- narrows the relevant period;
- recognises accounting/document production;
- or identifies a lower-court error,

those holdings must be preserved and used even if the overall result remains in Bucket B.

The project must never destroy favourable appellate evidence merely to maintain a binary public narrative.

Thus the AP outcome may be **strategically non-dispositive** while remaining **legally and evidentially highly material**.
