## RT-01 — Art. 164.1 general clause: failure to pursue third-party credits

### AC proposition

The AC alleged that LPB failed to exercise or adequately pursue collection actions concerning, among other items:

- **Sun Energy Spaces:** €101,724.93;
- **customers:** €518,908.69, including:
  - Fermín Pérez Rojas — €4,891.93;
  - Monterecco Sun Park — €41,516.76;
  - Comunidad de Explotación — €472,500;
- **CEXP dividend / short-term investment receivable:** €737,338.85.

The AC said supporting documentation was not produced after liquidation and treated the failures as aggravating insolvency. It also advanced a proposition that, because LPB held more than 70% of the CEXP, part of the €737,338.85 claim was effectively LPB owing itself.

### Same-source qualifications

The report's own documentary annex/communications show continuing attempts to answer documentation requests and identify accounting material. The factual existence, legal collectability and causal effect of the credits were not equivalent questions.

### 2019 opposition

The opposition disputed both the factual/corporate analysis and the causal leap from an uncollected receivable to culpable aggravation of insolvency.

### Sentencia 163/2023

The court materially rejected this branch under the general clause. It found relevant recovery uncertainty/difficulty and did not find the required gross-fault/causal basis. On the **€737,338.85** item it accepted the expert explanation that this was a valid LPB receivable and rejected the AC's self-debt construction.

The judgment also criticises the AC report for not adequately justifying the dolo/culpa-grave element necessary for the general clause.

### Appeal/current status

The rejected general-clause branch remains part of the evidential comparison because the narrower missing-document issue was used elsewhere under collaboration.

### Current classification

**REJECTED AS GENERAL-CLAUSE CULPABILITY / AC PROPOSITION MATERIALLY CORRECTED.**

## RT-02 — Art. 164.1 general clause: 6-Feb-2012 lease / Pink Canary / unpaid rent

### AC proposition

The AC relied on the 6-Feb-2012 hotel-operation lease to Monterecco Sun Park/Pink Canary and the subsequent non-payment/underpayment of rent. The report compares approximate monthly rent of **€24,575**, plus approximately **€33,915.29** in community charges, with approximate monthly mortgage instalments of **€70,518.96**.

The AC alleged that LPB failed to collect rent and that the AC had to litigate. It relied on the 24-Nov-2015 judgment/enforcement and ultimately sought **€3,032,010.34** as damages.

### Same-source qualifications

The report itself records a live operating/restructuring context, including proposals for future operation, customer demand and third-party operator discussions. The economic rationality of the initial lease and the later failure to collect accrued rent are distinct propositions.

### 2019 opposition

The opposition argued that Pink/Monterecco had operated to preserve the complex and that operating/maintenance costs and the actual economic context had to be accounted for rather than treating headline rent as a standalone cash entitlement.

### Sentencia 163/2023

The court did **not** accept that the lease was culpable merely because the rent figure was lower than mortgage instalments. It did, however, accept an adverse finding based on the failure to collect rent and imposed **€3,032,010.34** in damages against Gil, while declaring Pink an accomplice.

The reasoning includes a profitability inference/presumption that is specifically challenged on appeal.

### Current classification

**ADVERSE FINDING — LIVE ON APPEAL.**

# 6. MINISTERIO FISCAL — 12-MAR-2019 OPINION

## What Ricardo de Mosteyrín Sampalo actually signed

The signed opinion is two pages. It asks that LPB be classified culpable on five grounds:

1. the literal text refers to a `situación de insolvencia agravada por dolo o culpa del administrador concursal`;
2. relevant accounting irregularities;
3. alzamiento de bienes;
4. failure to file the insolvency proceeding within the legal period; and
5. failure to collaborate with the AC.

It identifies Uri Omid and Gil Marer as responsible social administrators and Patricia Domínguez and Pink Canary Services as accomplices.

It requests:

- loss of credit rights;
- damages; and
- deficit coverage.

It requests as evidence the documents in the proceeding, expressly including the AC report and incorporated material, company accounting, testimony of the AC and testimony of the affected persons.

## Facial role contradiction

The opinion literally attributes aggravation to intent or fault of the **insolvency administrator** and then identifies the social administrators as responsible. It may be a drafting error. Radical transparency requires reproducing the defect rather than silently repairing it.

## Analytical depth

The two-page opinion does not set out a proposition-by-proposition reconciliation of the contradictory or qualifying material already embedded in the AC report. It adopts a severe accusation package and seeks extreme personal/patrimonial consequences.

The later judgment:

- rejects the alzamiento branches;
- rejects the annual-accounts presumption;
- rejects or narrows the third-party-credit general-clause theory;
- attributes late filing to Uri, not Gil;
- narrows non-collaboration;
- rejects Patricia's complicity;
- rejects deficit liability because the AC/Fiscal request lacked necessary justification;
- retains materially adverse findings on rent, accounting and narrower collaboration.

## Controlled accountability assessment

On the present record, a **first-person allegation of willful, conscious or reckless prosecutorial negligence is well grounded as an accusation**: prosecutorial authority was placed behind a maximum-severity accusation without the signed opinion demonstrating independent reconciliation of the contrary material in the source record.

The stronger allegation — **knowing, self-serving falsehood** — must be tested proposition by proposition against proof that Ricardo de Mosteyrín personally knew the contrary fact when adopting the opposite proposition. Institutional availability of a document is not automatically personal knowledge.

Gil's present position for publication is therefore:

Where the certified record proves personal knowledge of a contrary fact followed by adoption of the opposite proposition, I allege something worse: knowing, self-serving falsehood.**
