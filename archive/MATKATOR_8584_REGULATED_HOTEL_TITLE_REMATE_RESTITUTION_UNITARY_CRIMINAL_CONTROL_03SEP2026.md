## 1. Controlling proposition

> **ONE REGULATED HOTEL PRODUCTIVE SYSTEM, SEVERAL LEGALLY DISTINCT TITLE COMPARTMENTS.**

Finca 8584 must not be analysed as an autonomous apartment or an ordinary free-standing real-estate asset. Its historic registry description may be quoted as a registry fact, but the present analytical object is a **registered title component inside the functionally unified hotel establishment operated as MYND Yaiza**.

The title is the juridical anchor from which Matkator asserts property, possession, access, Community, hotel-exploitation, accounting, evidential, regulatory, restoration and restitution rights. Therefore, **adjudication itself may create a new patrimonial and remedial injury before any later cession of the remate occurs**.

This control does not determine criminal guilt, ownership disputes, operator authority or the validity of the Cambiario/ETJ. It fixes the correct object, causal architecture and evidential questions.

## 2. Five mandatory distinctions

### 2.1 Hotel ≠ autonomous apartment

The relevant physical and economic use is alleged to be integrated into a hotel with unified access, reception, services, guest allocation, commercialisation, room inventory, revenue collection and operation. A registry unit can continue to exist juridically without functioning as an independent tourism business.

### 2.2 Title ≠ physical room, but title anchors the rights

The project must not argue that physical transformation automatically extinguished finca 8584. The stronger proposition is:

- title may continue;
- the corresponding physical space may have been altered, divided, fused, renumbered, occupied or incorporated into the hotel;
- the title remains the legal reference for reconstructing physical identity, possession, authority, exploitation, income and restitution.

### 2.3 Adjudication injury ≠ cession-only injury

The prejudice is not limited to a later cession to Acosta Matos or another recipient. Adjudication may itself:

- remove or weaken Matkator’s title-based standing;
- fragment accrued, continuing and future injury between old and new holders;
- impair restoration and restitution;
- introduce a claimed third-party registered position;
- detach future hotel use and revenue from Matkator;
- weaken accounting and evidential leverage;
- harden the disputed hotel configuration into apparent normality.

### 2.4 Formal adjudicatario ≠ formal cesionario ≠ real or terminal beneficiary

The formal assignee is not presently proved. The specific evidence-based beneficiary hypothesis is nevertheless not open-ended: it is the **CAM / Hotel New Trend / Canarian Hospitality / Acosta Matos-controlled MYND Yaiza perimeter**, because that is the perimeter alleged to possess, integrate and exploit the corresponding hotel space.

The source-safe formulation is:

> No autonomous, ordinary hotel-use route has presently been identified for an unrelated purchaser acting solely from the registered title. A nominal purchaser’s practical value would appear to depend either on reversing the existing integration through litigation or on reaching an arrangement with the perimeter controlling the hotel operation.

This is an `EVIDENCE_BASED_INFERENCE / PARTY_POSITION`, not proof of a completed cession, nominee arrangement, clandestine payment or concert.

### 2.5 No restitution yet ≠ restitution impossible

The project alleges that inspection, accounting, possession, restoration and effective judicial protection have been impeded by the very civil, concursal, procedural and institutional conduct under challenge, including allegations of obstruction and prevaricación. The present absence of restitution cannot silently become evidence that the rights no longer exist or that damages are the only possible remedy.

That allegation requires actor-specific proof and competent determination.

## 4. The title-right bundle

Subject to title, contract and statutory verification, finca 8584 anchors seven connected classes of right:

1. **Property and common-element rights** — ownership of the private title compartment and inseparable rights in relevant common elements and services.
2. **Possession and access** — identification, access, control, delivery, protection against unauthorised alteration and recovery.
3. **Community/governance rights** — participation, notice, voting, challenge and consent rights where applicable.
4. **Hotel/tourism authority** — the owner-side legal basis relevant to inclusion of the corresponding space in a unified tourism exploitation and any enabling title relied upon by the operator.
5. **Accounts, fruits and revenue** — claims to accounts, compensation, rents, fruits or exploitation proceeds where supported by contract, title or law.
6. **Evidence and standing** — standing to seek plans, licences, operator records, room mappings, inspection, accounting and judicial relief.
7. **Restoration, restitution and damages** — the title reference for unwinding or compensating alleged occupation, physical transformation, exclusion and hotel exploitation.

## 5. Three periods of injury

### 5.1 Accrued injury

Alleged prior occupation, exclusion, destruction or alteration of the original physical configuration, unauthorised use, lost access, lost exploitation and retained income.

### 5.2 Continuing injury

The alleged continuing inclusion of the physical space in the MYND Yaiza operation, continuing possession/access control, room inventory, bookings, revenue and absence of accounting or restoration.

### 5.3 Prospective adjudication injury

Loss of title, fragmentation of remedies, possible third-party Registry protection, reduced restitution prospects, loss of future hotel rights and evidential disconnection between the historical title and the current hotel configuration.

Past criminal responsibility is not automatically extinguished by transfer. The critical risk is that transfer may make restitution, restoration and continuing-right relief materially harder.

## 6. Closed economic structure and beneficiary analysis

This is not an ordinary sale of a self-contained apartment. An unrelated holder would acquire a title embedded in a hotel whose access, operation, services, inventory and revenue are controlled by another perimeter. The holder would not thereby obtain an independently operating hotel asset.

Accordingly, the real-beneficiary inquiry cannot stop at the name on an adjudication or cession document. It must examine:

- for whose account the recipient acts;
- who funds or credits the consideration;
- who retains physical possession and hotel use;
- who receives revenue;
- whether the recipient is connected to CAM, HNT, Canarian Hospitality or their controllers;
- whether the title is intended to remain with, be transferred to, or be economically neutralised for the existing hotel perimeter.

A lawful, transparent settlement is not automatically criminal. The criminal concern would arise if a nominal recipient were used to conceal the real beneficiary, preserve an alleged prior appropriation, avoid accounts/restitution, procure silence or regularise the existing position through materially incomplete judicial disclosure.

## 7. Cuatrecasas knowledge and mandate-to-enforcement inversion

Cuatrecasas entered the history through the wider Aweswell/LPB/Sun Park rescue, finance and insolvency-exit mandate, not as a creditor wholly external to the asset. The controlled record supports historical knowledge of mixed ownership, hotel unity, possession/access risk, financing, operator delivery and the Acosta Matos perimeter.

A genuine fee claim and a potentially valid cambial obligation do not answer the separate question whether later adjudication machinery was used with complete and accurate disclosure of the hotel-title, possession, exploitation, beneficiary and restitution context.
