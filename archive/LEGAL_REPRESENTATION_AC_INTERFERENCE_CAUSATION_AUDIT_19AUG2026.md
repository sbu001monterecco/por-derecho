## 1. Question tested

This pass tested the hypothesis that Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte, as Administrador Concursal of Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U., had a material role in interfering with owner-side legal representation and effectively causing or contributing to successive lawyers leaving, being replaced or reducing their mandates.

The audit was run as a hostile causation test, not as an instruction to confirm the hypothesis. Gmail, Google Drive and the current repository were searched for both supporting and contradicting material. Private correspondence remains in its source system; this repository records only public-safe metadata, evidential classification and analytical consequence.

The governing distinction is:

> **AC conduct may materially burden or constrain a lawyer's work without being the reason that lawyer leaves.**

Chronology is not causation. Professional conflict with the AC is not proof of improper interference. Objective effect, foreseeability and intent are separate questions.

## 2. Causation scale

- **C0** — no identifiable causal connection on the current record.
- **C1** — temporal/background connection only; direct causes point elsewhere.
- **C2** — plausible contributing factor, materially mixed or incomplete.
- **C3** — materially supported contribution to workstream burden, disruption, cost or strategy; not necessarily to exit.
- **C4** — strong evidence of substantial causal contribution to the representation change.
- **C5** — direct contemporaneous evidence that AC conduct caused or materially precipitated the representation change.

No lawyer is assigned C4/C5 exit causation in this pass.

## 3. Executive finding

The hostile test **does not support the broad proposition that Borja caused the succession of lawyers or systematically pushed the principal lawyers away**.

It does support a narrower and more defensible proposition:

> Across several independent legal teams, AC-controlled procedural, authority and information dependencies repeatedly created additional work, strategic constraints, conflict or workaround requirements for owner-side counsel. Those burdens were material in particular workstreams, but the contemporaneously documented reasons for actual departures are more often unpaid fees, client-lawyer strategy/confidence, or failed replacement continuity.

The present record also shows a genuine **continuity asymmetry**: the AC remained institutionally continuous throughout a very long proceeding while the owner side repeatedly had to onboard, coordinate or replace lawyers. That asymmetry may have increased the practical cost of each transition, but it does not prove that the AC caused the transitions.

A recurring and independently documented counterfactor is **non-payment or disputed professional fees**. Any future attrition theory that suppresses that evidence would be unreliable.

No direct evidence has been located in this pass that Borja deliberately intended to isolate, discourage or push away a lawyer.

## 5. Key episodes

### A. Garrigues → early replacement, 2012

Garrigues was deeply involved immediately before the Concurso: litigation with the Comunidad, Article 5 bis/pre-insolvency work, Bankia enforcement/subasta and LPB insolvency preparation. At the point representation changed, the private contemporaneous record contains a serious client-side dispute over fees/value. Juan Tomás was then being asked to take over pending litigation within days of the Concurso opening.

**Result:** the present evidence does not support Borja as a cause of the Garrigues transition. This is a material counterexample and must remain visible.

### B. Juan Tomás Parrilla replacement, May–June 2020

The substitution communication was expressly framed as a mutual loss of confidence. Parrilla accepted the client's decision, said it was best for both sides, granted venia and made himself available to the successor.

The AC became materially involved in the subsequent dispute over fees/costs and how the outgoing lawyer's position would be handled in the Concurso. That post-exit involvement should be audited separately.

**Result:** AC impact on the handover mechanics is plausible/material; AC causation of the initial replacement is not established.

### C. Cuatrecasas renunciation, 26 May 2020

This is the strongest direct counterevidence to the broad hypothesis. Cuatrecasas' own formal correspondence says that non-payment of its fees for more than two years prevented it continuing to advise Aweswell in the LPB Concurso. The firm granted Daniel Jiménez venia and assisted with handover.

Cuatrecasas had nevertheless been dealing with difficult AC/liquidation/registry issues, so AC-generated work and exit causation must not be conflated.

**Result:** exit causation C0 for Borja on the current record.

### D. Daniel Jiménez — June 2020 AC friction vs formal resignation

Daniel's correspondence documents genuine professional friction with the AC. In the days before resignation he objected to the idea that his own fee position should depend on what the AC thought, and described a breakdown in negotiations involving the outgoing LPB lawyer and AC. Separate correspondence shows disagreement about which lawyer/client channel should put questions to the AC and what information could properly be requested.

However, Daniel's formal resignation identifies three reasons: strategy divergence with Gil Marer; disagreement over who controlled legal strategy; and unpaid agreed fees. It does not name Borja as the reason for leaving.

**Result:** material workstream pressure C3; exit causation only C1–C2. Any stronger statement would suppress direct counterevidence.

### E. DWF/RCD — accepted transition but no formal personation

The DWF/RCD team actively received the proposed handover, requested venia/information and analysed recovery, removal of the AC, conclusion/convenio and Bankia-related routes. Later correspondence records that they did not formally appear.

The current search has not located the decision record explaining why.

**Result:** this is a high-value open evidential gap. It cannot presently be used as an AC-caused attrition episode.

### F. Luis Miguel López — prolonged unpaid continuation despite AC conflict

Luis Miguel repeatedly sought to renounce or transfer matters, yet continued acting to avoid indefensión when successor counsel did not formally take over. His February 2023 renunciation correspondence records that he had received no payment since starting in 2020 and that he and Cristo had continued responding to court acts despite this.

The same correspondence contains significant independent criticism of the AC and recommends AC removal/liability review. It also describes AC-controlled conditions affecting contemplated actions, including the swap workstream.

Crucially, Luis Miguel remained active after the renunciation episode and filed the LPB calificación appeal in late 2023.

**Result:** AC conflict/constraint C3; AC-caused exit only C1–C2. The more direct attrition mechanism is prolonged non-payment plus failed replacement continuity.

### G. Sixto — September 2023 LPB swap/power episode

This is one of the clearest examples of **objective workstream interference/constraint without lawyer attrition**.

The AC's position on whether LPB would grant/validate the required authority led Javier and Estefanía to reconsider standing, filing strategy and the risk that the claim could fail procedurally. Sixto explored alternative creditor/Aweswell routes and preserved the litigation position.

The strongest defence is also substantial: an AC responsible for the debtor/estate may legitimately verify that proposed litigation corresponds to the action previously considered and is in the estate's interest.

**Result:** C3 workstream impact. Exit C0. Sixto adapted rather than withdrawing.

### H. Sixto — 2025–2026 separation/removal litigation

Javier and Estefanía continued through direct adversarial proceedings against the AC, including the LPB and Aweswell separation/removal appeals, opposition to AC filings, evidence submissions and AP accumulation issues.

This matters methodologically. If direct conflict with Borja automatically caused owner-side lawyers to leave, Sixto should be a strong example. Instead, the evidence currently shows the opposite: sustained representation despite direct procedural opposition.

**Result:** strong counterevidence to a generalized push-away theory.

### I. Carlos Llamas — current representation

Carlos is active across current Matkator, criminal/procedural and recovery work and coordinates filings with procuradora Adriana Hernández Díaz. AC-related subject matter is part of the broader case environment, but this pass has not identified direct Borja-to-Carlos conduct materially interfering with Carlos's current mandate.

**Result:** no exit issue; continue monitoring rather than infer a pattern.

## 6. Pattern test

### Supported

- recurring AC/estate authority dependency;
- recurring extra procedural work and strategy adaptation;
- recurring professional conflict with the AC in important workstreams;
- owner-side continuity burden across a prolonged proceeding;
- recurring unpaid-fee pressure as an independent attrition factor.

### Not supported on the current record

- a recurring causal chain `Borja conduct → lawyer leaves`;
- a deliberate strategy by Borja to isolate or push lawyers away;
- treating every lawyer change as part of one continuous AC-caused phenomenon.

## 7. Strongest defence and what survives it

The strongest reasonable AC defence is that:

1. the AC was exercising statutory/fiduciary functions in a difficult Concurso;
2. litigation for LPB required estate-level control and verification;
3. requests for information/power/authority were legitimate safeguards rather than interference;
4. lawyer exits were driven by unpaid fees, client strategy or confidence issues;
5. opposition to AC removal is ordinary procedural defence;
6. no outgoing lawyer located in this pass directly says Borja caused the departure.

After accepting those points where supported, a narrower issue survives:

> **Did the cumulative way in which AC authority, information, standing and debtor-control powers were exercised create avoidable or disproportionate professional friction, duplicated work, delay, cost or strategic paralysis for successive owner-side lawyers?**

That is now the stronger investigation question.

## 8. Highest-value evidence still missing

1. **DWF/RCD non-personation decision record** — retainer, fee, conflict, onboarding and decision correspondence explaining why an apparently active July-2020 transition never became formal substitution.
2. **Complete 2012 Garrigues→Juan Tomás handover** — final bill/venia/file-transfer and first AC communications, to close the earliest causation cell.
3. **Lawyer-by-lawyer fee ledger** — engagement terms, invoices, payments and arrears, because fee attrition is a major confounder.
4. **Matter-specific Luis Miguel renunciation map** — distinguish each proceeding/mandate rather than treating the February-2023 subject line as a global exit.
5. **Quantified AC-generated workload** — identify extra filings, meetings, appeals, delays and costs causally attributable to specific AC positions while testing whether those positions were legitimate.
6. **Direct resignation-attribution evidence** — any contemporaneous statement by an outgoing lawyer that a specific AC act caused or materially precipitated withdrawal.
7. **Current counsel monitoring** — direct Borja interactions with Carlos/Sixto, preserving evidence both of disruption and of successful continued advocacy.

## 9. Public-writing lock

Do **not** publish:

> “Borja pushed our lawyers away.”

The present safe formulation, if this issue later becomes public, is closer to:

> “The record documents recurring procedural and authority conflicts with the Administrador Concursal that materially increased the complexity of several owner-side legal workstreams. The present evidence does not establish that those conflicts caused the succession of lawyers; where departures are contemporaneously explained, other direct causes—especially unpaid fees and client-lawyer strategy/confidence—predominate.”

Any future upgrade requires actor-specific C4/C5 evidence and must preserve the counterevidence.
