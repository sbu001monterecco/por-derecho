## English feed post — publication-ready

**CUATRECASAS: WHO WAS THE CONTEMPLATED THIRD PARTY?**

Aweswell Limited—a UK company and foreign investor in Spain—was the documented client. Cuatrecasas correspondence called it “our client, AWESWELL.” The firm’s July 2019 billing reconstruction attributed issued invoices to Aweswell and Luchy Playa Blanca, not Matkator.

Matkator later became pagaré debtor, judicial debtor and executed party through four promissory notes. A valid autonomous pagaré may create that liability. But the distinction matters: the former client/billed relationship and the later instrument-enforcement relationship are not the same thing.

In ETJ 163/2020, after seeking adjudication of finca 8584 at 70%, Cuatrecasas’ 10 February 2026 filing expressly reserved **“cession of the remate to a third party.”** The current record proves that reservation. It does not identify a recipient or prove a completed cession.

That creates a legitimate disclosure dilemma—not coercion and not a literal prisoner's dilemma. The pressure comes from making every possible answer auditable:

1. **No third party:** certify no one was contemplated, contacted, negotiated with, selected or used.
2. **Independent third party:** identify recipient/UBO where lawfully disclosable; reconcile consideration, applicable KYC/conflict controls and full debtor credit.
3. **Client or connected person:** explain enhanced former-client loyalty, own-interest conflict, confidentiality, information-barrier and institutional-approval controls.
4. **Lawful withholding:** identify the legal category, period and control owner; provide a non-privileged index or certification.

Silence is not proof. It leaves a precise, document-testable gap.

The only stable lawful outcome is one single-satisfaction ledger:

**invoice → WIP → pagaré → claim → remate/adjudication → cession/transfer → credit/recovery → balance**

It must reconcile the four pagarés, both claim principals, the €77,840 adjudication credit, the stated €104,677.72 remaining against other Matkator assets, and any contemplated or completed third-party transfer.

The former-client context is cross-border: Aweswell retained services spanning the UK and Spain. Since 2020–2021, I have reported concerns as a reporting person/whistleblower and directly and indirectly affected party. The precise application of Spanish, EU or UK protection is a legal question; that history must be preserved and addressed.

This post does not assert an assignee, completed cession, invalid pagarés, connected recipient, collusion or double recovery. It asks Cuatrecasas to close the record with documents. Any source-supported response will be published with parity.

## English long-form supporting note

**CUATRECASAS’ OWN PROCEDURAL POSITION CREATES A DISCLOSURE DILEMMA.**

The entities must not be blurred.

Aweswell Limited—a UK company and foreign investor in Spain—was the documented client. Contemporaneous Cuatrecasas correspondence called it “our client, AWESWELL.” The firm’s own July 2019 billing reconstruction attributed issued invoices to Aweswell and Luchy Playa Blanca, not Matkator.

Matkator later became the pagaré debtor, judicial debtor and executed party through four promissory notes. A valid autonomous pagaré may create that liability; this post does not deny that legal possibility.

But in ETJ 163/2020, after requesting adjudication of finca 8584 at 70%, Cuatrecasas’ 10 February 2026 filing expressly reserved **“cession of the remate to a third party.”** The current record proves the express reservation. It does not yet identify a recipient or prove that any cession was completed.

That creates a legitimate, document-production version of a game-theory dilemma. It is not a literal prisoner's dilemma and it is not coercion: the pressure comes from making every available position auditable, while complete disclosure and reconciliation remain the only stable lawful outcome. Every possible answer is testable:

1. **No third party existed or was contemplated.** Certify that no one was contacted, negotiated with, selected or used.
2. **An independent third party was contemplated.** Identify the recipient and beneficial owner to the extent lawfully disclosable, and reconcile consideration, KYC/conflict controls and the full credit to Matkator’s account.
3. **A client, former client, prospective client, affiliate, referral source, counterparty or connected person was contemplated.** Explain the heightened former-client loyalty, own-interest conflict, confidentiality, information-barrier, institutional-approval and—where legally applicable—KYC/beneficial-ownership controls.
4. **The answer is lawfully withheld.** Identify the legal category, date range and control owner, and provide a non-privileged index or certification.

Silence is not proof of wrongdoing. But silence leaves a precise, auditable gap.

The stable and legitimate outcome is one complete **single-satisfaction ledger**:

**invoice → WIP → pagaré → claim → adjudication/remate → cession or transfer → payment/credit → remaining balance**

That ledger should explain the relationship between:

- the former client and the entities originally billed;
- the later executed debtor;
- every invoiced and unbilled/WIP amount;
- the four pagarés and both judicial principals;
- the €77,840 adjudication credit;
- the stated €104,677.72 remaining against other Matkator assets;
- any contemplated or completed third-party transfer; and
- every payment, credit, recovery, surplus and remaining claim.

This is also cross-border former-client context. Aweswell is a British investor that retained services spanning the UK and Spain. Since 2020–2021, I have communicated concerns in a reporting-person/whistleblower capacity and as a directly and indirectly affected party. The precise application of any Spanish, EU or UK statutory protection is a legal question; the reporting history and cross-border relationship are facts that should be preserved and addressed.

This post does **not** assert that an assignee exists, that a cession occurred, that the pagarés are invalid, that a connected party received the remate, that anyone colluded, or that double recovery occurred. It asks Cuatrecasas to close the record with documents. Any source-supported correction or response will be published with parity.

**Who, if anyone, was the contemplated third party—and how would the transaction have been cleared, valued, credited and reconciled?**
