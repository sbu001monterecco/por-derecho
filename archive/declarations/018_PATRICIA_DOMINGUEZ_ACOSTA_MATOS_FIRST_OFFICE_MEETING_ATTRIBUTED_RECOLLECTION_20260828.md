## 1. Attributed recollection

Gil Marer states that Patricia Domínguez recalls that, during her first meeting with José Daniel Acosta Matos at the Acosta Matos office in Las Palmas, with lawyer **Cristo Suárez Pimentel** accompanying her, José Daniel explained in substance why the Acosta Matos side had become involved with the Sun Park / Luchy Playa Blanca matter.

According to Patricia's recollection as relayed by Gil:

1. José Daniel said that the relevant dissident owners were friends of his father, **Gerardo Nicanor Acosta Armas** (now deceased).
2. Those dissident owners had approached his father and asked for a favour: that the Acosta Matos side become involved, take over or help reunify the hotel.
3. José Daniel described the dissidents as being very unhappy with **Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. (LPB)** and wanting LPB removed from the hotel/project perimeter. Patricia recalls him conveying, in substance, that they hated Luchy/LPB and wanted it gone.
4. Patricia recalls José Daniel explaining that the premise was that, once LPB was gone, the hotel could be reunified and rebuilt/refurbished, and that this request and objective were the reason the Acosta Matos side became involved.
5. Patricia's recollection therefore attributes to José Daniel an origin account in which the Acosta Matos intervention did not arise in isolation: it followed an approach by dissident owners linked socially to his father and was connected, in his explanation as remembered by Patricia, to removing LPB and enabling the hotel to be rebuilt/reunified.

The word **“late”** is not attributed retrospectively to José Daniel at the meeting. Gerardo Nicanor Acosta Armas is described here as *now deceased* because his death was publicly reported in March 2024. The meeting itself may have predated that death.

## 4. Death, historical conduct and patrimonial-harm control

Gerardo Nicanor Acosta Armas's later death must not be used to erase the historical inquiry. The controlled legal/evidential distinction is:

- death does not erase historical acts, communications, knowledge, causation evidence, alleged patrimonial harm, benefit analysis, document-custody questions or the conduct and possible responsibility of surviving actors;
- under **Spanish Penal Code article 130.1.1**, the deceased person's own criminal responsibility is extinguished by death;
- separately, **LECrim articles 115 and 116** provide that extinction of the penal action by death does not automatically extinguish the civil action; article 115 expressly preserves the civil route against heirs and successors-in-title, subject to the applicable civil law, standing, limitation, estate and causation requirements; and
- accordingly, repository language must not say that personal criminal culpability “passes” to heirs or survives death, but neither may death be represented as extinguishing the underlying historical facts or all patrimonial/restitutionary consequences.

This distinction is especially important where the investigation concerns an alleged chain of conduct spanning a founder generation, later directors, companies, assets, benefits and successors. Every person's and entity's position remains actor-specific and source-led.
