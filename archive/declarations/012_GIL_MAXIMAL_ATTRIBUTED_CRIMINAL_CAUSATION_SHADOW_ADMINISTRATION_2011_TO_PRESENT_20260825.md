Gil Marer expressly advances the following **maximal criminal-causation allegation**, subject to proof and legal characterisation:

> Gil alleges that the 2011 reconfiguration of the governing organ of the Comunidad de Propietarios Sun Park was not merely a defective or hostile community-law transition but the beginning of an allegedly unlawful mechanism by which identified private actors obtained and maintained practical control over Community authority, documentation, debt attribution, voting eligibility, litigation and later access to or influence over assets connected with Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. (LPB).
>
> Gil further alleges that this private-actor perimeter subsequently operated both **outside and inside Concurso 36/2012** in a manner functionally comparable to de facto or shadow administration: exercising or influencing decisions over assets, access, Community authority, litigation positions, debt, possession, transactions, restructuring opportunities and later material control without being formally appointed as administrators of LPB's insolvent estate and without, in Gil's allegation, an adequate independent defence of the estate against that perimeter.
>
> Gil alleges that the conduct should be investigated as a potentially coordinated fraud/insolvency-misconduct sequence. He uses the expression **"estafa concursal"** as an attributed description of the alleged overall mechanism, not as a claim that Spanish criminal law contains one self-proving offence bearing that label or that every element of any offence has already been established.
>
> Gil alleges that the court-appointed insolvency administrator, Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte, materially enabled the private actors through a combination of **omissions, commissions, silence and inaction**, including alleged failures to investigate, challenge, prevent or correct the continued use of disputed Community authority and alleged failures to protect LPB's estate, restructuring opportunities, possession, income and other rights from private actors whose authority was contested.
>
> Gil does **not** limit the allegation to a single knowledge state. His position is that the evidence should test at least three alternatives: **(A)** the private actors deceived or materially misled the insolvency administrator and he failed to detect it; **(B)** the insolvency administrator became aware, or had sufficient notice and access to information that continued ignorance became arguably reckless or untenable, yet continued to enable the mechanism; or **(C)** further evidence may show actual knowledge, deliberate facilitation or coordinated participation.
>
> Gil further alleges that the resulting acts and omissions may have deceived the court, or may have allowed materially incomplete or misleading propositions to reach or remain before the court. This is a separate proposition requiring proof of the exact representation or omission, the actor responsible, the court's knowledge, causation and the relevant state of mind. Gil does not ask the repository to state as fact that the judge was deceived, knowingly participated, or committed wrongdoing merely because an adverse judicial act occurred.

## 3. "Shadow administrator" / de facto administration allegation

The repository may preserve Gil's allegation that specified private actors acted as **shadow administrators / de facto controllers** of LPB-related assets or decision-making, but must use those expressions as **functional allegations to test**, not as adjudicated legal status.

For each alleged shadow/de facto actor, future analysis must identify:

1. the specific asset, right or decision affected;
2. the date and proceeding context;
3. what authority the person formally held, if any;
4. what act or instruction demonstrates practical control;
5. whether LPB, the AC, the Community, CEXP, Matkator or another owner formally authorised the act;
6. whether the AC followed, ratified, tolerated, failed to oppose or independently originated the decision;
7. the resulting economic or possessory consequence; and
8. the strongest innocent or lawful explanation.

No person should be labelled a de facto administrator merely because they were a creditor, owner, lawyer, Community officer, purchaser, investor or beneficiary.

## 4. Insolvency administrator — maximum attributed theory and knowledge ladder

Gil's allegation concerning Rodríguez-Batllori should be tested in ascending evidentiary stages:

### A. Possible deception of the AC
The private-actor perimeter may have presented Community debt, authority, documentation, ownership, possession or entitlement in a way that caused the AC to rely on an incomplete or false picture.

### B. Recklessness / culpable non-inquiry allegation
Gil alleges that, even if the AC was initially deceived, there came a point at which he had enough notice, professional duty, access to records and contradictory evidence that failure to investigate or correct the position became, in Gil's view, **reckless**. This is an attributed legal/factual allegation requiring a date-specific duty and evidence analysis.

### C. Actual knowledge
Gil alleges that further evidence may establish that the AC actually knew material propositions were false, disputed or outside the private actors' authority and nevertheless continued relevant conduct or omissions.

### D. Deliberate facilitation / participation
The highest allegation — deliberate facilitation, common purpose or criminal participation — must not be inferred from stages A–C. It requires independent evidence of intent, agreement, knowing assistance, concealment, improper purpose or equivalent legally relevant conduct.

## 5. Possible court deception — separate proof track

The phrase **"deceiving the judge"** must always be unpacked into finite propositions:

`exact statement or omission`
→ `who authored / transmitted / adopted it`
→ `what contradictory information existed`
→ `who knew that information`
→ `what the judge actually received / knew`
→ `what judicial act relied on the proposition`
→ `whether the proposition was material`
→ `state of mind of the private actor / AC`
→ `alternative lawful explanation`.

No adverse order proves that the judge was deceived. No omission by the AC proves an intention to deceive. No court knowledge should be inferred from mere filing unless receipt/incorporation and relevance are established.

## 6. Criminal offence families to test — not conclusions

Gil's attributed overall phrase "estafa concursal" should be decomposed into potentially relevant offence families for counsel/prosecutorial assessment, including where factually and temporally appropriate:

- fraud / procedural fraud;
- insolvency-related offences and simulation or recognition of fictitious credits where evidence supports it;
- disloyal administration where a person with legally relevant powers over another's assets allegedly exceeded them and caused loss;
- documentary falsity / knowing use of false documents where a specific false document and state of mind can be proved;
- misappropriation, coercion, damage or execution-frustration theories where their elements are independently supported;
- participation, cooperation or omission-based theories only where Spanish law recognises the relevant duty and form of participation.

The repository must not convert the user's umbrella label into a categorical legal conclusion.

> A documented Community-governance/litigation intervention in 2011 was followed by a documented debt/vote/authority mechanism; identified private actors later continued to exercise or accumulate practical capacities affecting LPB-related rights and assets; the court-appointed insolvency administrator was repeatedly positioned to investigate and defend the estate against contested private authority; and later events require an actor-by-actor inquiry into whether failures or affirmative acts were innocent, negligent, reckless, knowingly facilitative or deliberately coordinated.

This formulation preserves Gil's maximum allegation while keeping the evidentiary question open at every stage.
