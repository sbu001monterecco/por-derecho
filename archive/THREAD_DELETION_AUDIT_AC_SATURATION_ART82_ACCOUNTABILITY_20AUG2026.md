## 5. Article 82 TRLC — strategy corrected in this thread

Official consolidated TRLC text was rechecked on 20 August 2026.

- **Article 80** anchors AC diligence, efficiency in the interest of the concurso, impartiality and independence.
- **Article 82** provides that the AC is subject to judicial supervision and that the insolvency judge may at any time require specific information or a report/memorandum about the state of the proceeding or another insolvency-related question.
- **Article 83** provides that decisions resolving questions concerning exercise of the AC office take the form of an `auto`, are not appealable, and the matter resolved may not then be raised by concursal incident.
- **Articles 94–99** separately regulate civil responsibility, including estate-loss responsibility, individual direct-loss actions, limitation and competent procedure.
- **Articles 100–104** separately regulate separation/revocation, replacement, accounts after removal and appeals concerning appointment/revocation/cessation.

### Consequence

A **broad Article 82 application asking the same court to determine historic AC wrongdoing is not a default next step**.

It risks doing the wrong job procedurally: converting a supervision/information mechanism into a merits forum, while Article 83 expressly limits appeal and later concursal-incident re-litigation of the matter resolved. It could also create avoidable overlap with the existing separation appeal, DP 1956, calificación, civil-responsibility and other accountability routes.

### Residual utility of Article 82

Article 82 may still have value **only after specific counsel validation** where the objective is narrowly operational and evidence-preserving, for example:

- requiring production of a defined accounting schedule, bank record, inventory or status report;
- preserving estate records/assets and preventing continuing information loss;
- obtaining a current, dated position on an unresolved operational matter;
- forcing a finite reconciliation that a competent later body can test.

Any such request should be drafted so it does **not**:

- ask the court to exonerate or condemn historic conduct through a supervisory shortcut;
- treat late compliance as retroactive validation;
- waive, abandon or narrow pending appeals, criminal routes, responsibility claims or third-party rights;
- ask for a ruling on a matter that should instead remain available in the dedicated responsibility/removal/appeal procedure.

**Operational lock:** before an Article 82 request is filed, counsel must map the requested proposition against Article 83, the exact pending AP object, DP 1956, calificación and any Articles 94–104 route. If the same historical issue is already being litigated or preserved elsewhere, the presumption is **do not create a duplicative Article 82 merits determination**.

## 6. No “redemption by cure” — evidential rule

The repository and public site must preserve this distinction:

> **Later production, correction, restitution or improved accounting can reduce continuing harm and can be relevant evidence, but it does not retroactively determine whether an earlier act or omission complied with the AC's duties and does not extinguish separate criminal, civil, concursal, disciplinary or appellate questions.**

The chronology must therefore record separately:

1. the original act/omission;
2. what the AC knew at that time;
3. contrary notice then available;
4. resulting harm/advantage/risk;
5. later correction or non-correction;
6. whether later correction mitigated damage;
7. whether it changes any legal element in a particular proceeding.

Do not erase a historical event because it was later corrected. Equally, do not treat later correction as proof of prior guilt; its significance is proceeding-specific.

### Judicial firewall — mandatory

Every event must be classified as one of:

1. direct AC act/omission;
2. judicial result materially conditioned by AC factual input/calculation/recommendation;
3. AC implementation/non-correction after a judicial authorisation;

**Control wording:** judicial approval of an act does not by itself convert the AC's preparation of factual premises, calculations, professional recommendation, material implementation, signature, custody of funds, restitution duties or later accounting into a purely judicial act. Conversely, an adverse judicial ruling cannot be attributed to the AC as criminal conduct without an actor-specific evidential bridge.
