## 4. Canonical redundancy / lender-comfort architecture

The funded-exit architecture must not be reduced to a single prospective lender or a single fragile condition. The contemporaneous record shows an intentionally redundant package designed to reduce bridge-lender risk and preserve alternatives.

### 4.1 Operating solution
- ONA/Clubotel was being developed as the professional hotel operator.
- Cuatrecasas reviewed and marked up the ONA contractual structure.
- The hotel-industry lease and suspensive-condition annex were signed on 6 Jun 2018.
- ONA's Nacho Barrau was directly engaged in the funding/exit workstream and remained available to prospective lenders.

### 4.2 Multiple financing routes
The record shows concurrent or backup routes, including:
- EG / CR Management bridge financing and term-sheet work;
- Stoneweg / Varia route;
- Ben Oldman Partners, Avenue Capital and Mediterranean Capital Management — identified on 20 Apr as the three funds already presented the transaction and showing interest; Ben Oldman and Mediterranean later specifically requested greater exit visibility / the SOCIMI LOI to move forward;
- Santander structured-finance / hotel-real-estate team review;
- additional HNW-plus-bank support discussed as a fallback if bank capacity did not cover the full requirement;
- further institutional outreach identified in the existing pre-7-Jun source map.

These routes were not equally mature. The repository must distinguish credit-approved / term-sheet / active diligence / preliminary interest / verbal fallback instead of describing every route as committed funding.

### 4.3 Independent sale / repayment backstop
The contemporaneous lender-facing rationale is explicit: a credible hotel sale route was used as additional lender comfort so that, if the bridge funded the exit from Concurso 36/2012 but the ownership side later could not refinance or continue, there would be a realistic asset-sale route capable of repaying the bridge.

Evidence presently controlled:
- 10 May 2018 lender-chain correspondence expressly says the team was working to “reduce the risk” and describes REIT/SOCIMI interest as a “clear exit” for the lender.
- That same correspondence records a positive Santander structured-finance discussion and parallel bank work, plus additional HNW/bank fallback support.
- Aweswell signed a preferential-acquisition right dated 25 May 2018 in favour of Lagune Hospitalidad, S.L. / Batipart Group. It was expressly linked to obtaining bridge financing through Ben Oldman Partners or Mediterranean Capital Management and was time-limited to six months from satisfaction of the stated conditions, subject to renewal/extension.
- On 28 May 2018 Carlos Sanz sent Nacho Barrau the “debidamente firmado” preferential-acquisition letter for the Elaia-related participating vehicle.
- The 30 May 2018 buyer-issued LOI from Lagune Hospitalidad, S.L. / Batipart Group offered **€26,000,000** for the Sun Park asset and stated that the buyer had the purchase price in **own funds**, so the offer was not subject to a financing condition.
- The LOI described its preliminary information as having been supplied directly or through ONA.
- The LOI was indicative/non-binding except for specified clauses, subject to due diligence, board approval, definitive sale documentation and other stated conditions; it expired if not accepted before 8 Jun 2018.
- On 6 Jun 2018 Nacho Barrau transmitted the Elaia/Lagune LOI and warned that they were genuinely interested in purchasing and that uncontrolled contact could affect the negotiation.
- On 8 Jun 2018 the lender package described the €26m SOCIMI purchase offer as the “opción de refinanciación / venta” alongside the bridge, ONA contract, ECO valuation and Cuatrecasas DD.
- On 12 Jun 2018 Gil stated contemporaneously that, although the ownership side did not wish to sell, it had a €26m purchase offer from a hotel SOCIMI as part of the financing-comfort package.

### 4.4 Canonical commercial interpretation
The preferred-owner outcome was **not a sale**. The sale route functioned as a time-bounded fallback / repayment backstop within a broader exit architecture:
1. obtain bridge or replacement capital;
2. pay/consign the relevant insolvency liabilities;
3. conclude the insolvency proceeding;
4. activate professional hotel operation through ONA;
5. refinance/replace the bridge through bank or other capital where possible;
6. only if continuation/refinancing failed, retain a credible sale route capable of protecting lender repayment.

This is a commercial/evidential reconstruction from contemporaneous documents. It must not be restated as proof that every lender had finally committed, that every condition was satisfied, or that the €26m acquisition would certainly have closed.
