## 1. Evidential classification rules

Every proposition derived from this source set must carry one of the following classifications:

- **VERIFIED PROCEDURAL FACT** — shown by a signed court order, court heading, filed pleading or identified procedural reference;
- **VERIFIED DOCUMENTARY FACT** — shown by the document itself, such as the existence and content of a complaint, pleading, email or visual dossier;
- **PARTY ALLEGATION** — asserted by Matkator, Aweswell, Gil Marer, CAM, the insolvency administrator or another party, but not thereby proved;
- **EVIDENCE-BASED INFERENCE** — a reasoned connection supported by identified material, expressly labelled as inference;
- **UNRESOLVED** — requires registry, expert, accounting, contract, revenue, procedural or custody evidence;
- **CONTRADICTED / CORRECTED** — an unsafe proposition narrowed by stronger or later material.

Three controls are especially important:

1. **Complaint ≠ finding.** The 2025 complaint and expansion establish what Matkator alleged and requested, not criminal responsibility.
2. **Photograph ≠ ownership attribution.** A photograph may show works or condition but does not, without a reliable unit map, prove whose finca is depicted, who performed the work or under what authority.
3. **Later title or operation ≠ retrospective authority.** A 2022 corporate, registry or operational event does not automatically validate earlier entry, access, works, exclusion or commercialisation.

- constitutive title and every registered amendment;

## 5. Controlled chronology

### 31 October 2017, 8 January 2018 and 1 March 2018

The 2-May-2018 Arrecife opening order records complaint allegations about José Daniel Acosta Matos entering or seeking access to Sun Park on those dates, making owner-like assertions and acting with others. The order held that, at that initial stage, a coercion theory could not be excluded and opened investigation on that limited episode.

**Do not expand:** the order did not establish whole-hotel ownership, possession, guilt or authorship of every reported damage/interference event.

### 6 and 19 March 2018

The same order provisionally archived two reported damage episodes because the complaint did not provide indicia identifying the author. This is an adverse procedural fact that must be disclosed accurately.

**Do not expand:** unknown authorship of those two incidents does not decide other entry/control allegations or later works.

### 2 May 2018 — DP 1132/2018 opening/severance order

The court:

- opened preliminary proceedings about the identified site-presence episodes;
- ordered statements from the complainant, witnesses and José Daniel Acosta Matos as investigated person;
- requested information from JM nº1 Las Palmas concerning Concurso 36/2012;
- provisionally archived the two damage episodes for unknown author; and
- inhibited the alleged “maquinación engañosa” directed at the Mercantile Court to the Las Palmas deanery because of territorial/connection considerations.

This creates an express **Arrecife ↔ Las Palmas transmission/jurisdiction bridge** that future proceeding scans must track.

### 12 May 2020 — LPB reposición order

The signed order rejected LPB’s reposición. For present purposes, its most important source-control point is that demolition/common-area/access allegations were not adjudicated on their merits; the court treated them as new facts and resulting requests that were extemporaneous within that appeal.

**Corrected proposition:** “the court rejected the demolition allegations” is too broad. The accurate formulation is: **the court declined to address those new requests in that procedural vehicle**.

### 18 December 2020 — Aweswell estate-preservation application

Aweswell alleged ongoing demolition/alteration, estate deterioration and restricted expert access. It asked for:

- permission for privately funded experts to inspect LPB assets;
- prohibition/notice against further works on LPB assets and on assets in the non-convalidated isolated transaction;
- judicial inspection;
- testimony to Fiscalía; and
- an AC report on the active estate and works.

This is a key contemporaneous notice-to-court/AC source, but the allegations remain party assertions unless independently verified.

### 16 February 2021 — CAM opposition

CAM asserted that:

- it was a creditor and did not own LPB apartments merely through the credit position;
- it separately owned 54 Sun Park apartments;
- the 2018 internal reforms concerned only its own units and were licensed; and
- Community resolutions supported access/security measures undertaken for safety.

This is material counterevidence. Any public or internal analysis must test it against exact finca schedules, deeds, licences, plans, dates and access instructions.

### 24 February 2021 — reasoned order

The signed Auto partially upheld Aweswell’s reposición only because the original motivation was too brief; it then maintained the denial of inspection/expert valuation.

The court recorded that photographs showed:

- works inside apartments, without being able to determine whether they belonged to LPB or to units CAM said it had acquired;
- debris in common areas, which the court characterised as producing no more than aesthetic effect once removed; and
- works in common areas involved in the non-convalidated isolated sale, initially supported by a public deed, with the court understanding from the photos/positions that they appeared paralysed.

The court also reasoned that:

- the estate-preservation function belonged to the AC;
- the pending productive-unit sale/improvement context reduced the utility of the requested inspection/expert exercise; and
- a request of this type could make sense in a possible responsibility action against CAM or the AC, but the court considered it dilatory in the liquidation at that time.

**DECIDED:** denial of the requested expert/judicial-inspection relief in that procedural posture, with fuller reasons.

**NOT DECIDED:** no general finding that no damage existed; no finca-by-finca ownership ruling; no ruling on Matkator units; no validation of every work or access act; no final rejection of a separately framed responsibility action.

### 23 February and 8 March 2021 — expert-access correspondence

Gil Marer asked the AC for written authority to enter with experts/notary, quantify alleged harm and prevent further interference. The email criticises the AC and alleges a longer Community/CAM control architecture. The AC’s earlier reply proposed a videoconference.

**Source status:** direct party correspondence. It is useful for notice, request and knowledge chronology, but assertions of responsibility, possession, instruction or coordination require independent proof.

### 11/16 November 2022 — structural transaction/publication

The 2025 complaint relies on BORME-C-2022-7271 concerning the structural transfer/segregation of the Sun Park economic unit from CAM to HNT for transformation/operation as MYND Yaiza. Future work must inspect the complete deed/project of segregation, schedules, title perimeter and registry consequences rather than rely only on the BORME summary or complaint paraphrase.

### 20 February 2025 — initial Matkator complaint

The complaint names HNT and Canarian Hospitality and alleges:

- unauthorised structural reform/division/fusion of Matkator apartments;
- replacement with digital locks and owner exclusion;
- commercial exploitation through MYND without accounts/payment; and
- resulting patrimonial loss.

The visual dossier links Registry/Cadastre, pre/post plans, aerial imagery, project material and public hotel-room information. Its graphics are valuable source-discovery tools but remain party-produced reconstructions.

### 4 March, 6 March and reported 7 March 2025 — version control

Two expansion versions are preserved:

- a version dated **4 March 2025**, signed through Adriana Hernández Díaz;
- a version dated **6 March 2025**, expressly headed **DP 552/2025**, signed through María Luisa Díaz Vecino.

The 13-March ETJ filing states that an expansion was **presented on 7 March 2025**.

**Mandatory control:** do not silently call either 4-Mar or 6-Mar document the controlling filed pleading. Obtain the filed-stamped copy/receipt and annex inventory. Preserve the different procurator/date metadata as a source-status issue, not as evidence of irregularity.

### 13 March 2025 — DP 552/ETJ 163 bridge

Matkator told the civil execution court that DP 552/2025 and its expansion were new facts relevant to:

- nullity/suspension of the auction;
- identity and existence of the auctioned object;
- obsolescence/inaccuracy of the valuation;
- appointment of a judicial expert;

### 8.2 2020–2021 estate-preservation record ↔ later Matkator transformation claim

## 9. Strongest alternative explanations / counterevidence

Any investigation or public draft must fairly test:

1. **CAM defined-unit ownership.** CAM said it owned 54 apartments and worked only inside those units.
2. **Municipal licence.** CAM said the 2018 works were licensed. Verify the exact applicant, title, plans, unit list, dates, conditions and whether the licence covered the works actually performed.
3. **Community safety authority.** CAM said security/access measures followed Community resolutions and technical safety concerns. Verify notices, attendance, title/votes/proxies, minutes, contracts, instructions, invoices and legal scope.
4. **AC non-delivery position.** The AC stated it had not delivered LPB assets to CAM and had sought information about works. Verify the actual requests, responses, inspections and follow-up.
5. **Procedural posture.** The 2020 and 2021 denials may reflect timing, liquidation posture, evidential uncertainty and allocation of estate-preservation functions rather than a merits acceptance of CAM’s account.
6. **Registry/corporate mechanics.** The 2022 HNT entries may result from a corporate structural transaction with schedules that must be read in full; a surprising registry result is not by itself proof of falsification.
7. **Hotel-operating geometry.** Hotel room renumbering or internal commercial allocation does not automatically amend legal boundaries; conversely, an unchanged registry description does not prove that the physical unit remained intact.

These explanations do not resolve the dispute. They define the evidence needed for a reliable conclusion.
