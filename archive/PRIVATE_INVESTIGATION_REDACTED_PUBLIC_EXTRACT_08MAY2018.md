## Evidential status

The original describes itself as a **first-phase extraction and public-source analysis** commissioned to identify and analyse possible links among actors affecting Sun Park. It is investigative material, not a judgment, expert determination or independent proof that every proposition recorded in it is true.

This extract therefore distinguishes:

- the **existence, date and scope of the report**;
- public-source professional/corporate information recorded by the investigator at that time;
- client-supplied investigative leads;

## What the May 2018 report shows

### 1. The investigation pre-dated the 7 June 2018 control event

By 8 May 2018, a licensed private-investigation exercise had already been commissioned to examine the longer Sun Park conflict and possible relationships among minority-owner, Community/administration, insolvency and incoming commercial/project actors.

The report's methodology section says the first phase relied on public-source extraction and analysis and contemplated later correlation of findings and relationship mapping.

### 2. The investigation treated the conflict as extending back to 2008/2011

The report's chronology described:

- the 2008 exploitation-transfer structure;
- the minority-owner dispute;
- a 2011 rupture concerning Community control, exploitation, debt and voting;
- continuing conflict through the insolvency period;
- the later arrival of Construcciones Acosta Matos through the credit position.

Those descriptions include client allegations and analytical characterisations. They are not republished here as adjudicated facts. Their significance is that the continuity hypothesis was already being investigated before the June 2018 control event.

### 3. A hotel executive with a Meeting Point / Labranda background was already a specific investigative subject

The original report contains a dedicated section concerning a hotel executive whose **name is redacted from this public extract**.

The investigator recorded from public sources then available that the executive's professional/corporate background included:

- senior hotel-development/management experience;
- a reported **Meeting Point Hotel Management Canarias** leadership role during 2015–2017;
- reported powers/roles associated with **Meeting Point Investment, S.L.** and **Meeting Point Hotel Management, S.L.**;
- a professional background connected with Labranda/hotel development.

The report says this person was examined because the client had identified a potentially relevant hotel-access/relationship lead. That reason for inquiry is a **client-supplied lead**, not independent proof of wrongdoing.

### 4. Why this matters to the later Sun Park → Lava Verde → Club Sei chronology

A separate January 2019 legal-preparation communication records that José Daniel Acosta Matos attended Sun Park before the 7 June 2018 control event on several occasions with investors/advisers, including the same hotel executive whose identity is redacted here.

The May 2018 report is important because it demonstrates that the executive's Meeting Point/Labranda background was already being investigated **before 7 June 2018** and long before the later public Lava Verde / Club Sei marketing sequence.

It does **not**, by itself, prove that:

- the executive attended Sun Park on any particular date;
- the executive participated in the 2 October 2017, 31 October 2017 or 7 June 2018 events;
- the executive entered any property without authority;
- Meeting Point authorised or participated in any access event;
- a Club Sei contract already existed in 2017 or early 2018;
- any Meeting Point legal entity had acquired title or possession rights.
