## 3. Chronology that must survive deletion of the originating chat

### 23 February 2012 — minority-owner litigation before the insolvency

CF-09 is a one-page pleading in **Juzgado de Primera Instancia nº 4 de Arrecife, Juicio Verbal 1260/2011**. The procurator for Accions Canarias, Muruga, Roque Prieto, Amenem, Tengolf, Miguel Molina Betancor and Francisco Luis Molina Molina states that the existing eviction action was against the then operating entity and that, after learning of a change in exploitation, the claim was expanded against **Monterecco Sun Park, S.L.**, identified at the same Sun Park address/telephone/fax. A hearing was sought for 6 March 2012.

**Directly establishes:** a documented pre-June-2012 owner/operator litigation and change-of-operator controversy; minority-owner litigation did not begin after LPB entered insolvency.

**Does not establish:** the merits of the eviction claim, that the operator change was invalid, that the minority claimants caused the later insolvency, or the final outcome of JV 1260/2011.

**Calificación relevance:** this is a pre-concurso causal/context record. Any later account of LPB's insolvency, hotel operation, CEXP/Community receivables or operator conduct should test the pre-existing unit/operator conflict rather than treating the 2012 insolvency as the origin of every operational problem.

### 12 July 2016 — alleged quantified warning to the AC

CF-02/CF-04 state that Gil sent the AC a burofax asserting that LPB's liabilities were artificially inflated by at least **€2,874,076.19**, with alleged causes including minority-owner eviction litigation, disputed Community debts and costs arising from rupture of the unitary operation.

**Status:** this bundle contains the 2026 pleading's description of the 2016 communication, not the native 12-Jul-2016 burofax and annexes. Treat the amount and causal explanation as a party allegation/source locator until the original is matched.

### 14 January 2019 — DI 248/2018 expansion

CF-08 is the contemporaneous expansion in **Diligencias de Investigación 248/2018** by Gil/Aweswell against the AC/CAM perimeter. It alleges, among other things:

- LPB's relevant properties and the wider Sun Park structure formed a tourism exploitation unit;
- a registered **OB REM** linkage constrained separate disposal of specified properties;
- the AC had allegedly allowed CAM possession/control beyond its creditor position;
- a sale of linked/local/common-area properties occurred around **28 November 2018** during the contested/suspended liquidation setting;
- the complainants were pursuing a former Article-176 LC conclusion route and stated that financing had been obtained; and
- Fiscalía was asked to obtain statements and transaction documents.

**Directly establishes:** the allegations, requested diligences and rescue/exit assertion were formally articulated contemporaneously in the Fiscalía track before the 12-Mar-2019 adverse Fiscal classification opinion and 7-May-2019 DI 248 archive.

**Does not establish:** that the alleged sale, possession or commissions were unlawful/criminal; that the financing route was legally sufficient or would necessarily have concluded the insolvency; or that Fiscalía accepted the allegations.

### 13 May 2019 — Aweswell opposition and the unitary-acquisition text

CF-10 is an Aweswell pleading in Concurso 36/2012 opposing AC escrito 1760/19 and the 7-May-2019 decree. Its evidential importance is two-layered:

1. **Aweswell's position:** the Plan's principal path was a combined dación of the mortgaged apartments plus direct sale of the linked/common properties; the June-2018 suspension therefore prevented isolated completion; the 28-Nov-2018 sale preceded the 12-Dec-2018 court-office authorisation; Registry of Tías suspended registration/cancellation of the OB REM linkage on 9-Jan-2019; and the AC sought convalidation on 20-Mar-2019.
2. **Reproduced AC/Plan material:** pages 5–6 reproduce the Plan language describing CAM's offer as an **“oferta de adquisición unitaria de la unidad productiva de la concursada (sus activos inmobiliarios)”**. The reproduced text also states that the reception/restaurant/bar/toilets/pool/gardens formed common equipment/dotations, were not susceptible to independent commercial exploitation by third parties, and that CAM intended to exploit the complex once it obtained the apartments. The same reproduced passage records a valuation of **€6,607,551.87** for 159 apartments, secured debt of **€12,251,446.21**, a **€400,000** direct-sale price for linked/common properties against a stated valuation of **€489,361.96**.

**Why this is high value:** the reproduced adverse-side/AC Plan language is materially stronger than a bare later allegation that the assets were economically unitary. It is a direct retrieval lead showing that the liquidation architecture itself was presented in unitary-production terms and that the common/linked properties were described as lacking independent commercial exploitation.

**Limit:** CF-10 is still a party pleading. The original Plan, CAM offer, valuation, 16-Apr/4-Jun/26-Jun-2018 orders, 28-Nov deed, 12-Dec diligence, 9-Jan Registry qualification, 20-Mar AC filing and later convalidation decision remain the stronger sources for exact legal effect.

### 13 January 2026 — complaint alleging judicial prevarication

CF-01 is a criminal complaint to Fiscalía Provincial de Las Palmas concerning the judicial handling of the AC-separation request in Concurso 36/2012, anchored to the **12-Sep-2025** and **11-Nov-2025** orders. The complaint argues that the court recognised ex-officio supervisory powers but declined to examine the underlying allegations, producing what the pleading characterises as a circular standard: no examination because no indicia, and no indicia because no examination.

**Public-safe formulation:** Gil Marer filed a complaint asking Fiscalía to investigate whether the handling of the AC-supervision/separation material could have criminal relevance. The filing does not establish prevaricación, intentional omission or any criminal liability.

### 19 January 2026 — Valencia banking-origin criminal complaint

CF-07 alleges that a 2008 financing package consisting of a mortgage loan and a functionally linked risk-coverage instrument was later separated, with the mortgage credit transferred independently while the linked product remained with the original banking perimeter. It asks Fiscalía Valencia to investigate the decisions and obtain internal documentation from **CaixaBank, S.A.**, as successor to Bankia, and from the CAM/successor perimeter.

**Status:** party criminal theory. It does not determine the validity of the financing, assignment, default, enforcement or current Valencia civil claim.

### 24–25 January 2026 — unit-instrumentalisation and reciprocal-validation theory

CF-02/CF-04 allege a structural chain in which minority-owner litigation, disputed Community debt/cost allocation, fragmentation of hotel units, later valuations and economic exploitation contributed to artificial insolvency and transfer of value. CF-03 expands the theory into an alleged reciprocal-validation mechanism between certain minority/Community actors and the AC.

CF-03's most useful analytical device is the claimed three-way contradiction requiring source testing:

1. Community-side assertions of substantial/full maintenance debt;
2. extra-concursal operation/maintenance reality and valuations said to show continuing upkeep; and
3. the post-control narrative that the hotel was ruined/dangerous and required comprehensive reform.

**Controlled use:** this is a contradiction matrix for investigation. It is not proof that one side fabricated documents or that reciprocal criminal coordination existed.

### 2 February 2026 — EG 19/2026 banking expansion

CF-06 states that the 19-Jan Valencia complaint was inhibited to the Fiscalía territorial route in Arrecife as **EG 19/2026**, and develops the allegation that separation of the mortgage/coverage package created a distorted appearance of the credit and neutralised defences. It requests CaixaBank internal material about the separation and assignment.

**Controlled use:** treat the banking mechanism as the complainant's pleaded theory and EG 19/2026 as a procedural reference requiring the signed inhibition/current-status record for full verification.

### 8 February 2026 — expansion of the judicial-prevarication complaint

CF-05 adds three claimed continuity nodes: a later AC-fee resolution, the live Valencia civil proceeding, and a reported **27-Jan-2026 Fiscalía Provincial de Santa Cruz de Tenerife decree in Diligencias 20/2026** said to have opened investigation and inhibited part of the matter to Las Palmas.

## 4. Calificación significance — the strongest defensible synthesis

The new bundle does **not** prove that the calificación proceeding was criminally instrumentalised. It materially improves the documentary counter-record against a fragmented or purely debtor-conduct narrative in five ways:

1. **Pre-concurso dispute existed:** minority-owner/operator litigation was active by February 2012, before LPB's June-2012 insolvency.
2. **Unitary-asset language was contemporaneous and adverse-side derived:** the 2019 pleading reproduces Plan/AC language describing CAM's proposal as acquisition of the unitary production unit and the linked/common properties as lacking independent commercial exploitation.
3. **The 2018–2019 isolated-sale/convalidation issue was contemporaneously challenged:** DI 248 and the 13-May pleading show that the sale, OB REM linkage, suspension and convalidation questions were not reconstructed years later.
4. **A financed-exit narrative was contemporaneously placed in the Fiscalía record:** CF-08 records the Article-176/conclusion route and claimed financing in January 2019, before the adverse Fiscal classification opinion.
5. **The 2026 criminal complaints show formal escalation and issue preservation:** judicial-supervision, Community/unit, banking and cross-proceeding theories were formally put to prosecutors. That demonstrates notice/request chronology; it does not demonstrate prosecutorial acceptance or criminality.

### Public calificación frame

The public page may therefore state, with source labels:

> The calificación record should not be read in isolation from a pre-existing owner/operator conflict, the liquidation plan's own unitary-production language, the contemporaneous 2018–2019 dispute over separate disposal of linked hotel assets, the documented DI 248 role reversal, and the later formal requests for external prosecutorial review. These sources create a serious causation and institutional-knowledge counter-record. They do not by themselves prove criminal instrumentalisation, prevaricación or coordinated wrongdoing.
