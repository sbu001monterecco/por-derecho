# CALIFICACIÓN — ALLEGATION 05: PINK / OVERSEAS RECEIPTS / `CONNIVENCIA` ALZAMIENTO THEORY

**Date:** 17 August 2026  
**Proceeding:** Concurso ordinario 36/2012 — Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. (`LPB`)  
**Source allegation:** AC calificación report of 11 February 2019  
**Current serial status:** `CURRENT — DEEP AUDIT STARTING POINT`

## 1. Scope

This ledger opens the fifth substantive allegation in the serial audit.

The allegation is the AC's inference that an alzamiento / concealment arrangement existed because:

- Pink Canary Services allegedly lacked attachable Spanish assets;
- hotel-related receipts may have been paid into England/UK-connected accounts or entities;
- the AC inferred `connivencia` between Pink, LPB/Gil and/or connected persons from those circumstances.

The exact allegation, exact accounts, exact receipts, exact legal owner of each receipt and exact disposition/concealment act must now be reconstructed from the primary record.

## 2. Starting judicial status

Sentencia 163/2023 rejected this specific `connivencia` / alzamiento branch for insufficient evidential corroboration.

Controlled proposition:

`AC CONCEALMENT / CONNIVENCIA THEORY → REJECTED AT FIRST INSTANCE FOR INSUFFICIENT CORROBORATION`.

This is materially different from the surviving rent/non-collection and statutory-accounting findings.

The rejection does **not** by itself establish that the AC knowingly fabricated the allegation, acted in bad faith, colluded with another person, committed prevaricación or pursued a criminal purpose. Those are separate knowledge/motive propositions requiring actor-specific evidence.

## 3. Mandatory entity separation

Do not conflate the Spanish and UK legal persons:

- **Monterecco Sun Park, S.L. → Pink Canary Services, S.L.** — Spanish company;
- **Monterecco Sun Park Limited → Aweswell Limited** — UK company.

A UK banking connection, UK receipt, English payment route or relationship with Aweswell is not itself proof that an LPB asset was concealed or placed beyond creditors.

For every receipt identify:

`payer → contractual basis → invoice/booking/operation → legal payee → bank-account owner → beneficial entitlement → accounting treatment → LPB right, if any → AC knowledge → later use in calificación`.

## 4. Mandatory separation from Allegation 03 and Allegation 06

### Not Allegation 03

A05 is not the same question as whether Pink owed rent to LPB or whether Gil should have pursued/terminated the operating agreement sooner. Those matters are audited under A03.

The A05 question is narrower and more serious: whether an identified LPB asset/right was concealed, transferred or kept outside the estate through a Pink/UK-connected arrangement and alleged `connivencia`.

### Not Allegation 06

A05 must not be merged with the separate €19,140.25 deposit/account branch. That branch concerns a different identified asset and was separately rejected after the swap/pledge explanation and disposal-power issue were accepted.

## 7. Anti-overstatement controls

Do not publish as established fact that:

- hotel receipts were `LPB money` merely because they arose from activity at Sun Park;
- a UK account was hidden or illicit merely because it was outside Spain;
- Pink's lack of Spanish assets proves concealment;
- common ownership/control of companies proves `connivencia`;
- the judicial rejection proves deliberate fabrication;
- the rejection of A05 disproves the separate rent/non-collection branch;
- the existence of Pink/Aweswell/Monterecco relationships eliminates corporate separateness.

Conversely, do not use corporate separateness as a slogan to avoid tracing money. If a primary payment trail shows an LPB asset was deliberately routed elsewhere, that evidence must be incorporated even if adverse.

## 9. What would change the current view?

The present accountability concern would materially weaken if primary records show that the AC had a coherent, contemporaneously corroborated tracing case identifying:

- an LPB-owned asset/right;
- a specific transfer or concealment act;
- a recipient/account controlled by a connected person;
- knowledge of the LPB entitlement;
- resulting creditor prejudice;
- and evidence materially stronger than the inference later rejected by the court.

It would materially strengthen if primary records show that:

- the AC knew the relevant receipts legally belonged to Pink or another non-LPB entity;
- no LPB disposal power or ownership existed;
- explanations/accounting/bank material contradicting concealment were already before him;
- the allegation nevertheless presented geography/collectability/connection as a substitute for the missing asset-and-transfer proof;
- or the rejected theory was later reused institutionally without disclosing its rejection or evidential weakness.
