# Aweswell Limited — accounting recognition and third-party misuse control

**Control date:** 27 August 2026  
**Status:** repository-wide interpretive and publication rule  
**Entity:** Aweswell Limited, company no. 07716847  
**Supplied accounts:** year ended 31 July 2025; internal title “Consolidated Provisional Group Statutory Accounts”

## 1. Purpose

This control governs every repository, website, correspondence and litigation reference to the £1 carrying presentation in Aweswell Limited’s accounts.

It has two purposes:

1. prevent an accounting placeholder from being misrepresented as a valuation, admission, waiver or recovery ceiling; and
2. prevent the project itself from overstating the accounting consequence by claiming that all property, future income and damages can automatically be recognised immediately.

## 2. Primary-source position

The supplied accounts state that:

- the accounts are provisional and rights-preserving because material Spanish records, control questions and recovery matters remain unresolved;
- an “underlying group asset interest” is carried at £1;
- the £1 is described in the filing as symbolic, prudential, provisional and non-valuation-based;
- the filing says the amount is not fair value, recoverable value, commercial value, damages value or a cap on recovery;
- the directors intend later updating when reliable evidence, forensic valuation/accounting, subsidiary information, recoveries or determinations permit it;
- the reported recovery perimeter includes asserted property, business, hotel-income, creditor, restitution, damages and related rights.

These are statements made by Aweswell’s directors in the filing. Companies House’s acceptance of the filing is not verification of those statements and not an endorsement of any asserted value or claim.

## 3. Locked interpretation of £1

The following paragraph is the canonical repository position:

> The £1 reported by Aweswell Limited at 31 July 2025 is the directors’ provisional, prudential and non-valuation carrying presentation under constrained reporting conditions. It is not evidence that Sun Park / MYND Yaiza, the hotel business, the group, any property interest, operating income, creditor position, recovery claim or damages claim had a market, fair, recoverable, commercial or patrimonial value of £1 or zero. It is not a waiver, abandonment, admission, estoppel, recovery cap, insolvency finding, culpability finding or judicial determination. Any replacement or expansion of the carrying amount must nevertheless comply with the applicable asset-specific recognition, control, classification and reliable-measurement requirements and with the higher threshold applicable to contingent recoveries.

This paragraph must be used whenever the £1 figure is explained outside a literal transcription of the statement of financial position.

## 4. What the £1 does not prove

The accounts do **not**, merely by showing £1, prove that:

- the hotel or group was worth £1 or €1;
- LPB, Matkator or Aweswell had no economic value;
- title or beneficial ownership was lost or abandoned;
- hotel income had no value;
- damages or restitution were waived;
- recoveries are capped at £1;
- the directors admitted insolvency, culpability, absence of loss or absence of standing;
- the UK filing replaces LPB’s own Spanish statutory accounts;
- Companies House, an auditor, the FRC or a court valued the hotel at £1.

## 5. What the £1 does prove

Subject to source authentication, the figure proves only that the filed statement of financial position used £1 as the carrying presentation for the line described as the underlying group asset interest and that the accompanying directors’ material qualified that presentation as provisional and non-valuation-based.

## 6. UK recognition framework

For the year ended 31 July 2025, the applicable edition must be established before analysis. The repository presently uses the January 2022 edition of FRS 102 for the core principles unless reliable evidence proves early adoption of later requirements.

### 6.1 Prudence

FRS 102 paragraph 2.9 permits caution under uncertainty so that assets and income are not overstated and liabilities and expenses are not understated. It also expressly forbids deliberate understatement and bias.

Therefore:

- uncertainty can justify non-recognition or cautious estimation where recognition criteria are not met;
- prudence cannot by itself justify keeping a recognisable and reliably measurable material asset at an arbitrary nominal amount;
- the £1 must remain a provisional source presentation pending item-by-item technical review, not be elevated into an independent measurement basis created by the repository.

### 6.2 Recognition and reliable measurement

Under paragraphs 2.27–2.32, an item is recognised when it meets the relevant definition, future economic benefits are probable and cost/value can be measured reliably. Where a reasonable estimate cannot be made, the item is not recognised; later circumstances may permit later recognition, and relevant disclosure may still be required.

### 6.3 Property and business assets

For each asserted hotel/property/business asset, establish:

- which reporting entity legally owns or controls it;
- whether it belongs in consolidated financial statements;
- the correct asset class;
- acquisition cost and subsequent transactions;
- impairment or derecognition evidence;
- whether the cost model, revaluation model or investment-property fair-value model applies;
- whether title, control and value can be supported by registry records, transactional documents and a professionally qualified independent valuation.

No single aggregate “hotel value” may be booked without resolving entity ownership, classification and measurement.

### 6.4 Hotel income

Income already earned by a group reporting entity and reliably measurable belongs in the relevant reporting period. Income received or retained by another actor but claimed by the group must be analysed as a receivable, restitution/accounting claim, damages claim or other asset according to the underlying right and certainty.

Forecast future hotel income is not booked wholesale as a present asset merely because the hotel is operating. It may support a valuation model where the relevant asset is recognised and the applicable measurement basis permits it.

### 6.5 Damages, restitution and compensation

FRS 102 paragraphs 21.13 and 21.16 require contingent assets not to be recognised while the inflow is less than virtually certain. Where inflow is probable but not virtually certain, appropriate disclosure is required when practicable. When the inflow becomes virtually certain, the item is no longer contingent and recognition becomes appropriate.

Compensation from third parties for impaired, lost or surrendered property, plant and equipment is included in profit or loss only when virtually certain under paragraph 17.25.

Accordingly, the asserted full recovery perimeter may be economically and legally material without all components qualifying for immediate balance-sheet recognition.

## 7. Incremental recognition ladder

The repository must present recognition as an evidence-based sequence:

1. **Identify the right or asset.** Exact entity, legal basis, date and counterparty.
2. **Establish control/title.** Registry, contract, judgment, accounting records and operational evidence.
3. **Classify correctly.** PPE, investment property, financial asset, receivable, income, compensation, damages or contingent asset.
4. **Measure reliably.** Cost records, independent valuation, account of income, expert report or binding award.
5. **Apply probability threshold.** Probable for ordinary recognition; virtually certain where the specific contingent-asset/compensation rule requires it.
6. **Recognise or disclose.** Use the applicable accounting period and entity.
7. **Reconcile and update.** Preserve a bridge from £1 to every later recognised amount.

## 8. Restatement and amendment rule

Do not use “restatement” as a generic synonym for every future increase.

- A material prior-period error is corrected retrospectively under the applicable Section 10 requirements.
- A change in estimate is accounted for as a change in estimate.
- A revaluation follows the applicable property measurement rules.
- A later recovery, award or event is normally recognised in the period in which its recognition conditions are met, unless it supplies evidence of conditions existing at the earlier reporting date.
- Companies House amended accounts for the same period must state that they replace the original accounts, are now the statutory accounts and are prepared as at the date of the original accounts; the original filing remains on the register.

The directors should obtain UK professional accounting advice before filing amended accounts or recognising material recovered values.

## 9. Technical citation clarification

The supplied filing is preserved unchanged. It contains references to FRS 102 paragraphs 17.21–17.23 in the discussion of prudence/symbolic measurement. In the January 2022 FRS 102 edition, those paragraphs concern useful life and depreciation methods, not the general prudence principle.

The repository must therefore append, not conceal, this clarification:

- prudence: paragraph 2.9;
- general recognition/reliable measurement: paragraphs 2.27–2.32;
- asset recognition: paragraphs 2.37–2.41 as applicable;
- consolidation: Section 9;
- investment property: Section 16;
- property, plant and equipment: Section 17, especially 17.4 and 17.15–17.15F;
- contingent assets: 21.13 and 21.16;
- errors and estimates: Section 10.

This is an independent repository technical note, not an audit opinion and not an alteration of the Companies House document.

## 10. LPB Spanish-accounts boundary

Aweswell’s filing may be used to evidence:

- the parent’s stated funding, creditor, investor and recovery position;

It may not be described as having automatically formulated, approved, deposited or replaced LPB’s Spanish annual accounts during liquidation. Counsel’s correspondence records that, with management powers suspended, LPB’s accounting obligations and any route to reconstructed/provisional accounts required Spanish procedural analysis and potentially independent forensic accounting.

This boundary applies in the concurso, calificación, civil, criminal, tax, enforcement, regulatory and professional-accountability contexts.

## 11. Third-party misuse test

A statement is materially incomplete or potentially misleading when it uses the £1 without the provisional/non-valuation qualification to assert or imply substantive worth, solvency, ownership, loss, waiver, standing, liability or damages.

Examples requiring correction include:

- “Aweswell valued the hotel at £1.”
- “The group’s assets are worth zero/£1.”
- “The accounts admit no loss or no damages.”
- “The accounts waive title, income or recovery.”
- “The accounts prove LPB had no value.”
- “The accounts cap any claim at £1.”
- “Companies House confirmed the hotel value.”

Literal transcription of the balance-sheet line is not misuse if the full context and qualification are supplied.

## 12. Response protocol

Where misuse occurs:

1. capture the exact statement and source;
2. cite the complete filing qualification and this control;
3. distinguish carrying amount, market/fair value, recoverable amount and claim recognition;
4. state the correct limited proposition;
5. request a written correction or clarification;
6. preserve the response and any refusal;
7. assess procedural and reputational consequences with legal/accounting advisers;
8. update the repository misuse log.

Do not allege bad faith merely because a person misunderstands the accounts. Attribute intent only where evidence supports it.

## 15. No-waiver rule

Nothing in the £1 carrying presentation, this control or the public explanatory pages waives or reduces any asserted property, income, creditor, restitution, compensation, damages, regulatory or public-authority claim. Equally, the no-waiver statement does not itself establish legal entitlement, quantum, recognition or recoverability; those remain evidence- and forum-dependent.
