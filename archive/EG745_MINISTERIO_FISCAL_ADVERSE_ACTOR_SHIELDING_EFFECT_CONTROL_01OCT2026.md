## Controlled institutional posture

Por Derecho / Gil Marer no longer treats the Ministerio Fiscal as a merely neutral recipient of the allegations for evidential and recovery analysis.

The controlled posture is narrower and actor-specific:

> **Ministerio Fiscal is treated as an adverse institutional actor whose documented acts, omissions, handling, reuse of propositions, routing and evidential decisions must be tested for a possible shielding effect around the Acosta Matos position.**

“Shielding effect” is a functional hypothesis about cumulative institutional effect. It is **not** proof that Ministerio Fiscal, any prosecution office or any prosecutor:
- is a member of the Acosta Matos perimeter;
- agreed or coordinated with private actors;
- shared a criminal purpose;
- accepted a bribe, influence payment or instruction;
- knowingly protected criminal conduct; or
- bears collective responsibility for another actor's acts.

Any stronger conclusion requires actor-specific proof of act or omission, capacity, available corpus, knowledge, duty, lawful alternative, causal effect and, where relevant, intent.

This sequence supports investigation of a **possible shielding effect**. It does not establish a common plan or criminal organisation.

## Anti-regression rules

1. Do not state as fact that Ministerio Fiscal is “part of the Acosta Matos perimeter”.
2. Do not collapse office-level posture into personal knowledge or responsibility.
3. Do not convert an adverse decision, archive, referral, silence or error into proof of corruption or criminality.
4. Preserve contrary and lawful explanations with the same source discipline.
5. Keep the 20 September preservation/identification filing separate from the 21 September reposición.
6. Keep DP 1901, DP 1956, DIP 2, DI 22, DI 248 and the insolvency appeals procedurally distinct unless a primary source proves a defined connection.
7. No parallel oversight, whistleblower, extraordinary-review or liability route is assumed to suspend the contentious-administrative clock.
