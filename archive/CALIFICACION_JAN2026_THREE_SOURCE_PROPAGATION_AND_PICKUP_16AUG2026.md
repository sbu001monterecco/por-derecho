## 3. The three documents should be presented as three different questions

### 3.1 13 January 2026 — judicial supervision / alleged circularity

The complaint is anchored to the 12-September-2025 and 11-November-2025 rulings concerning the requested separation/supervision of the insolvency administrator. The pleading alleges that the court recognised ex-officio supervisory powers while declining to examine the underlying alleged breaches because it said it did not first perceive sufficient indicia.

The strongest public question is therefore not `the complaint proves prevaricación`.

It is:

> **Where the court itself recognises an ex-officio supervisory power over the insolvency administration, what substantive verification was performed on the concrete allegations before concluding that there was no basis to activate that supervision?**

**Classification:** the rulings are primary judicial acts when separately sourced; the criminal characterisation in the 13-Jan complaint is a **PARTY ALLEGATION / REQUEST FOR INVESTIGATION**.

### 3.2 24 January 2026 — alleged pre-concurso / Community / unit causation and the 12-Jul-2016 warning

The complaint frames the insolvency not as an isolated debtor failure but as allegedly connected to prior minority-owner/unit-operation conflict, disputed Community liabilities, loss of unitary exploitation and later use of that damaged operating condition in the insolvency process.

Its highest-value source locator is the pleading's statement that on **12 July 2016** Gil Marer warned the insolvency administrator that the passive was allegedly inflated by at least **€2,874,076.19**, attributing the figure to minority-owner eviction litigation, disputed Community debts and costs linked to the alleged rupture of unitary exploitation.

**Control:** the complaint is not the native 12-Jul-2016 burofax. Until the burofax and annexes are matched, the amount and causal breakdown remain a **PARTY ALLEGATION / SOURCE LOCATOR**.

The page should therefore ask:

> **Was the alleged 2016 warning received, what supporting records accompanied it, what was verified, and how—if at all—was the challenged passive reconciled before later culpability/causation conclusions were drawn?**

### 3.3 25 January 2026 — the `three realities` contradiction matrix

The expansion supplies a useful investigative structure that must remain expressly attributed to the pleading:

1. **inside the concurso:** substantial Community maintenance debt was allegedly asserted/certified against LPB;
2. **outside the concurso:** operators/LPB allegedly continued effective hotel operation/maintenance, while 2017–2018 valuations are said to show an asset inconsistent with total abandonment; and
3. **after the 2018 control change:** CAM/Community-side material allegedly presented the hotel as ruined/dangerous and requiring comprehensive reform.

The pleading calls those realities incompatible and develops a `validación recíproca` theory between Community actors and the insolvency administrator.

### Mandatory evidential correction

Do **not** adopt the pleading's suggestion that convergent effects alone eliminate the need for direct actor-specific proof.

The controlled project method remains:

`ACTOR → POWER / DUTY → VERIFIED KNOWLEDGE → COMMUNICATION / ADOPTION / USE → ACT / OMISSION → BENEFIT / CONSEQUENCE → CAUSATION → LEGAL CHARACTERISATION`.

Convergence may justify an investigation or an evidence-based inference. It does not by itself establish a criminal agreement, fabricated accounts, false certifications, knowing misuse, or shared intent.
