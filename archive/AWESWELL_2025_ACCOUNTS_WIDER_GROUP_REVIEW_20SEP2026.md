## 3. Aweswell's roles cannot be reduced to its shares

The 69-page foreign-investor section at p.10 describes Aweswell as **parent, sole shareholder, asserted creditor, funder, foreign investor and recovery principal**. It links the creditor position to the 2011 acquisition of the Multimatrix position, including shareholder, funding, advance, debt, subrogation and recovery rights.

The directors state an asserted/maintained LPB creditor position **exceeding EUR1 million**, whose recognition was requested and/or maintained in the concurso. Other passages use 'registered creditor'. Preserve both source formulations; the exact recognised amount, classification, challenge history and present status require the definitive texts and decisions. Do not convert a directors' assertion into a verified recognised balance.

The 2011 acquisition is described more broadly in the 69-page version as acquisition of 100% of Multimatrix's rights. The acquisition/assignment instruments remain necessary to determine which shareholder, creditor, contractual, option and litigation rights actually passed. Aweswell was formerly UK Monterecco Sun Park Limited; it is not simply Multimatrix under a later name. Preserve the separate Israeli and UK Multimatrix identity question in the 2008 review.

Funding and support since 2012 are described as including legal coordination, asset protection, restructuring/exit routes, the productive unit, CEXP economics, income and damages. The notes say shareholders financed the defence for and on behalf of the group at parent level, with reimbursement contingent on successful recovery. This calls for separate equity, debt, advance, payment-on-behalf-of and cost-allocation ledgers; the narrative itself is not proof that every expense is a recoverable receivable.

## 4. Wider platform: a disclosed business architecture

Both versions describe the hotel as the foundation for a wider UK-facing commercial business, including:

- brand and commercialization;
- customer acquisition, sales, marketing and distribution;
- digital systems and platforms;
- travel and retirement-living propositions;
- community membership;
- strategic partnerships, restructuring/finance pathways and expansion opportunities.

The directors say displacement of the hotel and operating control impaired that wider architecture. It is therefore inaccurate to summarise the source as a hotel-rent dispute alone. Equally, the accounts do not individually value or authenticate every brand, system, customer relationship or opportunity. Recover the contracts, development expenditure, intellectual-property evidence, customer data provenance, bookings, partner commitments and contemporaneous plans before quantifying a particular component.

The accounts separately mention future use of the group's commercial standing, claims and prospective liquidity by counterparties considering it as sponsor, guarantor, co-investor or strategic principal in unrelated projects (69-page p.31). This is prospective capacity, not proof those acquisitions occurred or authority to publish private transaction-development discussions.

## 5. Historical and continuing-harm position already stated in the accounts

The directors describe a single productive-unit transfer architecture in 2008: property, exploitation, operating continuity and associated commercial value. They allege non-completion or frustration by an approximately 23% minority, followed by boycott, parallel/non-compliant exploitation, weaponised liabilities, operating decapitalisation and insolvency distortion. The Community machinery is alleged to have supplied apparent authority, exclusion, debt production and documentary cover.

The fuller version at pp.5–7 expressly addresses later actors who allegedly adopted or operationalised the earlier wrongdoing, while creating independent liability through their own conduct. The June 2018 allegation concerns not just possession but operation, commercialization, revenue, contracts, restructuring, refinancing and the wider enterprise.

These are source-derived directors' positions. Their inclusion in financial statements does not independently corroborate the underlying events or establish that every successor assumed every predecessor's personal debt. The 2008 and later-adoption review identifies the instruments and legal distinctions needed.

## 6. Inside and outside the concurso

The fuller version at pp.17–18 distinguishes:

**Inside:** estate and creditor injury, balances, accounting, recoverable value, surplus, liquidation/adjudication treatment, protection and creditor-protective exit routes.

**Outside:** Aweswell's parent/creditor/funder/foreign-investor position, Matkator interests, CEXP economics, income, control, goodwill, commercial/financing/restructuring opportunities, wider business architecture, cadastral/registry coherence and potential public-authority claims.

This supports a whole-enterprise reconstruction with claimant-specific allocation. It does not pool ownership, extend LPB's insolvency to all entities, turn every operating receipt into parent income, or permit double recovery of the same loss at subsidiary and shareholder levels.

## 7. Four financial measures: do not substitute one for another

| Measure | Source meaning | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| GBP1 balance-sheet carrying amount / net assets | Directors' symbolic, prudential, non-valuation-based presentation under claimed constrained reporting | Not a valuation of all rights or a legal cap on recovery |
| Approximately EUR26–28m | Historical professional valuations cited in the notes | No new valuation or verification of their exact asset perimeter, date or assumptions in this review |
| More than EUR1m | Asserted/maintained creditor position against LPB | Recognition, classification and current status need the primary concurso record |
| Potential nine-figure profile | Fuller version p.2: directors' view of aggregate continuing harm and recovery potential | Expressly not recognised as an accounting asset; currency, full methodology, overlap and enforceable amount not established by that sentence |

The 40-page pp.9–10 and 69-page p.21 financial statements show the GBP1 presentation. The latter says the profit-and-loss account was not delivered under the small-company regime and claims audit exemption. At p.22 it says the company did not trade in the ordinary commercial sense during the reporting year, while remaining active in recovery and preservation. Do not erase either its historical business or that period-specific statement.

The notes cite FRS102 sections 17.21–17.23 in explaining the symbolic carrying approach. **This review records that citation without endorsing that it legally or technically supports the treatment.** Neither Companies House acceptance nor the text's 'provisional' or 'non-waiver' label is an independent accounting-compliance opinion. No independent auditor's opinion was identified in the two reviewed copies.

The source contains broad non-waiver and alleged misuse/retaliation language. Preserve it as the directors' stated position; it does not prevent lawful scrutiny, determine evidential admissibility, immunise mistakes or automatically make a contrary interpretation unlawful retaliation.

## 8. Administrative/public-interest anchors in the fuller version

The 69-page version expressly identifies Articles 32–37 of Ley 40/2015 at p.10. It also discusses Catastro/Matkator, BORME/HNT, RICPE materials, CNMV supervision and the 15-December Government notification followed by internal transfers and integrity-commission consideration (pp.13–18).

Those passages report the directors' reading of external documents. The accounts are not a substitute for the signed Catastro reply, BORME notice, supervisory response or transfer. The absence of a visible type-902 file is not proof that no other cadastral procedure ever existed; administrative consideration is not a finding of crime. The 2026 documents are subsequent events relative to the 31-July-2025 reporting date, not ordinary trading transactions in that year.
