## 6. Creditor or lender in possession — functional liability framework

“Lender in possession” is used as a comparative functional description, not as a standalone Spanish statutory status.

The allegation is that a secured creditor crossed from protecting or enforcing collateral into actual possession, direction, management, use or appropriation of assets before formal adjudication.

The required accounting starts at the first proven material-control date and must address:

- keys and access;
- guest admission and exclusion;
- reservations and deposits;
- rents, revenues and fruits;
- staff and contractor instructions;
- security, maintenance, utilities and insurance;
- works and deterioration;
- operator and tour-operator relationships;
- access provided or denied to valuers, investors, ONA or bidders;
- information asymmetry in later sale/adjudication;
- value generated using LPB and third-party assets;
- expenses charged to the estate, Community or other owners;
- debt set-off or other economic benefit;
- later project, finance and public-support benefit.

Any liability and remedy must be allocated to the correct claimant—LPB/the estate, Aweswell, CEXP, Matkator, Pink, individual owners or another person—and must avoid double recovery.

## 7. Shadow or de facto administration — four separate lanes

### 7.1 De facto administration of LPB as a company

Spanish company law extends administrator responsibility to persons who in reality perform administrator functions without title or under whose instructions the formal administrators act.

The hypothesis requires proof that identified CAM-perimeter persons exercised sufficiently continuous and autonomous LPB management functions or that formal officeholders habitually followed their instructions.

Potential indicators include:

- deciding who could enter LPB property;
- deciding whether the hotel would remain open or close;
- selecting or excluding operators;
- directing security, maintenance and works;
- controlling records, reservations or revenue;
- directing insolvency proposals, disposals or settlement positions;
- using LPB assets in project promotion;
- determining access for bidders, experts or advisers.

Creditor pressure, negotiation or benefit alone is insufficient.

### 7.2 De facto possession or management of insolvency-estate assets

A private actor need not replace LPB’s entire corporate board to incur responsibility for practical control of estate assets. The actor-by-asset test is:

> `OWNER → ESTATE STATUS → FORMAL CUSTODIAN → ACTUAL KEY/ACCESS HOLDER → USE → INCOME → WORKS → TRANSFER → BENEFIT`.

### 7.3 De facto hotel operation or control

The hotel-operation analysis must identify who actually controlled:

- reception and guests;
- booking systems;
- merchant/bank accounts;
- employees and contractors;
- cleaning and maintenance;
- utilities and insurance;
- tourism registrations and licences;
- commercial contracts and inventory;
- income and expenditure.

### 7.4 Interference with assets outside LPB

CAM cannot be “shadow administrator of LPB” in respect of a Matkator or other third-party finca merely because it is located inside Sun Park. The legally correct allegations are unauthorised possession, interference, use, works, exclusion, exploitation or appropriation of third-party property or rights.

CEXP collective/operating rights must also be analysed separately from LPB title.

### 8.2 Gil’s strongest allegation

> **Gil alleges that the insolvency administrator criminally approved or enabled the 7 June operation: first by supplying or facilitating the authority route through the 18 May security decision; then by failing to delimit and supervise it; and, after the alleged excess became known, by failing to recover control, protect estate and non-estate assets, demand an account, reverse the effects or present the material-control change accurately to the court.**

### 8.3 Four-level evidential ladder

1. **Lawful narrow mandate:** security or maintenance access was validly authorised and the physical actors exceeded it without the administrator’s knowledge. No criminal approval follows.
2. **Negligent design or supervision:** the administrator created a foreseeable risk, failed to define limits or failed to supervise. This may support civil, professional or insolvency responsibility depending on duty and causation.
3. **Knowing tolerance or ratification:** the administrator learned of the excess, had legal and practical capacity to intervene, and consciously allowed or retained the result. This may support knowing facilitation or criminal omission if the applicable offence and subjective elements are proved.
4. **Affirmative instruction or common purpose:** the administrator intended the wider transfer of control or coordinated it with private actors. This is the strongest allegation and requires direct or compelling circumstantial proof.

## 9. Judicial omission / “omissionary prevarication” allegation

### 9.1 Attribution

Gil alleges that the competent judge’s omission to protect, restore, investigate, account for or causally attribute the change in actual control amounted to **omissionary judicial prevarication**.

That label must be published as Gil’s allegation, not as an established legal conclusion.

### 9.2 Legal classification must be precise

Spanish criminal law separately addresses:

- knowingly issuing an unjust judicial resolution;
- grossly negligent or inexcusably ignorant issuance of a manifestly unjust resolution;
- refusal to judge without legal cause;
- malicious delay intended to achieve an illegitimate purpose;
- and, in appropriate offences, commission by omission where a specific legal/contractual duty or prior risk-creating conduct creates an equivalent duty to act.

There is no safe public shortcut from “the judge did not grant the remedy sought” to criminal prevarication.

### 9.3 Mandatory judicial proof matrix

For each alleged judicial omission identify:

| Element | Required proof |
|---|---|
| Judge / procedural capacity | Exact judge, court and date |
| Matter properly before court | Pleading, request, evidence and filing receipt |
| Competence and duty | Why this judge had power and a legally relevant duty to decide or protect |
| Resolution, refusal, delay or silence | Exact procedural act or omission |
| Evidence before the judge | What the judge demonstrably knew at that moment—not what was discovered years later |
| Objective injustice or arbitrariness | Why the outcome was not merely legally debatable or erroneous |
| Knowledge / subjective element | Evidence the judge knew the result was unjust or intentionally delayed/refused for an illegitimate purpose |
| Causation | What loss, control change, procedural advantage or benefit followed |
| Contrary explanation | Jurisdiction, evidential insufficiency, procedural limits, appeal availability, workload, legal interpretation or later order |

### 9.4 Strongest presently publishable formulation

> **Gil alleges that the court was repeatedly placed on notice of a creditor/private-actor change in actual control, yet the concursal record and later causation analysis did not provide an equivalent actor-by-actor account of who then controlled access, preservation, operation, works, income and rescue. He alleges that identifiable judicial resolutions, refusals, delay and omissions knowingly preserved or legitimised that private-control result. Whether those acts satisfy judicial prevarication, refusal to judge, malicious delay or any commission-by-omission theory requires the exact evidence-before-judge and subjective proof matrix.**

A judicial error, incomplete reasoning or adverse ruling is not by itself criminal.

## 10. Criminal-law lead map

The criminal investigation should test, without prejudging:

- disposition or representation of property without sufficient authority;
- disloyal administration of another’s property or interests;
- coercion, damage, occupation/usurpation and arbitrary self-help;
- procedural deception and documentary use;
- frustration of estate recovery or creditor equality;
- insolvency offences;
- knowing facilitation, necessary cooperation, inducement and common purpose;
- corporate attribution through natural persons;
- omission where a legally equivalent duty to prevent or reverse is proved;
- judicial prevarication/refusal/malicious delay under the exact statutory elements;
- concealment or later ratification;
- subsidy/public-funds offences only if later applications contain the required false or concealed conditions.

Every offence must be mapped:

> `ACTOR → CAPACITY → DUTY/POWER → KNOWLEDGE → ACT/OMISSION → SOURCE → RELIANCE → EFFECT → BENEFIT → SUBJECTIVE ELEMENT → CONTRARY EVIDENCE`.
