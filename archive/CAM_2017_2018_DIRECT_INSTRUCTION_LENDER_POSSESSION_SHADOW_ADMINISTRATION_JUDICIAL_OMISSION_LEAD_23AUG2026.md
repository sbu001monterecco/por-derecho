### 8.3 Four-level evidential ladder

1. **Lawful narrow mandate:** security or maintenance access was validly authorised and the physical actors exceeded it without the administrator’s knowledge. No criminal approval follows.
2. **Negligent design or supervision:** the administrator created a foreseeable risk, failed to define limits or failed to supervise. This may support civil, professional or insolvency responsibility depending on duty and causation.
3. **Knowing tolerance or ratification:** the administrator learned of the excess, had legal and practical capacity to intervene, and consciously allowed or retained the result. This may support knowing facilitation or criminal omission if the applicable offence and subjective elements are proved.
4. **Affirmative instruction or common purpose:** the administrator intended the wider transfer of control or coordinated it with private actors. This is the strongest allegation and requires direct or compelling circumstantial proof.

Criminal omission is not inferred from silence, deficient supervision or professional breach alone. It requires the particular protective duty, knowledge, practical capacity, causal equivalence and the mental element of the underlying offence. Criminal negligence is available only where the Criminal Code expressly provides it.

## 9. Judicial omission / “omissionary prevarication” allegation

### 9.1 Attribution

### 9.2 Legal classification must be precise

Spanish criminal law separately addresses:

- knowingly issuing an unjust judicial resolution;
- grossly negligent or inexcusably ignorant issuance of a manifestly unjust resolution;
- refusal to judge without legal cause;
- malicious delay intended to achieve an illegitimate purpose; and
- in appropriate offences, commission by omission where a specific legal/contractual duty or prior risk-creating conduct creates an equivalent duty to act.

There is no safe public shortcut from “the judge did not grant the remedy sought” to criminal prevarication.

Spanish case law has exceptionally examined omission within administrative prevarication where a public official was legally compelled to decide and silence was equivalent to an arbitrary express decision. That doctrine must not be transferred automatically to judicial conduct. Judicial conduct must be classified through the exact resolution, refusal or delay under Criminal Code Articles 446–449.

The presently identified allegation concerns Judge Alberto López Villarrubia. The controlled notice/decision nodes include the 27 April 2017 filing, a reported 13 June 2018 discussion, the 4/17 February 2021 filed allegation/registration, the signed 24 February 2021 order and Sentencia 163/2023. They also include contrary or corrective acts, including the 26 June 2018 suspension, the 24 October 2019 non-validation result and partly estimatory aspects of the 24 February 2021 order. Each act must be tested separately; informal or draft material is not attributed to the judge without receipt proof.
