# PRESCRIPTION / CADUCITY / CRIMINAL-CIVIL RECOVERY CONTROL

**Control date:** 26 August 2026  
**Status:** CANONICAL PUBLIC-SAFE LEGAL / FORENSIC CONTROL  
**Purpose:** preserve viable recovery positions without making blanket claims that criminal proceedings stop every civil clock, that historical rights are necessarily still alive, or that every downstream loss is automatically recoverable.

## 1. Controlling proposition — there is no single clock

Por Derecho must never use a generic statement such as “the criminal case stops prescription” or “the limitation period is suspended”. The correct analysis is claim-by-claim and clock-by-clock.

For every possible recovery route, identify:

`CLAIMANT → DEFENDANT → ACT/OMISSION → LEGAL BASIS → PRESCRIPTION OR CADUCITY → DIES A QUO → INTERRUPTION/SUSPENSION EVENT → CRIMINAL LINK → CURRENT PROCEDURAL STATE → INTERIM RELIEF → FINAL RELIEF → DAMAGES / RESTITUTION → COUNSEL STATUS`

The principal clock classes are:

1. criminal prescription under Penal Code arts. 131–132;
2. general civil prescription under Civil Code arts. 1964, 1968, 1969 and 1973;
3. strict civil caducity / special short periods, including arts. 1535 and 1524 CC and LPH art. 18.3 where applicable;
4. insolvency-administrator liability under historical Insolvency Act art. 36 / current TRLC arts. 94–98;
5. judicial-error / abnormal-administration-of-justice damages under LOPJ arts. 292–293;
6. any other statutory, contractual, administrative, company, property, tax or public-law period specific to the remedy.

**Publication rule:** no page may state a right is `LIVE`, `EXPIRED`, `INTERRUPTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVIVED` without identifying the exact legal clock and the supporting act/date.

## 2. Criminal prescription — Penal Code arts. 131–132

Current art. 131 links the criminal prescription period to the maximum penalty: generally 20, 15, 10 or 5 years, with one year for light offences and insult/slander, and special non-prescription rules for the offences expressly listed by the Code.

Current art. 132 provides that the period normally runs from commission of the offence. For a legally established continuing offence, permanent offence or offence requiring habituality, the statutory start may instead be the last infringement, elimination of the unlawful situation or cessation of the conduct.

### Mandatory boundary

A long-running economic consequence, continuing hotel operation, continuing possession, continuing enrichment or repeated CAM-favourable effect does **not** by itself establish a continuing/permanent criminal offence. The criminal type and its temporal structure must be proved separately.

### Interruption / six-month suspension mechanism

Under current art. 132.2:

- a reasoned judicial resolution attributing suspected participation to a sufficiently identified person directs the proceeding against that person and interrupts prescription;
- a complaint or private prosecution filed **before a judicial body** that attributes suspected criminal participation to an identified person suspends computation for a maximum of six months;
- if the qualifying judicial resolution is made within that window, interruption operates retroactively from filing;
- if the complaint is finally rejected / the procedure is not directed against that person, or no qualifying judicial resolution is made within six months, the clock continues under the statutory rule.

**Important operational consequence:** reports to an ordinary Fiscalía, police or administrative authority are important evidence/preservation acts but must not be described as automatically interrupting criminal prescription under art. 132. A `JUDICIALISATION LEDGER` must identify the court, judicial date, person, resolution and effect for each criminal track.

### Connected offences / concurso

Art. 131.4 provides that in concurrence of offences or connected offences the prescription period is that corresponding to the most serious offence. This can be important in a unitary reconstruction, but only if the legal relationship between offences is actually established; it cannot be assumed because the same actors or assets recur.

## 3. Civil prescription — actio nata, interruption and criminal proceedings

### General Civil Code controls

- Art. 1964.2: personal actions without a special period generally prescribe in five years from when performance can be demanded.
- Art. 1968.2: the art. 1902 tort/negligence action generally has a one-year period from when the injured party knew of the harm.
- Art. 1969: unless another rule applies, time runs from when the action could be exercised.
- Art. 1973: prescription is interrupted by exercise before the courts, an extrajudicial creditor demand, or acknowledgment of the debt by the debtor.

Every extrajudicial interruption must therefore be mapped by claimant, defendant, legal basis, scope, date, proof of delivery/receipt and the specific obligation asserted. A generic protest to the world is not automatically an interruption against every possible defendant or cause of action.

### Same-facts criminal proceedings can materially affect civil prescription

The Supreme Court's Civil Chamber has repeatedly held, in the same-facts context, that criminal proceedings can delay the start of civil prescription because arts. 111 and 114 LECrim can create a legal obstacle to separate civil proceedings. STS 1599/2023, summarising prior case law including STS 112/2022, also records that a criminal complaint can constitute an exercise of the civil action for art. 1973 purposes, and that where the party is personated the civil clock generally begins when the criminal judgment / dismissal or archive becomes final and is correctly notified.

### Mandatory boundary

This is **not a universal rule for every civil claim**. Apply it only after testing:

- whether the criminal and civil claims concern the same material facts;
- whether the civil action was legally prevented or materially dependent upon the criminal determination;
- whether civil liability was exercised, reserved or renounced in the criminal process;
- the finality and notification date of the criminal closure;
- the particular statutory period governing the civil remedy.

## 4. Prescription is not caducity

This distinction must appear across all retracto/tanteo, Community and recovery pages.

### Short caducity examples in this matter

- Art. 1535 CC: nine days from the legally relevant assignee demand for payment, subject to the strict statutory conditions and the unresolved Day-Zero analysis.
- Art. 1524 CC: nine days for legal retracto from Registry inscription or, failing inscription, knowledge of the sale.
- LPH art. 18.3: Community-resolution challenges generally expire after three months, or one year where contrary to law/statutes; for absent owners the period runs from communication under art. 9.

Criminal proceedings must **not** be described as automatically suspending these caducity periods. Supreme Court authority in other caducity contexts confirms that prior criminal proceedings do not automatically interrupt a statutory caducity period merely because facts overlap.

### LO 1/2025 / MASC

The website already controls the new art. 7 LO 1/2025 mechanism: a properly configured request to initiate an appropriate dispute-resolution mechanism can interrupt prescription or suspend caducity under the conditions of that law.

For Sun Park short-period rights this remains `COUNSEL VALIDATION REQUIRED`:

- it can only assist a right that is legally still alive;
- it does not automatically revive a right already expired before the mechanism became relevant;
- its precise application to art. 1535, art. 1524 or another special caducity period must be validated transaction by transaction.

## 5. Insolvency-administrator damages — special four-year clock

Historical Ley 22/2003 art. 36 and current TRLC arts. 94–98 provide a specific administrator-liability framework.

The historical statute provided liability to debtor/creditors for estate damage caused by unlawful acts/omissions or lack of due diligence, together with a four-year prescription period counted from the claimant's knowledge of the damage and, in any event, from cessation in office. The current TRLC preserves the same core four-year rule in art. 97 and separately preserves direct individual actions in art. 98.

### Required analysis for the Sun Park / LPB track

For every alleged AC breach, build:

`AC ACT/OMISSION → DUTY → ESTATE OR DIRECT HARM → DATE HARM BECAME ACTIONABLE/KNOWN → AC CESSATION STATUS → INTERRUPTION EVENT → CRIMINAL SAME-FACTS LINK → DAMAGES → CURRENT COUNSEL VIEW`

Do not calculate one global “AC limitation date”. Different acts, damages, claimants and the distinction between estate and direct individual harm can generate different analyses.

The loyalty / conflict / constructive-functional-agency hypothesis does not itself extend the statutory period. Its relevance is to duty, knowledge, causation, validity and potential criminal characterisation, each of which must be connected to the correct clock.

## 6. Judicial / State damages — special route, special periods

LOPJ arts. 292–293 distinguish damages from judicial error and abnormal functioning of the Administration of Justice.

Key controls:

- the alleged damage must be effective, economically evaluable and individualised;
- reversal or annulment of a judicial decision does not by itself establish compensation;
- a judicial-error claim ordinarily requires the prior error declaration and art. 293.1(a) imposes a strict three-month period to seek that declaration from when it could be exercised;
- art. 293.2 provides a one-year prescription period for the compensation claim from when it could be exercised;
- abnormal-functioning claims follow their own State-liability route and must not be merged with a personal criminal accusation against a judge.

**No public exact deadline calculation without current counsel review.** Pending appeals, notification/finality and the precise nature of the alleged damage can change the analysis.

## 7. Criminal route as a recovery route — not only punishment

Penal Code arts. 109–112 expressly recognise civil consequences of crime:

1. restitution;
2. repair of damage;
3. compensation for material and moral loss.

Art. 111 requires restitution of the same asset where possible, including in certain third-party possession situations, subject to statutory protection for an irreivindicable acquisition. Art. 121 can create subsidiary public-entity civil liability under its conditions where a criminally responsible authority/agent/employee caused direct service-related harm. Art. 122 requires a person who participated for profit in the effects of an offence to restore/rescind up to the value of that participation.

### Sun Park consequence

The criminal workstream must therefore maintain a recovery ledger, not merely an offence ledger:

`PROPERTY / RIGHT / INCOME / VALUE → ALLEGED PREDICATE ACT → CURRENT HOLDER/BENEFICIARY → TRACEABLE VALUE → RESTITUTION POSSIBILITY → THIRD-PARTY PROTECTION → MATERIAL DAMAGES → LOST PROFITS → MORAL DAMAGES WHERE LEGALLY APPLICABLE → INTEREST/COSTS → NO-DOUBLE-COUNTING ADJUSTMENT`

No difference between purchase cost and later value is automatically “criminal proceeds” or unjust enrichment. The causal/legal basis of each increment must be proved.

## 8. Interim and preservation relief — civil and criminal

### Criminal

Where the legal predicates exist:

- LECrim art. 589 permits bail/security and embargo to secure possible pecuniary liabilities when criminal indicia exist against a person;
- art. 764 permits precautionary measures to secure pecuniary liability in abbreviated proceedings, using relevant civil-measures principles;
- Penal Code art. 127 octies permits seizure/embargo/deposit from the first investigative steps to secure confiscation.

Possible requests must remain proportional, asset-specific and tied to the proceeding. They are not automatic consequences of making an allegation.

### Civil

LEC arts. 721, 727 and 728 provide the general precautionary framework. The applicant must establish the statutory requirements including risk from procedural delay, a provisional basis for the claim and normally security. Available measures can include those specifically listed in art. 727 and other necessary measures capable of preserving effective final relief.

LPH art. 18.4 also expressly allows a court, on request, to suspend execution of a challenged Community resolution as an interim measure.

### Preservation objective

The correct interim-relief question is:

> **What asset, title, income stream, evidence, Registry position or economic value could become harder to restore if no measure is taken before final judgment?**

For each candidate measure record proportionality, third-party rights, security/caución, urgency, forum and evidential foundation.

## 9. Interim damages / accounting versus final damages

Do not use “interim damages” as if Spanish procedure automatically grants advance merits compensation.

The practical interim objective is to preserve the **ability to satisfy final relief** through measures such as:

- security / embargo where legally available;
- non-disposal / Registry measures where legally available;
- preservation of documents and digital evidence;
- deposit/accounting of disputed income where legally supportable;
- protection against dissipation or irreversible transformation;
- provisional suspension of a challenged act where the statute permits it.

Final remedies can include, depending on the cause of action and standing:

- restitution / delivery of property or rights;
- declaration of invalidity, ineffectiveness, rescission or unenforceability;
- repair / specific performance;
- accounting and return of traceable value;
- patrimonial damages and lost profits;
- material/moral damages where legally available;
- interest and costs;
- State/AC/third-party civil liability under the correct legal route.

## 10. Quantitative denominator — mandatory no-double-counting model

For each claimant within the Aweswell / LPB / Matkator / CEXP perimeter, calculate separately before consolidation:

- property/title value;
- hotel/operating income actually lost;
- use of third-party/extraconcursal units;
- lost exploitation distributions;
- lost refinancing / transaction opportunity only where causation can be proved;
- professional and protective expenditure where legally recoverable;
- damage from credit/control events;
- downstream value / enrichment only to the extent traceable and legally attributable;
- interest;
- moral/personal damage only for a claimant and cause of action that legally supports it;
- amounts already recovered, avoided, credited or received;
- third-party contributions and overlapping heads.

Then reconcile:

`GROSS LOSS + RESTITUTION VALUE + TRACEABLE BENEFIT - DUPLICATION - RECOVERIES - LAWFUL CONSIDERATION / INDEPENDENT VALUE = CLAIM-SPECIFIC NET DENOMINATOR`

Never add the full asset value, all historical income, all later enterprise value and every damages head without eliminating overlap.

## 13. Public language lock

Safe:

- “criminal proceedings can affect civil prescription where the same facts create a legal obstacle or constitute exercise of the civil action”;
- “criminal prescription is interrupted only under the statutory requirements of art. 132”;
- “caducity must be treated separately from prescription”;
- “a live right may have preservation mechanisms; no mechanism should be presented as reviving a right already finally expired”;
- “damages and restitution must be claimant-, act- and remedy-specific”;
- “interim measures require statutory predicates and proportionality”.

Not safe / not accurate:

- “a Fiscalía complaint stops criminal prescription”;
- “a criminal case freezes every civil deadline”;
- “ongoing harm means the offence is continuing”;
- “all retractos remain alive because there is criminal litigation”;
- “all AC acts have one limitation date”;
- “a criminal allegation automatically freezes CAM/HNT/MYND assets”;
- “the full current hotel value is automatically recoverable damages”.

## 16. Control conclusion

The recovery strategy should be **anti-forfeiture, not deadline-denial**. Preserve and interrupt every interruptible prescription period through legally effective acts; identify and protect every live caducity period using the specific mechanisms legally available; judicialise criminal claims correctly where the evidence supports them; and seek proportionate interim preservation before assets, evidence or value become harder to restore.

At the same time, do not rely on criminal proceedings to resurrect rights that a competent court determines were already extinguished. Where the original remedy is no longer mechanically available, analyse independent restitution, damages, title, accounting, AC liability, State liability and civil-ex-delicto routes on their own clocks and elements.
