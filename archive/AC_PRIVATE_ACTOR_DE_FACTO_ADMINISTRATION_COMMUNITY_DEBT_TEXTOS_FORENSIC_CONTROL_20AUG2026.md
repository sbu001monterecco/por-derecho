## Executive finding

The present record supports a **serious, structured investigation** of whether a contested Community apparatus and later CAM-linked private actors exercised de facto management functions over access, security, maintenance, debt certification and the practical use of LPB assets, while the Administrador Concursal supplied, adopted or tolerated parts of that architecture.

The evidence is materially stronger than a theory based only on parallel litigation positions:

1. the Community/Pamalexsha structure controlled debt records, voting, access verification, security instructions and maintenance entry;
2. after liquidation opened, the AC personally requested Community security, attended Community meetings, authorised access into LPB premises and treated the Community as a practical implementation channel;
3. private-actor declarations describe Community decisions as communicated to or approved by the AC and identify CAM liquidity as supporting Community/security measures;
4. Community-certified debt moved from a definitive-list claim, through a formal 2017 contingency modification, to later creditor-generated current certifications and spreadsheets used by the AC in court and in better-bid conditions;
5. CAM later invoked the same Community/AC security and access structure in opposition to Aweswell/LPB and in support of maintaining the liquidation route.

This supports a **functional-management / knowing-facilitation hypothesis** requiring investigation. It does not yet prove:

- a criminal agreement between the AC and private actors;
- habitual private direction of all AC decisions;
- that any identified person was an `administrador de hecho` of LPB for every corporate-law or criminal-law purpose;
- falsification of a specific document with the required knowledge and intent;
- that every Community expense was fictitious or charged twice;
- procedural fraud, because the exact deceptive proposition, knowledge, induced judicial error, dispositive act, benefit and patrimonial prejudice must be proved actor by actor.

The strongest present formulation is therefore:

> **Documented private performance of important management functions, combined with documented AC delegation/reliance and a creditor-generated debt architecture, creates a substantial de facto-administration and knowing-facilitation investigation. Criminal collusion remains a hypothesis, not an established fact.**

### Legal-characterisation boundary

`Administrador de hecho`, shadow director, creditor in possession, delegated estate manager and knowing facilitator are not interchangeable. The present evidence most directly supports inquiry into **de facto control over discrete estate and hotel functions**. A full company-law or criminal-law `administrador de hecho` conclusion requires proof of stable autonomous management or habitual influence over decision-making, not merely creditor pressure, a Community office, a one-off authorisation or parallel litigation.

### Double-charge hypothesis

The operator-side corpus contains substantial payment evidence for electricity, water, maintenance and insurance. That is probative of actual hotel-operation expenditure and undermines any assumption that the Community alone funded the complex. It does **not**, without a transaction-level match, prove every Community charge was a duplicate.

The required test is:

`vendor → invoice → service period → payer → asset/beneficiary → Community recharge → LPB allocation → payment/non-payment → creditor treatment → later use`.

A charge becomes a supported double charge only where the same service, period and beneficiary were already paid and then charged again without a distinct legal/economic basis.

### Strong current inference

The debt operated as a **procedurally mobile burden**:

- quantified in 2016;
- rendered contingent/no own amount in 2017;
- later represented by current creditor certificates and spreadsheets;
- imposed as a better-bid burden;
- capable of excluding voting and increasing third-party funding requirements.

That mobility is a major forensic issue. It was not wholly hidden: the later figures were filed and contested. The open question is whether they were **lawfully, independently and accurately verified**, not whether they appeared nowhere in the record.

## Direct answer: was there a later incidente concursal changing the texts?

**Located proceedings and mechanisms:**

1. **Incident 07** — Garrigues fee invoices; completed before the 2016 definitive filing.
2. **Incident 08** — Monterecco lease/rent; judgment 24 November 2015; completed before the definitive filing.
3. **20 January 2017 Article 97 bis order** — formally changes the Community pre-concurso claim to ordinary contingent/no own amount; post-concurso estate claim not changed.
4. **15 February 2018 Article 97 bis order** — formally substitutes CAM for Promontoria as holder of the two recognised specially privileged claims; no enlargement of amount or reclassification.
5. **2019 and 2021 liquidation orders/edict** — determine liquidation and better-bid conditions; the 2019 appellate order expressly states it is not modifying the definitive texts.

**Current answer:** no post-liquidation incidente concursal has been located that unequivocally changes:

- the Community pre-concurso claim from contingent/no own amount to a newly quantified final claim;
- the specially privileged total of €9,052,251.69;
- the classification of the two mortgage claims;
- each later interest component into definitively recognised concursal credit.

This is not proof that no further procedural act exists. The complete certified Section 1/5 docket and modification index remain P0 evidence.

### Was there a clandestine factual modification?

The current record supports a more exact answer:

- **Formal changes were not clandestine:** the 2017 Community modification and 2018 holder substitution were court-processed.
- **Later figures were not wholly concealed:** the AC filed the Community certifications, disclosed their category problems and sought judicial treatment.
- **Functional substitution remains a serious issue:** later liquidation documents used quantified figures for bidding and implementation even though those figures did not acquire the same legal status as definitively recognised claims.

Therefore, the currently supportable description is **possible functional bypass or category collapse**, not proven clandestine falsification.

## Strongest defence

The strongest combined defence is substantial and must remain visible:

- the complex was inactive and presented genuine safety and deterioration risks;
- the AC had statutory liquidation and preservation powers;
- Community action was facially supported by meetings and court orders;
- CAM owned units and was a recognised secured creditor, not LPB’s owner before adjudication;
- post-concurso Community liabilities could continue to accrue and claims against the estate could be updated;
- contingent sums could be reserved without deciding final entitlement;
- better-bid requirements protect the estate from an offer that ignores liabilities;
- AC/CAM communications included open settlement facilitation and disagreement, not only convergence;
- CAM expressly denied authority over LPB units and characterised works as limited to its own units;
- no current source proves the AC delegated hotel operation or habitually obeyed private instructions.

The prosecution hypothesis becomes materially stronger only if the missing records show that actors knew the debt, authority or asset premise was false or duplicated and nevertheless engineered its use to obtain control/title or exclude alternatives.

## Causation and benefit chain

The working chain is:

`contested Community authority → debt/vote exclusion → security/access apparatus → private practical control → creditor-supplied valuation/offer/debt inputs → AC recommendation → better-bid burden → adjudication/deed/Registry → operation/value/finance`.

Each arrow is presently classified separately. The chain is **not** yet proved as one coordinated criminal plan. The strongest documented links are Community debt/access functions, AC delegation/reliance, CAM use of the structure and the liquidation transaction dependency.
