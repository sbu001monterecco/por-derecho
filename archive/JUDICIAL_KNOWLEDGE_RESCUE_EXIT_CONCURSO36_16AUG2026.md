# Judicial knowledge of LPB rescue, exit and defence efforts — Concurso 36/2012

**Status:** controlled evidence ledger  
**Updated:** 16 August 2026  
**Scope:** what was demonstrably before the court, what is reported as having been put personally to the judge, and what contemporaneous evidence shows about LPB/Aweswell efforts to preserve the company and Sun Park before Sentencia 163/2023.

## 1. Executive proposition

The source set now supports a materially stronger proposition than “LPB later said it wanted to leave the concurso.”

1. **Formal court-record rescue effort is proved from 27 April 2017.** A LexNET acknowledgement records transmission to Juzgado de lo Mercantil nº 1 de Las Palmas, Concurso ordinario 36/2012, of a principal pleading presenting a **propuesta de convenio**, accompanied by a **plan de viabilidad** and **plan de pagos**. The signed/notarial convenio states that the purpose was to overcome LPB's financial crisis, preserve business activity and satisfy creditors, and argues that liquidation would destroy future operating resources capable of servicing the debt.
2. **Direct personal judicial notice is evidenced by 13 June 2018.** Daniel Irigoyen Fujiwara's same-day email reports that he personally told the judge that he had been engaged to resolve the matter by coordinating a fund, the shareholder/company and a hotel chain; that the preferred route was conclusion of the concurso by paying the concursal debt; and that conclusion would restore LPB's autonomy, control of apartments and ability to defend itself against the minority/private perimeter. The email records a substantive judicial response and an invitation to present the conclusion request immediately. This is a contemporaneous lawyer's account of an oral meeting, not a verbatim judicial transcript.
3. **The June 2018 proposal was commercially concrete, not merely rhetorical.** Contemporaneous material includes a signed 19 April 2018 ONA Corporation management proposal requiring refurbishment of apartments/common areas, a May 2018 Sun Park reform/operator presentation, and a 12 June 2018 conditional financing term sheet for up to €15.5m, with €13.84m allocated to cancel concursal/post-petition debt. The financing copy reviewed has blank signature boxes and must not be described as an executed loan commitment.
4. **The exit/rescue course continued.** September 2018 counsel circulated a written request for an updated debt certificate “para salir del concurso”; 2019 material developed an art. 176.1.4 LC payment/consignation route; later operator/refinancing work continued. The later art. 176 copies reviewed remain drafts/final-form drafts absent independent LexNET/asiento proof and therefore do **not** close ME-020.
5. **This matters to the calificación because it narrows the innocent-ignorance explanation.** Sentencia 163/2023 can no longer be analysed as if the court had never been exposed to a rescue/preservation/payment strategy. That does not prove criminal intent, prevaricación, bias or collusion. It does require proposition-by-proposition testing of later adverse findings against the earlier notice and court record.

## 2. Knowledge taxonomy

Do not collapse these categories.

### A — Direct personal judicial knowledge
Material contemporaneously reported as communicated personally to the judge or stated by the judge.

### B — Formal procedural/court-record knowledge
Filed through LexNET or otherwise proved incorporated into Concurso 36/2012. This proves the material was before the court institution; it does not automatically prove personal reading of every page or annex by the judge.

### C — Knowledge evidenced by judicial response
A signed judicial act or other reliable source showing a response to the relevant proposition.

### D — Probable institutional/docket knowledge
Material apparently in the court file but without proof of personal judicial review. Keep distinct from A–C.

## 5. The 13 June 2018 direct-knowledge event

Daniel Irigoyen's same-day email reports that he introduced himself to the judge as the lawyer tasked with resolving the matter by coordinating:

- a fund;
- the shareholder/company; and
- a hotel chain/operator.

He records telling the judge that the preferred route was conclusion of the concurso by paying the concursal debt because the asset could support payment, LPB could regain legal autonomy, regain full control of apartments for hotel exploitation, and then defend or bring actions against the minority/private actors outside the concurso.

The account further records that the judge considered the route worth exploring, asked for the conclusion request immediately, contemplated asking the AC to update the debt and requiring a deposit/guarantee, and indicated that the improved-offer route should remain alive in the meantime. Irigoyen records the judge saying he would speak with the AC.

The same email's contemplated protective measures are expressly framed in terms of **tutela judicial efectiva** and **the survival of the company and its business**.

### Publication-safe formulation
> A same-day professional account records that on 13 June 2018 the judge was personally told of a funded rescue/conclusion strategy intended to pay concursal debt, preserve hotel operation, restore LPB's autonomy and allow it to defend its interests against private actors. The account also records a substantive judicial response. It is a contemporaneous account of the meeting, not a verbatim transcript.

## 7. Rescue versus defence against private actors

The rescue strategy was not presented as simply “pay and disappear.” The June 2018 meeting report expressly links conclusion to LPB being able to:

- recover corporate autonomy;
- regain control of apartments for hotel operation;
- defend itself against the minority;
- challenge alleged fraud/irregularities in the Community perimeter;
- seek accounts; and
- litigate against responsible parties outside the concurso.

The 11–12 June preparation emails similarly show counsel considering the alleged occupation/control and Community conflict when preparing the judicial meeting.

This is relevant because the later calificación should not be analysed in a factual vacuum in which LPB is assumed never to have pursued preservation, recovery or creditor-payment routes.

## 9. Criminal/institutional significance — controlled wording

### Verified legal framework
Historical art.176.1.4 LC allowed conclusion in any state of the proceeding when all recognised credits were paid/consigned, creditors were fully satisfied by another means, or insolvency no longer existed.

Article 446 CP criminalises a judge or magistrate who **knowingly** issues an unjust judgment/resolution. Supreme Court authority treats judicial prevaricación as requiring an objectively unsustainable/arbitrary resolution and knowledge of its injustice; mere legal error, disagreement or an appealable decision is insufficient.

Official sources checked 16 Aug 2026:
- BOE, Ley 22/2003, historical art.176: https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2003-13813&p=20150715&tn=1
- BOE, Código Penal art.446: https://www.boe.es/eli/es/lo/1995/11/23/10/con
- Poder Judicial / TS jurisprudence on art.446, including STS 4217/2021 (17 Nov 2021) and the Garzón prevaricación judgment explanation.

### Evidence-based inference
The 2017/2018 record makes the later calificación **more serious to examine as a knowledge-and-reasoning question**, because it is no longer safe to posit that rescue, creditor-payment and business-preservation efforts were simply unknown to the court.

### What is NOT established by this ledger
This ledger does **not** establish as fact that:
- the judge committed prevaricación;
- the judge was corrupt or biased;
- the judge conspired or colluded with CAM, the AC or any private actor;
- the judge personally reviewed every rescue annex;
- every proposed art.176 filing was actually lodged;
- the rescue financing was unconditional or certain to close.

The Project/Marer allegation of criminal instrumentalisation remains an allegation for competent authorities to determine.

## 13. Canonical short formulation
