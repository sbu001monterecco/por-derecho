# ARRECIFE JV 1260/2011 — FALSE-ASSET CHARACTERISATION / LEGAL RESEARCH GATE

**Date:** 19 August 2026  
**Status:** CANONICAL RETRIEVAL / LEGAL-RESEARCH GATE  
**Scope:** Sun Park owner/operator eviction litigation in Arrecife; juridical identity of hotel units; procedural-fraud hypothesis; civil review; Canary tourism law.

## 1. Controlling question

For any work on Juicio Verbal 1260/2011 or analogous Sun Park desahucio/precario/possession proceedings, ask:

> **Did the Arrecife court decide possession of an ordinary autonomous apartment, or did it decide possession of a hotel/tourist accommodation unit after material parts of its juridical identity, exploitation structure, rights and obligations had been omitted or misrepresented?**

This is a research question, not a finding.

Do not reduce the issue to whether a title or pleading used the word `apartamento`. Distinguish physical accommodation unit, registered finca, horizontal-property apartment, residential dwelling, tourist accommodation unit, unit within an operating hotel, ownership, civil possession, contractual possession, tourist exploitation rights, operator rights, Community/CEXP authority and planning/tourism restrictions.

## 2. Evidential status lock

The current repository controls only the narrow chronology that the 23-Feb-2012 pleading in Juzgado de Primera Instancia nº 4 de Arrecife, JV 1260/2011, records minority-owner eviction/operator litigation, an existing action against the then operator, expansion against Monterecco Sun Park, S.L. after a change in exploitation, and a hearing sought for 6 March 2012.

That source does **not** prove:

- the merits of the eviction;
- unlawful occupation by the operator;
- that the unit was legally residential rather than touristic;
- that the claimant's characterisation was complete or incomplete;
- deception of the court;
- estafa procesal;
- maquinación fraudulenta;
- the final judgment, appeal or enforcement outcome.

Until the complete court file is reviewed, any allegation that the claimants obtained relief under a false residential/apartment premise or by withholding hotel-exploitation context remains **PARTY ALLEGATION / LEGAL HYPOTHESIS**.

## 4. Date-specific Spanish-law scan

Do not apply present law retrospectively. Build legal snapshots for 2008, 2011, filing of JV 1260/2011, 23-Feb-2012, hearing/judgment/enforcement, commencement of Concurso 36/2012 and any present remedy.

### Criminal law

Analyse at minimum:

- Código Penal Articles 248–251;
- Article 250.1.7 CP — estafa procesal;
- manipulation of evidence / `otro fraude procesal análogo`;
- whether materially deceptive omission can satisfy the fraud element;
- intent versus mistake, negligence or aggressive legal interpretation;
- judicial error;
- causation;
- patrimonial prejudice;
- attempt versus consummation and exhaustion;
- prescription;
- Articles 390–396 CP only where documentary falsity is independently supported;
- false testimony / false accusation only where the procedural role fits;
- actor-specific lawyer/client/company participation without imputing knowledge automatically.

Civil invalidity, procedural irregularity or an unsuccessful legal theory must not be converted automatically into criminal fraud.

### Civil procedure

Analyse the historically applicable versions of:

- Article 24 CE;
- Articles 11 and 241 LOPJ;
- Article 247 LEC;
- Articles 225–230 LEC;
- Article 250 LEC and the exact possession/desahucio cause of action actually used;
- Article 444 LEC where applicable;
- Articles 501–508 LEC;
- Articles 509–516 LEC;
- Article 510 LEC — revisión / maquinación fraudulenta;
- Article 512 LEC — relative and absolute limits;
- standing, litisconsorcio, res judicata, nullity, enforcement and restitution.

Always separate: (a) whether facts could substantively fit maquinación fraudulenta, from (b) whether revision or another remedy remains procedurally available today.

### Property / contracts / horizontal property

Analyse historically applicable versions of:

- Código Civil Article 348 and possession provisions;
- Articles 1091, 1255–1258 and 1281–1289;
- lease/use/mandate/management/exploitation structures;
- Ley de Propiedad Horizontal;
- Registry and mortgage-law effects;
- ownership versus immediate possession;
- opposability of exploitation rights;
- LAU in force in 2011–2012.

## 5. Canary tourism-law scan

At minimum analyse the historical and current relationship among:

- Ley 7/1995 de Ordenación del Turismo de Canarias;
- historical versions of Articles 38–42;
- Ley 5/1999;
- Ley 14/2009;
- Decreto 142/2010;
- Decreto 10/2001 where relevant;
- predecessor hotel/extrahotel accommodation rules;
- Ley 2/2013;
- later reforms only insofar as they affect present interpretation/remedies;
- transitional provisions;
- Sun Park-specific tourism and planning authorisations.

Determine specifically:

1. whether Sun Park and the relevant unit were subject to `unidad de explotación` in 2011–2012;
2. what that principle did and did not mean for private title and possession;
3. whether units could lawfully leave common exploitation and on what terms;
4. whether an owner could recover physical possession while the hotel operation continued;
5. whether CEXP or an operator held a valid/opposable title affecting possession;
6. whether public tourism regulation itself created or defeated private possessory rights;
7. whether the civil court needed the tourism/exploitation context to adjudicate possession lawfully.

## 8. Mandatory adverse / exculpatory testing

Actively search for evidence and law that supports the claimant, including whether:

- registered title carried an ordinary right to exclude;
- the operator lacked a valid or opposable title from that owner;
- CEXP/operator authority was invalid, expired or limited;
- the owner lawfully withdrew the unit from common hotel exploitation;
- tourism law regulated public operation but did not confer private possession;
- `apartamento` accurately described the registered finca;
- hotel/exploitation context was disclosed;
- the defendant appeared and could fully defend;
- the court considered and rejected the hotel argument;
- the omission was immaterial;
- the claimant reasonably relied on advice;
- the dispute was an arguable legal interpretation rather than deceit;
- no patrimonial prejudice resulted;
- any present remedy is time-barred.
