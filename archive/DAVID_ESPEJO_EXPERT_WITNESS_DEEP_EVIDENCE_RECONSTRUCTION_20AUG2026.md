**Date:** 20 August 2026

**Scope:** David Espejo Navarro / Expert Witness, reported VIDISPIR linkage, adviser custody, filing, court access, 25 July 2023 calificación vista, judgment, appeal, CAM, RIC/RIC Private Equity and publication controls.

> This memorandum is an evidence map, not a merits finding. It distinguishes documents, attributed statements, inference and unresolved questions. It does not publish privileged advice, raw emails, private attachments or live litigation strategy.

## Executive reconstruction

The strongest supportable chronology is **professional first**. The surviving correspondence shows a paid expert/economic workstream by at least 2016, continuing through 2017–2019 and later court-facing expert activity. Later 2024 correspondence also shows a cordial/networking dimension, including a journalist introduction. That later evidence is important contrary material: the relationship should not be described as exclusively professional for all time. It does not, however, reverse the chronology: the documented expert engagement materially predates the later networking overlap.

The April 2019 report question is now materially resolved for the **LPB opposition to the culpable-classification request filed on 23/24 April 2019**. Four signed final David Espejo reports were delivered to counsel on 23 April 2019. The integrated opposition and its filed bundle used **two separate Espejo report annexes**: the CEXP-rights/credit report as document 3 and the swap/pledged-deposit report as document 5. The separate `Análisis de razonabilidad de deuda` and `Nota Económica` were not separately annexed in that specific opposition bundle. This conclusion is deliberately scoped to that filing; it does not establish that the other two reports were never lodged, used or referred to elsewhere.

The 24 July 2023 incident should be described as an **access/device-media problem, not a proved defective or inaccessible court CD**. Espejo told the court that the copies in his possession did not correspond to the versions filed in the proceeding, that he had been given a court-supplied CD by the debtor, and that he could not read it because he did not have a computer with a CD reader. The court said it could not then send the material telematically and he would need to try another computer. The presently located email record does not establish whether he obtained the filed versions before giving evidence the following morning.

The 25 July 2023 vista is established by the judgment. Espejo appears in the judgment as a `testigo-perito`. The later recording-completeness concern remains **unresolved rather than proved**. Three video files were reported. A contemporaneous calculation asserted a 27-minute difference, but the same email gave endpoints of approximately 09:45–12:23 and runtimes of approximately 60 + 25 + 31 minutes. Those stated figures yield a 158-minute wall-clock span and about 116 minutes of recorded runtime — a raw difference of about **42 minutes**, not 27. A recess could explain part or all of that difference, but the currently reconstructed record does not establish its duration. No deletion, alteration or manipulation should be asserted without the original recordings, metadata/hash information and/or a certified complete court copy.

The judgment demonstrates that Espejo's evidence was not simply ignored. It was used on material subissues, including the CEXP-credit analysis and the swap/pledged-deposit issue. The court did not uphold culpability on the latter ground. The overall concurso was nevertheless declared partly culpable on other grounds, subject to the appeals then brought.

## 3. Adviser custody and filed-version control

The evidence exposes an important distinction between four things that must never be collapsed:

1. a report Espejo drafted or signed;
2. a report held by the client/advisers;
3. a report selected and annexed to a pleading;
4. the exact version residing in the court file.

The 24 July 2023 email is particularly significant because Espejo himself said that his own copies did not correspond to the copies contributed to the proceeding. That does not prove that any version was wrong, altered or improperly withheld. It does prove that **version identity and custody were live practical issues immediately before his evidence**.

The repository should therefore maintain a version-control table for every material report with at least: filename, date, signature status, hash where available, sender/recipient custody event, pleading/document number, LexNET or court receipt, and later judgment/appeal reference.

## 4. 24 July 2023 court-access incident

### Documented sequence

On the afternoon before the hearing, Espejo told the court that:

- he was preparing for his 25 July expert appearance;
- he could not access the versions of his reports that had been contributed to the proceeding;
- his own copies did not correspond to the contributed versions;
- a CD supplied through the debtor and originating from the court was said to contain the complete case copy;
- he did not have a computer with a CD reader and therefore had not accessed its contents;
- he requested telematic access to the reports in the court file.

The court replied that it could not send the documents telematically at that time and that he would need to try to open the CD on another computer. Espejo acknowledged that response and said he would try to obtain access.

### Correct evidential characterization

**Proved:** an immediate pre-hearing device/media-access barrier.  
**Not proved:** a corrupt, defective, encrypted, incomplete or wrongly prepared CD.  
**Unresolved:** whether Espejo successfully obtained and reviewed the exact filed versions before giving evidence the next morning.

Any public page should use the first formulation and preserve the second and third as express limits.

## 6. Recording-completeness audit

### Located contemporaneous record

Three recording files were reported after the vista. The contemporaneous email gave approximate start/end points of 09:45 and 12:23 and described file runtimes of about 1 hour, 25 minutes and 31 minutes.

### Arithmetic correction

09:45–12:23 is approximately 158 minutes.  
60 + 25 + 31 is approximately 116 minutes.  
The raw arithmetic difference on those numbers is therefore about **42 minutes**.

The contemporaneous suggestion that the difference was 27 minutes is not mathematically consistent with the figures stated in the same message.

### Evidential limit

A recess could explain some or all of the difference. The current reconstruction has not located a certified recess duration or a forensic examination of the original files. An early comment suggesting possible manipulation was speculative and followed an initial misreading of the available runtime; it is **not evidence of manipulation**.

**Required before any stronger proposition:** original recording files; metadata; hashes if available; certified/full court copy or audiovisual index; any log of recess/off-record periods; and comparison against the court's official hearing record.
