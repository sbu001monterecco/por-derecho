1. **verified source fact** — supported by a primary/official record;

### Track B — Ley 2/2023 protection / anti-retaliation

This is a **separate route** and depends on proving the statutory material, personal and procedural predicates.

Current legal anchors:

- **Article 3.4:** protection can extend, where applicable, to related natural persons and to legal persons for which the informant works, has a professional relationship or holds a significant participation.
- **Article 35:** protection requires, among other things, reasonable grounds to believe the information was true when communicated and within the law's material scope, plus use of a qualifying communication/revelation route.
- **Article 36.2:** retaliation includes direct or indirect unfavorable treatment because of informing.
- **Article 36.3:** illustrative examples include economic/reputational harm and discrimination/unfair treatment.
- **Article 36.5:** retaliatory/discriminatory administrative acts after protected communications are described by the statute as null and may entail corrective, disciplinary or responsibility measures, including possible damages consequences.
- **Article 38.4:** in proceedings concerning harm, once the informant reasonably establishes a qualifying protected communication/revelation and subsequent harm, the statute creates a rebuttable presumption of retaliation; the actor responsible for the detrimental measure must prove duly justified reasons unrelated to the communication.
- **Article 63.1(b):** intentional retaliation against protected persons is classified as a very serious infringement.
- **Additional Provision Six:** protection measures extend to qualifying article 2 acts/omissions occurring since the entry into force of Directive (EU) 2019/1937; a 2021 communication is therefore not automatically outside protection solely because Ley 2/2023 was enacted in 2023.

Do not collapse these rules into a finding that the Pink/Patricia measures were retaliatory. First prove material scope, protected capacity, timing, subsequent detriment and the relevant decision link.

### Track C — patrimonial liability / Ley 40/2015 + Ley 39/2015

Potential patrimonial redress is claimant-specific.

- **Ley 40/2015 art. 32:** compensation requires an effective, economically assessable and individualized injury caused by normal or abnormal functioning of public services; annulment of an act does not by itself create a right to compensation.
- **Art. 33:** do not plead several administrations as jointly liable merely because their files touch the same project; concurrent responsibility requires the statutory connection.
- **Art. 34:** the injury must be one the claimant has no legal duty to bear.
- **Art. 35:** preserves the public-liability regime where public administrations act through private-law forms in covered circumstances.
- **Art. 36:** principally regulates the Administration's recourse against authorities/personnel after compensation where the statutory culpability threshold is met; it is not ordinarily the claimant's direct damages action against a named official.
- **Art. 37:** preserves criminal responsibility and civil liability arising from crime as a separate route.
- **Ley 39/2015 art. 67:** the patrimonial claim is generally subject to a one-year prescription period from the statutory accrual trigger, with specific rules for annulled acts and personal injury. Continuing effects must not be assumed automatically to preserve every old claim.

Claimants must be separated:

- **Gil:** only his own direct personal/patrimonial/professional injury;
- **Aweswell Limited:** its own corporate loss;
- **Pink Canary Services:** its own assessment/sanction/enforcement loss;
- **Patricia:** her own derivation/enforcement/personal loss;
- **other wholly owned entities:** only their own individualized injury.

A shareholder cannot simply claim a company's loss as personal loss, and a company cannot absorb a shareholder/family member's distinct loss without a legal basis.

## 8. AIPI — operational 2026 route

The Autoridad Independiente de Protección del Informante (AIPI) is operational in 2026. Its official materials confirm an external information channel, protection/support functions and sanctioning competence under Ley 2/2023; for the state public sector, AIPI has sanctioning competence subject to the statutory allocation of powers.

Practical controlled use:

- a communication to AIPI can seek the external-channel/protection machinery where the material and personal scope requirements are met;
- protection/support measures can be requested on the statutory basis;
- any sanctioning proceeding is initiated **ex officio** by the competent authority; the informant should not describe the route as a private claim for a fine;
- if the material indicates a possible very serious intentional retaliation, article 63.1(b) is the relevant sanction classification, subject to proof and competence;
- any request should separate AEAT/state-sector conduct from Canary autonomous/local conduct because sanctioning competence is allocated by sector/territory.
