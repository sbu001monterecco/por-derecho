## Status

**CONTROLLING DEEP-AUDIT SUBLEDGER FOR ALLEGATION 02.**

This audit deliberately does **not** label the entire allegation false. The controlled primary record requires a more powerful and credible decomposition:

- a concrete diligence criticism existed and the first-instance court accepted an omission in pursuing some receivables;
- the AC's attempt to transform that omission into **Art. 164.1 culpable aggravation by dolo/culpa grave** failed in the court's own analysis;
- the AC's `LPB owes itself` construction for the €737,338.85 item was expressly defeated by the expert evidence and rejected by the court;
- the AC's causal counterfactual — that recovering the third-party sums could have taken LPB out of insolvency — was asserted without the evidential bridge the judgment says the general clause required;
- a narrower missing-support-document issue survived under the collaboration presumption and remains subject to appeal.

The strongest public criticism is therefore **causal inflation + evidential inversion + a materially corrected €737k theory**, not the inaccurate claim that no diligence issue existed at all.

# 1. Exact AC proposition

Under Art. 164.1 LC the AC alleged `inactividad de la concursada en cuanto a la reclamación de los créditos que ostenta frente a terceros` and relied on accounting entries including:

- **Sun Energy Spaces:** €101,724.93;
- **customers:** €518,908.69, including:

Headline value of those listed credit items: **€1,357,972.47**.

The AC said that after liquidation it requested supporting documents so that it could bring recovery actions, that the material had not been delivered, and that this prevented recovery during liquidation.

It then made the decisive causal leap:

> the debtor's inactivity aggravated insolvency or prevented it from overcoming insolvency because, had the sums owed by third parties been recovered, LPB could have emerged from insolvency.

# 2. The AC's own source already qualified recoverability

The same adverse report reproduces the AC's Article-75 inventory treatment of the receivables. It repeatedly says that recovery depended on the **liquidity and solvency of the debtor entities / Community**.

For Sun Energy Spaces and the customer balances, the AC valued the assets using LPB's accounting records while expressly recording that actual recovery depended on the debtor's financial position.

For the €737,338.85 CEXP item, the AC likewise treated the booked amount as an asset while saying recovery depended on the Community's liquidity and solvency.

### Evidential consequence

The AC therefore knew from his own earlier analysis that:

`booked receivable ≠ readily collectible cash`.

That distinction matters because the 2019 calificación report moved from uncertain recoverability to the much stronger proposition that recovery **could have taken LPB out of insolvency**, without a reproduced probability/timing/cost/enforcement/counterfactual calculation demonstrating that result.

### Classification

`DOCUMENTED SAME-SOURCE QUALIFICATION`

# 3. The €737,338.85 contradiction — AC values the credit, then says LPB substantially owed itself

The AC report states, in colloquial terms, that because a community of property lacks separate legal personality and LPB held more than 70% of the Comunidad de Explotación, `LUCHY se debería a sí misma` approximately 70% of the amount.

The same report's conclusion states that:

- LPB's accounting showed the Community owing LPB **€737,338.95** in dividends (the report alternates €737,338.85/€737,338.95);
- LPB held **88.96%** of the Comunidad de Explotación;
- the AC did not know the destination of the sum and considered Gil's explanation unconvincing.

This created an unresolved internal problem in the AC theory: the report both treated the amount as an LPB asset capable of supporting the culpability narrative and suggested that most of it was economically a self-debt.

## Judicial correction

Sentencia 163/2023 records the expert evidence of David Espejo Navarro and expressly concludes that the €737,338.85 booked amount was a **valid right of collection of LPB** and could **not** be treated as LPB owing itself a fraction of the amount. The judgment says that evidence **enervated** the AC report's assertion.

### Classification

`AC PROPOSITION MATERIALLY CORRECTED / JUDGMENT REJECTION`

### Knowledge boundary

This establishes that the proposition was wrong in the first-instance evidential test. It does **not yet by itself prove that the AC knew it was wrong when he wrote it**. A knowing-falsehood allegation requires proof of what Community/CEXP accounting, statutes, allocation mechanics, expert analysis and source documents the AC personally possessed before 11-Feb-2019.

# 4. The court rejected the culpability transformation under Art. 164.1

Sentencia 163/2023 is explicit about the requirements of the general clause:

1. action/omission;
2. dolo or culpa grave;
3. causal relationship with generation/aggravation of insolvency;
4. burden of proof on the AC for those elements.

The court accepted that a diligence omission existed in relation to pursuit of the receivables. But it then criticised the AC report because it **did not justify or argue the dolo/culpa-grave requirement**, stating that use of the general clause demanded a greater argumentative effort and that the court could not simply presume that non-recovery amounted to gross negligence.

The judgment specifically reasons that the two major Community items — **€472,500** and **€737,338.85** — did not appear easy to recover because of the extensive conflict surrounding the Comunidad de Explotación and because expert analysis had been required even to establish the reality/validity of the larger credit.

It further relies on the AC's own statement that recovery depended on debtor/Community liquidity and solvency.

### Controlled conclusion

The first-instance court itself rejects the decisive transformation:

`failure to pursue / diligence issue → dolo or culpa grave causing/aggravating insolvency`.

### Classification

`GENERAL-CLAUSE CULPABILITY REJECTED`

---

# 5. The “LPB could have exited insolvency” counterfactual was not proved as pleaded

The AC's 2019 report said that if the money owed by third parties had been recovered, LPB could have overcome its insolvency.

But the judgment's own analysis establishes why that sentence cannot be treated as a proved economic conclusion:

- the principal claims were not easy to recover;
- their success required litigation;
- the outcome of those proceedings was uncertain;
- enforcement depended on debtor liquidity/solvency;
- the AC report did not sufficiently establish gross fault;
- the general-clause causal mechanism required proof, not assumption.

The public site may therefore describe the `could have exited insolvency` proposition as an **unproved counterfactual used to amplify the accusation**.

Do not state that recovery could not have helped LPB. The correct point is that the AC did not prove the amount, probability, timing and enforceability required to turn the headline accounting entries into a demonstrated insolvency counterfactual.

### Classification

`UNPROVED CAUSAL COUNTERFACTUAL / MATERIAL OVERSTATEMENT`

# 7. The economic inversion: money allegedly owed TO LPB became an accusation AGAINST LPB/Gil

This is the central accountability insight of Allegation 02.

The source entries themselves describe **assets / receivables of LPB** — money LPB's accounting said was owed by third parties or by the Community structure.

The calificación narrative then inverted that direction of scrutiny:

`Why was value owed to LPB not paid / recoverable?`

became

`Why did Gil fail to recover it, and why should that make the insolvency culpable?`

That inversion is not automatically unlawful. Directors have duties to pursue company rights where appropriate. But a complete causation inquiry must examine both sides:

- origin and validity of each debt;
- who owed the money;
- why it remained unpaid;
- whether the Community/minority-owner structure created or withheld the economic value;
- what claims/actions already existed;
- what the AC knew about those claims;
- what authority/support the AC gave or withheld during the common phase;
- once liquidation opened and the AC said it intended to bring the actions, what documents it possessed, what it requested, what it could independently obtain, what actions it actually brought or did not bring, and why;
- limitation, litigation cost, enforcement prospects and debtor solvency;
- actual loss to the estate from any omitted action.

### Controlled public conclusion

It is fair to state that the accusation had a **self-protective / self-serving evidential effect** if it converted unrecovered estate value into blame focused solely on Gil without a reciprocal audit of the AC's own recovery decisions and the underlying third-party non-payment.

It is **not yet established** that the AC intentionally designed the allegation to shield the Community, minority owners, CAM or any other actor.

# 8. The 2019 opposition alleged the inversion contemporaneously

Gil's 6-Jun-2019 opposition did not invent this criticism in 2026. It expressly alleged, among other things, that:

- the AC was wrong to say supporting material had not been supplied;
- in liquidation the AC itself should pursue/realise estate assets;
- proceedings concerning the Community perimeter were already pending;
- the AC had refused to collaborate with LPB in recovery efforts while, according to the opposition, taking positions favourable to the Community's own creditor claims.

These are **PARTY ALLEGATIONS**, not adjudicated facts.

The opposition also explained the €737,338.85 item as a valid economic compensation/receivable channel arising from costs and contributions within the CEXP / minority-owner structure.

The later judgment materially supports one important part of that defence: it accepts the expert conclusion that the €737,338.85 was a valid LPB receivable and rejects the AC's self-debt construction.

# 9. Narrow adverse branch that survives — do not erase it

The judgment later revisits substantially the same receivable documentation under the **Art. 165 collaboration presumption**.

There it finds that, once liquidation had opened, LPB did not deliver supporting material for the Community/CEXP claims in the required way. The judgment says it is unknown whether the actions would have succeeded, but reasons that at least some amounts might have entered the estate and treats this as an adverse non-collaboration cause.

That reasoning is challenged on appeal. The appeal specifically attacks the internal tension in saying both:

- under the general clause, the claims were difficult/uncertain and did not establish gross culpability; and
- under collaboration, although success is expressly unknown, the missing support is treated as causally aggravating insolvency.

### Classification

`NARROW ADVERSE FINDING — LIVE ON APPEAL`

### Public integrity rule

Do not say `the court rejected Allegation 02 entirely`. It did not. It rejected the **Art. 164.1 gross-fault culpability theory**, corrected the €737k self-debt theory, but retained a narrower documentation/non-collaboration finding elsewhere.

# 11. What the allegation risked distracting from

The public page may identify the following **questions**, not pre-judged answers:

1. **Who actually owed LPB money?** Each receivable needs debtor, legal basis, source document, due date, defences, limitation and enforcement status.
2. **Why was it unpaid?** Non-payment by third parties may itself be economically relevant to LPB's distress; it cannot be erased by focusing only on collection diligence.
3. **What was the Community/CEXP economic mechanism?** The €737k judgment correction makes the origin/allocation mechanics central rather than peripheral.
4. **What did minority-owner non-participation/cost allocation actually do to LPB/CEXP?** This remains a causation thesis requiring accounts, statutes, owner-by-owner obligations and judgments.
5. **What actions already existed?** The 2019 opposition says procedures were pending. Recover the complete original actions and outcomes before strengthening this publicly.
6. **What did the AC do with estate claims?** Request → documents actually held → independent retrieval possibilities → decision to sue/not sue → limitation → outcome → accounting.
7. **What was the real insolvency counterfactual?** A booked €1.358m headline is not the same as net collectible cash available at a particular date.

# 13. Strongest defensible public wording

### Spanish

> **Alegación 02 — los créditos frente a terceros.** La AC convirtió activos que la propia contabilidad de LPB decía que eran dinero adeudado **a LPB** en una causa de culpabilidad **contra Gil**. Había una cuestión real de diligencia que debe mostrarse: la sentencia considera que determinados créditos no fueron reclamados y mantiene, en otro fundamento, una controversia sobre documentación soporte. Pero la construcción acusatoria fue mucho más lejos. La propia AC había advertido que la recuperación dependía de la liquidez y solvencia de los deudores; sostuvo a la vez que el crédito CEXP de €737.338,85 era en gran parte una especie de deuda de LPB consigo misma; y afirmó, sin un puente económico reproducible, que cobrar las partidas podría haber sacado a LPB de la insolvencia. Sentencia 163/2023 rechazó la culpa grave del art. 164.1, dijo que los principales créditos no eran de fácil reclamación y que la AC no había hecho el esfuerzo argumentativo necesario, y aceptó la pericial que desmontó la tesis de la “deuda consigo misma”. **La pregunta que queda no es sólo por qué Gil no cobró: es quién debía el dinero, por qué no se pagó, qué acciones existían y qué hizo la propia AC para recuperar esos activos una vez que asumió la liquidación.**

### English

> **Allegation 02 — third-party receivables.** The AC turned assets that LPB's own accounting said were money owed **to LPB** into a culpability case **against Gil**. There was a genuine diligence issue that must remain visible: the judgment considers that certain claims were not pursued and elsewhere preserves a dispute over supporting documents. But the accusation went much further. The AC's own earlier analysis had warned that recovery depended on debtor liquidity and solvency; the AC simultaneously suggested that much of the €737,338.85 CEXP credit was effectively LPB owing itself; and, without a reproducible economic bridge, asserted that collecting the entries could have taken LPB out of insolvency. Judgment 163/2023 rejected gross-fault culpability under Art. 164.1, said the principal claims were not easy to recover and that the AC had not done the required argumentative work, and accepted the expert evidence defeating the “self-debt” theory. **The remaining question is not only why Gil did not collect: it is who owed the money, why it remained unpaid, what actions already existed, and what the AC itself did to recover those estate assets once liquidation was under its control.**
