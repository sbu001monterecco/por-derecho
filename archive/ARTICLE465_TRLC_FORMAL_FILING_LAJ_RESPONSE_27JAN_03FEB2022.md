## 3. What the 27 January pleading actually said

Aweswell, as sole shareholder of LPB, informed the court that it had again obtained the economic arrangements necessary to support the debtor in resolving and concluding the insolvency proceeding, expressly invoking **Article 465 TRLC, formerly Article 176 LC**.

Material propositions in the pleading include:

- Aweswell says it has reached the necessary economic arrangements to support LPB in resolving/concluding the concurso;
- it expressly identifies **Article 465 TRLC** as the successor route to former Article 176 LC;
- it states that the circumstance would shortly be **expressly communicated by LPB itself through its procedural representation**;
- it asks the court, parties and insolvency administration to act responsibly so that the contemplated conclusion/continuity route is not frustrated;
- it specifically seeks a halt to mortgage-creditor actions that could prejudice or make the proposed solution ineffective;
- the `suplico` asks the court to accept the pleading and statements and to adopt what is appropriate so as not to obstruct their proper implementation.

### Critical classification limit

This means the 27 January filing is best described as:

> **a formal Article 465 conclusion-related notice and protective request by Aweswell, expressly anticipating an imminent debtor-side LPB conclusion application/communication.**

It should **not automatically be relabelled as the complete final LPB Article 477 merits application** because:

1. the filing itself says LPB would shortly make the express debtor-side communication/request; and
2. its operative prayer is protective/open-textured rather than a fully particularised request for an immediate auto declaring the concurso concluded.

This distinction is important both for evidential accuracy and when assessing the LAJ's subsequent clarification requirement.

## 7. Applicable legal framework at the January 2022 date

Use the **pre-Law 16/2022** TRLC wording applicable in January 2022.

### Article 465.6 TRLC

At that date, conclusion with archive was available **in any state of the proceeding** when payment/consignation of all recognised credits, full satisfaction of creditors by another means, or disappearance of insolvency was established.

Official control: BOE, Real Decreto Legislativo 1/2020, original wording in force before the September 2022 reform.

### Article 477 TRLC

The procedural provision governing conclusion by creditor satisfaction allowed the debtor, insolvency administration or a creditor to invoke total payment/consignation or full satisfaction as a conclusion cause. Where the AC did not make the application, the provision contemplated a transfer to the AC for a report and then procedural treatment/opposition before judicial determination.

### Article 216 LEC

Article 216 embodies the civil-procedure principle of party disposition / `justicia rogada`: civil courts decide on the basis of the facts, evidence and claims advanced by the parties, except where the law provides otherwise.

### Significance for the 3 February Diligencia

A strong contrary/defensive reading of the LAJ's act must be preserved:

- the 27 January pleading was made by **Aweswell as shareholder**, while the pleading itself said LPB would shortly make the debtor-side request;
- the operative prayer was not a tightly particularised demand for a specific Article 477 merits order;
- asking what concrete office action was sought is therefore not facially inexplicable.

The accountability question becomes materially stronger only if the record shows that:

1. the clarification was actually supplied, or an unmistakably complete LPB Article 477 request was filed;
2. the statutory transfer/report/opposition route was nevertheless not activated; and/or
3. irreversible asset-transfer mechanics were allowed to proceed despite a procedurally complete and known conclusion application without reasoned treatment.

Those bridges must be proved from the primary docket before escalating the characterisation.
