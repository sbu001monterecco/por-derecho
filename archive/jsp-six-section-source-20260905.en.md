## 1. Who is who—and the most important ownership correction

### JSP is José Sánchez Peñate, S.A.

The current canonical register identifies **José Sánchez Peñate, S.A.**, NIF **A35020726**, as **`PD-SP-O-0085`**. It distinguishes that company from group branding and from the unverified **“J.S.P., S.L.”** wording appearing in a later property workbook. That suffix cannot simply be treated as another spelling of the same legal person.

**The strongest correction is that the reviewed records put JSP’s direct shareholding in Monte Lanza at 26.82%, not a majority.** The executed review reports that percentage in the 2008 and 2012 accounts, with the certified June 2009 meeting extract recording **59 of 220 company shares**. Those 220 shares must not be confused with the hotel’s 220 apartments. Indirect control, contractual control or control exercised through other shareholders remains a separate evidential question.

### Montelanza / Monte Lanza is a separate company

The official 2008 tourism notice identifies **Montelanza, S.L., CIF B35279850**, in connection with **Hotel Apartamento Sun Park**. Importantly, the notice also reproduces the company’s account of having ceased operating Sun Park. This provides an official anchor for its historical operating role, not proof that it owned every component of the hotel. ([Gobierno de Canarias](https://www.gobiernodecanarias.org/boc/2008/245/033.html "BOC - 2008/245. Martes 9 de Diciembre de 2008 - 4927"))

The BORME record for **MONTE LANZA SOCIEDAD LIMITADA**, registry sheet **GC1339**, records its voluntary dissolution in 2015. It names **Anastasio Molina López** and **José Sánchez Rodríguez**, alongside José Manuel Romero Domínguez and José Vicente Llinares Dorta, among the outgoing directors, and appoints **Atilio Rodríguez Beltrá** as liquidator. Those are concrete corporate connections; they do not establish a single common intention among everyone bearing a Molina name. ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/diario_borme/txt.php?id=BORME-A-2015-205-35 "BORME-A-2015-205-35 LAS PALMAS"))

### “Molina” describes several distinct people and interests

The repository expressly requires separation between, for example, **Anastasio Molina López’s corporate role**, **José Luis Molina Marrero’s property/creditor position**, and the positions of **Francisco Luis Molina Molina**, **Miguel Molina Betancor**, **Cristina Molina Petit** and other named owners or representatives. Some name reconciliations remain open. Their identities, units, representatives and positions change across different meetings and proceedings.

## 2. What the 2008 transaction actually requires us to reconstruct

Your recorded position is that the intended acquisition covered the whole hotel and that retained interests frustrated that acquisition. The latest source review preserves that allegation, but it also shows why **“JSP/Montelanza sold the hotel but retained some units” is too compressed to identify the actual breach, its author or its consequences**.

The executed review identifies several separate components:

| Component | What the reviewed sources report |
| --- | --- |
| **Underlying property structure** | The 26 May 2008 operating-assignment contract describes **220 apartments belonging to their respective owners**, plus **40 commercial premises and two pools belonging to Monte Lanza**. |
| **Operating transfer** | The contract assigns defined hotel operation to the **Comunidad de Explotación**, or CEXP. Its **€6,000 consideration is not the price of the hotel’s real estate**. |
| **Completed Monte Lanza transaction** | The 2008 accounts refer to a **12 March option exercised on 17 June 2008**, with an asset sale recorded at **€1,368,000**. That figure must not be presented as the whole-hotel acquisition price. |
| **Residual transaction** | The accounts record a separate same-day option over **11 unsold premises**, running to **31 December 2010**. |
| **Intermediate dispositions** | The 2012 accounts describe sales in **2009** and transfers of certain premises to shareholders in **2010**, against company debts or advances. |

These are findings reported by the repository’s executed source review. I have read that review; I have not independently reopened every private original behind it in this scan.

**The decisive question is therefore contractual and property-specific:** which seller committed to transfer which property or operating right, what completed, what remained subject to an option or other obligation, and what happened to that obligation before any later sale or transfer?

The accounting reference to an option’s expiry does **not**, by itself, establish that the buyer’s rights were lawfully extinguished. Equally, retained title does not automatically prove a dishonest breach. The signed options, seller mandates, exercise attempts, completion instruments, releases and payments are what distinguish those possibilities. The repository identifies those as outstanding records.

## 3. How the seller/owner background connects to the later “dissidents”

The connection is not merely retrospective speculation. The repository identifies a sequence of contemporary records.

**In April–May 2008**, the CEXP was formed and the operating arrangement documented. The 29 April meeting records **74.90% approval** and **10.01% opposition/reservation**. Participation was described as voluntary/open, and the recovered material does not yet allocate that non-participating percentage to every individual unit. Several companies that later became possession claimants were present or represented at that meeting.

**By April 2009**, the historical reconstruction identifies a Campanario email describing a defined Molina-linked group as unwilling to join CEXP and proposing an alternative one-year rental arrangement. That is evidence of an existing operational split. It is not, without the underlying obligations, proof that refusal to join was unlawful.

**By February–June 2011**, the same broad dispute had developed into Community governance and litigation. The reconstruction places Álvaro Campanario inside the 2 February meeting as representative of much of the later claimant group, meeting secretary and proponent of litigation strategy. The 22 June ACTA records LPB’s substantial represented coefficient but excludes it from the separate vote-entitled list, while debts, recovery and governance measures were addressed. The document supports investigation of a **debt → voting exclusion → decision-making mechanism**; its legal validity and any deliberate fabrication remain separate questions.

There is also an important distinction within that meeting:

**The 22 June 2011 record already associates José Sánchez Peñate, S.A. with commercial premises 7–11, and Montelanza with premises 3–4, represented by Pablo Rivero Correa.** Asunción Aizpurúa represented a different collection of owners. Consequently, neither JSP nor Montelanza should automatically be folded into every act performed by Asunción or every later claimant.

That 2011 record is an especially useful cross-check against the later 2018 workbook: **the search for how premises 7–11 reached JSP must examine the earlier acquisition history, including the possible 2010 transfers.** The meeting entry is not a title certificate, but it is an earlier documentary lead than the workbook.

### The adverse possession judgment must remain part of the explanation

The linked **JV 1260/2011 → AP 89/2014** reconstruction concerns seven claimants and 18 apartments: Acciones Canarias, Muruga, Roque Prieto, Amenem, Amenem/Tengolf, Miguel Molina Betancor and Francisco Luis Molina Molina. It records the appeal’s recognition of the claimants’ individual title/possessory position. CEXP’s overall participation percentage did not establish authority over every one of those apartments. That adverse outcome cannot be omitted when explaining the background.

## 4. The later liquidation does not produce one uniform title chain

Monte Lanza’s voluntary dissolution was registered in **October 2015**; its extinction was registered on **9 October 2018**. This is its corporate winding-up, not JSP’s later insolvency. ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/diario_borme/txt.php?id=BORME-A-2015-205-35 "BORME-A-2015-205-35 LAS PALMAS"))

The executed review of the **18 November 2016 deed, protocol 2026**, is particularly important because it distinguishes three properties previously capable of being compressed into a single narrative:

| Property | Position reported from the reviewed material |
| --- | --- |
| **Finca 8499 / commercial unit 3** | Conditional allocation to the **Sun Park Owners’ Community**. |
| **Finca 8500 / commercial unit 4** | Conditional allocation in undivided shares: **Ayuntamiento de Yaiza 57.78%**, **José Luis Molina Marrero 25.28%**, and **José Miguel Molina Petit 16.94%**. |
| **Finca 8498** | **Not included** in that deed. Its registry extract points to a separate transaction requiring recovery. |

The review reports express conditions of **acceptance by all creditors and approval by Monte Lanza’s general meeting**. It does not establish the complete set of acceptances and approval. That is an unresolved proof question—not a finding that the conditions necessarily failed.

It also distinguishes the participants’ capacities. Atilio appears as liquidator. Asunción is identified as Community president for notification, **not as an appearing seller or an expressly accepting co-signatory**. Notification cannot simply be rewritten as acceptance.

The April 2017 informational registry extracts still show Monte Lanza for these properties. They are dated snapshots, not complete title histories. Therefore, **a completed “Monte Lanza → Community → CAM” chain cannot responsibly be asserted for all three properties without the separate instruments**.

This does not erase the wider CAM connection. The litigation reconstruction identifies later Community/CAM convergence and a separate 2018 transfer involving **finca 8718**, formerly associated with Tengolf/Amenem. But proof concerning finca 8718 cannot supply missing conveyances for 8498, 8499 or 8500, or establish CAM’s participation in the 2008 arrangements.

## 5. The 2017 JSP notice supplies an independent documentary route

The new dossier’s central source is genuinely significant.

The official **10 August 2017 BORME notice** records a request from shareholders holding **more than 5% of JSP’s capital** to add agenda items to its general meeting. One requested further explanations about the **Sun Park sale and the sale of a majority interest in Explobeach**, expressly referring to explanations given at earlier general meetings. The notice identifies **JOSILAC, S.L.**, represented by **José Sánchez Rodríguez**, as JSP’s sole administrator. ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/diario_borme/txt.php?id=BORME-C-2017-7368 "BORME-C-2017-7368 JOSÉ SÁNCHEZ PEÑATE, S.A."))

**Its importance is that it identifies a specific corporate documentary trail independent of the project’s later allegations:** the shareholder request, earlier meeting minutes, management explanations and subsequent meeting record. It does not establish the sale price, the precise rights sold, the purchasers, a transfer of all 262 properties or criminal conduct. Those limitations are also stated in the new repository dossier. ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/diario_borme/txt.php?id=BORME-C-2017-7368 "BORME-C-2017-7368 JOSÉ SÁNCHEZ PEÑATE, S.A."))

Explobeach is therefore relevant, but separate. The register identifies **Explobeach, S.L.** and records a 2015 administrator change involving **Hotel Royal Playa Blanca, S.L.** It expressly warns that appointment as administrator is not proof of buying the company’s shares.

## 6. JSP’s 2021 insolvency is a later, distinct accountability question

The official record confirms **insolvency 440/2021**, declared on **21 July 2021**, naming **José Sánchez Peñate, S.A. and Celgán, S.A.** It identifies the historical court and appointments, including **Praxis de Reestructuraciones y Concursal, S.L.P.** as insolvency administrator and **Miota & Alonso Asociados, S.L.P.** as delegated auxiliary. ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/diario_borme/txt.php?id=BORME-A-2021-194-38 "BORME-A-2021-194-38 SANTA CRUZ DE TENERIFE"))

The relevant question is what reached that insolvency estate: direct properties, proceeds, unpaid prices, shareholder entitlements, receivables or potential claims arising from the earlier transactions. The repository does **not** certify an omission from the inventory without having examined that inventory, and it leaves JSP’s final insolvency conclusion/extinction unverified. Nor does it attribute the 2015–2018 acts retrospectively to office-holders appointed in 2021.
