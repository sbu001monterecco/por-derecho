## Purpose

Record the lawyers and firms who acted for Gil Marer, LPB, Aweswell or the related Project-side perimeter, and test whether conduct by the Insolvency Administrator affected the defence.

Every entry preserves one integrated mandate account:

`ACCEPTED / BILLED SCOPE → AC ACT OR OMISSION → PROFESSIONAL RESPONSE OR LIMIT → RESULT → DETERIORATION / TRANSITION → CLIENT POSITION + CONTRARY EVIDENCE`

A formal withdrawal reason is relevant evidence, but it must not be used as a complete characterisation of the earlier mandate, its practical effectiveness or the effect of AC conduct. Internal source-status analysis remains necessary; the public account must not reduce a nuanced mandate to a binary causation badge.

## €400,000 sale / Parrilla payment / “false peace” control

The controlled account has two connected tracks. They must be read together without turning a documented sequence into a proved bribe or a single-fund assertion:

1. **Asset and estate-funds track.** The record documents an active finance-backed exit and the 26 June 2018 suspension of realisation of the listed mortgaged apartments. The AC treated the 31-finca locales/pools branch as outside that suspension. Protocol 2150 of 28 November 2018 recites a €400,000 sale to CAM; the AC plan had reported ACTÚA value of €489,361.96 for that branch. The AC's 20 December report says the cheque became effective, an LPB account was opened and the AC paid itself remuneration for identified phases. The 24 October 2019 order later held the deed occurred without court authority and refused convalidation. The complete debit→estate credit→ledger/payee→post-nonconvalidation bridge remains open.
2. **Counsel-transition track.** During the May–June 2020 Parrilla→Jiménez transition, Parrilla conveyed the AC's request despite the client's contemporaneously recorded anti-intermediation instruction. Jiménez's call report records “peace”, possible complaint withdrawal, assurance that Parrilla and the procurator would be paid from proceeding funds, praise of Parrilla and IA/CAM positions. A July AC report separately records €26,750 for Parrilla.

Gil/Patricia call the second track a **“false peace”** and allege that the combined sequence created a perverse economic incentive capable of breaking client-lawyer trust and influencing incoming counsel before protective actions were filed. That is a supported investigative inference about the sequence and motive for investigation. It is not an adjudicated finding of bribery, collusion or improper payment. The current record does not yet prove that the €26,750 came specifically from the €400,000. If the complete invoice, authority, bank statements and estate ledger establish that link, it would materially strengthen the incentive thesis; until then the source-of-funds proposition stays open.

## Daniel / Parrilla / AC finding

The record supports a disputed but materially documented influence sequence:

- 22 May 2020: bilingual SLJ proposal accepted; direct client instructions, intended AC removal and possible AC/CAM claims;
- 28 May: Jiménez reports that Parrilla conveyed the AC's wish to speak. Patricia records that Parrilla had repeatedly been told not to intercede for the AC or proceed off-record and warns Jiménez of the precise influence risk;
- 1 June: Jiménez takes the call and reports the IA's peace/complaint-withdrawal proposal, assurance that Parrilla/procurator would be paid, praise of Parrilla and the IA/CAM narrative;
- 5 June: Jiménez says CAM's lawyer asked the IA to arrange contact. Patricia instructs that claims should precede contact and that no information be provided. The proposed CAM call is not proved to have occurred;
- 11 June: Jiménez objects to AC position in draft correspondence;
- 18 June: AC sends private/confidential lawyer email; Jiménez forwards it and says AC is losing his manners;
- 18 June: Jiménez says he ended negotiations with Parrilla/AC because they tried to condition work on fee payment;
- 23 June: Jiménez announces resignation. The client response restates the pre-engagement no-contact instruction; Jiménez's withdrawal cites strategy, client direction and non-payment;
- 3 July: proposed formal court resignation filing; the later fee ruling finds no signed Jiménez/SLJ procedural filing during the mandate;
- 4 December: SLJ's fee-account opposition files a 179-page inventory naming the contents of 4,458 client documents, plus selected client material;
- 29 September 2021: Decree 316/2021 upholds Aweswell's opposition, finds no Jiménez/SLJ-signed filing and treats the claimed court filing as unproved; 2 November 2021 finality/archiving follows without appeal.

Public conclusion: Gil/Patricia allege that the AC used Parrilla as an access route and sought to instrumentalise the transition to influence incoming counsel despite protective client instructions. The 28 May–5 June chain supports publication of that proposition as an investigative inference. It does not prove unlawful intent, collusion, that the CAM call occurred or that the contact caused Jiménez's resignation. Jiménez's resistance to several IA positions remains contrary evidence.

The separate data-room proposition must also remain exact. SLJ told the court that Aweswell had supplied 4,458 documents, retained an indexed copy in its own database and filed the resulting 179-page inventory. It did not file all 4,458 underlying files. The LexNET wrapper records copy-transfer to Aweswell's procurator only. Whether the IA, CAM or another adverse party later obtained actual access requires the separate-piece access, service and consultation history. The client's description of the taking as theft is an allegation, not a proved legal characterisation; the controlled questions are authority to retain, necessity, confidentiality, proportionality and resulting access or prejudice.

## Publication locks

1. The 19 February 2020 *providencia* granted the prior procedural licence required by Article 215.2; it is not a merits ruling or proof that a complaint was filed, admitted, upheld or resulted in liability. Its procedural character does not erase its documented practical effect on the defence.
2. “Abusive” is Gil/Patricia’s characterisation, not an adjudicated finding.
3. A temporal sequence alone is not a causal finding, but an integrated mandate history must include practical and cumulative effects shown by the record.
4. A professional’s stated withdrawal reasons and strongest contrary evidence remain visible without being treated as an exhaustive explanation of mandate performance or AC effect.
5. Parrilla’s substitution remains a client decision.
6. Cuatrecasas’s formal withdrawal reason remains attributed to unpaid fees. Public wording must also preserve the firm’s broader billed work, favourable results, mandate boundaries, conditioned responses and the client’s attributed protection-failure allegation.
7. Garrigues’s work predates the relevant AC conduct; no causal claim is made.
8. Current Kepler–Karst identities are professional-continuity notes, not corporate-successor liability.
9. Remaining professionals are B1, B2, B3… pending verified client, mandate, dates and publication decision.
10. Privileged advice, strategy and weaknesses remain private.
11. The Daniel entry may state the client's pre-engagement oral instruction as client testimony corroborated by contemporaneous written sequencing instructions; it must not imply that the exact oral wording has been independently recovered.
12. The 4 December 2020 filing disclosed a full inventory of the client file and selected documents, not every one of the 4,458 underlying documents. Actual IA/CAM receipt is not established by the LexNET wrapper.
13. The sale price is €400,000, not €1,000. The plan's ACTÚA figure for the 31-finca branch was €489,361.96.
14. The 26 June 2018 suspension applied to the listed mortgaged apartments; the AC treated the locales/pools branch as outside it. LPB challenged that treatment, and the court later refused convalidation of the deed. Do not collapse those distinct propositions into a judgment that the sale breached the suspension.
15. The 2018 AC report of receipt and self-payment, the 2020 assurance/praise and the later €26,750 Parrilla payment are separate proved records. Their combination may support an attributed perverse-incentive/“false peace” inference, but bribery and the €400,000→€26,750 bank link remain unproved.
