## Controlling entity distinction

1. **Former client / billed relationship:** contemporaneous correspondence identifies Aweswell Limited as “our client, AWESWELL.” Cuatrecasas' 3 July 2019 schedule attributed issued invoices to Aweswell and Luchy Playa Blanca, not Matkator.
2. **Instrument / judicial debtor and executed party:** Matkator later became payer on four pagarés, defendant/debtor in the cambiario and executed party in ETJ 163/2020. The existence of a valid autonomous instrument liability remains legally possible and is not negated by the invoice-entity distinction.
3. **Possible remate recipient:** the 10 February 2026 Cuatrecasas filing expressly reserved cession of the remate to a third party. The located record does not yet identify an assignee or prove a completed cession.

## Game-theory disclosure control

The visual uses a four-answer disclosure dilemma:

- no third party: responsible certification;
- independent third party: identity/UBO, consideration, KYC/conflict and debtor-credit reconciliation;
- client or connected person: heightened former-client, own-interest, confidentiality, barriers, KYC and approval control;
- lawful withholding/no answer: category, date range, control owner and non-privileged index.

This is a document-production framework, not a claim of collusion or a literal prisoner's dilemma. Its only stable answer is a complete single-satisfaction ledger from invoice and WIP through pagaré, claim, remate/cession, credit, recovery and balance.

## Mandatory boundaries

Do not state, without new primary evidence, that:

- a remate cession occurred;
- any identified person was the assignee or intended assignee;
- Cuatrecasas acted in concert with an underlying counterparty;
- the pagarés or Matkator liability were invalid;
- KYC/AML duties applied to every litigation or remate act in the same way; or
- double payment or double recovery occurred.

The verified reservation, former-client relationship, billing-entity difference, execution steps, credits and remaining-claim figures support the demand for a document-backed reconciliation. Silence may preserve the gap; it is not proof of the missing fact.
