## 2. Controlling conclusion

The strongest source-controlled analysis is not “the same case twice”. It is a **procedural-propagation problem across two distinct tracks sharing disputed assumptions about exploitation authority, possession, keys, Community/CEXP competence, operating costs and the legal/economic status of the hotel as an integrated business over divided titles.**

### Track A — private actors / minority-unit exploitation litigation

The 23-Dec-2011 JV 1260/2011 claim was brought by a private-owner perimeter concerning 18 units. The controlled primary reconstruction identifies Acciones Canarias, Muruga, Roque Prieto, Amenem, Tengolf, Miguel Molina Betancor and Francisco Luis Molina Molina in the claimant perimeter. Álvaro Campanario Hernández represented much of the same perimeter in Community governance before acting in the litigation.

The claim was later expanded against Monterecco after the claimant side asserted a change in exploitation. First instance found insufficient proof that Monterecco possessed/exploited the 18 units. AP 89/2014 reversed that result after considering a post-judgment May-2012 key-delivery event. Contemporary material describes those keys as CEXP maintenance copies, but the complete notarial/appellate evidential package remains keystone evidence.

**Boundary:** AP 89/2014 is materially adverse evidence. It does not, by itself, adjudicate universal whole-hotel authority, extinguish all CEXP rights, decide the legal status of every non-claimant unit, establish that LPB owned the whole hotel, or establish that Pink validly succeeded to every owners-derived exploitation right.

### Track B — AC-initiated Pink / rent / termination / calificación chain

The AC, Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte, later pursued the LPB–Monterecco/Pink operating-contract/rent dispute. The controlling Allegation-03 ledger identifies the AC's 17-Jan-2014 contract-resolution/rent claim and the 24-Nov-2015 Pink judgment as a separate procedural chain.

The AC's later calificación case divided into propositions that must remain separate:

1. **03-A — entry into the 6-Feb-2012 operating agreement was itself economically culpable:** Sentencia 163/2023 rejected the AC's rent-versus-mortgage reasoning as sufficient to make entry into the contract culpable.
2. **03-B — alleged non-payment/non-enforcement:** Sentencia 163/2023 made materially adverse findings of gross fault, causation and Pink complicity. Those findings are appealed.
3. **03-C — underlying authority/validity/CEXP continuity:** not resolved merely by the 03-A economic-rationality holding. The Project's nullity/ineffectiveness theory remains an attributed legal position subject to the contrary case and primary-source proof.

The same ledger records that the AC ultimately litigated the contractual-resolution/rent claim and later used the Pink/rent history in calificación. The exact euro bridge from the monthly contractual burden to later judgment/execution figures remains a controlled reconciliation task.
