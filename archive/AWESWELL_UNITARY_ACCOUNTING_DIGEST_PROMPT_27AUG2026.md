## Accounting recognition analysis — asset by asset

Do not assert that the entire hotel, all future income and all damages must be booked immediately as one number. Test each item separately:

### A. Owned or controlled property and business assets

Determine legal title/control, reporting-entity ownership, consolidation perimeter, classification, original cost, impairment history and reliable measurement evidence. Test the applicable FRS 102 treatment, including property, plant and equipment, investment property, financial assets, business combinations and consolidation. Identify whether cost, revaluation or fair-value measurement is available and what independent valuation evidence is required.

### B. Hotel trading income and past operating proceeds

Distinguish:

- income already earned by a reporting entity;
- income received by another actor but claimed to be attributable to the group;
- future income forecasts;
- lost-profit or account-of-profits claims.

Recognise income only in the correct period and entity when the asset/income definition, probability and reliable-measurement tests are met. Do not capitalise all forecast future hotel income merely because the hotel is operating.

### C. Damages, restitution, compensation and recovery claims

Classify each claimed recovery. Apply the contingent-asset threshold. Where inflow is probable but not virtually certain, disclose rather than recognise. Recognise only when the claim ceases to be contingent under the applicable standard. For compensation relating to impaired, lost or surrendered property, test the specific virtually-certain threshold.

### D. Subsequent recognition and correction

Distinguish:

- correction of a material error/non-compliance existing at the original reporting date;
- change in accounting estimate;
- newly obtained evidence about conditions existing at the reporting date;
- later events and later recoveries that belong in later accounting periods.

Do not call every later recognition a “restatement”. Use amended accounts, retrospective correction, revaluation, estimate change or later-period recognition only where technically appropriate.
