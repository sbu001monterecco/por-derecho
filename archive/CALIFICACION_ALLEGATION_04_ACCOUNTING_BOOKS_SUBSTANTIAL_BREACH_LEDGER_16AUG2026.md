# CALIFICACIÓN — ALLEGATION 04: ACCOUNTING BOOKS / SUBSTANTIAL ACCOUNTING BREACH

**Date:** 16 August 2026  
**Proceeding:** Concurso ordinario 36/2012 — Luchy Playa Blanca, S.L.U. (`LPB`)  
**AC:** Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte  
**Legal ground pleaded:** former Art. 164.2.1 LC  
**Public routes:** `/es/calificacion-concurso-36-2012-vidas-paralelas/` and `/en/insolvency-classification-parallel-lives/`

## Status

**CONTROLLING DEEP-AUDIT LEDGER FOR ALLEGATION 04.**

The strongest source-controlled conclusion is narrower than both slogans `LPB had no accounting` and `there was no accounting breach`:

> **The AC's own report acknowledges that LPB continued accounting and that accounting journals and trial balances for 2008–2012 were delivered. Sentencia 163/2023 itself rejects mere non-legalisation of the pre-concurso books as enough, but nevertheless upholds an absolute culpability ground focused on the absence/non-production of the statutory Libro Diario during the concurso. That adverse finding is under appeal. The documentary dispute is therefore about statutory-book completeness and the later-period Libro Diario — not an absence of accounting information in general.**

This distinction is mandatory for all repository and public wording.

---

## 1. Exact AC proposition

The 11-Feb-2019 AC report invokes the statutory alternatives of Art. 164.2.1 LC: substantial failure to keep accounts, double accounting, or a relevant accounting irregularity.

But the factual case actually pleaded was principally:

1. LPB had not supplied/legalised the company's **official accounting books** for the previous four years;
2. the AC said it did not know that, during the concurso, LPB had kept accounting and legalised the books;
3. annual accounts had not been formulated with the AC's intervention; and
4. a Jan-2018 request still showed accounting material as pending from Jonathan.

At the same time, the AC expressly reproduced from his earlier Art. 75 report that:

> `La sociedad ha seguido contabilizando las operaciones efectuadas durante el ejercicio 2012 y se nos ha hecho entrega, en formato PDF de los diarios contables y balances de sumas y saldos de los ejercicios 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011 y 2012, este último cerrado parcialmente a junio.`

### Control

The AC therefore did **not** have a source-consistent basis to portray the company as having produced no accounting at all. His own report records both continued accounting and delivery of journals/trial balances.

### Classification

`AC ALLEGATION — OFFICIAL/STATUTORY BOOK DEFICIENCY; GLOBAL NO-ACCOUNTING SHORTHAND CONTRADICTED BY AC'S OWN REPORT`

## 2. What was actually delivered at the beginning of the concurso

Gil's 2019 opposition reproduces June-2012 emails from accountant **Jonathan Simó Morales** copied to members of the AC team. The material identified in those emails includes:

- 2008 accounting journal;
- 2009 accounting journal;
- 2010 accounting journal;
- 2011 accounting journal;
- partial 2012 accounting journal; and
- balances of sums and balances for 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011 and 2012.

The exact private attachment filenames are withheld. The AC's own report independently corroborates the essential point: journals and trial balances for 2008–2012 were received, with 2012 partial to June.

### Evidential limit

This does **not** by itself establish that every document was a duly legalised statutory book, that every later post-concurso Diario existed in complete form, or that the contents satisfied every Commercial Code obligation.

### Classification

`DOCUMENTED DELIVERY OF SUBSTANTIAL ACCOUNTING MATERIAL / FORMAL-STATUTORY SUFFICIENCY SEPARATE`

## 3. Sentencia 163/2023 narrows the AC's pre-concurso theory

The judgment expressly says it will focus on the **substantial accounting breach during the concurso**, because:

> mere failure to legalise the books does not itself integrate Art. 164.2.1 unless it obstructs the reflection/understanding of the debtor's patrimonial or financial situation.

That is a direct judicial narrowing of the AC's earlier reliance on non-legalisation of the 2008–2011 books.

### Controlled conclusion

`pre-concurso books not legalised → automatically Art. 164.2.1 culpable`

is **not** the basis the court ultimately accepted.

### Classification

`PRE-CONCURSO MERE-NONLEGALISATION THEORY — MATERIALLY NARROWED / NOT SUFFICIENT BY ITSELF`

---

## 4. The adverse core the court did accept

The judgment records that Gil supplied:

- LPB trial/checking balances for 2013, 2014, 2015 and 2016;
- accounting statements for 2012–2016; and
- emails showing annual accounts had been sent.

But the court held that these were not, by themselves, the statutory accounting required by Art. 25.1 CCom, which it identified through the **Libro Diario** and **Libro de Inventarios y Cuentas Anuales**.

The decisive sentence is that the court considered it proven that **the Libro Diario had not been supplied to the AC**, but rather checking balances and annual accounts. Given what it described as the Diario's central importance for chronological, detailed tracking of economic operations, it held the substantial accounting breach established.

### Important tension

The same judgment therefore simultaneously records:

- substantial accounting information existed and was supplied; and
- the statutory Diario for the relevant post-concurso period was not established as supplied/available.

The finding is not accurately described as `there were no accounts`.

### Classification

`ADVERSE FIRST-INSTANCE FINDING — SUBSTANTIAL BREACH BASED ON MISSING/NON-PRODUCED LIBRO DIARIO DURING CONCURSO — APPEALED`

---

## 5. Collaboration and accounting cannot be collapsed

Elsewhere in Sentencia 163/2023, when deciding the separate collaboration allegation, the court says that LPB **delivered the accounting documentation it had** and therefore refuses to find a collaboration breach merely from failure to deliver accounting documents.

That does not cancel the Art. 164.2.1 finding. It does prove that two propositions must remain separate:

- `LPB failed to keep/prove the complete statutory Diario during the concurso` — adverse accounting finding;
- `LPB refused to hand over all accounting it possessed` — not accepted as a general collaboration finding.

### Classification

`JUDICIAL DISTINCTION — KEEPING/FORMAL STATUTORY ACCOUNTING ≠ DELIVERY/COLLABORATION`

---

## 6. Separate annual-accounts presumption was rejected

The AC separately alleged, under the iuris-tantum annual-accounts branch, that during the concurso annual accounts had not been formulated with AC intervention, approved or deposited and that he had no record of accounting after declaration.

Sentencia 163/2023 labels this separate ground:

**`Falta de formulación de las cuentas anuales. Desestimación.`**

The public record must therefore not merge that rejected presumption back into the surviving Art. 164.2.1 finding.

### Classification

`SEPARATE ANNUAL-ACCOUNTS PRESUMPTION — REJECTED BY SENTENCIA 163/2023`

---

## 7. No double-accounting or false-document finding

Art. 164.2.1 contains several statutory alternatives. The available AC report does not establish a developed allegation that LPB maintained **double accounting**. Nor does it identify a particular accounting entry as the separate Art. 164.2.2 category of serious inaccuracy/false document; under that branch the AC expressly says essentially that nothing is known to him.

### Publication rule

Do not headline Allegation 04 as `double accounting`, `false accounts`, `falsified accounts`, or `fabricated accounting`.

The surviving first-instance issue is the statutory **substantial failure to keep the required accounting**, centred by the judgment on the Libro Diario.

---

## 8. The AC's own extensive use of LPB accounting is relevant — but not dispositive

The AC used LPB accounting information throughout his report to calculate or discuss, among other matters:

- historic net equity;
- working-capital and liquidity/solvency ratios;
- third-party receivables;
- short-term financial investments;
- insolvency timing; and
- year-by-year financial evolution.

That use is strong evidence against a blanket narrative that LPB's economic position was wholly unknowable because there was no accounting information.

It is **not**, however, proof that all mandatory books were properly kept. The correct evidential question is:

> **How much of LPB's patrimonial and financial history could the AC actually reconstruct from the accounting he possessed, and what additional material fact remained unknowable specifically because the post-concurso statutory Diario was missing or not produced?**

The judgment treats absence of the Diario itself as sufficient for the absolute statutory presumption. The appeal challenges that reasoning and the factual premise.

These are **appeal/party positions** until the appellate court decides them or the underlying native records close the factual questions.

## 10. Knowing overstatement / self-serving effect

There is a strong documentary basis for saying the AC's broad accounting rhetoric was **overinclusive**:

- he knew substantial accounting had been supplied;
- he quoted that fact himself;
- he nevertheless used broad language that can read as though accounting/documentation was simply absent.

But the strongest current formulation is not `the AC invented the accounting breach`. The court accepted a narrower statutory-book failure.

### Current knowledge grade

- **AC knew journals/trial balances had been delivered:** VERIFIED by his own report.
- **AC knew there was some continuing accounting:** VERIFIED by his own report.
- **AC could reasonably distinguish unlegalised/non-statutory material from a complete statutory Diario:** YES; this is the actual legal dispute.
- **AC knowingly lied that no accounting existed:** NOT ESTABLISHED as a global proposition; his report itself records the contrary material.
- **AC knowingly overstated the evidential picture by collapsing formal defects/missing items into broader absence language:** STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED ACCOUNTABILITY PROPOSITION, subject to contextual reading of each sentence.

### Self-serving effect

The framing shifted attention from the quality, completeness and actual information content of what the AC possessed to a binary `books/no books` culpability structure. That effect is demonstrable. Deliberate motive to manufacture culpability is not yet proved.

## 11. Attribution and period control

Gil became administrator in January 2012. The judgment itself attributes the late-filing cause based on 2010/2011 conditions to the previous administrator, not Gil.

For Allegation 04, the court deliberately focuses on the **during-concurso** accounting period. Therefore:

- do not attribute 2008–2011 historical bookkeeping defects wholesale to Gil;
- do not use pre-2012 legalisation defects as if they were the accepted basis of his accounting culpability;
- identify the exact post-6-Jun-2012 period for which a Diario is said to be missing;
- identify who was preparing the accounting in each year, what was generated, what was sent, what the AC received, and what the AC was required to sign/intervene in.

## 14. Strongest public wording

### Spanish

> **Alegación 04 — contabilidad: el expediente no permite decir honestamente que LPB «no tuviera contabilidad».** La propia AC escribió que la sociedad siguió contabilizando en 2012 y que recibió en PDF diarios contables y balances de sumas y saldos de 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011 y parte de 2012. La Sentencia 163/2023 también descarta que la mera falta de legalización de los libros anteriores al concurso baste por sí sola. Sin embargo, mantiene un pronunciamiento adverso distinto: considera que durante el concurso no quedó aportado el **Libro Diario** exigido por el Código de Comercio, sino balances de comprobación y cuentas anuales, y por esa ausencia declara probado el incumplimiento sustancial del deber de llevar la contabilidad. Ese pronunciamiento está recurrido. La cuestión real no es «contabilidad sí/no», sino **qué Libro Diario existía para cada ejercicio, qué recibió realmente la AC y qué información adicional era imposible conocer con los registros que sí tenía**. La presunción separada por falta de formulación de cuentas anuales fue desestimada, y no hemos localizado una acusación probada de doble contabilidad o falsificación contable.

### English

> **Allegation 04 — accounting: the record does not honestly support saying that LPB simply “had no accounting”.** The AC itself wrote that the company continued accounting in 2012 and that it received PDF accounting journals and trial balances for 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011 and part of 2012. Judgment 163/2023 also rejects the proposition that mere non-legalisation of the pre-insolvency books is enough by itself. It nevertheless makes a different adverse finding: during the insolvency the statutory **Daily Journal (Libro Diario)** was not established as supplied, only checking balances and annual accounts, and on that basis the court found a substantial accounting breach. That finding is on appeal. The real question is not “accounts/no accounts”; it is **which statutory Diario existed for each year, what the AC actually received, and what material information remained impossible to know from the records it did possess**. The separate annual-accounts presumption was dismissed, and no proved double-accounting or falsified-accounting finding has been located.
