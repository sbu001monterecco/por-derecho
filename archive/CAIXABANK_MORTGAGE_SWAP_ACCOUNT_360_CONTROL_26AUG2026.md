## 1. Controlling attributed allegation

Gil Marer and the reserved declarant allege that the banking relationship must not be fragmented into an apparently independent first mortgage, swap, second mortgage, pledged deposit, current account and enforcement file. Their position is that Caja Insular de Ahorros de Canarias / La Caja de Canarias designed or required an interdependent package; the second facility absorbed or secured swap-related exposure; the lender controlled the relevant account circuit and application of receipts; Bankia then treated the package as being in default and accelerated/enforced it immediately after principal grace; and that sequence became the immediate documented trigger for LPB's defensive insolvency filing.

They allege lender-side liability should be tested across Caja Insular, BFA, Bankia and CaixaBank, with the later mortgage-credit chain analysed separately. This is a **party allegation and legal-recovery theory**, not an adjudicated finding that any entity acted unlawfully or is jointly liable for all later losses.

## 3. Swap mechanics: the stronger allegation and its limits

### 3.1 Net-difference formula

The source statement's constant `3.50% + 4.85% = 8.35%` formulation is incomplete because LPB received Euribor 3m under the swap. In a low-rate period subject to the 4.85% customer leg:

`combined covered cost ≈ mortgage rate + (4.85% − E3m)`.

If the mortgage floor fixed the mortgage rate at 3.50%:

| E3m | Approximate swap net outflow | Approximate combined rate on €5m covered amount |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.00% | 3.85% | 7.35% |
| 0.50% | 4.35% | 7.85% |
| 0.00% | 4.85% | 8.35% |

Earlier structured periods used 5.15%, increasing those examples by 0.30 points. Every historical figure must use the actual reset date, E3m fixing, customer leg, mortgage E12m fixing, floor, loan balance and swap notional.

### 3.2 Double-floor / no-cap contention

The technically stronger claimant contention is not that 8.35% applied constantly. It is that:

1. the 3.50% mortgage floor prevented LPB from receiving the full benefit of falling E12m;
2. the swap simultaneously required a fixed structured leg while LPB received falling E3m;
3. the €5m notional therefore generated negative settlements exactly when the floor was already holding the loan rate up;
4. above the 5.55% barrier, the swap appears to give only about 0.15 points of relief rather than a hard maximum rate; and
5. E12m/E3m mismatch, partial notional and short tenor introduced basis, amount and duration gaps.

That architecture supports a serious allegation of an asymmetric and potentially ineffective hedge. It does not, without the precontractual record and full cashflow model, establish deception, invalidity or recoverable damages.

### 3.3 TIN / TAE boundary

- TIN is the nominal contractual rate; TAE is a regulated annual-equivalent measure derived from specified cashflows and assumptions. It is not simply TIN plus every linked product.
- The 2010 second deed states **TAE 3.717%**.
- An exact first-loan TAE is not responsibly stated from the present public record.
- The litigation should also model an ex-post all-in cost including loan interest/floor, swap net settlements, fees, pledged-fund opportunity cost and refinancing cost, while avoiding the misleading label TAE unless calculated under the applicable rules.

## 4. Corrections and non-negotiable controls

1. **Sales arithmetic and recovered ledger:** the voice account's €55,000 × 2 is €110,000, not €210,000. The recovered LPB ledger instead records €60,000 + €59,000 = **€119,000 gross** on 31-Jan/4-Feb-2011, with €100,584.80 posted to first principal and €10,559 to second. Preserve both layers; the ledger is not the native deeds/statements.
2. **Gross is not net or annual coverage:** neither €110,000 nor €119,000 establishes net cash or payment of every annual loan/swap obligation.
3. **Grace covered principal:** the second deed now located fixes the first principal repayment at 1-Jan-2012. It did not eliminate interest or swap obligations.
4. **Acceleration tension:** a 29-Nov-2011 Bankia burofax demanded the full €839,441 capital before the first scheduled principal instalment; the later filing signed 10-Jan and received 23-Jan alleges unpaid principal amortisation from 7-Jul-2011, while the deed schedules first principal amortisation for 1-Jan-2012. Obtain proof of notice, monthly certificate and contractual trigger. The tension is serious but not alone dispositive if interest arrears permitted acceleration.
5. **€19,500 account assertion:** preserve as attributed and unlocated. The available ledger's 30-Sep balance is approximately –€99,758.80. Different dates mean neither controls 9-Dec; obtain the native statement and prove availability.
6. **Pledge was not shown untouched:** the ledger records material movements during 2011. No public assertion that the bank simply seized an intact €405,000 at execution is permitted until every movement and set-off is reconciled.
7. **Knowledge of falling rates:** no evidence proves a secret Banco de España advance warning or June knowledge of the later collapse. Public chronology includes an ECB rise in July and cuts beginning in October 2008.
9. **Partial notional/term:** relevant to efficacy and suitability, but not proof of improper purpose by itself.
10. **Account control:** mandate, pledge, set-off and payment-imputation rights must be reconstructed; control is not wrongdoing by itself.
11. **Succession:** CaixaBank's universal corporate succession to Bankia is officially documented. Merits liability, earlier Caja/BFA allocation, prescription, causal loss and quantum remain contested. Later mortgage-credit assignees do not automatically inherit every upstream personal/product liability.
12. **Immediate trigger is not sole cause:** enforcement is the immediate documented insolvency trigger; Community conflict, liquidity, advice, business decisions and later insolvency conduct remain potential concurrent causes.

Use one ledger but separate loss windows:

`origination/product cost → account application → alleged default/acceleration → execution/auction threat → defensive concurso → concursal credit/assignments → possession/title/income events → current recovery`.
