## Decision B — `001-0113069` / portal `00001-00113069`

**Subject:** administrative traceability and handling of a Ley 2/2023 communication, reference `RGE449073832025`.

### What the request asked

The transparency request was expressly limited to administrative traceability, metadata and handling criteria. It requested, with redaction where necessary:

- date/time and registration mapping;
- receiving/competent units and processing chain;
- the administrative classification or act adopted;
- the legal criterion used;
- internal notes/checklists/resolutions/templates used in handling;
- any referral/re-routing and the document effecting it;
- the applicable channel-scope, triage/admission and retention/traceability protocols.

It expressly disclaimed access to the substance of any ongoing investigation or third-party personal data.

### What AEAT now expressly admits

AEAT states that:

- on **5 December 2025 at 13:27**, `RGE449073832025` **entered AEAT's internal information channel under Ley 2/2023**;
- on **12 December 2025**, the communication was **sent to the AEAT Special Delegation in the Canary Islands**;
- AEAT says it did so because it classified the material as a **public tax complaint under article 114 LGT**, outside the competence of the internal channel.

The resolution first states **`CONCEDE PARCIALMENTE`** (partial access granted), but later the operative section says **`DENEGAR a trámite la solicitud`** for the remaining access. Preserve that drafting tension; do not report this as a clean total refusal.

### Controlled English summary

> AEAT partially discloses the administrative routing of the 5 December 2025 Ley 2/2023 communication. It confirms receipt in the internal whistleblowing channel at 13:27 and a transfer on 12 December to the Special Delegation in the Canary Islands, after classifying the material as an article 114 General Tax Law public complaint. AEAT then refuses further access on the basis that tax-complaint follow-up is subject to the special confidentiality regime of articles 114 and 95 LGT.

This proves receipt and routing. It does **not** prove the merits or outcome of any tax investigation, nor that the classification/re-routing was legally correct.

## Current legal-response analysis

### 1. The refusal is broader than the current CTBG doctrine

On 26 January 2026 the Consejo de Transparencia y Buen Gobierno issued **R CTBG 0072/2026** (AEAT / Ministerio de Hacienda). That decision states, in substance, that:

- article 95 LGT creates a **partial**, not absolute, specific access regime;
- article 114 LGT does **not** itself constitute a specific transparency-access regime;
- the fact that a tax complainant is not an interested party is not, by itself, decisive for LTAIBG access;
- article 114's rule against informing a complainant of the *results* of tax actions does not automatically bar a transparency request;
- if a complaint was archived, the complainant may have access to reports/acts leading to archive, excluding third-party tax data;
- if derived proceedings have finished, access can extend to information other than third-party tax data;
- if investigation is still pending, access can be restricted to protect the ongoing proceedings.

Official CTBG source:
`https://consejodetransparencia.es/content/dam/ctransparencia/portal-ctbg/reclamaciones/nuestras-resoluciones/resoluciones-%C3%A1mbito-estatal/2026/01--enero/ESTIMADAS/R_CTBG_0072-2026_Resolucion%20Expte.1496-2024__Censurado.pdf`

That 2026 authority is directly relevant to AEAT's 16 September 2026 blanket use of articles 114/95 and should be put before CTBG.

### 2. Partial-access and item-by-item treatment remain important

Article 16 LTAIBG requires partial access when a limit affects only part of the requested information, unless the remainder would be distorted or meaningless. The requests expressly sought administrative routing metadata and public/general channel protocols, not third-party tax merits.

Official text:
`https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2013-12887#a16`

### 3. The time taken is procedurally material

The decisions themselves say the access requests reached AEAT's access unit on **5 January 2026** and were not resolved until **16 September 2026**. Article 20.1 LTAIBG sets a one-month decision period, extendable once by another month for volume/complexity with prior notice. Preserve the delay as a procedural ground/context; do not overstate its automatic legal consequence.

Official text:
`https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2013-12887#a20`

### 4. Ley 2/2023 creates a separate channel-governance question

Ley 2/2023 article 13 requires public investigative bodies to distinguish at least between:
- an internal channel for wrongdoing of the body/its staff; and
- an external channel for third-party infringements within the body's investigative competence.

AEAT itself publicly maintains a **Ley 2/2023 external channel** for tax-related information obtained in a work/professional context, handled by its Inspection Department. Whether either original communication qualified for that route must be decided from the original source and context; it is **not** established merely by today's resolutions.

Official AEAT sources:
- `https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/procedimientos/ZZ55.shtml`
- `https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/procedimientos/ZZ30.shtml`
