## Controlling conclusion

The liquidation baseline was not one self-contained document. It was a documentary chain:

1. the AC report under the former Article 75 LC, dated 15 January 2013, including the underlying inventory and creditor analysis;
2. the AC filing of the **textos definitivos**, dated 28 April 2016 and electronically signed on 29 April 2016;
3. the separate definitive creditor-list annex presented with that filing;
4. the 8 February 2018 order substituting Construcciones Acosta Matos, S.A. for Promontoria Holding 122, B.V. as holder of the same two specially privileged credits, without enlarging the recognised quantum merely because of the assignment;
5. the 2018 liquidation plan, which expressly based the assets to be realised on the inventory annexed to the AC report **elevado a definitivo**;
6. the later judicial, notarial, Registry and accounting implementation chain, which must still be reconciled separately.

Accordingly, the 2018 plan must not be read as creating a new inventory from scratch, and the 2016 special-privilege classification figure must not automatically be relabelled as the amount payable, extinguished, realised or used as consideration in 2022.

### Definitive specially privileged creditor entries

The separate 2016 creditor list records Promontoria Holding 122, B.V., as successor to Bankia, with:

- €857,373.81 — mortgage loan no. 2801;
- €8,194,877.88 — mortgage loan no. 30000;
- contingent, unquantified mortgage-enforcement costs.

The two quantified entries total **€9,052,251.69**. The 8 February 2018 order recognises CAM as holder of those same quantified entries. It does not, merely through assignment, convert mortgage caps, later accruals, better-bid thresholds or every amount stated in a later deed into the definitive recognised concursal credit.

## Asset perimeter used by the liquidation plan

The liquidation plan expressly states that the assets to be realised were those in the inventory annexed to the AC report and elevated to definitive. It identifies LPB as owner of:

- 159 apartments;
- 29 premises / locales;
- two pool-and-solarium properties.

That inventory concerns LPB. It does not by itself absorb Matkator or other third-party property into the insolvency estate, nor does it establish whole-hotel title or authority over every operational/common-area right.

The plan also distinguishes:

- the 2016 definitive real-estate inventory valuation of approximately **€15.375m**; and
- an updated 2018 appraisal allocation of **€7,096,913.83** to LPB's identified real-estate assets.

Inventory value, updated appraisal, mortgage responsibility, classified credit, payable debt, better-bid threshold, debt used as non-cash consideration, cash received and actual realisation proceeds are separate quantities.

## Internal inconsistency / traceability register

The 2016 filing contains apparent internal drafting or transposition inconsistencies. They are evidence-quality and certification questions, not proof of misconduct:

1. The principal tables give active mass **€19,486,498.94**, passive mass **€10,125,752.00** and claims against the estate **€304,779.42**.
2. A later paragraph abruptly states active **€2,310,880.99**, passive **€1,940,402.06** and claims against the estate **€94,701.53**.
3. The same paragraph refers to an Article 75 report dated **1 February 2011**, whereas the controlled LPB report is dated **15 January 2013**.
4. The filing is dated 28 April 2016 but the electronic-signature metadata is 29 April 2016.
5. The summary table and separate definitive creditor list differ by three cents in ordinary and subordinated totals.

No repository page may silently choose one side of these discrepancies. The complete certified filing package, annexes, court receipt/index and any correcting resolution must control.
