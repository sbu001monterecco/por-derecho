## 1. Executive finding

The Pink Canary problem is not one disputed proposition. It is a sequence of changing legal and factual descriptions that must be placed on one timeline.

Across the record, Monterecco/Pink is variously treated as:

1. a real hotel operator / “new operator”;
2. an operator with an official 159-unit / 477-bed perimeter;
3. a connected tenant/debtor of LPB;
4. an alleged non-paying debtor whose conduct was used in insolvency-causation reasoning;
5. a declared accomplice in the surviving rent branch of Sentencia 163/2023;
6. an operator later compressed in an adverse criminal-archive decision into a conditional “would have abandoned” narrative;
7. an entity that, according to the underlying contemporaneous source, had stopped new commercialisation while still servicing existing stays, supplies, maintenance and an orderly exit;
8. an historical exploiter/operator relevant to later tax and AEAT narratives;
9. a component of a €3,032,010.34 culpability/damages outcome that is still invoked in September 2026 even when Pink is not named expressly.

The prosecutorial question is whether these descriptions can be reconciled chronologically and legally, or whether different actors selected and propagated different versions for different procedural/economic purposes without confronting the contradictory parts of the same record.

This is a narrative-propagation and knowledge inquiry. It is not a finding of criminal instrumentalisation.

## 4. Narrative problem map

### N1 — Identity: Monterecco vs Pink

**Documented:** Monterecco Sun Park, S.L. and Pink Canary Services, S.L. are successive registered names of the same Spanish legal person, NIF B76564517.

**Problematic narrative to prohibit:** treating the 2013 name change as a transfer, successor operator or new debtor.

**Prosecutorial significance:** identity errors can manufacture false discontinuities in contract, payment, tax, litigation or operator history.

**Status:** resolved identity; exact notarial/registry name-change completion remains a source-completion point.

---

### N2 — “New operator” vs authority/scope

**Documented adverse source:** the 1-February-2012 CEXP–Monterecco notice exists and presents Monterecco as the new operator / subrogated activity.

**Also documented:** tourism material identifies a 159-unit / 477-bed operating perimeter.

**Open problem:** no located competent-organ resolution yet proves that CEXP could convey a universal collective mandate, every owner’s rights, dissenters’ rights, third-party rights or all common areas.

**Narrative issue:** “Pink was a real operator” is supportable; “Pink necessarily held lawful authority over the whole hotel” is not presently established.

**Prosecutorial test:** who later relied on operator status as if it were universal title/authority, and what source did they possess?

---

### N3 — 159 units / 477 beds vs whole-hotel shorthand

**Documented:** official/administrative operating baseline of 159 units / 477 places.

**Problem:** later descriptions often concern “Sun Park” as one hotel. The economically integrated hotel and the legally divided titles are not the same legal perimeter.

**Prosecutorial test:** identify each transition in which a 159-unit operating fact became a statement about the entire hotel, whole-site authority, whole-site income or whole-site possession.

---

### N4 — JV 1260/2011 / AP 89/2014: adverse possession evidence vs expansion of scope

**Adverse evidence:** AP 89/2014 found possession/operation affecting the 18 claimant units and relied on key/relationship evidence.

**Limit:** it did not adjudicate LPB whole-hotel title, universal CEXP invalidity, Pink universal succession, Matkator ownership, third-party estate inclusion or later CAM authority.

**Narrative issue:** an adverse possessory result can become a portable proposition if later actors treat it as resolving much more than the 18-unit dispute.

**Prosecutorial test:** every later citation or functional reliance must be decomposed into the exact AP89 proposition actually adjudicated.

---

### N5 — “Pink abandoned the hotel” vs the underlying contemporaneous operating facts

**Adverse source:** Auto 804/2018 used conditional language summarising an earlier communication as Pink having “abandoned” the installations.

**Underlying source as reconstructed on the public site:** Pink had stopped new commercialisation but was still dealing with prior stays, supplies, services, maintenance and an orderly exit; the original source said Pink still had not delivered keys and remained the entity “taking care of everything.”

**Later evidence:** a facially signed 22-May-2017 Pink→LPB document states delivery of “keys of access to the Sun Park complex,” which defeats a literal assertion that Pink never had/delivered any physical keys, while leaving master/maintenance keys and authority unresolved.

**Narrative issue:** “commercial wind-down,” “residual operation,” “formal key delivery,” “possession,” and “lawful power to transfer the whole hotel” are separate propositions.

**Prosecutorial test:** did a compressed “abandonment” narrative later enable or legitimise access/control steps that depended on a stronger proposition than the source supported?
