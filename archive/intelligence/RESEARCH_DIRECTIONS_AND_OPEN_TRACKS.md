## Canary Islands / Las Palmas / Gran Canaria ecosystem

A major research direction is whether Las Palmas / Gran Canaria / the Canary Islands present a distinctive institutional, economic or legal ecosystem compared with other Spanish autonomous communities. This is a **research question, not an established conclusion**.

Future work should test, with comparative evidence:

- concentration and repeat-player effects in insolvency, property, banking/NPL, professional-adviser and tourism matters;
- size of the local legal/professional ecosystem and frequency of recurring relationships;
- judicial/prosecutorial workload, specialisation and institutional design;
- patterns of referrals, archives, jurisdictional fragmentation and effective judicial protection;
- public-administration, tourism, tax/RIC, planning and professional-regulatory overlaps;
- whether Canary Islands island geography and market concentration create risks or protections materially different from Madrid, Catalonia, Valencia, Andalusia, Balearics or other autonomous communities;
- whether any apparent pattern is actually explained by ordinary specialisation, workload, lawful professional networks or small-market effects.

The phrase **“ground zero”** has been used internally as a research framing for Las Palmas/Gran Canaria. It should not be published as a finding of systemic wrongdoing without comparative evidence.

## NPL / banking / servicing ecosystem

An explicit research rail is the Spanish post-financial-crisis NPL/servicing chain and how it intersects with the LPB/Sun Park credit history.

Priority names/themes include:

- Bankia;
- SAREB;
- Meridian;
- Promontoria / PH122 / Cerberus perimeter;
- loan servicing and delegated administration;
- legal/economic ownership versus registered/procedural standing;
- portfolio transfers, servicing mandates, data custody and enforcement authority;
- how large NPL portfolios move from banks/public restructuring vehicles through funds/servicers to local purchasers or enforcement beneficiaries.

Do not infer a specific transfer or mandate merely from sector practice. Build the LPB chain from primary contractual, notarial, registry, accounting and procedural records, then use sector research as context.

## The “one asset” consequence analysis

For every material act affecting Sun Park, ask beyond the immediate legal step:

1. Where was the underlying asset/right held before and after the act?
2. Who gained or lost title, control, income, leverage, procedural standing, information or bargaining power?
3. What first-order consequence followed?
4. What second-order consequence did that enable?
5. What later institution or private actor relied on the resulting state as an input or premise?
6. Did a later formal act depend on an earlier unresolved premise?
7. What would the counterfactual history have been if the earlier premise had been tested differently?
8. Which consequences affected LPB, Aweswell, Matkator, minority/private owners, creditors, public authorities, investors, guests/operators and later counterparties differently?

The purpose is to resist artificial fragmentation while respecting distinct legal rights and proceedings.

## US / external-scrutiny pathway

The originating work considered strong US attention as a possible source of independent scrutiny, technology, legal-tech expertise, compute credits, media interest and commercial support. Preserve the useful part of that idea without nationalist or saviour framing.

The defensible proposition is:

> Can external AI, legal-tech, academic and entrepreneurial resources help reconstruct complex legal histories, expose missing context and improve access to justice while preserving source provenance and human/legal review?

Potential audiences include AI companies, law schools, legal-tech founders, podcasts, investigative journalists, philanthropists, cloud/compute providers and strategic investors.

## Book structure / “underworld” and structural ecosystem

The book should give more than a single incidental chapter to the **underlying economic/institutional ecosystem** that makes the case intelligible: NPLs, servicing, insolvency, local professional networks, tourism/property economics, public incentives and the interaction of formal and informal control.

“Underworld” is a working metaphor, not a factual label for identified people. The published treatment should describe documented structures, incentives and relationships and distinguish lawful market architecture from alleged misuse.
