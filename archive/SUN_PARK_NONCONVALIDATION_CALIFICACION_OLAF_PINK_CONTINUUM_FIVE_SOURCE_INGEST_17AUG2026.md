## 4. Source-by-source digest

### FS-05 — 16 January 2019: contemporaneous opposition to the 28-Nov-2018 disposal

FS-05 is important because it fixes what Gil's side was telling the Mercantile Court **before** the later 24-Oct-2019 order resolved the convalidation issue.

The pleading:

- identifies the 4th liquidation report as the immediate trigger;
- argues that during suspension the AC's duty was preservation rather than further disposal;
- invokes the approved liquidation architecture and alleged requirement of unitary disposal;
- identifies the LPB property perimeter as approximately 73% of Sun Park and characterises it as a unitary tourism-exploitation object;
- reproduces the AC's earlier report language that it was awaiting lifting of the 26-Jun-2018 suspension before continuing liquidation operations;
- then reproduces the 4th report's statement that the AC had sold the `locales` and `piscinas con solárium` on 28-Nov-2018 to CAM for **€400,000** and had used receipt of the cheque to pay AC fees and mass-credit claims;
- alleges an `ob rem` linkage and says separate disposition was legally impossible;
- alleges that CAM had been given possession/control beyond title and asks the court to correct what the pleading characterises as incomplete or misleading information;
- records that the 12-Dec-2018 court-office step came **after** the sale said to have occurred on 28-Nov-2018; and
- opposes the reported liquidation operations as contrary to the suspended plan.

#### What FS-05 directly proves

- A detailed challenge to the 28-Nov-2018 sale/disposal was placed before the Mercantile Court in January 2019.
- The challenge expressly raised unitary-sale, suspension, `ob rem`, €400,000 proceeds, possession and chronology issues.
- The pleading therefore predates the 24-Oct-2019 judicial ruling and is a material **notice / knowledge-routing source**.

#### What it does not prove

- that the deed was automatically void or criminal;
- that the AC intentionally concealed information;
- that CAM had whole-hotel legal possession;
- that the alleged `ob rem` consequences were exactly as characterised by the pleading;
- that the €400,000 was misappropriated or unreconciled.

For underlying legal/procedural facts, the later signed court and Registry records control.

### FS-02 — 2018–2021 traceability annex: stronger primary-document chain

The annex's own front page is deliberately restrained: it says its limited purpose is **not** to assert as already proved partiality, intimate friendship, crime, illicit payment or disciplinary liability. Its central verification question is what acts neutralised the contractual, registry, possessory, physical and economic effects of the 28-Nov-2018 non-convalidated deed before the May-2021 tender, and what equivalent information bidders received.

Its chronological table and embedded sources establish or reproduce the following sequence:

#### 26 June 2018 — partial suspension

The signed order literally suspended realisation of a specified list of mortgaged fincas while leaving the rest of the liquidation pronouncements unchanged and allowing liquidation to continue for other estate assets.

**Control:** do not rewrite this order as though it expressly suspended every asset. The later 24-Oct-2019 order itself is the stronger source explaining why the earlier partial approach was legally erroneous in light of the unitary-sale architecture.

#### 17–22 October 2018 — AC obtains court testimonios

The AC requested testimony of the 16-Apr and 26-Jun-2018 orders; the court office ordered delivery. The request did not state its purpose.

**Control:** receipt of testimony is a procedural fact. Do not infer concealment or improper intended use merely from the request.

#### 28 November 2018 — deed of desvinculación / sale

The annex records the public deed concerning the locals/pools/solarium in favour of CAM.

#### 9 January 2019 — Registry qualification

The available Registry material suspended in part the inscription/cancellation of the `ob rem` linkage for want of sufficient judicial resolution.

#### 14/16 January 2019 — Fiscalía/court notice

The DI 248 expansion and FS-05 court opposition put the disposal and related objections into two different institutional lanes in January 2019.

#### 7 May 2019 — DI 248 archive

The Fiscalía archive relied, among other points, on the general premise that the insolvency administrator acted under the direct supervision of the Mercantile Court. The current DI-248 contradiction ledger separately controls the institutional circularity issue and the identity of the prosecutors involved.

#### 24 October 2019 — decisive signed court ruling

This is the critical primary anchor. The signed Auto states, in substance, that:

- the AC's separate sale of the relevant fincas under the 28-Nov-2018 deed contradicted the liquidation plan architecture and the Provincial Court's interpretation;
- the June-2018 partial-suspension approach had been erroneous because the approved disposal was unitary and the appeal should have paralysed both mortgaged and non-mortgaged components;
- the court **could not convalidate** the deed;
- the operation had been carried out **without the court's authorisation** and the court was informed only after it had been consummated; and
- the operation breached the approved plan and was to be left without effect if a better third-party offer were authorised.

The dispositive part expressly:

1. leaves the 12-Dec-2018 Diligencia without effect;
2. refuses convalidation of the 28-Nov-2018 deed; and
3. reopens the better-offer/publicity route on the terms established.

#### What the 24-Oct-2019 order does **not** decide

It does not, by itself:

- declare a crime;
- establish corrupt coordination between the AC, CAM or judge;
- finally trace the €400,000;
- prove that every contractual/physical/registry effect of the deed persisted afterward;
- establish final civil liability for works or possession.

Those are distinct questions.

#### 24 February 2021 — court knowledge of works / condition and denial of inspection/pericial

The signed order records Aweswell's allegation that relevant fincas had been demolished / reduced to rubble, the request for an expert report and judicial inspection, and the claimed inability to enter because security prevented access.

The order also records the AC's position that:

- the AC had not delivered possession of LPB assets to CAM;
- CAM had acquired many apartments from third parties and therefore had access as another owner;
- the AC had asked the Community and CAM about the works; and
- no potential bidder had requested inspection after the sale notice.

CAM's position included that it owned 54 apartments and that works were confined to its own units / Community-authorised safety actions.

The court itself records that photographs showed:

- interior works whose ownership location was not determined;
- rubble in common zones; and
- works in common zones that had been the object of the isolated sale the court had not convalidated.

The court denied the requested judicial inspection and expert valuation, treated conservation as an AC function, and expressly observed that a request of that kind could make sense in a possible responsibility action against CAM or the AC, while denying it in the ongoing liquidation context.

**High-value knowledge point:** by 24-Feb-2021, the signed judicial record itself demonstrates that the court was dealing with a dispute about the physical condition/works in the common-area perimeter linked to the non-convalidated sale.

**Boundary:** the order did **not** find CAM or the AC liable and did not adopt Gil/Aweswell's full factual characterisation.

#### 12 May 2021 — later tender includes the same locals/pools

The signed providencia opened bidding among interested offerors for LPB's listed fincas and expressly included the `locales` and `piscinas con solárium` with registry numbers 8.508–8.536, 8.653 and 8.654, scheduling the tender for 18-May-2021. It fixed Ottawa's bond at **€735,694.02**.

This is a strong later-status anchor: the court was again tendering the same property perimeter as property of LPB after the 2018 deed had not been convalidated.

#### The precise open traceability question

The bundle supports a stronger and narrower question than saying merely that the 2018 transaction was “illegal”:

> **After the 24-Oct-2019 refusal to convalidate the 28-Nov-2018 deed, what specific acts reconciled or reversed every contractual, registry, accounting, possession, works and €400,000 consequence before the same property perimeter was tendered again in May 2021, and what did bidders receive about that history and physical condition?**

That question is finite and document-testable.

Open evidence remains:

- the exact final filed version of Annex 5;
- complete finca-by-finca Registry history;
- bank/accounting trail of the €400,000 and any refund/set-off/reconciliation;
- executed act(s) reversing or regularising the 2018 deed and possession consequences;
- complete tender pack / bidder disclosures;
- 18-May-2021 tender record and subsequent October-2021 / January-2022 acts;
- final 2022 deed/title bridge.

FS-03 is historically important because it shows that the **same-asset / parallel-finance theory was already being put to an EU anti-fraud channel by January 2025**.

It alleges, among other things:

### Critical correction / current evidence boundary

The repository's 2026 multiple-financial-lives controls are **stronger and more precise** than the 2025 alert because they now distinguish verified project-specific anchors from broader historical allegations.

Accordingly:

- the fact that FTI received Bavarian COVID support does **not** by itself prove that those funds financed Sun Park;
- the fact that Meeting Point/related entities received SEPI support does **not** by itself prove a €31m or other drawdown was applied to Sun Park;
- a public rescue at group level is not automatically collateralised by, or traceable into, this hotel;
- the 7-Jan-2025 allegation that the same Sun Park asset/income supported those rescue lines remains **PROJECT/PARTY ALLEGATION requiring transaction-level source-and-use evidence**;
- terms such as `triple dipping`, `blanqueo`, `fraude`, `malversación` or criminal collusion are not upgraded by the alert itself.

### What remains strong and current

The OLAF filing is still valuable as:

1. **notice chronology** — an EU/public-funds concern was formally articulated by Jan-2025;
2. **lead generation** — it identifies FTI/Meeting Point rescue and commercialisation records worth tracing;
3. **continuity evidence** — the later 2026 same-asset allegation did not arise only after the RIC/regional/FEDER layers were reconstructed; and
4. **adverse-testing input** — the current repository must separate any older overbroad inference from what the later native evidence actually proves.

The current controlled same-asset chain therefore remains:
