## Purpose

This control prevents an unfair or evidentially flattened description of how the Administrador Concursal, **Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte**, came to be admitted as a witness in the pending Valencia banking proceeding:

## 1. Primary contemporaneous counsel evidence — verified

In a direct email dated **30 October 2024**, sent immediately after the Audiencia Previa, Javier Sixto reported:

> “Se admitió la testifical de Borja, a petición de Caixabank, a la cual nos adherimos.”

Source control: Gmail message ID `192de7e1c6b3837f`, Javier Sixto, subject `RE: Valencia y próxima videollamada`, 30-Oct-2024.

The same email separately records that the request for an oficio to CaixaBank and the Administración Concursal had not been admitted and would be challenged.

### Canonical factual proposition

The evidence supports this sequence:

1. **CaixaBank requested/proposed Borja's witness evidence.**
2. **Aweswell's lawyers adhered to CaixaBank's request.**
3. Later court citations use the formal wording `testigo propuesto por la parte actora y demandada`.

The third proposition describes the final procedural posture after adhesion. It must not erase the first two propositions or be rewritten as if Aweswell and CaixaBank independently, equally or simultaneously selected Borja as their own witness.

## 2. Correct treatment of “both parties”

### Unsafe / superseded shorthand

- “Both parties asked for the AC.”
- “Both parties independently proposed Borja.”
- “Aweswell chose Borja as its own witness” without explaining the prior CaixaBank request and Aweswell's adhesion.

### Controlled wording

Use:

> **CaixaBank requested/proposed the Administrador Concursal, Francisco de Borja Rodríguez-Batllori Laffitte, as a witness. Aweswell's counsel then adhered to that request. Later court citations therefore record him procedurally as a witness proposed by claimant and defendant. That formal both-sides wording must not be mistaken for evidence of equal or independent sponsorship.**

Short form:

> **Borja's testimony was requested by CaixaBank; Aweswell adhered.**

## 3. Why Aweswell adhered — evidence-status boundary

Gil Marer's present account is that Aweswell's lawyers considered that they had **no sensible procedural choice**, did not want to create procedural risk or prejudice by refusing to adhere, and felt effectively compelled to accept the witness position once CaixaBank had requested Borja.

That explanation is important strategic context, but the present refresh has **not located a contemporaneous lawyer email using the literal formulations “no choice”, “coerced”, or an equivalent express statement that procedural risk compelled the adhesion**.

Accordingly:

- **VERIFIED FACT:** CaixaBank requested Borja; Aweswell adhered.
- **VERIFIED FACT:** later court citations record the witness as proposed by both claimant and defendant.
- **CLIENT-REPORTED PROCEDURAL CONTEXT:** the adhesion was defensive, undertaken to avoid procedural risk/prejudice rather than because Aweswell affirmatively wanted Borja as its own independent witness; the client describes the position as effectively compelled/coerced.
- **OPEN EVIDENTIAL TARGET:** locate any contemporaneous lawyer communication, note, pleading or hearing record expressly explaining the procedural-risk rationale for adhesion.

Do not silently upgrade the client-reported rationale into a documentary fact. Equally, do not omit it when the question is why the court record later says both sides proposed the witness.
