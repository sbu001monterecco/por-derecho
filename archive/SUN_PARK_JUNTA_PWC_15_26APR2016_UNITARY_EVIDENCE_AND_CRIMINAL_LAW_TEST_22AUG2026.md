## Contemporaneous lawful explanations that must be tested

- An Insolvency Administrator could object to a new estate expense where liquidity, necessity and fees had not been established.
- Article 15.2 of the Horizontal Property Act provided that an owner with overdue Community debts could deliberate but not vote, unless the debt had been challenged judicially or deposited judicially/notarially.
- The email therefore contains facially legal explanations. Their presence is not conclusive if a false premise, excess of authority, improper purpose or deliberate patrimonial consequence is later proved.

Routes such as coercion, corporate offences or misconduct by a public official should not be pleaded merely because the conduct was forceful or occurred within insolvency proceedings. Their subject, means and protected-interest requirements need separate proof and may not fit an Insolvency Administrator or an owners’ Community.

## Separation / removal / extraconcursal perimeter

The former Insolvency Act recognised an inventory of the debtor’s estate and a separate right to recover third-party property held by the debtor. The later repository record treats LPB, Matkator, Aweswell, the Community, the operating entity and individual owners as distinct. Any demand or application for removal of the Insolvency Administrator must be searched separately from a claim for separation of third-party assets; the words “separación del AC” and “derecho de separación de bienes” are not interchangeable.
