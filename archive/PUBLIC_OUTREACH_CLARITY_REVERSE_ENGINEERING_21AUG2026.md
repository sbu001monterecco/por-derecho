# PUBLIC OUTREACH CLARITY — REVERSE-ENGINEERING CONTROL

**Control date:** 21 August 2026  
**Repository:** `sbu001monterecco/por-derecho`  
**Status:** ACTIVE PUBLICATION / OUTREACH CONTROL  
**Scope:** worker/informant invitations, correction routes and professional/commercial collaboration boundaries. This control does not alter the substantive evidential dossiers or convert any allegation into a finding.

## 1. Why this control exists

A reverse-engineering pass of current `main`, the public-facing worker letters, the collaboration page, recent repository handovers, recent institutional email traffic and the controlling conversation identified a specific asymmetry:

- the substantive repository has become progressively stronger at source-status classification, actor-by-actor attribution, non-conflation and finite-document questions;
- the public invitation layer still contains some older advocacy language that can make ordinary employees, suppliers or commercial partners feel that relationship itself is being treated as evidence of wrongdoing;
- protected reporting, factual correction and commercial/professional collaboration are not yet separated as clearly as the evidential method requires.

The remedy is not to weaken the recovery or accountability thesis. It is to make the entry point reflect the same discipline already required in the evidence layer.

## 2. Controlling public rules

### Rule A — relationship is not responsibility

Employment, former employment, investment, financing, professional advice, supply, contracting, franchising, partnership or another commercial relationship with a named organisation does not by itself establish participation, knowledge, intent, benefit or responsibility.

Any responsibility must be tested:

`person → act → date → capacity/authority → knowledge → instruction/authorship → use/reliance → prejudice → benefit → causation → correction/continuation after notice`.

### Rule B — four equally legitimate responses

The public invitation is not "support our case". It is:

`CLARIFY → CORRECT → CONFIRM → REPORT`

Evidence that narrows or defeats a Por Derecho inference has the same methodological status as evidence that confirms it. Corrections should be date-stamped, source-linked and scoped rather than silently overwritten.

### Rule C — no requirement to contact Por Derecho

A person with information about a possible infringement may use an official or protected reporting route directly. The website should not imply that Por Derecho is the necessary intermediary, gatekeeper or evidential destination.

### Rule D — protected reporting is separate from collaboration

`PROTECTED / OFFICIAL REPORTING ≠ EDITORIAL COLLABORATION ≠ PROFESSIONAL COLLABORATION ≠ FUTURE HOTEL / ASSET / INVESTMENT CONVERSATION`.

The collaboration page may remain a useful professional gateway, but it must expressly state that it is not a protected informant channel and that future commercial discussions are separate from factual reports or corrections.

### Rule E — ordinary email is not a secure evidence vault

Public ordinary email should not be described or implied as a protected whistleblower intake route. Do not invite sensitive originals, unlawfully obtained material, trade-secret dumps, databases or material requiring protected chain of custody through ordinary first-contact email.

### Rule F — status verbs must remain separate

For institutional and professional communications preserve:

`SENT ≠ DELIVERED ≠ RECEIVED ≠ ACKNOWLEDGED ≠ JOINED TO FILE ≠ ADMITTED ≠ INVESTIGATED ≠ ACCEPTED ≠ ENDORSED ≠ PROVED`.

Recent repository/email practice already follows this rule and it governs future public text.

### Rule G — source-derived statements do not replace primary proof

A recollection, declaration, transcript, visual index, derivative chronology or later email may support notice, chronology, source discovery or a question. It does not replace the primary judicial, administrative, corporate, banking, accounting, technical or native record needed to prove the underlying proposition.

### Rule H — emotive collective language stays out of staff-facing entry points

Terms such as "innocent staff", "born in sin / nacido en pecado", "come forward" and "do not destroy the truth" can create an unnecessary implication of collective suspicion when used at the first-contact layer. They may remain in historical or attributed advocacy context where material, but they should not govern the worker/informant gateway.

Preferred vocabulary:

- current/former staff;
- people professionally connected to an entity mentioned here;
- clarify / correct / confirm / report;
- preserve lawfully held information without alteration;
- use an appropriate lawful or protected route.

## 3. Immediate selective implementation

The public clarity layer applies only to:

- `/es/carta-abierta-trabajadores-acosta-matos/`;
- `/en/open-letter-workers-acosta-matos/`;
- `/es/carta-abierta-trabajadores-mynd-yaiza/`;
- `/en/open-letter-workers-mynd-yaiza/`;
- `/es/colaborar/`;
- `/en/collaborate/`.

It does four things:

1. replaces the Acosta worker-letter opening request to "not destroy the truth" with neutral lawful-preservation and correction language;
2. adds a prominent `RELATIONSHIP IS NOT RESPONSIBILITY / RELACIÓN NO ES RESPONSABILIDAD` rule to all four worker routes;
3. replaces the MYND worker-letter "born in sin" explanation with the controlling actor-specific attribution rule;
4. separates the collaboration/future-business gateway from protected reporting and warns against sending sensitive originals through ordinary email.

It also adds a correction-first block near the end of each worker letter.

## 4. Why this supports the recovery/accountability objectives

This change strengthens rather than dilutes the substantive case because it:

- reduces guilt-by-association risk around employees, suppliers, franchises, hotel partners, investors and advisers;
- increases the credibility of actor-specific allegations by making the non-attribution rule visible before the reader reaches them;
- improves evidential quality by welcoming contradiction and reducing pressure to align with a pre-existing narrative;
- reduces testimony-contamination risk by treating a factual correction as valuable in its own right;
- keeps official/protected reporting independent from Por Derecho's commercial and professional collaboration interests;
- makes later institutional use of the website easier because the site states what each relationship does **not** prove;
- creates a reusable publication rule for future perimeter pages without having to add more accusatory invitations.

## 5. Supersession / historical-language rule

Earlier advocacy communications may contain more categorical formulations of fraud, criminal conduct, regulatory failure, concealment or collective responsibility. Those communications remain historical evidence of what was alleged or communicated at the time. They are **not** the controlling drafting standard for current public publication.

Current publication must classify propositions as appropriate, including:

- documented fact;
- source-derived statement;
- party allegation;
- inference;
- open question;
- missing primary bridge;
- institutional receipt/routing status;
- adjudicated finding, only where the competent decision actually establishes it.

## 6. Non-derogation

Nothing in this control withdraws Por Derecho's right to make serious, attributed allegations where the evidential basis and public-interest rationale justify them. It requires those allegations to remain bounded by the actor, act, source and legal status actually proved.

The objective remains full lawful recovery, restitution, accounting and damages where available, together with preservation, investigation and accountability. Clarity about uninvolved people and unproven bridges makes that objective more credible, not less.
