## 3. Complete controlled Spanish reconstruction

### 3.1 Citación judicial

Una citación judicial emitida en Arrecife en septiembre de 2014 identifica el número 3205/2014 ante el Juzgado de Instrucción nº 1 de Arrecife y lo rotula como «Juicio de faltas». Distingue a una persona denunciante descrita en las fuentes como presidenta de la Comunidad, a una persona perjudicada descrita como administrador, y a dos personas denunciadas: Gil Marer y una segunda persona cuya identidad se mantiene omitida. Las instrucciones normalizadas indican que la persona citada debe comparecer e identificarse, puede acudir asistida de letrado aunque no sea preceptivo, debe aportar los medios de prueba de que pretenda valerse y, en caso de una eventual multa, documentación económica pertinente. La citación no prueba que la vista se celebrara ni contiene sentencia, declaración de culpabilidad, archivo o firmeza.

### 3.2 Denuncia policial

La denuncia policial registra que, el 3 de septiembre de 2014, una persona descrita como presidenta de una comunidad de propietarios de apartamentos denunció una histórica falta de lesiones e identificó como perjudicado a una persona descrita como administrador.

Según su declaración, ambos acudieron al complejo hotelero en las calidades que alegaban de presidenta y administrador para realizar trabajos no especificados. Manifestó que encontraron cambiado el mecanismo de acceso a una zona a la que pretendían entrar. Después de llamar sin obtener respuesta, apareció un hombre que se identificó como turista y consiguieron acceder. Afirmó que el hombre los invitó a hablar con otra persona y que, tras invocar ella su condición de presidenta, les pidió que se marcharan.

La denunciante dijo que se negó a abandonar el complejo y llamó a la Guardia Civil para solicitar mediación. Mientras esperaban, afirmó que revisaron apartamentos de propietarios y dejaron una llave en la puerta de una unidad concreta. Alegó que el hombre que se había identificado como turista, y que se comunicaba frecuentemente por teléfono móvil, intentó llevarse la llave, que ella trató de impedirlo y que se produjo un forcejeo. Dijo que el hombre la sujetó fuertemente del brazo y posiblemente le dio un codazo en la espalda. Afirmó que, al intervenir para que la soltara, el administrador sufrió primero una luxación del pulgar izquierdo y después un empujón. El formulario también registra que la denunciante describió sus propias lesiones como leves. Son alegaciones de la declarante, no constataciones médicas.

La denunciante atribuyó el conflicto a que propietarios que consideraba verdaderos no podían acceder a sus inmuebles por causa de Gil Marer, a quien describió como explotador de parte del complejo. Preguntada por la relación entre Gil Marer y el presunto autor físico, contestó únicamente que podría ser una persona relacionada con él. La denuncia no atribuye a Gil presencia en el incidente, una instrucción, una relación de agencia ni contacto físico.

La denunciante dijo que su marido no había presenciado la agresión en sí, aunque sí el resto de los hechos y el conflicto anterior. La Guardia Civil llegó después de los hechos e identificó a las partes; el documento no dice que los agentes presenciaran el contacto físico. Añadió que, después, había recibido una llamada desde un número omitido para intentar alcanzar un acuerdo relativo al conflicto sobre las viviendas. La denuncia no identifica a Gil Marer como autor ni destinatario de esa llamada.

## 4. Full controlled English translation

### 4.1 Court summons

A court summons issued in Arrecife in September 2014 identifies case number 3205/2014 before Arrecife Investigating Court No. 1 and captions it as a `Juicio de faltas`, or minor-offence trial. It distinguishes a complainant described in the sources as Community president, an affected party described as administrator, and two reported respondents: Gil Marer and a second person whose identity remains withheld. It summoned Gil Marer to an October 2014 oral hearing concerning the historical `falta de lesiones` classification.

The standard instructions state that the summoned person must attend and identify himself, may be assisted by counsel although counsel is not mandatory, must bring the evidence on which he intends to rely and, if a fine might be imposed, relevant financial material. The summons does not prove that the hearing occurred and contains no judgment, finding of guilt, dismissal or finality.

### 4.2 Police complaint

The police complaint records that, on 3 September 2014, a person described as president of an apartment owners' community reported a historical minor-injury offence and identified a person described as administrator as the affected party.

According to her statement, they attended the hotel complex in their claimed capacities as president and administrator to carry out unspecified work. She said they found the access mechanism to an area they intended to enter had been changed. After calls went unanswered, a man appeared, identified himself as a tourist and they obtained entry. She said he invited them to speak with another person and, after she invoked her claimed status as president, told them to leave.

The complainant said she refused to leave and called the Guardia Civil to seek mediation. While they waited, she said they inspected owners' apartments and left a key in the door of a specified unit. She alleged that the man who had identified himself as a tourist, and who was frequently communicating by mobile telephone, tried to take the key, that she tried to stop him and that a struggle followed. She said the man held her tightly by the arm and possibly elbowed her in the back. She said that, when the administrator intervened to make him release her, the administrator first suffered a dislocation of the left thumb and was then pushed. The form also records that the complainant described her own injuries as mild. These are the declarant's allegations, not medical findings.

The complainant attributed the conflict to owners whom she regarded as the true owners being unable to access their property because of Gil Marer, whom she described as operating part of the complex. Asked about the relationship between Gil Marer and the alleged physical actor, she said only that the person might be related to him. The complaint attributes no presence at the incident, instruction, agency relationship or physical contact to Gil.

The complainant said her husband had not witnessed the alleged assault itself, although he had witnessed the rest of the events and the preceding conflict. The Guardia Civil arrived after the events and identified the parties; the document does not say the officers witnessed physical contact. She added that she had later received a call from an omitted number in an attempt to reach agreement concerning the housing dispute. The complaint does not identify Gil Marer as the caller or recipient of that call.

## 6. Why the two Community-capacity actors said they were there

The contemporaneous complaint supplies the clearest stated reason: they said they attended as president and administrator to carry out unspecified work. It also records that, once inside, they inspected owners' apartments. A later account by the source-reported president says she was seeking access to property belonging to relatives of her husband; that later account is a candidate incident bridge, not a formally proved DP3205 source.

A regulatory and safety complaint bearing a 27 August 2014 date requests inspections under claimed Community authority. The located copy was officially received after 3 September 2014. Its internal date therefore cannot be used to prove that it was filed, received or acted upon before the incident. It may still supply context for a genuine regulatory concern or for an attributed pressure hypothesis, but it proves neither motive.

The located 2011 appointment material is historical authority evidence only. It does not establish a date-effective office, mandate, owner consent, work order or right to enter on 3 September 2014. A disputed Community record dated 28 August 2014 is contrary governance context, not a certified determination of authority.

## 7. Meaning of the reported tourist and physical altercation

The phrase “identified himself as a tourist” is a statement reported by the complainant, not a booking or guest-registration record. No incident-date booking, accommodation registration, employment record, agency instruction or exact capacity proof has been located in the public control.

If genuine guest status were proved, it would provide an ordinary independent reason for the person's presence and would not by itself make him Gil's employee, security provider, agent or co-participant. If an operational role were proved, instruction, scope of authority and agency would still require separate evidence. Guest and operational capacities are not logically exclusive, but neither is established here for the incident date.

The complaint places the altercation within an access, possession and key dispute, not a documented random encounter. That makes authority to enter, authority to inspect units, the request to leave, control of the key, protection of guests and property, mutual or defensive force, and the hotel's response legitimate questions. It does not answer them.

## 8. Account comparison and allegations against the Community-capacity actors

The primary complaint presents the president and administrator as complainant and affected party. A later account by the president describes the alleged actor as a person who said he worked for Gil. A 2016 participant transcript appears to attribute the assault more broadly to Gil or his client and expands the group said to have been assaulted. Those later accounts may reflect dual capacity, colloquial attribution or compressed memory; they are not formally linked to DP3205 and must not be fused with the primary complaint.

Gil Marer's contemporaneous counteraccount alleges forced access, aggression against the on-site manager and a later reversal of the facts in the complaint. It is a materially adverse attributed allegation, not a finding. The referenced manager account and recording have not been located in this recovery set.

The combined evidence supports a narrow inquiry into whether Community titles were validly held and used, whether access or inspection exceeded any mandate, who controlled the key, who initiated or lawfully responded to physical contact, and whether later accounts were accurate. It does not establish false accusation, harassment, arbitrary self-help, abuse of process, agreement, shadow administration, assault, defence, civil liability or criminal responsibility.
