## Controlling simulation premise

The most institutionally plausible useful outcome is **not** that the Comisión Permanente declares judicial prevaricación, corruption, bias or the legal invalidity of the insolvency adjudication. Those are not conclusions this simulation asks the CGPJ to make.

The stronger institutional question is whether the original complaint and appeal can properly be treated as nothing more than disagreement with judicial rulings when they also raise a separable question of:

- protection of the insolvency estate (*masa activa*);
- supervision of the Insolvency Administrator;
- court functioning and traceability;
- incorporation/examination of material submitted to DI 169/2026 / Alzada 286/2026;
- attribution of LAJ/court-office acts;
- and a separate, finite appearance-of-impartiality module.

## Simulated visitor reaction

A disciplined institutional reader is expected to begin with the adverse record:

- DI 169/2026 archived on 14 May 2026;
- the Promotor maintained the archive on 10 July 2026;
- appeal or later filing does not equal reversal, acceptance or truth.

The reader room is designed so that a member can disagree with every Por Derecho inference and still use the site productively. The intended reaction is therefore not “accept the allegations,” but:

> Which questions are truly jurisdictional merits, which concern supervision/functioning, which concern the office/LAJ, which are disciplinary, which are criminal, and which official records would resolve each one?

## Stronger post-scan simulated outcome

After restoring *masa activa* and Administrator supervision as the spine, the strongest institutionally plausible reasoned outcome became:

> Maintain the prohibition on using the disciplinary route to revisit the legal merits of judicial rulings, while recognising that the originating complaint raised an autonomous question of judicial supervision and court functioning that should be differentiated from jurisdictional disagreement; require an indexed examination of the seven supervisory nuclei; route any genuine functioning aspects to the competent Inspection lane; treat the LPAM/abstention module separately; separate LAJ/court-office matters; and leave criminal qualification exclusively to the competent prosecution/court route.

## Model of a reasoned institutional resolution

The simulated resolution would, in substance, contain the following components:

1. **Jurisdictional-merits boundary preserved.** The CGPJ does not recalculate debt, annul adjudication, decide title, substitute the Audiencia Provincial or determine criminal liability.

2. **Archive does not establish merits.** Confirmation of a disciplinary archive is not a finding that all disputed insolvency acts were substantively correct and is not a merits determination in separate civil, insolvency or criminal proceedings.

3. **Administrative traceability.** Identify which submissions and annexes were actually incorporated and examined in DI 169/2026 / Alzada 286/2026, distinguishing filing/routing from substantive examination.

4. **Supervision/functioning classification.** Determine whether any of the seven nuclei contains a separable court-functioning or supervision question capable of treatment within the competent CGPJ/Inspection framework without revisiting judicial merits.

5. **Insolvency Administrator supervision.** Reconstruct what the Administrator knew, what was reported, what the Court required and what alternative supervisory control existed where protective measures or removal were refused or deferred.

6. **Office/LAJ separation.** Route testimony, finality, certification, notification or other court-office matters to the legally competent framework rather than attributing them automatically to the Judge.

7. **Appearance of impartiality kept discrete.** If finite, individualised allegations are within scope and not already examined, decide whether the official checks proposed are relevant or unnecessary, without treating the allegations as proven.

8. **Criminal route reserved.** No CGPJ determination of prevaricación or other offences; criminal relevance remains for the competent prosecution/judicial bodies.
