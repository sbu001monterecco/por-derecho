## 2. What Control 22 actually alleged

The 18 June complaint did **not** say that every irregularity, omission, adverse decision or poor outcome was criminal. Its core provisional theory was possible **administración desleal**, and its method was already structurally sound:

`facultad encomendada → información/conocimiento verificables → actuación/decisión/omisión de medida razonablemente disponible → perjuicio patrimonial → eventual ventaja correlativa para terceros`

The complaint asked whether the AC, holding court-supervised entrusted powers over the debtor estate, knowingly exercised, withheld or failed to correct those powers in a manner that caused patrimonial harm or loss of protection/value while producing a corresponding advantage for other actors.

The complaint also expressly preserved the distinction between:

- error / disagreement / ordinary insolvency discretion;
- negligence or poor administration;
- and a knowing disloyal exercise or excess of entrusted powers capable of satisfying the criminal threshold.

That restraint remains necessary.

The criminal threshold still requires, act by act:

1. the precise entrusted power/duty;
2. actual contemporaneous knowledge;
3. breach/excess or knowing failure to use a legally required/available protective power;
4. identifiable patrimonial damage or loss of an estate right/value;
5. causal connection;
6. the required subjective element; and
7. rebuttal of a rational, documented insolvency-estate justification.

The complaint is strongest where those seven links can be joined by primary documents rather than by retrospective interpretation.

### Active + passive/deferential pattern

The strongest unitary judicial theory is not simply that LPB lost cases. It is that a repeated pattern must be tested in which:

- the court **actively** recognised CAM and rejected LPB's Article 1535 theory;
- the court **deferred** to AC verification for the CAM substitution rather than itself requiring all documentary material LPB sought;
- later supervision questions arose over possession/control, suspended or non-convalidated acts, restoration and estate preservation;
- a later CAM-centred competition/adjudication path became difficult to reverse externally; and
- the record must establish what the judge knew at each point, what supervisory/legal options were available, what was actually required, and what neutralisation or independent verification occurred.

### Critical counter-record

The 15 February 2018 order also corrected amounts in LPB's favour. Judicial reliance on an insolvency administrator can be lawful. The court may have had procedural/jurisdictional reasons for rejecting requested measures or for treating different applications differently.

That counter-record is not a weakness to hide. It is necessary to distinguish **possible selective non-neutral supervision** from the false proposition that every judicial act favoured CAM.

### What would be required for penal promotion

A judge-specific criminal case must identify:

- the exact resolution or omission;
- the complete record before the judge then;
- the governing law and actual judicial duty/power;
- why the result was objectively unjust or manifestly unjust under the relevant offence;
- the knowledge / grave-negligence / malicious-delay element required by the specific provision;
- the causal consequence; and
- why ordinary judicial discretion, error, lawful reliance on the AC or missing evidence cannot explain the act.

Control 24 must therefore remain **resolution-specific**, even when the wider pattern is used as context.
