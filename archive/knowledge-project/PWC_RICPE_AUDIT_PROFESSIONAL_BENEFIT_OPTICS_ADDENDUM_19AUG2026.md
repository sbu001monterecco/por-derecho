## Purpose

This addendum records a material two-direction rescan of the PwC / Carlos Saavedra / RIC Private Equity / Acosta Matos relationship. It must be read with:

- `archive/knowledge-project/PWC_CARLOS_SAAVEDRA_2016_SUN_PARK_UNITARY_EVIDENCE_GATE_19AUG2026.md`;

- the canonical RICPE / multiple-financial-lives and public-funds controls;
- `archive/CORRECTION_REGISTER.md` and `archive/MISSING_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md`.

The new issue is not whether PwC had some later proximity to RICPE or Grupo Acosta Matos. Several independent layers of later professional convergence are now documented. The question is whether that convergence is merely ordinary, separately mandated professional activity or whether native engagement, conflict, independence, information-access, fee and work-product records establish a causal bridge from the earlier Aweswell/Sun Park mandate or omissions to later professional benefit or participation.

## Controlling vocabulary

Do **not** state as fact that PwC or Carlos Saavedra was "rewarded" for failing Aweswell, that a quid pro quo existed, or that PwC audited/validated the Sun Park project or the RICPE brochure.

Use instead:

- **VERIFIED FACT:** later professional/institutional convergence or relationship, where source-supported;
- **INFERENCE / OPTICAL PROFESSIONAL-BENEFIT PATTERN:** the chronology creates an appearance capable of justifying a conflict/reward inquiry;
- **OPEN QUESTION:** whether later engagements, fees, appointments or opportunities were causally connected to earlier omissions, information or assistance;
- **NOT ESTABLISHED:** quid pro quo, corrupt reward, knowing facilitation, misuse of confidential information, criminal complicity.

A later professional opportunity is not a reward unless the evidence establishes the required causal/intentional bridge.

# 1. Critical correction: the 4-Jun-2020 RICPE Sun Park brochure itself names PwC as auditor

The preserved 26-page `INVERSORES_Folleto_1 RIC CAPITAL.pdf`, dated **4 June 2020**, is the same RICPE investor-brochure source family that identifies the **Sun Park / Construcciones Acosta Matos / Hotel en Playa Blanca, Lanzarote** project.

On page 2 the brochure states:

- depositary: **Cecabank, S.A.**;
- auditor: **PricewaterhouseCoopers Auditores, S.L.**

This is an important institutional-assurance fact because the PwC name appears **on the face of the investor document in which Sun Park/CAM appears**.

### Mandatory correction

This does **not** establish that PricewaterhouseCoopers Auditores, S.L. audited, reviewed, approved or validated:

- the brochure itself;
- the Sun Park project;
- CAM's title or availability;
- the €12m investment narrative;
- the webinar;
- any legal/fiscal due diligence.

The brochure's identification of the statutory auditor is a service-provider disclosure, not a project-level assurance opinion unless a separate source proves one.

### CNMV audit-year reconciliation

The current official CNMV audit register for RIC Private Equity records:

- **FY2019 — PricewaterhouseCoopers Auditores, S.L.**
- **FY2020 onward in the relevant register years — EUDITA AH Auditores 1986**.

Therefore do **not** write `PwC was RICPE's FY2020 statutory auditor` merely because the 4-Jun-2020 brochure names PwC as `Auditor`.

The correct proposition is:

> The Sun Park-containing RICPE investor brochure dated 4 June 2020 identified PricewaterhouseCoopers Auditores, S.L. as RICPE's auditor. The current CNMV register records PwC as auditor for FY2019 and Eudita for FY2020. The date, reason and governance of that auditor transition, and what RICPE projects/related parties were known during PwC's client-acceptance/audit/closing period, require reconstruction.

This transition is a new **OPEN QUESTION / EVIDENCE TARGET**, not evidence of wrongdoing.

# 2. Forward reconstruction — earlier Aweswell knowledge to later PwC convergence

## 2015–2016 — former-client / prior-knowledge baseline

The controlling 2016 gate establishes detailed Carlos Saavedra/PwC Tenerife knowledge of Sun Park's ownership, private-unit, Community, exploitation, debt/voting, access, insolvency and governance architecture. PwC professionals represented specific private-owner/unit interests and Carlos had direct substantive dealings with FMMM.

This is the knowledge baseline. It does not prove any later adverse mandate.

## 2019 — PwC Auditores enters RICPE's institutional perimeter

The official audit record places **PricewaterhouseCoopers Auditores, S.L.** as RICPE's auditor for FY2019, RICPE's first registered period. This is a **different PwC legal entity** from Landwell/PwC Tax & Legal and must remain legally separate.

Its significance is institutional, not automatically informational: obtain the 2019 audit engagement/client-acceptance/independence file to establish what connected parties, board members, projects and risks were identified and whether any PwC-network conflict/independence systems surfaced the Aweswell/LPB/Matkator/Sun Park history.

## 4 June 2020 — investor brochure: Sun Park and PwC auditor disclosure coexist

The same investor-brochure source family contains both:

- the PwC auditor disclosure; and
- the Sun Park / CAM hotel-project entry.

This creates a stronger **optical institutional-assurance link** than mere later conference attendance, while still not proving project review by PwC.

## November 2020 — PwC Canarias appears publicly in RICPE's professional ecosystem

Contemporaneous Canary media identifies **Jorge Gutiérrez, Director del Área Legal y Fiscal de PwC Canarias**, participating in a RICPE professional/investor event alongside RICPE and other hotel/investment actors. This establishes PwC Canarias's public legal/fiscal presence in the RICPE ecosystem.

It does not establish that Jorge Gutiérrez or PwC Canarias reviewed Sun Park, CAM title, the brochure, the webinar or any RICPE investment decision.

## 11 November 2020 — Sun Park is publicly identifiable, not hidden in an anonymous project

The preserved RICPE webinar material identifies José Acosta as Director General of Construcciones Acosta Matos and Consejero of RIC Private Equity and presents the former **Aparthotel Sun Park in Playa Blanca**, approximately 220 tourist apartments, as a project to be converted to a four-star hotel with an investment narrative of approximately €12m.

For Carlos and the 2016 PwC team, `Sun Park` was already a highly specific former-client asset. This does not prove they watched the webinar or received the brochure, but it eliminates any analytical need to treat the project name itself as obscure or unrecognisable if later shown to them.

## March–April 2021 — explicit PwC Network notice checkpoint

This is now one of the strongest control dates.

Direct Gmail evidence establishes:

- **1 Mar 2021:** Polly Miles, PwC UK Legal / Regulatory & Commercial Disputes, states after a call that she escalated Gil Marer's concerns to PwC's **Head of Risk Management**;
- **4 Mar 2021:** Polly states she escalated the matter to the `appropriate persons within the business`;
- **5 Mar 2021:** Pablo Fernández Guerra, **Deputy General Counsel of PwC Spain**, states that he received information **from PwC Network** about the complaint concerning practices in the Canary Islands / Sun Park Living; Carlos del Cubo, PwC Spain Chief Compliance Officer, is copied;
- **8 Mar 2021:** the Sun Park side sent PwC Spain/UK a package including RICPE web/publication material and the January-2021 CNMV investor-fraud complaint;
- March/April 2021 calendar material records `Call SUN PARK` meetings; a later invite references a possible recording `Call SUN PARK (2021-03-10 at 08:32 GMT-8).mp4`, still requiring recovery/authentication.

### Consequence

Whatever internal informational separation may have existed before March 2021, the PwC Network's UK Legal/Risk and Spain General Counsel/Compliance functions were **expressly on notice of the Sun Park/RICPE complaint by March 2021**.

Do not infer automatically that Carlos Saavedra or PwC Canarias received that internal escalation. The decisive question becomes: **where did the escalation go, which PwC entities/partners were notified, what conflict/independence checks were run, and what action or no-action decision was recorded?**

## Later joint RICPE / PwC / MYND event — Carlos visible in the later ecosystem

Publicly indexed event material identifies an **RIC Private Equity and PwC event at MYND Hotels** concerning the Canary hotel sector and names **Carlos Saavedra** among participants together with other PwC/RICPE/hotel actors.

This is VERIFIED public-event proximity, not proof of a Sun Park mandate, confidential-information use or paid CAM/RICPE engagement.

## Later PwC collaboration with Grupo Acosta Matos — actual collaboration at project level, scope separate from Sun Park

A public professional exchange concerning a separate Grupo Acosta Matos hotel project contains a PwC professional thanking José Acosta for giving **PwC the opportunity to collaborate** in the success for Grupo Acosta Matos and Canarias.

This moves the record beyond event co-attendance: **some later PwC professional collaboration with Grupo Acosta Matos is publicly acknowledged**.

Do not attribute that engagement to Carlos or Sun Park without the engagement file.

## 2026 — formal Carlos / Acosta governance adjacency

Official BORME material for **Socios Inversores Canarios, S.L.** records:

- Grupo Acosta Matos, S.L. as a board member/entity;
- José Daniel Acosta Matos as its representative;
- Carlos Gabriel Saavedra Rodríguez del Palacio as **secretario no consejero**.

This proves a current formal professional/governance adjacency. It does **not** prove compensation, causal reward, a prior Sun Park mandate, or any impropriety.

# 4. The controlled `reward` hypothesis

## INFERENCE / optical pattern

The chronology can reasonably create the appearance that:

1. PwC/Landwell was positioned to understand and potentially help protect the Aweswell/Sun Park perimeter before CAM/RICPE obtained the later economic position;
2. the protective response is alleged by Aweswell to have been inadequate or incomplete;
3. a separate PwC entity then entered RICPE's assurance perimeter at inception;
4. the Sun Park-containing investor brochure carried the PwC auditor name;
5. PwC Canarias later entered RICPE's visible professional ecosystem;
6. PwC Network risk/legal/compliance later received explicit warning about Sun Park/RICPE;
7. Carlos and PwC later appear in continuing professional proximity to RICPE/MYND/Acosta; and
8. some later PwC collaboration with Grupo Acosta Matos and a current Carlos governance role alongside the Acosta perimeter are documented.

This supports investigation of whether PwC later received **professional opportunity, fees, appointments, referrals or governance roles** from a perimeter that benefited from the loss/displacement alleged by the former client.

## NOT ESTABLISHED

It does **not** currently establish:

- that any later engagement was consideration for an earlier omission;
- a promise, agreement, referral or quid pro quo;
- that Carlos personally earned fees from CAM/RICPE/HNT/MYND on Sun Park;
- that PwC caused CAM to obtain Sun Park;
- that PwC Auditores shared its RICPE file with Carlos/Landwell or vice versa;
- that the statutory audit validated Sun Park title or investor claims;
- that post-2021 PwC engagements ignored an identified internal conflict warning;
- knowing assistance, concealment or criminal complicity.

Use `post-omission professional convergence` or `professional-benefit optics` until the causal bridge is evidenced.

# 5. Highest-yield `reward bridge` evidence

Future searches must prioritise:

1. **PwC Auditores / RICPE FY2019 audit engagement:** engagement letter, acceptance/continuance, independence/conflict search, related-party identification, audit plan, board minutes, project/pipeline materials, subsequent-events work, completion file and resignation/replacement records.
2. **Auditor transition:** corporate resolution and acceptance/termination records showing when and why PwC ceased and Eudita became FY2020 auditor; reconcile that with the 4-Jun-2020 brochure still naming PwC.
3. **RICPE brochure production:** drafts, approval chain, legal review, service-provider confirmations, whether PwC was asked to consent to or verify its identification, and whether PwC received the final brochure.
4. **Exact PwC references in RICPE webinar/publications:** authenticate whether PwC was described as a legal/fiscal filter for Sun Park or RICPE generally; identify speaker, wording, source and scope.
5. **March–April 2021 internal PwC file:** risk escalation, General Counsel/Compliance file, call recording/notes, recipients, internal referrals, conflict/independence alerts, decisions and follow-up.
6. **PwC Canarias/Carlos notification:** establish whether Carlos, Jorge Gutiérrez, Miguel Hernández, Elena Santos or other earlier Sun Park professionals were told of the 2021 complaint or later RICPE/CAM engagements.
7. **Later Acosta/RICPE/HNT/MYND engagement files:** contracting PwC legal entity, scope, client/connected parties, matter code, opening/closing dates, team, fees, time entries, work product, conflict and information-barrier records.
8. **Economic benefit to relevant professionals:** billing/fee records, referrals, appointments, governance remuneration or other value; distinguish ordinary market work from any causal reward.
9. **DMS/access evidence:** whether later teams had actual access to prior Aweswell/LPB/Matkator/Sun Park files and whether information barriers were created or breached.
10. **Causal bridge:** any communication linking prior Aweswell work/omission, knowledge or assistance to a later PwC appointment, fee, project approval, audit position or Acosta/RICPE benefit.

A negative result on items 6–10 would materially weaken a culpability/reward theory even if the optical chronology remains striking.

PwC may be able to establish that:

- PwC Auditores and Landwell/PwC Tax & Legal were separate legal entities, teams and files;
- the FY2019 statutory audit concerned RICPE financial statements, not Sun Park title/DD;

- Eudita's FY2020 appointment shows PwC did not audit the project year in question;
- public RICPE/PwC events were general professional/market events, not Sun Park mandates;
- later Acosta work concerned separate hotels and unrelated issues;
- Carlos's 2026 company-secretary role is temporally and substantively independent;
- the March-2021 complaint was assessed by Risk/Legal/Compliance and no conflict or actionable PwC involvement was found;
- earlier Aweswell information was not accessible or material to later work;
- any conflict was identified, disclosed and managed appropriately.

Future analysis must seek native evidence supporting these explanations, not merely contrary evidence.

# 7. Controlling conclusion

The two-direction rescan supports an upgraded but carefully bounded proposition:

> **The PwC chronology is no longer adequately described as an isolated 2016 former-client episode followed by unrelated later appearances. The surviving record shows: deep earlier Carlos/PwC Sun Park knowledge; a separate PwC entity in RICPE's initial statutory-audit perimeter; the PwC auditor name on the face of the June-2020 RICPE investor brochure in which Sun Park/CAM appeared; PwC Canarias's later public participation in the RICPE ecosystem; explicit March-2021 PwC Network Risk/General Counsel/Compliance notice of the Sun Park/RICPE complaint; later Carlos participation in an RICPE/PwC/MYND professional environment; publicly acknowledged later PwC collaboration with Grupo Acosta Matos on another hotel project; and a 2026 formal Carlos governance role in a company whose board includes Grupo Acosta Matos.**
>
> **That sequence supports a serious former-client, conflict, independence, professional-benefit and possible participation inquiry. It does not, without engagement/fee/conflict/access/causation evidence, prove that PwC was rewarded for earlier omissions, that any later opportunity was a quid pro quo, that PwC audited or validated the Sun Park project, or that Carlos/PwC was criminally complicit.**

The decisive investigative transition is from **optics** to **causal proof**: who engaged whom, when, for what, for how much, after which internal conflict/notice event, with access to which former-client information, producing what work product, and causing what decision or benefit.
