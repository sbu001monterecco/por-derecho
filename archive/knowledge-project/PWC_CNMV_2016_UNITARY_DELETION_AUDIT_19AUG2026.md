## 1. PwC ↔ CNMV: the correct transparency question

The Por Derecho record must **not** state that CNMV contacted PwC, that PwC contacted CNMV, or that PwC had a legally established duty to report to CNMV unless primary evidence establishes the particular proposition.

The controlled position is:

- **VERIFIED FACT:** CNMV supervised RIC Private Equity within the relevant regulated perimeter. The preserved CNMV response does not establish contact with PwC or an auditor.
- **OPEN QUESTION:** did CNMV ever contact any PwC legal entity or professional concerning RICPE, Sun Park or related investor/disclosure matters?
- **OPEN QUESTION:** did any PwC legal entity or professional proactively contact, report, escalate or seek guidance from CNMV concerning RICPE, Sun Park, same-asset/former-client information, independence, conflict, investor-protection or disclosure concerns?
- **OPEN QUESTION:** if contact occurred, which legal entity/person, when, through what channel, under what reference and concerning what subject?
- **OPEN QUESTION:** if no contact occurred, what governance/compliance assessment, if any, considered whether contact or escalation was warranted?

Do not convert those questions into an assertion that a statutory reporting duty existed without separate legal research.

## 2. Lawful game-theory / repeated-game strategy

The objective is not threat, embarrassment, coercion or reputational quid pro quo. It is to create a **cooperation equilibrium** in which early preservation, independent review and verifiable answers are PwC's rational lower-risk strategy.

### Cooperation dividend offered to PwC

A constructive institutional response can consist of:

1. confirmation of legal hold / preservation across the relevant PwC entities and former personnel;
2. designation of one senior Madrid-level institutional contact and reference;
3. independent review separated from the professionals whose historic conduct is being examined;
4. finite yes/no or date/reference answers on former-client, conflict, KYC, independence, information-barrier and CNMV-contact questions;
5. identification of the PwC legal entity responsible for each relevant engagement, without requiring privileged advice to be disclosed;
6. a privilege/confidentiality-safe route for material that cannot properly be made public;
7. correction of any Por Derecho factual error, with equivalent prominence.

### Reciprocal Por Derecho commitment

If PwC cooperates, Por Derecho should accurately record that cooperation; narrow or close open questions as evidence resolves them; distinguish privilege from avoidable opacity; publish substantiated corrections/replies with equivalent prominence; and continue to avoid prejudging liability.

### Non-cooperation consequence — factual, not coercive

If finite questions remain unanswered, Por Derecho may accurately record that they were put and remain unanswered and may submit the same documentary questions to competent independent institutions. It must not threaten adverse publicity in exchange for cooperation and must not imply that silence proves wrongdoing.

### Repeated-game / prisoner's-dilemma matrix

- **PwC cooperates + institutions cooperate:** uncertainty contracts fastest; PwC supplies its own contemporaneous context and controls the accuracy of its factual account.
- **PwC cooperates + other actors remain silent:** PwC obtains the strongest first-mover integrity position because its preservation and review steps are independently visible.
- **PwC remains silent + independent institutions later produce records:** PwC faces the weakest informational position because the chronology may be reconstructed from third-party records without PwC's contextual explanation.
- **All remain silent:** uncertainty persists; preservation, transparency and documentary-production requests continue. Silence remains silence, not proof of culpability.

The strategic goal is to make **truthful early cooperation the dominant low-risk choice**, not to manufacture fear.

## 4. 26 April 2016 act — controlling meeting facts

The signed act records a General Ordinary Meeting of the Sun Park Owners' Community on **26 April 2016**. Before business began, the administrator asked whether the meeting could be recorded and the act states that all present consented without exception. A security guard was present at the president's proposal. Juan Carlos Prieto Puente was presented as the Community's lawyer and Leopoldo Soler Cullen as its tax/accounting adviser.

The act records **89.727%** of participation present or represented. It separately records only eight owners, totalling **11.039%**, as entitled to vote after the asserted current-payment/arrears check.

The act records, among other representations:

- Elena Santos Ramos representing Víctor Fernández Cox / Local 1;
- Miguel Hernández Lorenzo representing Juan Carlos Fernández Cox / bungalow 507;
- Carlos Saavedra Rodríguez representing Simon Peter Thompson / bungalow 708;
- Patricia Domínguez representing LPB and Mariano Palacios Fernández;
- Francisco Matos Matas representing Francisco Luis Molina Molina;
- Antonio Cogolludo Rojas representing Inversiones Salinetas S.L.

LPB itself was recorded with **72.976%** participation. The safe formulation is therefore:

> The act records 89.727% of participation present or represented, but only 11.039% as vote-eligible under the arrears/payment filter applied at the meeting. LPB alone was recorded with 72.976% and was outside that voting pool.

Do **not** publicly substitute phrases such as “hijacked organ”, “illegal disenfranchisement” or “fabricated debt” for the documentary facts unless a competent finding or a carefully attributed allegation supports the characterisation.

### Specific April act points

- Accounts for 2010–2015 were brought to the meeting despite the administrator stating the available information was incomplete. Miguel Hernández, Carlos Saavedra, Elena Santos and Patricia Domínguez are recorded questioning why the item was presented without the full information. The accounts were not approved.
- The presidency and administrator were then approved by the vote-eligible pool, with dissent by the LPB/Cox/Palacios/Thompson represented perimeter recorded.
- The quota proposal moved from approximately EUR171.89 to EUR22.26 per bungalow and was given proposed retroactive effect from January 2010 through December 2015.
- The act then says Community arrears fell considerably as a result of that recalculation and proceeds to debt-certification/recovery authorisations.
- The meeting authorised the board's bank signatories and contemplated additional accounts.
- Under requests/questions, Patricia Domínguez said accounting documentation held by LPB would be provided and requested treatment of payments said to have been made for the Community. The administrator committed that within 15 days of receiving the documents there would be an exhaustive audit report, a dedicated meeting and, if there was no agreement, an extraordinary meeting. Whether that promised audit/reconciliation sequence occurred is an OPEN QUESTION.

## 7. 11 June 2016 Las Palmas recorded meeting

The source family is a later transcription of a 144-minute voice recording dated **11 June 2016**. Contemporaneous emails place the meeting at Juan Carlos Prieto's office, Calle Emilio Castelar 4, fifth floor, Las Palmas. The native audio and metadata remain the evidential master; later square-bracket editorial comments in the transcript are not spoken evidence.

The transcript identifies Carlos Saavedra Rodríguez del Palacio and Miguel Hernández Lorenzo of PwC Tenerife as representing LPB. Important source-controlled propositions include:

- Carlos describes the exercise as establishing what had been paid, who paid it and on whose account;
- the participants expressly distinguish the Owners' Community from the exploitation vehicle(s);
- historic accounting, maintenance, utilities, owner charges, private-unit access, deeds, voting consequences, the insolvency administrator and prospective governance/operator arrangements are all discussed;
- Carlos and Miguel directly challenge the economic logic of generating owner charges where the exploitation structure had borne the relevant costs;
- Miguel links alleged Community debt to exclusion from voting and says the insolvency administrator needs to understand the objective data;
- Prieto proposes a later Junta to reconcile qualifying owner-paid expenses against alleged Community debt;
- Carlos expressly describes the June exercise as work committed to at the earlier Junta, creating a direct documentary bridge between 26 April and 11 June;
- Francisco Mario Matos Matas participates in the later part of the meeting and, during discussion of competing positions, says in substance that they are playing a poker game and each side plays the strategy it chooses. The quote is evidence of that participant's description, not proof of an unlawful scheme.

## 8. The unitary mechanism that was plainly visible by June 2016

The documents support a **knowledge-of-mechanism** proposition, not a culpability finding:

`historic allocation of common/exploitation costs → asserted Community debt → arrears status → voting eligibility/exclusion → Community governance and litigation authority → interface with the insolvency administrator/concurso → access/private-unit/operation constraints → need for accounting and governance reconciliation`.

PwC did not have merely incidental exposure to that mechanism. Its professionals represented named private owners at the April meeting, challenged incomplete financial presentation, performed legal/accounting work around the debt/governance dispute and then participated in the June reconciliation meeting.

What remains to be proved is the later duty/action chain:

`actual knowledge retained/available → later PwC legal entity/team/mandate → former-client/conflict/independence duties → information barriers / escalations → action or omission → materiality / causation`.

Without that chain, the proper classification is **prior professional knowledge-holder / witness / records-and-conflict inquiry**, not culpable participant.
