# RICPE / San Telmo webinar control — 2020–2021

Status: mandatory evidence-control / continuity file  
Date: 19 August 2026

## Purpose
This file preserves the source-controlled record of two separate public events that are material to the Sun Park / RIC Private Equity / professional-perimeter chronology:

1. the **RIC Private Equity webinar of 11 November 2020**, in which the CAM / Sun Park project was presented to investors and the RICPE control, due-diligence and approval architecture was described; and
2. the **San Telmo Abogados y Economistas #UnCaféenSanTelmo event of 30 November 2021**, in which RICPE Director General Enrique Guerra was interviewed by San Telmo partner Eduardo Sánchez.

The events must not be collapsed. The first is a RICPE investor-facing presentation. The second is a later public San Telmo/RICPE professional-media event. Neither event, by itself, proves criminal coordination, a San Telmo mandate for RICPE, transfer of information from the Administrador Concursal to Eduardo Sánchez, or wrongdoing by any participant.

## Evidential grammar
Every proposition derived from these sources must be classified as one of:
- verified event/source fact;
- source-authored or speaker statement;
- contemporaneous party/client allegation;
- inference;
- open question;
- evidence gap.

A speaker's claim in a webinar is not converted into an independently verified fact merely because it was made publicly.

# SOURCE A — RIC PRIVATE EQUITY WEBINAR, 11 NOVEMBER 2020

## Source metadata
Primary surviving source: `RIC PRIVATE EQUITY WEBINAR 11NOV2020.pdf`, 48 pages, preserved in the Gmail evidential record and reviewed page-by-page on 19 August 2026.

The source records a RICPE investor-facing webinar in which Dunia Pérez, Murli Kessomal, Enrique Guerra and José Daniel Acosta Matos, among others, described RICPE, its control architecture and the CAM tourism-renovation projects.

## Page-level proposition matrix

### p.6 — control / CNMV representation
**Source statement — Dunia Pérez.** She describes RICPE as giving investors security through a team that studies projects across fiscal, mercantile and other areas and refers to the structure as being controlled/supervised by the CNMV.

**Boundary:** this is a speaker representation about the regulatory/control environment. It is not evidence that CNMV approved, endorsed or individually verified the Sun Park project or every RICPE step.

### pp.10–11 — governmental / tax reports and investor security
**Source statement — Murli Kessomal.** He describes projects as requiring reports involving the Canary Islands Government and AEAT and uses strong language about investor security and administrative assurance.

**Boundary:** the webinar wording must not be paraphrased as a finding that the Government, AEAT or another administration guaranteed the investment. The exact legal nature, date and project-specific scope of any report must be verified independently.

### p.12 — Enrique Guerra role
**Verified source fact.** Enrique Guerra is presented as **Director General de RIC Private Equity**.

### p.13 — investee selection representation
**Source statement — Enrique Guerra.** RICPE invests only in solvent, leading companies and refers to access to bank financing as part of the assessment.

### p.16 — named professional-control architecture
**Source statement — Enrique Guerra.** He describes the professional/control perimeter and states, in substance:
- legal work is handled by **Uría Menéndez Madrid**;
- technical/architectural review forms part of the structure;
- AON is given as an example in the insurance/risk perimeter;
- financial due diligence is undertaken within the RICPE investment process; and
- legal/fiscal due diligence of the company and, expressly, **“en especial del proyecto”** is attributed to **PRICEWATERHOUSE**.

**High-value control point:** this is materially stronger than a generic statement that a PwC entity audited RICPE. It is a public RICPE Director-General representation placing Pricewaterhouse within legal/fiscal due diligence of the company and especially the project.

**Boundary:** the statement does not by itself identify the PwC legal entity, engagement letter, responsible team, exact project, scope, qualifications, reliance terms or work product. Those remain production questions.

### p.17 — guarantees, risk control and continuing financial-state monitoring
**Source statement — Enrique Guerra.** He refers to shareholder/personal guarantees and mortgages over other property, quarterly risk management and continuing review/control of financial statements through the professional due-diligence perimeter, again referring to PRICEWATERHOUSE.

**Open questions:** identify the engagement, reporting cadence, recipient, exact investee/project files and whether the described monitoring covered CAM/Sun Park.

### p.18 — José Daniel Acosta Matos role
**Verified source fact.** José Daniel Acosta Matos is presented as Director General of Construcciones Acosta Matos and a member of the RICPE board/governance perimeter.

### pp.19–21 — acquisition / project-perimeter narrative
**Source statements — José Daniel Acosta Matos.** He describes CAM as having acquired assets with “ciertas complicaciones”, identifies three properties/projects and explains that three tourism-renovation projects were introduced into the RICPE portfolio.

**Boundary:** “acquired” is a speaker's ownership narrative to be tested against deeds, registry entries, insolvency orders, third-party ownership and later internal RICPE title records.

### p.27 — Sun Park project description
**Source statement — José Daniel Acosta Matos.** Sun Park in Playa Blanca is presented as a complex of approximately **220 tourist apartments** to be transformed into a **four-star hotel**, with an indicated investment of approximately **€12 million**.

### p.29 — contemplated timing / financing
**Source statement.** The presentation describes works as anticipated for the first quarter of 2021 and the financing/investment timetable extending through 2021.

### p.30 — RICPE portfolio / RIC materialisation
**Source statement.** The three renovation projects are described as incorporated into the RICPE portfolio for investors seeking to materialise RIC.

### p.31 — three-asset purchase narrative
**Source statement.** CAM is described as having bought the three assets.

### p.32 — ownership and encumbrance representation
**Source statement — high-value contradiction/control point.** The presentation says, in substance, that the three complexes had been bought with the company's own funds, that the three properties were free of charges, and that the product was owned by the company without mortgage charges.

**Mandatory boundary:** do **not** state this as verified title. It is an investor-facing ownership/encumbrance representation. It must be reconciled against registry/deed evidence, the insolvency record, individual/third-party units and the later 2021 RICPE internal certificate describing fragmented ownership.

### p.41 — additional mortgage-guarantee representation
**Source statement — José Daniel Acosta Matos.** CAM is described as offering investors an additional mortgage guarantee through another property/asset.

### p.42 — pipeline / institutional-event representation
**Source statement — Enrique Guerra.** Four projects are described, two as advanced, with a possible investment volume around €10 million that year. He also refers to an institutional event attended by the President of the Canary Islands and an AEAT delegate.

**Boundary:** attendance at an institutional event is not approval or validation of Sun Park, title, investor statements or RICPE's legal analysis.

### p.44 — Meeting Point / fund and due-diligence narrative
**Source statement — Enrique Guerra.** He discusses a Meeting Point-related structure and states that several projects owned by investment funds had undergone very extensive due diligence. He says RICPE analysis started around February 2020 and contrasts project timing, including delay in the José Acosta project relating to licensing.

**Boundary:** identify which projects and entities each “due” reference applies to. Do not migrate due diligence from one project to Sun Park without source proof.

### p.46 — future investment pipeline
**Source statement — Enrique Guerra.** He refers to an expected future volume around €50–60 million and another José Acosta investment of approximately €12 million.

### p.47 — responsibility language
**Source statement — Enrique Guerra.** He uses unusually strong accountability language, saying in substance that the Director General, Board and experts involved respond personally/patrimonially and that their maximum responsibility is to analyse and document projects.

**Boundary:** this is the speaker's description of responsibility/accountability. It does not itself determine the legal liability of any individual or adviser.

### p.48 — formal approval sequence
**Source statement — Enrique Guerra.** He describes a multi-stage process before Board approval, including:
1. a favourable binding report from the investment department;
2. a favourable risk-control report, described as externalised to **Intermoney**;
3. favourable tax/fiscal report and relevant administrative files;
4. a favourable recommendation from the Director General; and
5. Board consideration/approval thereafter.

**High-value production consequence:** the described process should generate identifiable records. For Sun Park/CAM seek the investment report, Intermoney risk report, tax/fiscal report, administrative files, DG recommendation, Board paper/minutes, conditions, reservations and later updates/corrections.

## Strong safe conclusions from Source A
1. By 11 November 2020, RICPE/CAM publicly presented Sun Park as a RICPE-portfolio tourism-renovation project of approximately €12 million.
2. RICPE Director General Enrique Guerra publicly attributed legal/fiscal due diligence of the company and especially the project to **Pricewaterhouse**.
3. RICPE publicly described a layered control/approval architecture capable of producing discrete records, including an investment-department report, external Intermoney risk control, fiscal/tax material, DG recommendation and Board decision.
4. José Daniel Acosta Matos publicly advanced an ownership narrative in which CAM/company had bought the relevant complexes and the properties were presented as owned/free of charges. This is a source statement requiring reconciliation, not verified title.
5. The later 2021 fragmented-title record therefore creates a finite chronology question: when did RICPE, CAM, its Board, advisers and control providers know the actual unit-by-unit position, and what correction, qualification, disclosure or governance action followed?
6. References to CNMV, AEAT, the Canary Islands Government or institutional attendance are not to be presented as regulatory endorsement of the project.

- common-firm association proves a common purpose, conspiracy or professional breach.

**Mandatory rule:** same firm ≠ shared knowledge. Public co-presentation ≠ mandate. Professional proximity ≠ wrongdoing.
