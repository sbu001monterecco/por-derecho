# Sun Park — unitary scan from 26 April to 11 June 2016

**Prepared:** 19 August 2026  
**Purpose:** reconstruct what was plainly visible to the participating advisers and parties before, during and after the 26 April 2016 Community meeting and at the later recorded Las Palmas meeting.  
**Status:** evidence-led reconstruction; no finding of professional breach or criminality.

## Executive proposition

The 26 April meeting was not an isolated Community event and PwC's presence was not incidental. The source family shows a continuous professional workstream:

`pre-meeting debt / accounts / authority dispute → PwC strategy and named owner representation at the 26-Apr Junta → immediate challenge / AC / convenio / regulatory follow-up → accounting reconstruction of 2008–2015 costs → PwC-organised follow-up with the Community-side lawyer/administrator → 11-Jun recorded reconciliation meeting in Las Palmas → later challenge / AC-contact work`.

The important knowledge proposition is therefore narrower and stronger than a generic claim that “PwC knew Sun Park”:

> By June 2016 the participating PwC Canary Islands professionals had direct exposure to the mechanism by which historic operating/common costs, asserted Community debt, arrears status, voting eligibility, Community governance, private-owner access, the exploitation structure and the insolvency process interacted.

The evidence does not, without a further duty/action chain, establish that PwC later breached professional duties or assisted any later wrongdoing.

## A. Before the 26 April Junta

### 6 April 2016 — Community-side preparation

The president's circular says the Junta could be called only after sufficient documentation had been obtained and says the president, lawyers, Community administrator, advisers and a group of owners had been conducting investigations/steps with banks, courts and public bodies.

**Status:** VERIFIED as the president's written statement; not independent proof of the accuracy of every asserted investigation or conclusion.

### 19–25 April — PwC work and the live dispute

PwC's own time record places its team in document review, strategy, legal research and meeting preparation. In parallel, LPB transmitted a written challenge to the debt/morosity/expense premise and asked for document-based correction/postponement. The Community rejected suspension on 25 April and maintained an arrears position.

**What was already plain before the room opened:** debt, accounts, authority, owner information rights, morosity, expenditure allocation and access were not afterthoughts. They were the subject of written, adviser-led dispute before the vote.

## B. 26 April 2016 — the Junta itself

### 1. The meeting was deliberately evidential

The act states that the administrator sought consent to record the meeting and that all attendees consented without exception. Security was present. Lawyers/advisers were identified.

This matters because the meeting was being conducted as a contested, documented institutional event rather than an informal owner gathering.

### 2. PwC occupied multiple roles inside the meeting

The act records:

- Elena Santos Ramos representing Víctor Fernández Cox / Local 1;
- Miguel Hernández Lorenzo representing Juan Carlos Fernández Cox / bungalow 507;
- Carlos Saavedra Rodríguez representing Simon Peter Thompson / bungalow 708.

Patricia Domínguez represented LPB and Mariano Palacios Fernández.

This gives PwC direct exposure not only to LPB's majority/insolvency position but also to named private-owner/unit questions.

### 3. Participation and voting were radically different quantities

The act records 89.727% present/represented, while only 11.039% was treated as vote-eligible after the asserted current-payment check. LPB alone was recorded at 72.976%.

The safe conclusion is **not** that a competent court had already found the voting exclusion unlawful. The relevant knowledge point is that PwC could see, in real time, that alleged arrears determined who could vote and therefore who could control Community decisions despite the much larger participation represented in the room.

### 4. Accounts incompleteness was explicit

The administrator said only limited records were available for the 2010–2015 accounts. The act records Miguel Hernández, Carlos Saavedra, Elena Santos and Patricia Domínguez questioning why the item was presented without all the information. The accounts were not approved.

This is important for later debt analysis: the same meeting acknowledged incomplete accounts and then proceeded into quota recalculation, arrears and governance decisions.

### 5. Quota/debt recalculation affected the governance problem

The act records a proposed shift from approximately EUR171.89 to EUR22.26 per bungalow, with retroactive treatment back to January 2010. It then records that morosity fell considerably and proceeds to approve debt-certification/recovery authority.

The evidential issue is the chain:

`incomplete historic financial record → new retroactive quota basis → changed arrears figures → voting/recovery/governance consequences`.

Whether every legal step in that chain was valid remains a separate legal question.

### 6. A reconciliation mechanism was promised

In requests/questions, Patricia offered to provide accounting records and sought answers concerning payments said to have been made for Community account. The administrator undertook to produce an exhaustive audit report within 15 days after receipt, hold a dedicated meeting and call an extraordinary Junta if no agreement was reached.

This creates a finite documentary follow-up:

`delivery of records → receipt → audit/report → dedicated meeting → extraordinary meeting if necessary → reconciliation outcome`.

Failure or completion of this chain must be proven, not assumed.

### Accounting reconstruction

On 6 June Jonathan Simó supplied PwC and the client team a revised analysis plus detailed historic ledgers/financial material. Miguel Hernández responded that the version was acceptable and anticipated that the Community would ask about the support/detail for the expenses.

This is strong evidence that PwC was not receiving a bare allegation; it was reviewing a structured accounting reconstruction supported by ledger-level material.

### Location and attendees

Contemporaneous PwC email places the follow-up meeting at Juan Carlos Prieto's office, **Calle Emilio Castelar 4, planta 5, Las Palmas**. Travel/coordination emails identify the PwC team and Jonathan Simó as preparing to attend.

The current source family supports **11 June 2016** as the recorded-meeting date. Do not silently change it to 10 June unless native metadata proves otherwise.

## E. 11 June recorded Las Palmas meeting — what the record plainly shows

### Authentication control

Current working source: later transcript of a 144-minute voice file, transcribed 17 March 2021. Native audio/metadata controls. Square-bracket editorial comments are not spoken evidence.

### 1. PwC's role was active and representational

The transcript repeatedly identifies Carlos Saavedra and Miguel Hernández of PwC Tenerife as representing LPB.

### 2. Carlos defined a forensic/reconciliation exercise

Carlos explains that the task is to identify what had been paid, who paid it and on whose account. Later, when Francisco Mario Matos arrives, Carlos says this was the work they had committed to at the earlier Junta.

This is the clearest bridge between April and June.

### 3. The Owners' Community and exploitation vehicle were expressly separated

Jonathan Simó and PwC distinguish the Owners' Community from the exploitation structure and analyse the contractual/economic premise that the exploitation vehicle bore categories of maintenance and supply costs.

That distinction is central because LPB was the debtor in Concurso 36/2012, while other ownership/operating perimeters were legally distinct.

### 4. The debt question was understood as a governance question

The conversation does not treat debt merely as a balance-sheet number. Miguel expressly links alleged debt to other owners being kept from voting and says the insolvency administrator needs to understand objective data. Prieto proposes a future Junta to reconcile qualifying expenses against alleged debt.

Thus, by June the following mechanism was visible to everyone in the room:

`cost allocation → debt assertion → arrears → vote exclusion → Community power → insolvency consequences`.

### 5. Private-owner/access/title questions were specific

The meeting discusses owners unable to access units, whether units remained in/out of exploitation, deeds and the future treatment of private units. Miguel asks specifically about Thompson and a deed. Coupled with the April representation list, this moves PwC's knowledge below the generic level of “there are minority owners.”

### 6. Majority / hotel-operation reality was acknowledged in discussion

Prieto tells the LPB/PwC side, in substance, that they had the majority and determined the hotel business, while attempting to find a workable treatment for private owners that would not frustrate tourist use.

This is a participant statement and must not be transformed into an independent legal finding about precise title percentages or operating rights on every date.

### 7. FMMM was directly engaged with Carlos/PwC

Francisco Mario Matos joins the meeting and debates expenses, access, authority and proof with PwC. At one point he describes the situation as a poker game in which each side plays its strategy.

The evidential significance is direct professional interaction and awareness of adversarial strategy. It is not evidence, by itself, of an unlawful common plan.

### 8. PwC was pressing a substantive position on the debt logic

Miguel characterises the large debt accumulation, in his view, as fictitious and argues that the objective data needed to be understood by the insolvency administrator. Carlos questions why owner quotas were imposed in periods in which the relevant costs were said not to correspond to owners.

These are attributable professional-side positions in a recorded discussion. They are not judicial findings.

### INFERENCE

That body of work created a substantial same-asset knowledge base capable of being relevant to later conflict/former-client/independence review if PwC later accepted a mandate touching Sun Park, RICPE, Acosta Matos, HNT or MYND.

### NOT YET PROVED

The current April–June corpus does not by itself prove:

- that the Community organ was unlawfully “hijacked”;
- that every asserted debt was legally fictitious;
- that PwC had a specific legal/deontological duty to take a particular later action;
- that PwC failed to perform a required safeguard;
- that PwC later acted for CAM/RICPE/HNT/MYND on Sun Park;
- that earlier confidential information was transferred or used;
- that any PwC act or omission caused later dispossession, financing or public-funds outcomes.

Those issues require the later-engagement / duty / knowledge-retention / action / causation chain.
