## Comparison controls

1. ACTÚA, the March GESVALT RICS opinion, the June GESVALT ECO certificate and the GESVALT pericial had different commissioning parties and valuation purposes. The comparison is evidential, not an assertion that every headline figure is arithmetically interchangeable.
2. ACTÚA itself describes the whole 262-title complex as a single functional unit and the 31-title branch as operational/common equipment. That internal description is directly relevant to operational prejudice even though ACTÚA then supplies an individual branch figure.
3. GESVALT’s whole-hotel figures do not make the standalone legal value of the 31 titles €25.6m or €26.4m. They test whether a replacement-cost allocation captured the destruction of whole-hotel operating value when control assets were detached.
4. ACTÚA’s €489,361.96 is not a judicial valuation finding. The €400,000 price is the transaction figure in Protocol 2150 and the AC’s reports/plan.

1. 28 November 2018: Protocol 2150 recites separation and sale of the 31 operational titles to CAM for €400,000.
2. 24 October 2019: the court refuses convalidation because the deed was executed without judicial authority.
3. No located controlled record completes the required reversal chain: purchaser refund, estate repayment, reconveyance, Registry cancellation, tax reversal and final ledger reconciliation.
4. The later 12 May 2021 tender/18 May 2021 authority again use a separate €400,000 locales/pools/solarium component, and the 2022 transaction retains that component alongside the apartment branch.

**Controlled inference:** the later reuse of the same asset branch and price is affirmative evidence that the 2018 transaction architecture and practical control advantage were carried forward; it is not solely an inference from missing records. Gil calls that earlier practical transfer the “golden trigger” for the later liquidation. Final title and restitution status still require certified Registry, bank and estate-ledger production.
